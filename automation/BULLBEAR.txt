BULL / BEAR: FUNDAMENTALS ANALYSIS RUNBOOK
=========================================

A separate analysis from the redline dashboards. The redlines (RUNBOOK.txt)
ask what the company changed in its wording. This asks what the filing does
to the investment case, for and against, on fundamentals. Each company has
one page, companies/<t>/bullbear/index.html, updated after every new 10-K,
20-F or 10-Q (after the redline update for that filing, in the same run).
The page is published unreviewed, like the dashboards, and the repository is
public: never write the SEC contact email anywhere.

The reader is an investor who owns or is considering the stock. They want
the evidence in this filing that should make them more or less confident,
weighed fairly. A page that only lists risks is as useless as one that only
lists positives.


1. INPUTS
---------
Use the same downloaded filings and extracted sections as the redline run
(RUNBOOK.txt section 2). Read, in this order:
  a. companies/<t>/bullbear/thesis.txt: the investment case as 5-8 pillars.
     Each pillar states what the bull case assumes, what the bear case
     fears, the current stance, and what tests it. If it does not exist,
     write it first from the dashboards' themes and tracked signals.
  b. the bull/bear page as it stands, especially the scoreboard, the
     fundamentals table and the stated-targets table.
  c. python scripts/financials.py <TICKER> --years 4 --quarters 6
     (reported XBRL figures and derived ratios; "!" marks a sharp move)
  d. redlines and full text of: notes, contingencies, subsequent_events,
     mda_liquidity, mda_critical_estimates, mda, risk_factors,
     legal_proceedings, business (in that order). Table rows appear as
     "label | value | value".


2. THE FUNDAMENTALS QUESTIONS
-----------------------------
Answer each from the new filing in one or two lines, citing the note or
MD&A heading, and say whether it got better, worse or held versus the prior
filing. Write the answers to companies/<t>/bullbear/<period>.txt.
  1. Growth and pricing: revenue drivers (volume vs price vs mix), segment
     growth, backlog / remaining performance obligations, bookings.
  2. Margins and unit economics: gross and operating margin by segment,
     cost drivers named in the MD&A, operating leverage.
  3. Earnings quality: how much of net income is one-off or non-operating
     (tax items, gains, fair-value marks, PIK, accruals, catch-up payments)
     in either direction.
  4. Cash conversion: operating cash flow vs net income and what drove the
     gap; free cash flow; DSO and other working capital.
  5. Capital allocation: capex and its stated return, buybacks, dividends,
     M&A, equity issuance, debt-funded vs cash-funded.
  6. Balance sheet and contingent exposure: debt, maturities, covenants,
     ratings language, liquidity; guarantees, purchase and supply
     commitments, leases not yet commenced, VIEs, TRAs.
  7. Concentration: customers, suppliers, distributors, geographies, payers.
  8. Accounting judgments: critical estimates, useful lives, reserve
     development, segment or metric redefinitions, restatements.
  9. Legal, regulatory and governance: proceedings, investigations,
     rule changes, related parties, insider plans.
 10. Guidance and commitments: every number, range or target management
     stated before (the stated-targets table). Met, beaten, moved, dropped
     or not mentioned?


3. THE TWO PASSES
-----------------
Do both passes over the same evidence, separately, before writing anything.

Bull pass: what in this filing makes a pillar stronger? Look for a risk
removed or narrowed, a hedge dropped, a target met or beaten early, margins
or cash conversion improving, debt repaid or refinanced on better terms,
concentration falling, a lawsuit dismissed or settled cheaply, a reserve
released for a stated reason, guidance raised, a new customer, product or
contract quantified, capital returned from free cash flow.

Bear pass: what makes a pillar weaker? Look for one-off earnings, cash
lagging earnings, new guarantees or commitments, rising leverage or
loosened covenants, concentration rising, reserves strengthened or
released without a reason, estimates changed in the company's favour,
targets dropped or missed, new proceedings, metrics no longer disclosed.

Each finding names its pillar, its side (Bull or Bear), the evidence quoted
verbatim, the size of it relative to something that matters (from
financials.py: "$45.95B maximum exposure = 55% of FY2025 operating income";
or "not quantified in the filing"), whether it is Stated by the filing or
Inferred by you, and what the next filing must show to confirm or refute it.

A side with nothing material says so in one line ("No bullish evidence on
the balance sheet this quarter"). Never pad a side to balance the count,
and never drop real evidence to balance it either. Aim for the 3-6 most
material findings per side.

Skeptical check before writing: for every finding ask whether an investor
who knows the company would change their confidence. Check every "first
time" or "since" claim against the earlier filings. Check that each bull
finding has been looked at as a bear finding too (and vice versa): a
buyback is bullish if funded from free cash flow and bearish if funded by
debt while cash conversion falls.


4. THE PAGE
-----------
Copy automation/templates/bullbear.html for a new company; keep its
structure, CSS and script. Sections, top to bottom:
  - header: company, ticker, latest filing covered, links to the 10-K
    dashboard and 10-Q scanner
  - scoreboard: one row per pillar: stance (Strengthening / Intact /
    Weakening / Broken), the strongest bull and bear evidence in one line
    each, and the filing that last moved it. A stance moves only when
    findings point the same way across two or more filings.
  - latest filing: Bull and Bear findings side by side, each a card with
    pillar, quote(s) labelled by period, magnitude, basis and next.
  - fundamentals table: the ten questions as rows, one column per filing
    covered (newest first), each cell the one-line answer with a better /
    worse / held marker.
  - stated targets: sentence, filing it was stated in, number, and the
    outcome in each later filing (met / beaten / moved / dropped / not
    mentioned).
  - earlier filings: previous Bull/Bear findings, collapsed, newest first.
Quotes must be verbatim and are checked by verify_dashboard.py like the
dashboards' quotes. Root index.html: the company's bullbear link (bb field).


5. VERIFY AND PUBLISH
---------------------
    python scripts/verify_dashboard.py companies/<t>/bullbear/index.html --since origin/main
must print OK. Commit the page, thesis.txt and the <period>.txt answers in
the same commit as that filing's redline update, or as the next commit.
