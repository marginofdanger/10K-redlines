NVDA 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of NVIDIA's 10-Q filings, from FY2024 Q3 (October 2023, filed just after the October 2023 export rules) through FY2027 Q2 (July 2026, filed August 26, 2026), including the FY2024, FY2025, and FY2026 10-K boundary transitions. Each 10-Q restates only the risk factors that changed ("Other than the risk factors listed below, there have been no material changes"), so a risk factor missing from a quarter has not been withdrawn; MD&A, Notes and Legal Proceedings are redlined in full. Transitions from FY2025 Q3 on were re-done in October 2026 from sentence-level signals, with a shift label and a phrase-history line on every finding.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001045810) | 9 quarterly filings + 3 10-K baselines | FY2024 Q3 – FY2027 Q2 | Analysis: June 2026, extended October 2026; FY2025 Q3 onward re-done with sentence signals October 2026

Era Overview

1. FY24 Q3 → FY2024 10-K

The baseline quarter disclosed that licensable destinations had contributed approximately 20-25% of Data Center revenue and guided to a significant fourth-quarter China decline "more than offset" by other regions. The 10-K then quantified the annual outcome: China fell from 19% to 14% of Data Center revenue, with a "mid-single digit" Q4 run-rate expected to persist. Purchase obligations edged down from $17.1B to $16.1B.

2. FY2024 10-K → FY25 Q1

Blackwell entered disclosure for the first time, with initial demand described as "well ahead of the projected supply." Customer concentration moved to a lettered-customer format (A 13%, B 11%) plus two indirect customers at 10%+, and the Singapore billing-vs-shipping footnote first appeared. Gross margin hit its series peak of 78.4%.

3. FY25 Q1 → Q2

NVIDIA disclosed a Blackwell GPU mask change and the first Blackwell inventory provisions, formalized an annual architecture cadence, and rewrote the demand-mismatch risk factor header to lead with manufacturing lead times. China commentary reversed from "down significantly" to "grew sequentially," and lettered customers expanded to four (A 14%, B 11%, C 11%, D 10%).

4. FY25 Q2 → Q3

The full-year gross-margin ("mid-70% range") and opex guides were dropped, as were the inference share ("over 40%") and Hopper's "demand is strong" sentence. In their place: Blackwell demand "expected to exceed supply for several quarters in fiscal year 2026," a mask change reported as "successful," and a margin cause that moved from "low-yielding Blackwell material" to a "mix shift ... to more complex and higher cost systems." The risk factors named Blackwell systems into license scope, added Beijing as a possible steerer of customers, and deleted generative AI's "long-term trajectory is unknown" hedge.

5. FY25 Q3 → FY2025 10-K

The 10-K first disclosed the January 15, 2025 "AI Diffusion" IFR with its worldwide licensing requirement. Three risk statements that had stood for six periods were restated as having happened: architecture transitions "have been" costly, NVIDIA "had to reduce average selling prices," and it "is subject to" export restrictions. Q3's Blackwell demand-exceeds-supply sentence and the $1.7B early-payment figure were dropped, and China went from "below" to "well below" pre-control levels. FY2025 closed at $130.5B revenue and 75.0% gross margin.

6. FY2025 10-K → FY26 Q1 (H20 charge quarter)

On April 9, 2025 the USG imposed a license requirement on H20 exports to China, producing a $4.5B charge and conditional foreclosure language ("we would effectively be foreclosed"). The AI Diffusion IFR "would have imposed" a worldwide requirement but was rescinded, and regulators' requests "have been" costly. New: Singapore billings with "over 99%" from U.S.-based customers, DeepSeek/Qwen, a "one-year product cadence," U.S. manufacturing plans, "often non-binding" purchase announcements, the "data centers, energy, and capital" constraint, CoreWeave, $6.2B of customer advances and the unnamed "AI research and deployment company." Gross margin fell to 60.5%.

7. FY26 Q1 → Q2

August 2025 H20 licenses arrived with no revenue and a USG expectation of "15% of the revenue," not codified. Beijing "publicly questioned" H20 "built-in vulnerabilities." MD&A deleted its China outlook (the market as "a significant opportunity," future revenue harm) and moved margin pressure from an "initial ramp" to the permanent shift to "full-scale datacenter systems." The commitments note stopped splitting supply from cloud and dropped DGX Cloud as a use; the controlled-product list and CoreWeave were de-named; the buyback purpose sentences went as a $60B authorization arrived. Customer A reached 23%.

8. FY26 Q2 → Q3

Three China sentences moved from hypothetical to actual in one filing: "we are unable to create and deliver" an approvable product (now needing Beijing's approval too), export controls "have" had a disproportionate impact, and results "will be further harmed" if the rules simply stay. Beijing "has encouraged" local purchases, SAMR found a Mellanox violation, and the USG levy widened to "15% or more" of all licensed products. New: OpenAI, Anthropic (up to $10B) and Intel ($5B) investments, a guarantee risk factor with an $860M lease guarantee, the GAIN AI Act, and the headquarters-basis geography that ended the Singapore footnote. Indirect concentration lost its count ("some").

9. FY26 Q3 → FY2026 10-K

The Anthropic agreement and Intel commitment named in Q3 vanished from the 10-K, while OpenAI moved from letter of intent to "finalizing." Lease guarantees grew from $860M to $3.5B, and the risk factor was retitled from "may be required to satisfy financial obligations" to "counterparty risks." Manufacturing commitments nearly doubled to $95.2B under a rewritten "longer future ordering horizons" note, commercial paper capacity went from $575M to $25.0B, and DGX Cloud was dropped from the stated purpose of the cloud commitments. China's government now "discouraged" rather than "restricted" purchases. The H200 license arrived with a 25% tariff. Customer letter labels were dropped, the investment risk factor dropped its past tense, the data-center lease pipeline tripled to $22.7B, and the share of Taiwan-HQ revenue attributed to U.S. and European end customers fell from 86% (Q3) to 76% (full year). FY2026 closed at $215.9B revenue and 71.1% gross margin, and Note 2 disclosed the $17B Groq license.

10. FY2026 10-K → FY27 Q1 (filed May 20, 2026)

Data Center was recast into Hyperscale (+115% y/y) and ACIE (+74%), and Gaming, ProViz and Automotive were folded into Edge Computing. Hopper shipments to China were zero (vs $4.6B), with a carve-out for gaming GPUs. OpenAI's name disappeared, and investees were now said to "may indirectly purchase or use" NVIDIA products, in a quarter with $18.6B of private investments and $16.0B of unrealized gains. Commitments reached $119B and for the first time ran to FY2031; investment commitments were $27B and a $32.4B lease pipeline was disclosed. The competition risk factor admits customer ASICs "may not require all of the features" NVIDIA sells. NVIDIA disclosed for the first time that it has assisted authorities on "attempted diversion." The quarter also brought an $80B buyback authorization, a dividend increase from $0.01 to $0.25, and three customers at 21/17/16%.

11. FY27 Q1 → Q2 (filed Aug 26, 2026)

NVIDIA moved from supplier to credit provider. It guaranteed up to $105B of OpenAI's 20-year leases at SB Energy's Ohio campus, committed $36B to backstop AI-cloud capacity, signed MOUs for $500B+ financing platforms, and wrote that its AI-cloud and model-maker customers "lack" investment-grade financing. Supply commitments rose from $119B to $279B, with payments now running into FY2029. NVIDIA issued $25B of notes and added a debt risk factor. Receivables grew to $63.1B on new payment terms of up to one year. H200 China licenses produced a $0.4B charge and under 1% of Data Center revenue. Competition and demand-mismatch risk factors left the update set; they are incorporated by reference, not withdrawn.

MD&A

▶ China Data Center contribution: "20-25%" quarterly framing becomes annual 19% → 14% with "mid-single digit" run-rate Changed

FY2024 Q3, filed weeks after the October 2023 export rules, disclosed the licensable-destination contribution and guided the decline:

"Our sales to China and other affected destinations, derived from products that are now subject to licensing requirements, have consistently contributed approximately 20-25% of Data Center revenue over the past few quarters. We expect that our sales to these destinations will decline significantly in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2024, though we believe the decline will be more than offset by strong growth in other regions."

The FY2024 10-K replaced the quarterly framing with annual percentages and a forward range:

"Our sales to China decreased as a percentage of total Data Center revenue from 19% in fiscal year 2023 to 14% in fiscal year 2024... China represented a mid-single digit percentage of our Data Center revenue in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2024 due to USG licensing requirements and we expect China to be in a similar range in the first quarter of fiscal year 2025."
▶ Baseline demand sentence: "surged over the last three quarters... visibility extends into next year" Signal

The FY2024 Q3 MD&A contains the starting point of the demand/supply sentence trace (Signal #9):

"Demand for our data center systems and products has surged over the last three quarters and our demand visibility extends into next year."

This formulation is later superseded by Blackwell-specific supply language in FY2025 Q1 and the demand-exceeds-supply statement in FY2025 Q3.

Themes

1. Export Controls: From Quantified Exposure to Licenses That Do Not Sell

The arc runs from "approximately 20-25% of Data Center revenue" (FY24 Q3) through annual quantification (19% → 14%), the AI Diffusion IFR and its rescission, the April 9, 2025 H20 license requirement with its $4.5B charge, conditional foreclosure (FY26 Q1), and actual foreclosure (FY26 Q3). FY27 Q2 shows the licensing route failing twice. The H20 licenses "would have allowed" sales, but the PRC blocked them and the inventory went unsold. The H200 licenses repeat the pattern: PRC restriction, a $0.4B charge, shipments under 1% of Data Center revenue, and a 25% re-import tariff NVIDIA has not passed on to any customer. The 15% revenue-share sentence, carried since FY26 Q2, was deleted without ever being codified.

2. Balance-Sheet Commitment Inflection, Now Debt-Funded

Manufacturing/supply commitments went from $17.1B to $30.8B over six filings, then $50.3B → $95.2B → $119B → $279B over four. The horizon lengthened as they grew: 80% was payable within the fiscal year in FY27 Q1, 33% in FY27 Q2, with $88B due in FY2029. Over the same span cancellability went from "are" to "may be". FY27 Q2 also shows $366B in Note 10 commitments plus $56B of additional commitments and $108.5B of guarantees. For the first time in the series, the spending runs alongside new borrowing: $25B of notes in June 2026, senior notes of $33.5B, and a new debt risk factor. The liquidity-sufficiency sentence has stayed unenumerated since FY26 Q3.

3. Ecosystem Financing Becomes Credit Support

Through FY27 Q1 the arc was equity. The non-marketable portfolio grew from $1.5B to $42.3B, and NVIDIA disclosed that some investees "may indirectly purchase or use our products in the cloud." In FY27 Q2 NVIDIA takes on its customers' credit risk directly: a $105B cap on guarantees of OpenAI's leases, $36B of AI-cloud capacity backstops, $20B of leases signed for reassignment, payment terms of up to one year, equity stakes in "infrastructure financiers," and $500B+ financing-platform MOUs with optional residual-value support. The stated reason is that these customers "currently lack the ability to secure long-term infrastructure contracts and investment-grade financing capacity." In the same quarter the circularity sentence was deleted and "expect to continue" investing softened to "may continue."

4. Concentration: Revenue Ratchet Breaks, Receivables Ratchet Doesn't

The top direct customer went from 12% (FY24 Q3) to 23% (FY26 Q2), the FY26 10-K dropped the letter labels, and FY27 Q1 reported three customers at 21/17/16%. FY27 Q2 breaks that pattern: only one direct customer reached 10% (16%), the first such quarter since FY24 Q3. Taiwan-headquartered revenue more than doubled in the same quarter, which is consistent with volume shifting to ODM-type direct customers rather than end demand diversifying (an inference). Receivables did not ease: five customers held 70% of a $63.1B AR balance, a series high, on newly extended payment terms. The "AI research and deployment company" sentence has appeared in every filing since FY26 Q1.

5. Reporting Recasts at Peak Visibility

Two re-presentations came at the point of maximum attention to each line. The geography table switched from billing location to headquarters location in FY26 Q3, eliminating the Singapore line one quarter after it peaked at 22% of billings. The market platforms were recast to Data Center (Hyperscale / ACIE) plus Edge Computing in FY27 Q1. FY27 Q2 adds a third kind of change: the restated risk factors. Competition, which led Q1, and the demand-mismatch factor are no longer restated, and commitments/guarantees now lead. That is incorporation by reference, not withdrawal, but it changes what a reader of the quarter sees first. A Hyperscale/ACIE customer reclassification with a prior-period recast also arrived in Q2.
