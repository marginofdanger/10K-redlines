NVDA 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of NVIDIA's 10-Q filings, from FY2024 Q3 (October 2023, filed just after the October 2023 export rules) through FY2027 Q1 (April 2026, filed May 20, 2026), including the FY2024, FY2025, and FY2026 10-K boundary transitions. NVIDIA restates its full risk factors every quarter, so each transition is a complete redline of Risk Factors, MD&A, Notes, and Legal Proceedings.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001045810) | 8 quarterly filings + 3 10-K baselines | FY2024 Q3 – FY2027 Q1 | Analysis: June 2026

Era Overview

1. FY24 Q3 → FY2024 10-K

The baseline quarter disclosed that licensable destinations had contributed approximately 20-25% of Data Center revenue and guided to a significant fourth-quarter China decline "more than offset" by other regions. The 10-K then quantified the annual outcome: China fell from 19% to 14% of Data Center revenue, with a "mid-single digit" Q4 run-rate expected to persist. Purchase obligations edged down from $17.1B to $16.1B.

2. FY2024 10-K → FY25 Q1

Blackwell entered disclosure for the first time, with initial demand described as "well ahead of the projected supply." Customer concentration moved to a lettered-customer format (A 13%, B 11%) plus two indirect customers at 10%+, and the Singapore billing-vs-shipping footnote first appeared. Gross margin hit its series peak of 78.4%.

3. FY25 Q1 → Q2

NVIDIA disclosed a Blackwell GPU mask change and the first Blackwell inventory provisions, formalized an annual architecture cadence, and rewrote the demand-mismatch risk factor header to lead with manufacturing lead times. China commentary reversed from "down significantly" to "grew sequentially," and lettered customers expanded to four (A 14%, B 11%, C 11%, D 10%).

4. FY25 Q2 → Q3

The filing added an explicit statement that Blackwell demand "is expected to exceed supply for several quarters in fiscal year 2026" and named Blackwell systems into export-license scope. New risks appeared for U.S. automotive import restrictions and Chinese government steering of customers to local competitors; the "long-term trajectory is unknown" caveat on generative AI was deleted.

5. FY25 Q3 → FY2025 10-K

The 10-K first disclosed the January 15, 2025 "AI Diffusion" IFR with its worldwide licensing requirement. FY2025 closed at $130.5B revenue and 75.0% gross margin, with purchase obligations of $30.8B attributed to Blackwell capacity, and the China sentence softened to "grew in fiscal year 2025... remains well below" pre-control levels.

6. FY2025 10-K → FY26 Q1 (H20 charge quarter)

On April 9, 2025 the USG imposed a license requirement on H20 exports to China, producing a $4.5B charge and conditional foreclosure language ("we would effectively be foreclosed"). The quarter also quantified Singapore at 20% of billings (over 99% for U.S.-based customers), first named DeepSeek/Qwen, the one-year product cadence, U.S.-based manufacturing plans, and the unnamed "AI research and deployment company" in the concentration note. Gross margin fell to 60.5%.

7. FY26 Q1 → Q2

August 2025 H20 export licenses were disclosed alongside a USG expectation of a 15% revenue share that had not been codified by regulation. MD&A recorded ~$650M of H20 revenue to a non-China customer with a $180M reserve release and zero H20 sales to China. China's government publicly questioned whether H20 chips contain built-in vulnerabilities; a $60B buyback authorization and the OBBBA tax law were disclosed.

8. FY26 Q2 → Q3

Foreclosure language moved from conditional to actual ("We have effectively been foreclosed"), and a new strategic-investment risk factor covered the OpenAI letter of intent, the up-to-$10B Anthropic agreement, and the $5B Intel commitment. Geography reporting was recast from billing location to headquarters location, eliminating the Singapore line, and a new guarantee/counterparty risk factor disclosed an $860M facility-lease guarantee. China's antitrust regulator published an adverse preliminary finding on the Mellanox commitments.

9. FY26 Q3 → FY2026 10-K

The 10-K moved OpenAI from letter of intent to "finalizing an investment and partnership agreement" and timestamped the China foreclosure as of fiscal year-end. FY2026 closed at $215.9B revenue with gross margin down to 71.1% on the Hopper-HGX-to-full-systems transition and the H20 charge; Note 2 disclosed the December 2025 Groq license: $14.4B goodwill plus a $2.5B intangible with no customer contracts, products, or equity acquired.

10. FY2026 10-K → FY27 Q1 (filed May 20, 2026)

The heaviest quarter in the series: manufacturing/supply commitments reached $119B ($95B payable within FY2027), investment commitments $27B, $18.6B of private-company purchases in one quarter (non-marketable portfolio $42.3B), a $32.4B future lease pipeline, commercial paper capacity raised from $575M to $25B, an $80B buyback authorization with a dividend increase from $0.01 to $0.25, a platform recast to Data Center / Edge Computing, Rubin's first appearance, H200-to-China licenses carrying a 25% re-import tariff, and three customers at 21/17/16% of revenue with letter labels dropped.

MD&A

▶ China Data Center contribution: "20-25%" quarterly framing becomes annual 19% → 14% with "mid-single digit" run-rate Changed

FY2024 Q3, filed weeks after the October 2023 export rules, disclosed the licensable-destination contribution and guided the decline:

"Our sales to China and other affected destinations, derived from products that are now subject to licensing requirements, have consistently contributed approximately 20-25% of Data Center revenue over the past few quarters. We expect that our sales to these destinations will decline significantly in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2024, though we believe the decline will be more than offset by strong growth in other regions."

The FY2024 10-K replaced the quarterly framing with annual percentages and a forward range:

"Our sales to China decreased as a percentage of total Data Center revenue from 19% in fiscal year 2023 to 14% in fiscal year 2024... China represented a mid-single digit percentage of our Data Center revenue in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2024 due to USG licensing requirements and we expect China to be in a similar range in the first quarter of fiscal year 2025."
▶ Baseline demand sentence: "surged over the last three quarters... visibility extends into next year" Signal

The FY2024 Q3 MD&A contains the starting point of the demand/supply sentence trace (Signal #9):

"Demand for our data center systems and products has surged over the last three quarters and our demand visibility extends into next year."

This formulation is later superseded by Blackwell-specific supply language in FY2025 Q1 and the demand-exceeds-supply statement in FY2025 Q3.

The FY2024 10-K showed $16.1B plus $4.6B other (including $3.5B cloud) — the only sequential decline in the entire series before the climb to $119B. Customer concentration: Customer A at 12% of Q3 revenue; one direct customer at 13% for full-year FY2024. The August 21, 2023 $25.0B repurchase authorization was the first rung of a ladder that reaches $80B by May 2026.

Risk Factors

▶ April 9, 2025: H20 license requirement, $4.5B charge, $4.6B of pre-restriction Q1 H20 sales New

FY2026 Q1 (new language) — the core disclosure of the period:

"On April 9, 2025, the U.S. government, or USG, informed us that it requires a license for export to China (including Hong Kong and Macau) and D:5 countries, or to companies headquartered or with an ultimate parent therein, of our H20 integrated circuits and any other circuits achieving the H20's memory bandwidth, interconnect bandwidth, or combination thereof. As a result of these new requirements, we incurred a $4.5 billion charge in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026 associated with H20 excess inventory and purchase obligations as the demand for H20 products diminished. The $4.5 billion charge was less than what we initially anticipated as we were able to re-use certain materials. Sales of our H20 products were $4.6 billion for the first quarter of fiscal year 2026 prior to the new export licensing requirements."
▶ Conditional foreclosure language: "we would effectively be foreclosed" New

FY2026 Q1 (new language):

"We may be unable to create a competitive product for China's data center market that receives approval from the USG. In that event, we would effectively be foreclosed from competing in China's data center computing/compute market, with a material and adverse impact on our business, operating results, and financial condition."

Themes

1. Export Controls: Quantified Exposure to Foreclosure in Ten Filings

The arc runs from "approximately 20-25% of Data Center revenue" (FY24 Q3) through annual quantification (19% → 14%), the AI Diffusion IFR and its rescission, the April 9, 2025 H20 license requirement with its $4.5B charge, conditional foreclosure (FY26 Q1), actual foreclosure with "will be further harmed" (FY26 Q3), and the February 2026 H200 licenses carrying a U.S. inspection requirement and 25% re-import tariff. FY27 Q1 reports zero Data Center Hopper shipments to China against $4.6B a year earlier. The 15% revenue-share expectation was disclosed (FY26 Q2), widened to "15% or more... of our products" (FY26 Q3), and moved to past tense without ever being codified (FY27 Q1).

2. Balance-Sheet Commitment Inflection

Manufacturing/supply commitments went $17.1B → $30.8B over six filings, then $50.3B → $95.2B → $119B over three, with $95B payable within FY2027. The same FY27 Q1 filing added a $32.4B future lease pipeline, raised commercial paper capacity from $575M to $25B, scaled lease guarantees from $860M to $3.5B maximum exposure, and rewrote the commitments header to "data center-scale production and longer future ordering horizons." The liquidity-sufficiency sentence shed its enumerated items quarter by quarter, reaching the unenumerated "our future obligations" in the same quarter the Intel/OpenAI/Anthropic commitments appeared.

3. Ecosystem Financing on Both Sides of the Income Statement

The non-marketable portfolio grew from $1.5B to $42.3B in two years, with $18.6B of purchases in FY27 Q1 alone and investment commitments laddering $6.5B → $11.4B → $27B in six months. The top direct customer went from 12% (FY24 Q3) to 23% (FY26 Q2); FY27 Q1 reports three customers at 21/17/16% of revenue (54%) and 30/18/16% of receivables (64%, series high) — with the Customer A/B/C letter labels dropped in the same quarter.
