NVDA 10-K Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of NVIDIA SEC filings from FY2020 through FY2026. Revenue grew from $10.9B to $215.9B. Self-description changed from "pioneered accelerated computing" to "data center scale AI infrastructure company." Export control language progressed from absent to "effectively foreclosed."
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001045810) | Fiscal Years 2020-2026 | Analysis: March 2026
FY2020 → FY2021

The Mellanox Pivot

Segments renamed GPU/Tegra → Graphics/Compute & Networking. Mellanox acquired. Arm deal announced ($40B). Revenue $10.9B → $16.7B (+53%). Interconnect competitors first listed. Amazon added to custom silicon competitor list.

FY2021 → FY2022

Arm Termination & ESG High-Water Mark

Arm deal terminated ($1.36B charge). CMP (crypto mining) disclosed at $550M revenue. ESG section added with demographics and 100% renewable goal. Revenue $16.7B → $26.9B (+61%). Data Center moved to #2 market listing.

FY2022 → FY2023

LLM and Export Control Entry

Data Center became #1 market listing. "Large language models" and "generative AI" entered filing. H100/Hopper launched. China export controls first disclosed. Revenue flat ($27B). Gross margin declined to 56.9% on $2.17B inventory provisions.

FY2023 → FY2024

Infrastructure Company

Self-description changed to "full-stack computing infrastructure company reshaping industry." Revenue $27B → $60.9B (+126%). Gross margin recovered to 72.7%. Customer concentration re-emerged at 13% direct. Huawei and Microsoft added as named competitors.

FY2024 → FY2025

Blackwell & $130B Revenue

Blackwell launched. Revenue $60.9B → $130.5B (+114%). "Agentic AI" entered filing. Omniverse description changed from "metaverse" to "industrial AI." Three customers at 10%+. AI Diffusion IFR introduced worldwide scope. Gross margin 75.0%.

FY2025 → FY2026

China Foreclosed & Groq Acquired

Revenue $130.5B → $215.9B (+65%). China described as "effectively foreclosed" with $4.5B H20 charge. Customer concentration: 22% + 14% direct. Groq IP licensed for $17B ($14.4B goodwill). NVLink Fusion introduced. ESG compressed to ~6 lines. Gross margin 71.1%.

FY2020 → FY2021: The Mellanox Pivot

Filed Feb 2021 | Revenue: $10.9B → $16.7B (+53%) | Gross Margin: 62.0% → 62.3% | Employees: 13,775 → 18,975 (+38%)

Segment & Identity Changes

▶Segments renamed: "GPU" and "Tegra Processor" → "Graphics" and "Compute & Networking"Changed
FY2020: "Our two reportable segments - GPU and Tegra Processor - are based on a single underlying architecture."
FY2021: "Our Graphics segment includes GeForce GPUs for gaming and PCs [...] Our Compute & Networking segment includes Data Center platforms and systems for AI, HPC, and accelerated computing; Mellanox networking and interconnect solutions"
The new Compute & Networking segment folded Mellanox networking alongside data center compute, while Tegra products were absorbed into both segments.
▶Corporate identity shift: AI described as established field, no longer "revolutionary"Changed
FY2020: "we extended our focus in recent years to the revolutionary field of artificial intelligence"
FY2021: "we have expanded to several other large and important computationally intensive fields [...] scientific computing, artificial intelligence, or AI, data science, autonomous vehicles"

Acquisitions & Strategy

▶Mellanox acquisition completed — networking products folded into Compute & NetworkingNew
FY2021: "In fiscal year 2021, we completed the acquisition of Mellanox [...] With Mellanox, we can optimize across the entire computing, networking, and storage stack to deliver data center-scale computing solutions."
InfiniBand, DPU, and DOCA software platform all entered the filing for the first time.
▶Arm acquisition announced: $40B, "creating the premier computing company for the age of artificial intelligence"New
FY2021: "The announced acquisition is expected to bring together NVIDIA's leading AI computing platform with Arm's vast ecosystem to create the premier computing company for the age of artificial intelligence"

Competitive & Regulatory

▶Interconnect competitor category added: Arista, Broadcom, Cisco, Marvell listedNew
FY2021: "suppliers of interconnect, switch and cable solutions such as AMD, Applied Optoelectronics, Inc., Arista Networks, Broadcom, Cisco, HP, Intel, Juniper Networks, Inc., Lumentum Holdings, and Marvell Technology Group"
This entire competitor category was absent in FY2020 and appeared as a direct consequence of the Mellanox acquisition.
▶Amazon added alongside Alphabet as custom silicon competitorNew
FY2021: "large internet services companies with internal teams designing chips [...] such as Alibaba Group, Alphabet, and Amazon"
FY2020 listed only Alphabet in this category.
▶Government Regulations section added; "responsible use of AI" first mentionedNew
FY2021: "Our worldwide business activities are subject to various laws, rules, and regulations of the United States as well as of foreign governments."
A dedicated Government Regulations section appeared in the business description for the first time. No equivalent section existed in FY2020.

Key Metrics

▶Revenue: $10.9B → $16.7B (+53%). CUDA developers: 1.6M → 2.2M.Signal
Revenue grew 53%. Gross margin held at 62.3%. Employees increased 38% to 18,975, largely from the Mellanox integration. CUDA developers grew from 1.6M to 2.2M.
▶Dell was 11% customer in FY2020; no 10%+ customer in FY2021Signal
Customer concentration dropped to zero -- no single customer exceeded 10% of revenue. This remained the case through FY2023.

FY2021 → FY2022: Arm Termination & ESG High-Water Mark

Filed Feb 2022 | Revenue: $16.7B → $26.9B (+61%) | Gross Margin: 62.3% → 64.9% | Employees: 18,975 → 22,473

Strategic Shifts

▶Arm acquisition terminated: "significant regulatory challenges." $1.36B write-off.New
FY2022: "The parties agreed to terminate because of significant regulatory challenges preventing the completion of the transaction. We intend to record in operating expenses a $1.36 billion charge"
▶CMP (cryptocurrency mining processors) added to Compute & Networking segmentNew
FY2022: "The Compute & Networking segment includes [...] cryptocurrency mining processors, or CMP."
CMP contributed $550M in FY2022 revenue.
▶Omniverse software for "building 3D designs and virtual worlds" added to GraphicsNew
FY2022: "Omniverse software for building 3D designs and virtual worlds"
By FY2025, the same product would be repositioned as "Omniverse Enterprise software for building and operating industrial AI and digital twin applications."

ESG & Supply Chain

▶ESG section added: Climate Change, Human Capital, Diversity and Inclusion with 100% renewable energy goalNew
FY2022: "NVIDIA invests computing technologies that improve lives and address global challenges. We integrate sound environmental, social and corporate governance, or ESG, principles and practices into every aspect of the Company."
FY2022: "By the end of fiscal year 2025, our goal is to purchase or generate enough renewable energy to match 100% of our global electricity usage"
The section included full demographic data, diversity metrics, and an Earth-2 climate digital twin initiative. By FY2026, all of this was compressed to approximately 6 lines.
▶Supply constraint language: "demand exceeded our supply" and "paid premiums and provided deposits"New
FY2022: "our demand exceeded our supply in several areas, and our revenue did not follow historical seasonal patterns"
FY2022: "We have placed non-cancellable inventory orders for certain products in advance of our historical lead times, paid premiums and provided deposits to secure future supply and capacity"

Competitive Landscape

▶Data Center moves from #3 to #2 market listing (Gaming remains #1)Changed
FY2021: "Gaming, Professional Visualization, Data Center, and Automotive"
FY2022: "Gaming, Data Center, Professional Visualization, and Automotive"
Gaming remained at #1 for one more year before being displaced by Data Center in FY2023.
▶Alibaba added as cloud competitor. Arm-based CPU competitor category created.New
FY2022: "large internet services companies with internal teams designing chips [...] such as Alibaba Group, Alphabet, and Amazon"
Alibaba was added to the custom silicon competitor list. Xilinx was dropped following its acquisition by AMD.

Key Metrics

▶Revenue: $16.7B → $26.9B (+61%). CUDA developers: ~3M. CMP contributed $550M.Signal
Revenue grew 61%. Gross margin improved to 64.9%. Employees grew to 22,473. CUDA developers reached approximately 3 million.

FY2022 → FY2023: LLM and Export Control Entry

Filed Feb 2023 | Revenue: $26.9B → $27.0B (flat) | Gross Margin: 64.9% → 56.9% | Employees: 22,473 → 26,196

AI Language Transformation

▶Market listing reordered: Data Center becomes #1, displacing GamingChanged
FY2022: "Gaming, Data Center, Professional Visualization, and Automotive"
FY2023: "Data Center, Gaming, Professional Visualization, and Automotive"
Data Center remained at #1 through FY2026.
▶"Large language models" and "generative AI" enter filing for first timeNew
FY2023: "large language models, which can recognize, summarize, translate, predict and generate text and other content based on knowledge gained from massive datasets, and generative AI, which uses algorithms that create new content"
Neither term appeared in any prior filing. Their introduction coincided with the ChatGPT launch in November 2022.
▶Hopper architecture and H100 GPU launched: "Transformer Engine...accelerate training by an order of magnitude"New
FY2023: "Transformer Engine, designed to accelerate the training of AI transformer models by an order of magnitude"
The H100 was described entirely in training terms. By FY2026, new architectures (Blackwell Ultra, Rubin) were described without any training references.

Export Controls & Regulation

▶China export controls first disclosed: "new license requirements...some of our data center products" (no product names)New
FY2023: "the U.S. government announced new license requirements that impact certain exports to China (including Hong Kong and Macau) and Russia of some of our data center products. The impact of the new license requirements is difficult to quantify"
No specific products were named. By FY2024, A100, H100, and six other products were listed by name, and Country Groups D1, D4, D5 were specified.

Products & Ecosystem

▶Networking elevated: "data center has become the new unit of computing"New
FY2023: "as AI and HPC workloads have become extremely large spanning thousands of compute nodes, the data center has become the new unit of computing, with networking as an integral part"
Networking received its own dedicated paragraph for the first time.
▶Grace CPU introduced; "CSPs" abbreviation created; Ada Lovelace / RTX 40 launchedNew
Grace CPU entered as NVIDIA's own CPU for AI infrastructure. "CSPs" (Cloud Service Providers) received its own abbreviation. Ada Lovelace / GeForce RTX 40 Series launched for gaming.

Key Metrics

▶Revenue flat at $27.0B but massive mix shift: C&N +36%, Graphics -25%Signal
Compute & Networking grew 36% while Graphics declined 25%. Gross margin declined to 56.9% on $2.17B in inventory provisions. Employees reached 26,196. CUDA developers grew to 3.8M. Inception startups exceeded 13,000.

FY2023 → FY2024: Infrastructure Company

Filed Feb 2024 | Revenue: $27.0B → $60.9B (+126%) | Gross Margin: 56.9% → 72.7% | Employees: 26,196 → ~29,600

Corporate Identity Transformation

▶Self-description transforms: "full-stack computing infrastructure company...reshaping industry"Changed
FY2023: "NVIDIA pioneered accelerated computing [...] Since our original focus on PC graphics, we have expanded to several other large and important computationally intensive fields."
FY2024: "NVIDIA is now a full-stack computing infrastructure company with data-center-scale offerings that are reshaping industry."
All references to PC graphics origin were removed from the opening self-description.
▶"More than half of our engineers work on software" — first software moat quantificationNew
FY2024: "More than half of our engineers work on software."
This was the first time NVIDIA quantified its software investment as a share of engineering headcount. The disclosure was retained in subsequent filings.
▶Historical founding narrative added: 1999 GPU → 2006 CUDA → 2012 AlexNet "Big Bang"New
FY2024: "Our invention of the GPU in 1999 sparked the growth of the PC gaming market [...] In 2012, the AlexNet neural network, trained on NVIDIA GPUs, won the ImageNet computer image recognition competition, marking the 'Big Bang' moment of AI."
Prior filings mentioned the GPU invention and CUDA but did not include the AlexNet narrative or the "Big Bang" framing.

Export Controls & Competition

▶Export controls name specific products: A100, H100, A800, H800, L4, L40, L40S, RTX 4090New
FY2023: "new license requirements that impact certain exports to China [...] of some of our data center products"
FY2024: "new and updated licensing requirements [...] for exports to China and Country Groups D1, D4, and D5 [...] including A100, A800, H100, H800, L4, L40, L40S and RTX 4090"
▶Export control language: "may be harmed" → "has been harmed...may be further harmed"Changed
FY2023: "The impact of the new license requirements is difficult to quantify"
FY2024: "Our competitive position has been harmed, and our competitive position and future results may be further harmed"
▶Huawei named as competitor in three categories. Microsoft added to two categories.New
Both were added in the same filing year that described export controls as having already "harmed" the business. FY2020 listed only Alphabet as a custom silicon competitor; by FY2024, Amazon, Microsoft, and Huawei were all named.

Products & Customers

▶Customer concentration: 13% direct (Customer A), ~19% indirect (estimated)New
After three years of no 10%+ customer, concentration re-emerged. The indirect customer methodology was disclosed for the first time, showing one customer at ~19% of revenue when channel sales were included.
▶CoWoS packaging technology first named; DGX Cloud launchedNew
CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate) entered the filing, identified as a supply constraint. DGX Cloud launched as AI-training-as-a-service. Quantum (InfiniBand) and Spectrum (Ethernet) networking brands were named.

Key Metrics

▶Revenue: $27.0B → $60.9B (+126%). Operating margin: 54.1%. Cash from ops: $28.1B.Signal
Revenue more than doubled. Gross margin recovered from 56.9% to 72.7%. Operating margin reached 54.1%. Cash from operations was $28.1B. Share repurchases totaled $9.7B. Employees grew to ~29,600 with turnover at 2.7%. CUDA developers reached 4.7M.

FY2024 → FY2025: Blackwell & $130B Revenue

Filed Feb 2025 | Revenue: $60.9B → $130.5B (+114%) | Gross Margin: 72.7% → 75.0% | Employees: ~29,600 → ~36,000

Architecture & Products

▶Blackwell architecture launched: "full set of data center scale infrastructure"New
FY2025: "we launched the NVIDIA Blackwell architecture, a full set of data center scale infrastructure that includes GPUs, CPUs, DPUs, interconnects, switch chips and systems, and networking adapters"
Blackwell was described as a complete system rather than an individual chip. Compare to FY2023 H100 described as a GPU with a "Transformer Engine."
▶"Agentic AI" enters filing: "generative and agentic AI-enabled products"New
FY2025: "Enterprises and startups across a broad range of industries use our accelerated computing platforms to build new generative and agentic AI-enabled products and services"
The term "agentic AI" appeared nowhere in prior filings.
▶NVIDIA NIM, NeMo, AI Blueprints — enterprise software becomes named product familyNew
FY2025: "In addition to software delivered to customers as an integral part of our data center computing platform, we offer paid licenses to NVIDIA AI Enterprise, a comprehensive suite of enterprise-grade AI software"
Prior filings described software capabilities generically under CUDA. FY2025 introduced NIM, NeMo, and AI Blueprints as specific named product offerings.
▶GeForce RTX 50 Series with "neural graphics" combining AI models with traditional renderingNew
FY2025: "The Blackwell architecture introduced neural graphics which combines AI models with traditional rendering to unlock a new era of graphics innovation."

Export Controls & Regulation

▶AI Diffusion IFR: worldwide licensing requirement. H200, B100, B200, GB200 named.New
FY2024: "new and updated licensing requirements [...] for exports to China and Country Groups D1, D4, and D5"
FY2025: "In January 2025, the USG published the AI Diffusion IFR [...] would have imposed a worldwide licensing requirement on our data center products, such as our H200, GB200 and GB300."
The scope expanded from country-specific to global.
▶Omniverse: "metaverse and 3D internet" → "industrial AI and digital twin applications"Changed
FY2024: "Omniverse Enterprise software for building and operating metaverse and 3D internet applications"
FY2025: "Omniverse Enterprise software for building and operating industrial AI and digital twin applications"

Key Metrics

▶Revenue: $60.9B → $130.5B (+114%). Gross margin: 75.0%. Three customers at 10%+.Signal
Revenue more than doubled. Gross margin reached 75.0%. Three direct customers crossed the 10% threshold (12%, 11%, 11%), with one indirect customer also at 10%+. Cash from operations reached $64.1B. Share repurchases totaled $34.0B with a $50B new authorization. Employees grew to ~36,000 with turnover at 2.5%. CUDA developers reached 5.9M.

FY2025 → FY2026: China Foreclosed & Groq Acquired

Filed Feb 2026 | Revenue: $130.5B → $215.9B (+65%) | Gross Margin: 75.0% → 71.1% | Employees: ~36,000 → ~42,000

Corporate Identity & Strategy

▶Self-description: "full-stack computing infrastructure company" → "data center scale AI infrastructure company reshaping all industries"Changed
FY2025: "NVIDIA is now a full-stack computing infrastructure company with data-center-scale offerings that are reshaping industry."
FY2026: "NVIDIA is now a data center scale AI infrastructure company reshaping all industries."
▶CUDA: "programming model" → "development platform"Changed
FY2025: "Our full-stack includes the foundational CUDA programming model that runs on all NVIDIA GPUs"
FY2026: "Our technology stack includes the foundational NVIDIA CUDA development platform that runs on all NVIDIA GPUs"
The software description also expanded from a paragraph to a full ecosystem section, adding "AI models and training data sets" to the stack components.
▶Revenue model: "performance and power efficiency" → "superior total cost of ownership"Changed
FY2025: "delivering significantly better performance and power efficiency relative to conventional CPU-only approaches"
FY2026: "delivering superior total cost of ownership relative to conventional CPU-only approaches"
The selling point changed from technical performance metrics to economic outcomes.

New Architectures & Products

▶Blackwell Ultra launched: "optimized for agentic, reasoning, and physical AI"New
FY2023 (Hopper): "Hopper includes a Transformer Engine, designed to accelerate the training of AI transformer models by an order of magnitude over the prior generation."
FY2026 (Blackwell Ultra): "we launched and scaled the NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra platform, optimized for agentic, reasoning, and physical AI"
Blackwell Ultra's description contained no mention of training.
▶Rubin platform unveiled: "10x reduction in cost per token compared to Blackwell"New
FY2026: "we unveiled the NVIDIA Rubin platform [...] Built for agentic AI and reasoning, it excels at processing multi-step problem-solving and massive long-context workflows, delivering up to a 10x reduction in cost per token compared to Blackwell."
Training was not mentioned. The Dynamo inference software was introduced alongside the hardware.
▶"Physical AI" emerges as market category with end-to-end platformNew
FY2026: "For physical AI, we provide an end-to-end platform spanning data center infrastructure, open models, systems, embedded compute modules, and software stacks to train, simulate, and deploy advanced automation and robotics solutions."
Omniverse's description also shifted from "industrial AI and digital twin applications" (FY2025) to "physical AI applications" (FY2026).
▶NVLink Fusion: "enable hyperscalers and custom ASIC designers to integrate custom CPUs and XPUs with our platform"New
FY2026: "we introduced NVIDIA NVLink Fusion to enable hyperscalers and custom ASIC designers to integrate custom CPUs and XPUs with our platform"
Networking revenue grew 142% in FY2026 vs. 59% for compute revenue.
▶Scale language: "tens of thousands" → "hundreds of thousands" of GPUsNew
FY2025: "compute and networking solutions that can scale to tens of thousands of GPU-accelerated servers interconnected to function as a single giant computer"
FY2026: "our data-center-scale offerings feature extreme co-design [...] Hundreds of thousands of GPUs can be interconnected to function as a single giant computer."

China & Export Controls

▶China declared "effectively foreclosed from competing in China's data center computing market"Changed
FY2025: "Our competitive position has been harmed, and our competitive position and future results may be further harmed"
FY2026: "we were effectively foreclosed from competing in China's data center computing/compute market, and our effective foreclosure from the China market helped our competitors build larger developer and customer ecosystems to challenge us worldwide"
The modal verb progression: FY2023 "difficult to quantify," FY2024 "has been harmed...may be further," FY2026 "effectively foreclosed" and "will be further harmed."
▶H20 write-down: $4.5B charge. Licensed H20 revenue: ~$60M. USG demands 15%+ of licensed revenue.New
FY2026: "the USG granted licenses that would allow us to ship certain H20 products to certain China-based customers. We generated approximately $60 million in H20 revenue under those licenses. USG officials expressed an expectation that the USG will receive 15% or more of the revenue generated from licensed sales"
▶AI Diffusion rescinded (May 2025), replacement pending. GAIN AI Act referenced.New
FY2026: "In May 2025, the USG announced that it would rescind the AI Diffusion IFR and implement a replacement rule. The scope, timing, and requirements of the forthcoming rule remain uncertain."
H200 was subject to 25% tariff on US import. The GAIN AI Act was referenced as potential legislation.

Acquisitions & Investments

▶Groq IP license: $17B total ($14.4B goodwill). "No customer contracts, existing products, or equity interests."New

▶OpenAI investment pending: "no assurance that we will enter into an investment"New

Key Metrics

Revenue grew 65% to $215.9B. Gross margin declined from 75.0% to 71.1%. Customer concentration peaked with one customer at 22% (~$47B) and another at 14%. OpenAI disclosed as indirect-indirect customer. Commercial paper grew from $575M to $25B (43x).
▶Purchase obligations: $95.2B within one year. Inventory provisions doubled. Warranty doubled.Signal
