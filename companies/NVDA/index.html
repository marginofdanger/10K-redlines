NVDA 10-K Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of NVIDIA SEC filings from FY2020 through FY2026. Revenue grew from $10.9B to $215.9B. Self-description changed from "pioneered accelerated computing" to "data center scale AI infrastructure company." Export control language progressed from absent to "effectively foreclosed."
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001045810) | Fiscal Years 2020-2026 | Analysis: March 2026
FY2020 → FY2021

The Mellanox Pivot

Segments renamed GPU/Tegra → Graphics/Compute & Networking. Mellanox acquired. Arm deal announced ($40B). Revenue $10.9B → $16.7B (+53%). Interconnect competitors first listed. Amazon added to custom silicon competitor list.

FY2021 → FY2022

Arm Termination & ESG High-Water Mark

Arm deal terminated ($1.36B charge). CMP (crypto mining) disclosed at $550M revenue. ESG section added with demographics and 100% renewable goal. Revenue $16.7B → $26.9B (+61%). Data Center moved to #2 market listing.

FY2022 → FY2023

LLM and Export Control Entry

Data Center became #1 market listing. "Large language models" and "generative AI" entered filing. H100/Hopper launched. China export controls first disclosed. Revenue flat ($27B). Gross margin declined to 56.9% on $2.17B inventory provisions.

FY2023 → FY2024

Infrastructure Company

Self-description changed to "full-stack computing infrastructure company reshaping industry." Revenue $27B → $60.9B (+126%). Gross margin recovered to 72.7%. Customer concentration re-emerged at 13% direct. Huawei and Microsoft added as named competitors.

FY2024 → FY2025

Blackwell Ships, Disclosure Thins

Blackwell shipped from Q4 on a new yearly cadence. Revenue $60.9B → $130.5B (+114%). Three direct customers at 12/11/11%. The inference share (~40%), cloud-provider share, China percentage and capex range were all dropped. Supply obligations $16.1B → $30.8B; warranty liability 4x. The AI Diffusion IFR took export controls worldwide. Indebtedness risk factor deleted. Gross margin 75.0%.

FY2025 → FY2026

China Foreclosed, Customers Financed

Revenue $130.5B → $215.9B (+65%). Harm from export controls went from "may" to "will"; NVIDIA "effectively foreclosed" from China, with a $4.5B H20 charge and Beijing discouraging purchases. Direct customers at 22% + 14%. $3.5B of lease guarantees and $17.5B of private investments, some in AI model makers that buy NVIDIA products. Commitments $95.2B. Groq license for $17B. DGX Cloud and the Sustainability section deleted. Gross margin 71.1%.

Segment & Identity Changes

▶Segments renamed: "GPU" and "Tegra Processor" → "Graphics" and "Compute & Networking"Changed
FY2020: "Our two reportable segments - GPU and Tegra Processor - are based on a single underlying architecture."
FY2021: "Our Graphics segment includes GeForce GPUs for gaming and PCs [...] Our Compute & Networking segment includes Data Center platforms and systems for AI, HPC, and accelerated computing; Mellanox networking and interconnect solutions"
The new Compute & Networking segment folded Mellanox networking alongside data center compute, while Tegra products were absorbed into both segments.
▶Corporate identity shift: AI described as established field, no longer "revolutionary"Changed
FY2020: "we extended our focus in recent years to the revolutionary field of artificial intelligence"
FY2021: "we have expanded to several other large and important computationally intensive fields [...] scientific computing, artificial intelligence, or AI, data science, autonomous vehicles"

Acquisitions & Strategy

▶Arm acquisition announced: $40B, "creating the premier computing company for the age of artificial intelligence"New

Key Metrics

FY2022 → FY2023: LLM and Export Control Entry

Filed Feb 2023 | Revenue: $26.9B → $27.0B (flat) | Gross Margin: 64.9% → 56.9% | Employees: 22,473 → 26,196

▶Market listing reordered: Data Center becomes #1, displacing GamingChanged
FY2022: "Gaming, Data Center, Professional Visualization, and Automotive"
FY2023: "Data Center, Gaming, Professional Visualization, and Automotive"
Data Center remained at #1 through FY2026.
▶"Large language models" and "generative AI" enter filing for first timeNew
FY2023: "large language models, which can recognize, summarize, translate, predict and generate text and other content based on knowledge gained from massive datasets, and generative AI, which uses algorithms that create new content"
Neither term appeared in any prior filing. Their introduction coincided with the ChatGPT launch in November 2022.
▶Hopper architecture and H100 GPU launched: "Transformer Engine...accelerate training by an order of magnitude"New
FY2023: "Transformer Engine, designed to accelerate the training of AI transformer models by an order of magnitude"
The H100 was described entirely in training terms. By FY2026, new architectures (Blackwell Ultra, Rubin) were described without any training references.

Export Controls & Regulation

▶China export controls first disclosed: "new license requirements...some of our data center products" (no product names)New

No specific products were named. By FY2024, A100, H100, and six other products were listed by name, and Country Groups D1, D4, D5 were specified.

Products & Ecosystem

▶Networking elevated: "data center has become the new unit of computing"New
FY2023: "as AI and HPC workloads have become extremely large spanning thousands of compute nodes, the data center has become the new unit of computing, with networking as an integral part"
Networking received its own dedicated paragraph for the first time.
▶Grace CPU introduced; "CSPs" abbreviation created; Ada Lovelace / RTX 40 launchedNew
Grace CPU entered as NVIDIA's own CPU for AI infrastructure. "CSPs" (Cloud Service Providers) received its own abbreviation. Ada Lovelace / GeForce RTX 40 Series launched for gaming.

Key Metrics

▶Revenue flat at $27.0B but massive mix shift: C&N +36%, Graphics -25%Signal
Compute & Networking grew 36% while Graphics declined 25%. Gross margin declined to 56.9% on $2.17B in inventory provisions. Employees reached 26,196. CUDA developers grew to 3.8M. Inception startups exceeded 13,000.

FY2023 → FY2024: Infrastructure Company

Filed Feb 2024 | Revenue: $27.0B → $60.9B (+126%) | Gross Margin: 56.9% → 72.7% | Employees: 26,196 → ~29,600

Corporate Identity Transformation

▶Self-description transforms: "full-stack computing infrastructure company...reshaping industry"Changed
FY2023: "NVIDIA pioneered accelerated computing [...] Since our original focus on PC graphics, we have expanded to several other large and important computationally intensive fields."
FY2024: "NVIDIA is now a full-stack computing infrastructure company with data-center-scale offerings that are reshaping industry."
All references to PC graphics origin were removed from the opening self-description.
▶"More than half of our engineers work on software" — first software moat quantificationNew
FY2024: "More than half of our engineers work on software."
This was the first time NVIDIA quantified its software investment as a share of engineering headcount. The disclosure was retained in subsequent filings.
▶Historical founding narrative added: 1999 GPU → 2006 CUDA → 2012 AlexNet "Big Bang"New
FY2024: "Our invention of the GPU in 1999 sparked the growth of the PC gaming market [...] In 2012, the AlexNet neural network, trained on NVIDIA GPUs, won the ImageNet computer image recognition competition, marking the 'Big Bang' moment of AI."
Prior filings mentioned the GPU invention and CUDA but did not include the AlexNet narrative or the "Big Bang" framing.

Export Controls & Competition

▶Export controls name specific products: A100, H100, A800, H800, L4, L40, L40S, RTX 4090New
FY2023: "new license requirements that impact certain exports to China [...] of some of our data center products"
FY2024: "new and updated licensing requirements [...] for exports to China and Country Groups D1, D4, and D5 [...] including A100, A800, H100, H800, L4, L40, L40S and RTX 4090"
▶Export control language: "may be harmed" → "has been harmed...may be further harmed"Changed
FY2023: "The impact of the new license requirements is difficult to quantify"
FY2024: "Our competitive position has been harmed, and our competitive position and future results may be further harmed"
▶Huawei named as competitor in three categories. Microsoft added to two categories.New
Both were added in the same filing year that described export controls as having already "harmed" the business. FY2020 listed only Alphabet as a custom silicon competitor; by FY2024, Amazon, Microsoft, and Huawei were all named.

Products & Customers

▶Customer concentration: 13% direct (Customer A), ~19% indirect (estimated)New
After three years of no 10%+ customer, concentration re-emerged. The indirect customer methodology was disclosed for the first time, showing one customer at ~19% of revenue when channel sales were included.
▶CoWoS packaging technology first named; DGX Cloud launchedNew
CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate) entered the filing, identified as a supply constraint. DGX Cloud launched as AI-training-as-a-service. Quantum (InfiniBand) and Spectrum (Ethernet) networking brands were named.

Key Metrics

▶Revenue: $27.0B → $60.9B (+126%). Operating margin: 54.1%. Cash from ops: $28.1B.Signal
Revenue more than doubled. Gross margin recovered from 56.9% to 72.7%. Operating margin reached 54.1%. Cash from operations was $28.1B. Share repurchases totaled $9.7B. Employees grew to ~29,600 with turnover at 2.7%. CUDA developers reached 4.7M.

FY2024 → FY2025: Blackwell Ships, Disclosure Thins

Filed Feb 26, 2025 | Revenue: $60.9B → $130.5B (+114%) | Operating income: $33.0B → $81.5B | Gross Margin: 72.7% → 75.0% | Operating cash flow: $28.1B → $64.1B | Employees: ~29,600 → ~36,000

Customers & Demand Visibility

▶Inference share (~40% of Data Center) and cloud-provider share (>50%) no longer disclosedRemoved
FY2024: "We estimate that in fiscal year 2024, approximately 40% of Data Center revenue was for AI inference. In the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2024, large cloud providers represented more than half of our Data Center revenue, supporting both internal workloads and external customers."
FY2025: "Data Center revenue for fiscal year 2025 was up 142% from a year ago. The strong year-on-year growth was driven by demand for our Hopper architecture accelerated computing platform used for large language models, recommendation engines, and generative AI applications."
These were the only figures in the 10-K that said what Data Center revenue was used for and who bought it. Both were dropped in the year Data Center growth was 142%, and neither returns in the FY2026 10-K.
▶One direct customer at 13% → three at 12%, 11%, 11%; indirect customer "approximately 19%" becomes "10% or more"Changed
FY2024: "One indirect customer which primarily purchases our products through system integrators and distributors, including through Customer A, is estimated to have represented approximately 19% of total revenue for fiscal year 2024, attributable to the Compute & Networking segment."
FY2025: "For fiscal year 2025, an indirect customer which primarily purchases our products through system integrators and distributors, including through Direct Customer B, is estimated to represent 10% or more of total revenue, attributable to the Compute & Networking segment."
Three direct customers now made up 34% of revenue, up from one at 13%, while the indirect estimate lost its number and gained a caveat ("Actual indirect customer revenue may differ from our estimates"). The FY2024 sentence "Our estimated Compute & Networking demand is expected to remain concentrated" was deleted even though concentration rose.
▶China share of Data Center (19% → 14%, then "mid-single digit") replaced by "well below" prior levelsRemoved
FY2024: "Our sales to China decreased as a percentage of total Data Center revenue from 19% in fiscal year 2023 to 14% in fiscal year 2024. ... China represented a mid-single digit percentage of our Data Center revenue in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2024 due to USG licensing requirements and we expect China to be in a similar range in the first quarter of fiscal year 2025."
FY2025: "Our Data Center revenue in China grew in fiscal year 2025. As a percentage of total Data Center revenue, it remains well below levels seen prior to the onset of export controls in October 2023."
China exposure now came with a direction and no percentage. The FY2026 10-K later shows China (including Hong Kong) at $25.0B of FY2025 revenue on a headquarters basis, so the business that had stopped being quantified was still large.
▶"Demand remains very strong" and "supply-constrained" outlook deleted; generative AI's "long-term trajectory is unknown" hedge also droppedRemoved
FY2024: "Supply of Hopper architecture products is improving, and demand remains very strong. We expect our next-generation products to be supply-constrained based upon demand indications."
FY2024: "Recent technologies, such as generative AI models, have emerged, and while they have driven increased demand for Data Center, the long-term trajectory is unknown."
FY2025: "Advancements in accelerated computing and generative AI models, along with the growth in model complexity and scale, have driven increased demand for our Data Center systems."
The 10-K stopped making a forward demand claim and, in the same edit, dropped its main hedge about generative AI's durability. A new demand-risk bullet points at what would actually limit demand: "our customers' ability to invest in AI infrastructure." FY2026 widens that bullet to capital and energy.
▶Yearly Data Center architecture cadence adopted; Q2 FY2025 margins hit by "low-yielding Blackwell material"New
FY2025: "We have introduced a new product and architecture cadence of our Data Center solutions where we seek to complete new computing solutions each year and provide a greater variety of Data Center offerings."
FY2025: "For example, our gross margins in the second quarter of fiscal year 2025 were negatively impacted by inventory provisions for low-yielding Blackwell material."
This is the first time the 10-K commits to a one-year cadence, and it pairs the commitment with a concrete cost: a Blackwell yield problem large enough to show up in margins. FY2026 adds Rubin to the cadence.

Commitments & Balance Sheet

▶Supply obligations $16.1B → $30.8B plus $14.3B other (incl. $10.9B cloud); warranty liability $306M → $1.3BSignal
FY2024: "As of January 28, 2024, we had outstanding inventory purchase and long-term supply and capacity obligations totaling $16.1 billion."
FY2025: "As of January 26, 2025, we had outstanding inventory purchase and long-term supply and capacity obligations totaling $30.8 billion, an increase from the prior year led by commitments, capacity and components for new product introductions, including our new Blackwell architecture. ... Other non-inventory purchase obligations were $14.3 billion, including $10.9 billion of multi-year cloud service agreements."
FY2025: "The estimated amount of product warranty liabilities was $1.3 billion and $306 million as of January 26, 2025 and January 28, 2024, respectively."
Supply obligations roughly doubled and NVIDIA was renting $10.9B of cloud capacity. The warranty liability grew more than fourfold in the year full Blackwell systems began shipping, a cost of selling racks rather than chips that FY2026 doubles again to $2.8B.
▶Indebtedness risk factor deleted after $1.25B of notes repaidRemoved
FY2024: "Our indebtedness could adversely affect our financial position and cash flows from operations, and prevent us from implementing our strategy or fulfilling our contractual obligations."
FY2024: "As of January 28, 2024, we had net outstanding a total of $9.7 billion in notes due by 2060."
With net debt carrying value down to $8.5B and $43.2B of cash and marketable securities, the risk factor and its summary bullet were removed. A debt risk factor returns in the FY2027 Q2 10-Q after NVIDIA issued $25B of notes (see the 10-Q scanner).
▶Capex guided at $3.5–4.0B, came in at $3.4B; the range is replaced by "expect to increase"Changed
FY2024: "During fiscal year 2025, we expect to use our existing cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities, and the cash generated by our operations to fund our capital investments of approximately $3.5 billion to $4.0 billion related to property and equipment."
FY2025: "During fiscal year 2025 and fiscal year 2024, we spent $3.4 billion and $1.1 billion on capital expenditures, respectively. We expect to increase capital expenditures in fiscal year 2026 relative to fiscal year 2025 to support the future growth of our business."
Spending landed just under the low end of the range, and the numeric guide was replaced by a direction. FY2026 repeats the same pattern for FY2027.

Export Controls & Supply Geography

▶AI Diffusion IFR: worldwide licensing for A100 through GB200; "over 150 countries" designated Tier 2New
FY2024: "In October 2023, the USG announced new and updated licensing requirements ... for exports to China and Country Groups D1, D4, and D5"
FY2025: "Unless a license exception is available, the worldwide licensing requirements will apply to the following NVIDIA products, and any others we develop that meet the characteristics of 3A090.a or 4A090.a, including but not limited to: A100, A800, H100, H200, H800, B100, B200, GB200, L4, L40S, and RTX 6000 Ada."
FY2025: "... prohibit us from exporting our products to any or all customers in one or more markets, including but not limited to China and over 150 countries designated ..."
Export controls went from country lists to every destination, and covered Blackwell for the first time. The rule was rescinded in May 2025 before it took effect (FY2026 10-K).
▶China regulators investigating whether complying with U.S. controls "discriminates unfairly"; Beijing's Action Plan sets an efficiency standardNew
FY2025: "For example, regulators in China are investigating whether complying with applicable U.S. export controls discriminates unfairly against customers in the China market."
FY2025: "As another example, an agency of the Chinese government announced an Action Plan that endorses new standards regarding the compute performance per watt and per memory bandwidth of accelerators used in new and renovated data centers in China."
This is the first sign of China pushing back as well as the U.S. restricting. The listed penalties gain "restrictions regarding our networking products and services," which points at the Mellanox approval. FY2026 reports a preliminary finding against NVIDIA.
▶Supply dependence: "Taiwan" → "Taiwan and South Korea"; SK Hynix moved ahead of Micron among memory suppliersChanged
FY2024: "Finally, our business depends on our ability to receive consistent and reliable supply from our overseas partners, especially in Taiwan."
FY2025: "Finally, our business depends on our ability to receive consistent and reliable supply from our overseas partners, especially in Taiwan and South Korea."
FY2024: "We purchase memory from Micron Technology, Inc., SK Hynix Inc., and Samsung."
FY2025: "We purchase memory from SK Hynix Inc., Micron Technology, Inc., and Samsung."
Both edits are consistent with Korean high-bandwidth memory becoming a constraint on the same level as TSMC. The 10-K does not say so, so this is an inference from the reordering.

Products & Positioning

▶Blackwell launched as "a full set of data center scale infrastructure," shipping from Q4; "agentic AI" enters the filingNew
FY2025: "In fiscal year 2025, we launched the NVIDIA Blackwell architecture, a full set of data center scale infrastructure that includes GPUs, CPUs, DPUs, interconnects, switch chips and systems, and networking adapters."
FY2025: "We began shipping production systems of the Blackwell architecture in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2025."
FY2025: "Enterprises and startups across a broad range of industries use our accelerated computing platforms to build new generative and agentic AI-enabled products and services"
NVIDIA now describes its product as a system rather than a chip. That framing is behind the warranty and supply-commitment growth above, and behind FY2026's statement that gross margin fell as the business moved "from offering Hopper HGX systems to Blackwell full-scale datacenter solutions."
▶Networking growth slows from 133% to 51%; the stated driver changes from InfiniBand to Ethernet/Spectrum-XChanged
FY2024: "Networking was up 133% due to higher shipments of InfiniBand."
FY2025: "Revenue from Data Center networking grew 51% driven by Ethernet for AI revenue, which includes Spectrum-X end-to-end ethernet platform."
Networking grew at less than a third of the 162% compute rate, and InfiniBand, the Mellanox franchise, is no longer named as a driver. FY2026 reverses this, with networking up 142% on NVLink.
▶"Hire, promote and compensate our employees based on merit" added at the top of the D&I listNew
FY2025: "•Hire, promote and compensate our employees based on merit;"
FY2025: "We committed to purchase or generate enough renewable energy to match 100% of our global electricity usage for offices and data centers under our operational control starting with our fiscal year 2025."
The merit language arrived a year earlier than the FY2026 cut. Here it sits inside an intact diversity program with demographics. FY2026 keeps only the merit idea and deletes the rest, including the renewable-energy goal that began in the year it would first have been measured.

Key Metrics

▶Revenue $60.9B → $130.5B; buybacks $9.7B → $34.0B with a $50B authorization; employees ~36,000Signal
FY2025: "In fiscal year 2025, we repurchased 310 million shares of our common stock for $34.0 billion."

FY2025 → FY2026: China Foreclosed, Customers Financed

Filed Feb 25, 2026 | Revenue: $130.5B → $215.9B (+65%) | Operating income: $81.5B → $130.4B | Gross Margin: 75.0% → 71.1% | Operating cash flow: $64.1B → $102.7B | Employees: ~36,000 → ~42,000

China & Export Controls

▶"May be further harmed" → "will be further harmed"; NVIDIA "effectively foreclosed" from China's data center marketChanged
FY2025: "Our competitive position has been harmed by the existing export controls, and our competitive position and future results may be further harmed, over the long term, if there are further changes in the USG’s export controls"
FY2026: "Our competitive position has been harmed by export controls, and our competitive position and future results will be further harmed, over the long term, if the restrictions remain in place or are expanded in geographic, customer, or product scope"
FY2026: "As of the end of fiscal year 2026, we were effectively foreclosed from competing in China's data center computing/compute market, and our effective foreclosure from the China market helped our competitors build larger developer and customer ecosystems to challenge us worldwide."
The trigger changed as well as the verb. Harm used to depend on new rules; now it follows from current rules simply staying in place. Headquarters-basis revenue from China (including Hong Kong) fell from $25.0B to $19.7B.
▶H20: $4.5B charge, ~$60M of licensed revenue; USG's 15% revenue-share expectation never codifiedNew
FY2026: "As a result of these requirements, we incurred a $4.5 billion charge in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026 associated with H20 for excess inventory and purchase obligations, as the demand for H20 products diminished."
FY2026: "In August 2025, the USG granted licenses that would allow us to ship certain H20 products to certain China-based customers. We generated approximately $60 million in H20 revenue under those licenses. USG officials expressed an expectation that the USG will receive 15% or more of the revenue generated from licensed sales of our products, but the USG did not publish a regulation codifying such requirement."
The license produced about 1% of the charge back in revenue. The February 2026 H200 license comes with a 25% U.S. import tariff instead, and has produced no revenue.
▶Beijing "may" steer customers away → "has encouraged" competitors and "discouraged" purchases, including China-specific partsChanged
FY2025: "The Chinese government may also encourage customers to purchase from our China-based competitors, or impose restrictions on the sale to certain customers of our products, or any products containing components made by our partners and suppliers."
FY2026: "The Chinese government has encouraged customers to purchase from our China-based competitors and discouraged customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products, including any China-specific product designed to comply with U.S. export controls."
FY2026: "For example, following U.S. legislative proposals calling for mandatory features in our chips, China’s government publicly questioned whether our H20 products have built-in vulnerabilities, discouraging customers from purchasing our products."
A hypothetical became a past event, and it explains the H20 failure: the compliant product was blocked on the Chinese side. NVIDIA also warns that U.S. chip-tracking mandates "could introduce system vulnerabilities."
▶China's antitrust regulator finds preliminarily that NVIDIA violated its Mellanox approvalChanged
FY2025: "For example, regulators in China are investigating whether complying with applicable U.S. export controls discriminates unfairly against customers in the China market."
FY2026: "On September 15, 2025, China’s antitrust regulators published their preliminary finding that our compliance with applicable U.S. export controls, which required us to offer degraded products to the Chinese market, discriminated unfairly against customers in the China market and therefore violated the terms of China’s approval of our Mellanox acquisition."
The investigation reached a finding. NVIDIA also describes its own China products as "degraded," and the possible remedies now include "other orders regarding our networking business."
▶Open-source models named as a risk: DeepSeek, Qwen, KIMMI; "if deployed on our competitors' platforms"New
