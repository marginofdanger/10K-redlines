AMZN 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of 14 SEC filings (eleven 10-Qs plus the FY2023, FY2024, and FY2025 10-Ks). The value is speed: catching language changes at quarterly cadence that annual 10-K comparisons would miss by 3–9 months.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001018724) | 14 Filings | Q1 2023 – Q2 2026 | Analysis: July 2026; Q1–Q2 2026 transitions re-analyzed October 2026

Reading This Filer

Three structural facts that determine where AMZN's quarterly signal lives

1. Legal Proceedings is a pointer, not a section. Part II Item 1 reads, in all eleven 10-Qs, exactly:

"See Item 1 of Part I, “Financial Statements — Note 4 — Commitments and Contingencies — Legal Proceedings.”"

Every quarter-over-quarter legal-proceedings diff in this dataset is zero lines. The legal signal is in the contingencies note, which this scanner diffs separately.

2. Note 4 restates only new or updated matters. Each quarter incorporates prior filings by reference and supplements them. A matter disappearing from one quarter to the next is a restatement roll-off, not a resolution or dismissal. Matters rotate in when there is a development and out when there is not.

3. Risk factors are near-frozen intra-year. Across seven intra-year quarterly transitions spanning four years, there are five substantive risk-factor edits in total; three transitions (Q2→Q3 2024, Q1→Q2 2025, Q2→Q3 2025) contain no substantive change whatsoever — only repagination. All meaningful risk-factor movement occurs at the annual 10-K boundary, which is why those four boundaries are included as transitions here.

Consequently the fastest-moving quarterly signals for this filer are MD&A driver sentences, the AWS remaining-performance-obligation disclosure, and the commitments table — not the risk factors.

Q1 2023 → Q2 2023

Filed Aug 4, 2023 | AWS growth: 16% → 12% | Capex: $13.1B → $10.4B | Backlog: $122.0B → $132.1B
▶ "Technology and content" renamed "Technology and infrastructure" across seven passages Changed

The line item, the subsection heading, both cost-classification cross-references, and every driver sentence change together. Q1 2023:

"Costs to operate our AWS segment are primarily classified as “Technology and content” as we leverage a shared infrastructure that supports both our internal technology requirements and external sales to AWS customers."

Q2 2023:

"Costs to operate our AWS segment are primarily classified as “Technology and infrastructure” as we leverage a shared infrastructure that supports both our internal technology requirements and external sales to AWS customers."

The forward-spending sentence loses a word in the same sweep: "We expect spending in technology and content to increase over time as we continue to add employees and technology infrastructure" becomes "We expect spending in technology and infrastructure to increase over time as we continue to add employees and infrastructure." The rename persists unchanged for the remaining ten quarters.

▶ AWS growth halves to 12% while the driver sentence stays verbatim Signal

Q1 2023:

"AWS sales increased 16% in Q1 2023 compared to the comparable prior year period. The sales growth primarily reflects increased customer usage, partially offset by pricing changes, primarily driven by long-term customer contracts."

Q2 2023:

"AWS sales increased 12% in Q2 2023, and 14% for the six months ended June 30, 2023 compared to the comparable prior year periods. The sales growth primarily reflects increased customer usage, partially offset by pricing changes, primarily driven by long-term customer contracts."

The explanatory clause is unchanged character for character. It remains unchanged in all eleven quarters of this dataset, across a growth range of 12% to 37%.

▶ Segment drivers drop "primarily by third-party sellers" and reorder the cost list Changed

Q1 2023, North America:

"...is primarily due to increased unit sales, primarily by third-party sellers, and increased advertising sales, partially offset by increased technology and content costs, increased fulfillment and shipping costs, and growth in certain operating expenses."

Q2 2023:

"...is primarily due to increased unit sales and increased advertising sales, partially offset by increased technology and infrastructure costs, increased shipping and fulfillment costs, and growth in certain operating expenses."

The same two edits are made in the International driver. The third-party-seller attribution is removed from the operating-income explanation while remaining in the sales driver.

▶ Capex forward statement unchanged for a third quarter; shipping-method qualifier dropped Signal

Unchanged verbatim in Q1, Q2, and Q3 2023:

"We expect cash capital expenditures to decrease in 2023, primarily due to lower spending on our fulfillment network."

One deletion elsewhere — Q1 2023 read "we use more expensive shipping methods, including faster delivery, and we offer additional services"; Q2 2023 drops the qualifier:

"We expect our cost of shipping to continue to increase to the extent our customers accept and use our shipping offers at an increasing rate, we use more expensive shipping methods, and we offer additional services."

Indian Rupee is added to the foreign-currency balance list in the same filing.

Q2 2023 → Q3 2023

Filed Oct 27, 2023 | AWS growth: 12% (flat) | Capex: $11.3B | First Anthropic investment
▶ First Anthropic investment disclosed — the start of the AI counterparty arc New

Appended to the acquisitions-funding paragraph, with no Q2 2023 predecessor:

"In Q3 2023, we invested $1.25 billion in a note from Anthropic, PBC, which is convertible into equity. We have an agreement that expires in Q1 2024 to invest up to an additional $2.75 billion in a second convertible note."

This is the first entry in a disclosure line that reaches $15.0B into OpenAI Series C preferred by Q1 2026 and a $10.0B Anthropic preferred position by Q2 2026.

▶ Cost-reduction vocabulary appears: "cost structure productivity", "lower transportation rates", "lower marketing spend" New

Three efficiency terms enter simultaneously, all in the quarterly (not year-to-date) driver sentences. AWS operating income, Q3 2023:

"The increase in AWS operating income in absolute dollars in Q3 2023, compared to the comparable prior year period, is primarily due to increased sales and cost structure productivity, partially offset by spending on technology infrastructure..."

Cost of sales adds "and lower transportation rates" to the fulfillment-network-efficiencies offset. Sales and marketing flips direction:

"The decrease in sales and marketing costs in absolute dollars in Q3 2023, compared to the comparable prior year period, is primarily due to lower marketing spend."

General and administrative also flips, "primarily due to a decrease in payroll and related expenses" — the only payroll-reduction statement anywhere in the 2023 filings. No severance, layoff, or restructuring term is used.

▶ Quarterly and year-to-date drivers split, narrowing the stated quarterly causes Changed

Five driver paragraphs are split into separate quarter and nine-month sentences, and the quarterly versions carry shorter cause lists. International operating loss, Q3 2023, has no offsetting cost clause at all:

"The decrease in International operating loss in absolute dollars in Q3 2023, compared to the comparable prior year period, is primarily due to increased unit sales and increased advertising sales."

The nine-month sentence retains the full list ("partially offset by increased fulfillment and shipping costs, increased technology and infrastructure costs, and growth in certain operating expenses"). Technology and infrastructure does the same, dropping payroll from the quarterly cause.

▶ "New and emerging technologies" added to forward-looking risk lists Changed

Inserted in two places — the Forward-Looking Statements paragraph and the Guidance preamble:

"...the rate of growth of the Internet, online commerce, cloud services, and new and emerging technologies, the amount that Amazon.com invests in new business opportunities and the timing of those investments..."

"Generative AI", "artificial intelligence", and "machine learning" appear zero times in the MD&A of any 2023 quarter. This phrase and the Anthropic note are the only AI-adjacent content in the 2023 MD&A.

Q3 2023 → FY2023 10-K

Filed Feb 2, 2024 | FY2023 capex: $48.1B | Risk factors +5.4% | Four AI risk categories added
▶ Capex forward statement reverses direction, from "decrease in 2023" to "increase in 2024" Signal

Q3 2023 10-Q carried a standalone forward sentence:

"We expect cash capital expenditures to decrease in 2023, primarily due to lower spending on our fulfillment network."

The FY2023 10-K deletes it and folds the opposite expectation into a trailing clause:

"Cash capital expenditures were $58.3 billion, and $48.1 billion in 2022 and 2023, which primarily reflect investments in technology infrastructure (the majority of which is to support AWS business growth) and in additional capacity to support our fulfillment network, which investments we expect to increase in 2024."

No magnitude qualifier is attached at this stage; "meaningfully" arrives one quarter later.

▶ Server useful-life extension first appears, quantified only in guidance New

In the Q1 2024 guidance bullet, with no predecessor:

"This guidance includes approximately $0.9 billion lower depreciation expense due to an increase in the estimated useful life of our servers beginning on January 1, 2024."

The Q3 2023 guidance bullet carried no such clause. This is the first of three useful-life revisions in the window; the MD&A never quantifies the later ones.

▶ Four AI risk categories added at once; the FTC is named for the first time New

The FY2023 10-K adds AI as an intellectual-property exposure, with no predecessor:

"In addition, our and our customers' use of artificial intelligence may result in increased claims of infringement or other claims, including those based on unauthorized use of third-party technology or content."

As a supply constraint, also new:

"For example, we rely on a limited group of suppliers for semiconductor products, including products related to artificial intelligence infrastructure such as graphics processing units. Constraints on the availability of these products could adversely affect our ability to develop and operate artificial intelligence technologies, products, or services."

Also added: public perception of AI development as a reputational factor, and AI services inside the litigation-exposure list. Separately, the Federal Trade Commission is named for the first time — Q3 2023 read "including those brought by state attorneys general"; the 10-K reads "including those brought by state attorneys general and the Federal Trade Commission."

▶ Antitrust investigations rewritten: "violate" to "infringe", AWS named, Europe clause deleted Changed

Q3 2023:

"For example, we face a number of open investigations based on claims that aspects of our operations violate competition rules, including aspects of Amazon's U.S. and European marketplace for sellers, particularly with respect to use of data, fulfillment services, and featured offers, and legislative and regulatory initiatives in Europe and elsewhere allow authorities to restrict or prohibit certain operations or actions pre-emptively without the need to assess specific competitive effects."

FY2023 10-K:

"For example, we face a number of open investigations based on claims that aspects of our operations infringe competition rules, including aspects of Amazon's operation of its stores including its fulfillment network, Amazon's acquisitions, and certain aspects of AWS's offering of cloud services. We strongly dispute these claims and intend to defend ourselves vigorously in these investigations."

AWS cloud services and acquisitions are named as investigation subjects for the first time; the pre-emptive-regulation clause is deleted; a defence assertion is added. A new sentence also introduces the "gatekeepers" framing without naming the Digital Markets Act.

▶ China seller-and-supplier concentration disclosed as a standalone paragraph New

Appended to the PRC/India risk factor, with no predecessor:

"In addition, because China-based sellers account for significant portions of our third-party seller services and advertising revenues, and China-based suppliers provide significant portions of our components and finished goods, regulatory and trade restrictions, data protection and cybersecurity laws, economic factors, geopolitical events, security issues, or other factors negatively impacting China-based sellers and suppliers could adversely affect our operating results."

The cost-input bullet adds land and water as constrained resources in the same filing: "availability of and increases in the prices of transportation (including fuel), resources such as land, water, and energy..." A climate-impact bullet and a corporate-culture bullet are also added.

▶ Cash-tax forward statement deleted; term loan repaid, commercial paper to zero Changed

Q3 2023 stated "As a result, we expect the cash taxes we pay in 2023 to increase significantly." The 10-K removes the sentence and adds a depreciation-election disclosure in its place. The line item is also renamed, from "Cash taxes paid (net of refunds)" to "Cash paid for U.S. (federal and state) and foreign income taxes (net of refunds)."

Liquidity, Q3 2023: "$567 million of borrowings outstanding under the commercial paper programs, $972 million of borrowings outstanding under our Credit Facility, and $5.0 billion of borrowings outstanding under the Term Loan." FY2023 10-K:

"We had no borrowings outstanding under the two unsecured revolving credit facilities or the commercial paper programs, we had $682 million of borrowings outstanding under the secured revolving credit facility, and the entire amount of the term loan has been repaid as of December 31, 2023."

FY2023 10-K → Q1 2024

Filed May 1, 2024 | AWS growth: 17% | Capex: $13.9B | "Meaningfully increase" enters
▶ Capex forward statement returns standalone with "meaningfully" and a technology-infrastructure attribution Signal

FY2023 10-K carried the expectation as a trailing clause ("which investments we expect to increase in 2024"). Q1 2024 restores a standalone sentence and adds two things at once:

"We expect cash capital expenditures to meaningfully increase in 2024, primarily driven by investments in technology infrastructure."

The three consecutive versions read: "decrease in 2023, primarily due to lower spending on our fulfillment network" (Q3 2023) → "which investments we expect to increase in 2024" (10-K) → "meaningfully increase in 2024, primarily driven by investments in technology infrastructure" (Q1 2024). The attribution clause has no predecessor.

▶ Technology and infrastructure expense declines for the first time, attributed to the server useful-life change Changed

FY2023 10-K described an increase driven by infrastructure spend and payroll. Q1 2024:

"The decrease in technology and infrastructure costs in Q1 2024, compared to the comparable prior year period, is primarily due to a reduction in depreciation and amortization expense from our change in the estimated useful life of our servers and decreased payroll and related costs associated with technical teams..."

The expense line grew 0% year over year ($20,450M to $20,424M) against +31% and +17% in prior comparisons. The same useful-life clause is added to the AWS operating income driver, where payroll simultaneously flips from headwind to tailwind ("decreased payroll and related expenses").

▶ AWS enterprise cost-optimization sentence deleted with no successor Signal

Carried in every filing from Q1 2023 through the FY2023 10-K:

"We also expect the current macroeconomic environment and enterprise customer cost optimization efforts to impact our AWS revenue growth rates."

Removed entirely in Q1 2024 and never restored. AWS growth inflected from 13% (FY2023) to 17% in the same filing.

▶ Antitrust investigations broaden to consumer protection and name Prime — the only substantive risk-factor edit of the quarter Changed

FY2023 10-K:

"...claims that aspects of our operations infringe competition rules, including aspects of Amazon's operation of its stores including its fulfillment network, Amazon's acquisitions, and certain aspects of AWS's offering of cloud services."

Q1 2024:

"...claims that aspects of our operations infringe competition or consumer protection rules, including aspects of Amazon's operation of its stores including its fulfillment network and Prime, Amazon's acquisitions, and certain aspects of AWS's offering of cloud services."

Apart from a cross-reference reversal, this two-word escalation is the entire substantive risk-factor change in the transition — the section grew by 32 characters.

▶ Segment sales drivers reweight third-party sellers and add "fast" to shipping offers Changed

In both the North America and International sales drivers:

"The sales growth primarily reflects increased unit sales, including sales by third-party sellers, advertising sales, and subscription services. Increased unit sales were driven largely by our continued focus on price, selection, and convenience for our customers, including from our fast shipping offers."

Prior filings read "primarily by third-party sellers" and "our shipping offers". Both edits persist unchanged through Q2 2026.

Q1 2024 → Q2 2024

Filed Aug 2, 2024 | AWS growth: 17% → 19% | Capex: $17.6B (qtr) | Backlog: $157.7B → $156.6B
▶ Technology and infrastructure reverses to growth; the useful-life benefit is demoted to an offset Changed

Q1 2024 led with the useful-life reduction as the primary cause of a decline. Q2 2024 inverts the causal order:

"The increase in technology and infrastructure costs in Q2 2024 and for the six months ended June 30, 2024, compared to the comparable prior year periods, is primarily due to an increase in spending on infrastructure, partially offset by a reduction in depreciation and amortization expense from our change in the estimated useful life of our servers and decreased payroll and related costs..."

Infrastructure spend moves from the offset clause to the primary cause; the useful-life benefit moves the other way. It stays in that demoted position through the FY2024 10-K and then disappears from MD&A entirely.

▶ Capex forward statement unchanged for three straight quarters while quarterly capex rises 53% Signal

Identical in Q1, Q2, and Q3 2024:

"We expect cash capital expenditures to meaningfully increase in 2024, primarily driven by investments in technology infrastructure."

The attribution parenthetical is also unchanged: "(the majority of which is to support AWS business growth)". Over the same three filings, discrete quarterly purchases of property and equipment ran $14.9B, $17.6B, and $22.6B. No AI or generative-AI attribution appears anywhere in the 2024 MD&A.

▶ Anthropic description compresses; discrete tax benefits attributed to share-based compensation Changed

Q1 2024: "we invested $1.25 billion in a note from Anthropic, which is convertible to equity." Q2 2024:

"In Q3 2023, we invested $1.25 billion in a convertible note from Anthropic. In Q1 2024, we invested $2.75 billion in a second convertible note."

Separately, the discrete-item attribution is stated for the first time:

"Our income tax provision for the six months ended June 30, 2024 was $4.2 billion, which included $1.9 billion of net discrete tax benefits primarily attributable to excess tax benefits from stock-based compensation."
▶ North America drops technology and infrastructure from its operating-income offsets Changed

Q1 2024: "...partially offset by increased shipping and fulfillment costs and increased technology and infrastructure costs." Q2 2024:

"The increase in North America operating income in Q2 2024 and for the six months ended June 30, 2024, compared to the comparable prior year periods, is primarily due to increased unit sales and increased advertising sales, partially offset by increased fulfillment and shipping costs."

The Leap Year quantification added in Q1 2024 guidance ("added approximately 120 basis points to the year-over-year net sales growth rate") is removed from Q3 guidance with no replacement.

Q2 2024 → Q3 2024

Filed Nov 1, 2024 | AWS growth: 19% (flat) | Capex: $22.6B (qtr) | Backlog: ~$164B | Risk factors: no substantive change
▶ Satellite network capitalization policy disclosed for the first time New

Appended to the Technology and Infrastructure discussion, with no predecessor:

"We currently expense the majority of the costs associated with the development of our satellite network for global broadband service (including production, launch, and payroll costs, and launch services deposits upon launch). We will capitalize certain of these costs once the service achieves commercial viability, including sales to customers."

The sentence carries forward unchanged through Q2 2026.

▶ Macro paragraph rewritten and shortened — capital market and supply chain volatility removed Changed

Q2 2024, unchanged since Q1 2023:

"Macroeconomic factors, including inflation, increased interest rates, significant capital market and supply chain volatility, and global economic and geopolitical developments, have direct and indirect impacts on our results of operations that are difficult to isolate and quantify. In addition, changes in fuel, utility, and food costs, interest rates, and economic outlook may impact customer demand and our ability to forecast consumer spending patterns."

Q3 2024:

"Macroeconomic factors, including changes in inflation and interest rates, as well as global economic and geopolitical developments, have direct and indirect impacts on our results of operations that are difficult to isolate and quantify. These may impact customer demand for our products and services and our ability to forecast spending patterns."

Capital market volatility, supply chain volatility, and the fuel/utility/food cost list are all deleted; "consumer" is dropped from spending patterns. This is the shortest the paragraph gets before tariffs and AI are added in Q1 2025.

▶ Credit Facility borrowings run to zero; restricted cash jumps from $503M to $3.6B Changed

The Credit Facility clause is deleted entirely. Q2 2024: "...and we had $183 million of borrowings outstanding under our Credit Facility as of June 30, 2024." Q3 2024:

"We had no borrowings outstanding under the two unsecured revolving credit facilities or the commercial paper programs as of September 30, 2024."

Restricted cash moves an order of magnitude in the same filing: "As of December 31, 2023 and September 30, 2024, restricted cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities were $503 million and $3.6 billion."

▶ Risk factors: zero substantive change — page renumbering only Signal

The risk-factor section is byte-identical between Q2 and Q3 2024 (59,529 characters both quarters, 248 lines both quarters, all 24 headings identical). The entire diff consists of page-number footers incrementing by one.

This is the first of three consecutive-quarter pairs in the dataset with no risk-factor change whatsoever. The others are Q1→Q2 2025 and Q2→Q3 2025.

Q3 2024 → FY2024 10-K

Filed Feb 7, 2025 | FY2024 capex: $77.7B | "Meaningfully" dropped | Tariff policy changes first appears
▶ "Meaningfully" is dropped from the capex forward statement Signal

Q3 2024:

"We expect cash capital expenditures to meaningfully increase in 2024, primarily driven by investments in technology infrastructure."

FY2024 10-K:

"We expect cash capital expenditures to increase in 2025, primarily driven by investments in technology infrastructure."

The qualifier is removed in the filing that reports FY2024 capex of $77.7 billion, up from $48.1 billion. No dollar figure, range, or percentage is attached to the 2025 expectation, and no AI attribution is added.

▶ Two useful-life revisions in opposite directions, quantified only in Note 1 New

Note 1 of the FY2024 10-K discloses both a shortening and a lengthening, with the AI reason stated:

"We completed our most recent servers and networking equipment useful life study in Q4 2024, and are changing the useful lives of a subset of our servers and networking equipment, effective January 1, 2025, from six years to five years... we anticipate a decrease in 2025 operating income of approximately $0.7 billion."
"In Q4 2024, we completed a useful life study for certain types of heavy equipment and are increasing the useful life from ten years to thirteen years... we estimate an increase in 2025 operating income of approximately $0.9 billion, which will be recorded primarily in “Fulfillment” and impact our North America and International segments."

The stated cause:

"These two changes above are due to an increased pace of technology development, particularly in the area of artificial intelligence and machine learning."

A further $920 million of accelerated depreciation was recorded in Q4 2024 for early server retirements. None of these figures appears in MD&A.

▶ "Tariff policy changes" enters the risk factors for the first time New

Added to the disruptions bullet, with no predecessor:

"...labor or trade disputes (including restrictive governmental actions impacting us, our customers, and our third-party sellers and suppliers in China or other foreign countries), tariff policy changes, and similar events; and"

"Tariffs" had existed only as a customs-duty input word. This single bullet is the seed of a six-location propagation one quarter later.

▶ The only risk-factor heading change in four years: "Breaches" becomes "Incidents" Changed

Heading, Q3 2024: "We Could Be Harmed by Data Loss or Other Security Breaches". FY2024 10-K: "We Could Be Harmed by Data Loss or Other Security Incidents". The body follows, and "prevent or mitigate" becomes "prevent, detect, or mitigate":

"...failure to prevent, detect, or mitigate data loss, theft, misuse, unauthorized access, or other security incidents or vulnerabilities affecting our or our vendors' or customers' technology, products, and systems, could:"

Across all six risk-factor versions in this dataset, this is the only heading added, removed, or retitled; the 24-heading set and its order are otherwise identical throughout.

▶ Satellite communications and healthcare enter the competition and regulation lists Changed

Competition factor: "...electronic devices, digital content, advertising, grocery, healthcare, communications, and transportation and logistics services." Government Regulation:

"...advertising, artificial intelligence technologies and services, satellite communications services, healthcare, and other products and services that we offer or sell."

In the same filing, "Amazon's acquisitions" is dropped from the antitrust investigations list — the only removal from that list in the window. The reputation bullet separates corporate positions from AI and adds automation: "public perceptions regarding our positions on social or ethical issues and our development and use of artificial intelligence, machine learning, and automation technologies, products, and services".

FY2024 10-K → Q1 2025

Filed May 2, 2025 | AWS growth: 17% | Capex: $25.0B (qtr) | Tariffs propagate; two FCF measures deleted
▶ Tariff language propagates to six risk-factor locations — the entire substantive change of the quarter Signal

Ten substantive risk-factor edits, every one tariff or trade. The FY2024 10-K's bare "tariff policy changes" is expanded:

"...tariff policy changes (such as tariffs proposed or implemented by the U.S. and other countries and any retaliatory actions), and similar events; and"

It is also inserted into the trade-laws bullet ("affecting trade (such as tariff policy changes)"), the China concentration paragraph ("tariff policy changes and trade disputes"), the retail-variability factor ("rising inflation (including as a result of tariff policy changes)"), the shipping-company paragraph, and the inventory-risk factor ("rapid changes in product cycles and pricing (including as a result of tariff policy changes)"). "Trade protection measures" becomes "trade protection or retaliatory measures".

AI, antitrust, tax, privacy, labor, and capacity language show zero edits in the same transition.

▶ Tariffs and AI enter the MD&A macro paragraph together Changed

FY2024 10-K:

"Macroeconomic factors, including changes in inflation and interest rates, global economic and geopolitical developments, and the development and adoption of technologies and services, including artificial intelligence, have direct and indirect impacts on our results of operations that are difficult to isolate and quantify. These could affect customer demand for our products and services, our ability to predict growth needs, expenses, and the benefits we gain from new technologies."

Q1 2025:

"Macroeconomic factors, including changes in inflation and interest rates, global economic and geopolitical developments, including unpredictable shifts in global tariff and trade policies, and the development and adoption of technologies and services, including artificial intelligence, have direct and indirect impacts on our results of operations that are difficult to predict, isolate, and quantify. These could affect customer demand for our products and services, our ability to forecast growth needs, expenses, and benefits from new technologies."

"Tariff and trade policies" is added to the Forward-Looking Statements list and the Guidance preamble in the same filing.

▶ Two of three free cash flow measures deleted, and the rationale for reporting multiples is removed Changed

"Free Cash Flow Less Principal Repayments of Finance Leases and Financing Obligations" and "Free Cash Flow Less Equipment Finance Leases and Principal Repayments of All Other Finance Leases and Financing Obligations" are removed with their headings and reconciliation tables. The framing changes from plural to singular:

"We provide multiple measures of free cash flows because we believe these measures provide additional perspective on the impact of acquiring property and equipment with cash and through finance leases and financing obligations."

Q1 2025:

"Our financial focus is on long-term, sustainable growth in free cash flow. We provide a free cash flow measure because we believe it provides additional perspective on the impact of acquiring property and equipment with cash."

The deleted measures were the two that netted finance-lease and financing-obligation repayments — the measures most affected by lease-funded infrastructure.

▶ The server useful-life reference disappears from MD&A in the quarter the estimate reverses Signal

Both MD&A references are deleted. FY2024 10-K, AWS driver: "...increased sales, decreased payroll and related expenses, and a reduction in depreciation and amortization expense from our change in the estimated useful lives of our servers, partially offset by..." Q1 2025:

"The increase in AWS operating income in Q1 2025, compared to the comparable prior year period, is primarily due to increased sales and decreased payroll and related expenses, partially offset by spending on technology infrastructure that was primarily driven by additional investments to support AWS business growth."

The phrase "useful li" occurs zero times in the Q1 2025 MD&A. The reversal itself is disclosed only in Note 1: "an increase in depreciation and amortization expense of $217 million and a reduction in net income of $162 million, or $0.02 per basic share".

▶ Anthropic third note and a forward funding commitment; sales and marketing driver flips Changed

The FY2024 10-K added, with no predecessor:

"In Q4 2024, we entered into an agreement and invested $1.3 billion in a third convertible note, and will invest an additional $2.7 billion by Q4 2025."

Q1 2025 also introduces the first Anthropic conversion gain: "The net gain of $2.7 billion in Q1 2025 is primarily from the reclassification adjustment for the gain on available-for-sale debt securities from the portion of our convertible notes investments in Anthropic, PBC that were converted to nonvoting preferred stock..." Sales and marketing reverses direction, with advertising and payroll swapping primary and offsetting roles.

Q1 2025 → Q2 2025

Filed Aug 1, 2025 | AWS growth: 17% (flat) | Capex: $32.2B (qtr) | Forward capex sentence disappears
▶ The standalone forward capex sentence is deleted and folded into a trailing clause Signal

Q1 2025 ended the capex paragraph with a separate forward sentence:

"...and in additional capacity to support our fulfillment network. We expect cash capital expenditures to increase in 2025, primarily driven by investments in technology infrastructure."

Q2 2025:

"...and in additional capacity to support our fulfillment network, both of which we expect to increase in 2025."

The technology-infrastructure attribution is removed and the expectation is broadened to cover fulfillment capacity as well. From this quarter forward, AMZN's MD&A carries no standalone forward capex statement — only a trailing clause — through Q2 2026.

▶ The 2025 Tax Act enters MD&A; two prior-law sentences are deleted New

Q1 2025 described phase-outs and R&D capitalization. Q2 2025 replaces them:

"The 2025 Tax Act makes changes to the U.S. corporate income tax, including reinstating the option to claim 100% accelerated depreciation deductions on qualified property, with retroactive application beginning January 20, 2025 and immediate expensing of domestic research and development costs, with retroactive application beginning January 1, 2025. While we are still evaluating the full extent of the 2025 Tax Act's impact, we expect these changes to significantly reduce U.S. cash taxes we pay in 2025."

The statement "These enhanced deductions are scheduled to phase out annually from 2023 through 2026" is deleted. The hedge is removed one quarter later.

▶ Anthropic forward commitment halves from $2.7B to $1.4B Changed

Q1 2025 carried the FY2024 10-K commitment: "will invest an additional $2.7 billion by Q4 2025." Q2 2025:

"...which primarily reflect investments in convertible notes from Anthropic, including $1.3 billion we invested in Q2 2025. Furthermore, we will invest an additional $1.4 billion in another convertible note by Q4 2025."

The full three-note chronology from prior filings is deleted. The residual commitment steps down as the Q2 tranche is funded.

▶ "Including depreciation and amortization" added as infrastructure spend accelerates Changed

Themes

1. The Numbers Moved Before the Words

AWS growth went from 12% to 37% and quarterly capex from $10.4B to $54.2B while the sentences explaining both stayed fixed. The AWS driver sentence is unedited across all eleven quarters; the capex attribution parenthetical is unedited across all fourteen filings. Where language did move, it moved by subtraction: "meaningfully" removed from the capex outlook, the standalone forward capex sentence deleted, the AWS cost-optimization warning deleted, two of three free cash flow measures deleted, and the server useful-life reference deleted in the quarter the estimate turned against earnings. Q2 2026 follows the same pattern: sales growth accelerated to 20% with MD&A citing unit sales, and the Prime Day timing shift that explains part of it appears only in the next quarter’s guidance.

2. The Signal Migrated to the Notes

For this filer the fastest-moving disclosures are not in MD&A or the risk factors. AWS backlog ($122.0B to $496B) and contract life (3.5 to 6.4 years) appear only in the revenue note. The OpenAI and Anthropic commitment expansions, each "over $100.0 billion", appear only in the revenue note. Every useful-life quantification appears only in Note 1. The commitments table ($287B to $650B) and the entire legal docket sit in Note 4. Reading MD&A alone would miss all of it.

3. Two Balance Sheets Growing at Once

Contractual commitments rose from $287B to $650B and senior notes from a $54.3B trough to $132.1B plus a $25.0B post-quarter issuance and a $17.5B undrawn term loan. On the asset side, strategic investments went from a $1.25B convertible note to a $50.0B OpenAI position and a $10.0B Anthropic preferred stake, generating $53.4B of other income in a single quarter and a $15.9B discrete tax charge. The two sides are linked: both AI investees also signed AWS commitments of $100B or more, and Anthropic’s $20.0B funding facility is released as AWS delivers compute. Q2 2026 net income of $62.6B exceeds operating income of $27.5B, and trailing-twelve-month free cash flow turned negative at $(7.6)B.

4. Risk Factors Are an Annual Document

Three of seven intra-year quarterly transitions contain zero substantive risk-factor change, and the other four contain one or two edits each. Everything material — four AI risk categories in the FY2023 10-K, "tariff policy changes" in the FY2024 10-K, seven AI escalations in the FY2025 10-K — arrives at the annual boundary. The lone exception to the annual pattern is Q1 2025, where tariff language propagated to six locations in a quarterly filing. That is what a genuine intra-year risk shift looks like in this filer, and it has happened once in four years.
