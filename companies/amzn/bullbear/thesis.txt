AMAZON.COM (AMZN): INVESTMENT THESIS PILLARS
Written October 2026 from the 10-K dashboard and 10-Q scanner themes and the eight filings from the
Q3 2024 10-Q (filed November 1, 2024) to the Q2 2026 10-Q (filed July 31, 2026); updated for the Q3 2026
10-Q (filed October 30, 2026). Stances move only when findings point the same way across two or more
filings; "last moved" names the second filing.

P1 AWS growth and backlog
  Bull assumes: AI and cloud demand keep AWS growing faster than 20% a year at operating margins in the
    mid-to-high 30s, and the remaining performance obligation (backlog) turns into revenue over 4-6 years.
  Bear fears: growth stalls while capacity is being built, pricing on long-term contracts dilutes
    margins, and a backlog concentrated in a few AI labs proves less firm than it looks.
  Stance: Strengthening (moved at the FY2025 10-K). AWS growth ran 19% (Q3 2024), 17% (Q1 and Q2 2025),
    then reaccelerated to 20% (Q3 2025), about 24% (Q4 2025, derived), 28% (Q1 2026), 37% (Q2 2026) and
    39% (Q3 2026). Backlog went $164B -> $177B -> $189B -> $195B -> $200B -> $244B -> $364B -> $496B -> $578B;
    Q3 2026 added $82B with no new named commitment, but the figure is now "estimated" and its average life
    eased from 6.4 to 6.1 years.
  Tests: AWS growth and segment margin each quarter; backlog and its weighted-average life; how much of
    the backlog comes from OpenAI and Anthropic.

P2 Retail margins (North America and International)
  Bull assumes: advertising, third-party seller services and fulfillment efficiency keep lifting North
    America margins toward 8% and International margins toward 5%.
  Bear fears: Amazon Leo (satellite), quick commerce, sharper international pricing, tariffs and
    shipping costs absorb the gains; one-off items flatter reported margins.
  Stance: Strengthening (moved at the Q2 2025 10-Q). North America margin 5.9% (Q3 2024) -> 6.4% (FY2024)
    -> 7.5% (Q2 2025) -> 6.9% (FY2025, after the $2.5B FTC charge) -> 7.9% (Q1 and Q2 2026) -> 7.7% (Q3
    2026, no refunds). International went from losses in 2023 to 4.1% (Q2 2025 and Q2 2026) and 3.7% (Q3
    2026). The Q2 2026 North America figure includes about $640M of IEEPA tariff refunds. Watch item from
    Q3 2026: advertising growth fell from 26% to 11% (12% excluding FX), guided to "high teens" for Q4.
  Tests: segment operating margins excluding named one-offs; Leo costs; shipping cost growth vs sales.

P3 Capital spending returns and free cash flow
  Bull assumes: the capex surge earns AWS-like returns; operating cash flow grows fast enough to fund it
    and free cash flow recovers once capacity comes online.
  Bear fears: capex keeps growing faster than operating cash flow, free cash flow stays negative, and
    shorter server lives raise depreciation before the revenue arrives.
  Stance: Weakening (moved at the Q2 2025 10-Q). Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow went $47.7B (Q3 2024)
    -> $38.2B -> $25.9B -> $18.2B -> $14.8B -> $11.2B -> $1.2B -> -$7.6B (Q2 2026) -> -$42.4B (Q3 2026),
    while trailing operating cash flow rose from $112.7B to $168.3B. Cash capex: $77.7B (2024), $128.3B
    (2025), $172.2B (nine months 2026), about $220B guided for 2026 and a "meaningfully" higher 2027. From
    Q3 2026 the free cash flow caveat names "non-marketable investments" as excluded, and the new Loan
    Facility's repayments will be financing outflows, so the headline measure understates cash spent.
  Tests: trailing free cash flow, capex vs operating cash flow, AWS margin as new capacity is depreciated.

P4 Balance sheet and funding
  Bull assumes: net cash, unused credit lines and investment-grade access let Amazon fund the build-out
    without strain.
  Bear fears: borrowing replaces free cash flow as the funding source; fixed commitments (leases not yet
    commenced, purchase obligations, investee funding) pile up off balance sheet.
  Stance: Weakening (moved at the Q1 2026 10-Q). Notes outstanding fell to $58.0B (FY2024) and $54.3B
    (Q3 2025), then rose to $68.0B (FY2025, $15B issued November 2025), $121.8B (Q1 2026), $132.1B
    (Q2 2026) and $162.5B (Q3 2026), plus the $17.5B term loan drawn in September 2026 (face value of
    long-term debt $180.9B). Q3 2026 added an $80.0B secured supplier Loan Facility (Q1 2027-Q3 2028), the
    first secured borrowing since 2024. Total contractual commitments $333.0B (FY2024) -> $439.7B (FY2025)
    -> $650.0B (Q2 2026) -> $883.6B (Q3 2026, incl. a new $159.6B property and equipment row).
  Tests: notes outstanding, interest expense vs operating income, cash and marketable securities,
    commitments table, any rating language.

P5 Earnings quality
  Bull assumes: reported earnings track operating income; accounting estimates are conservative.
  Bear fears: Level 3 mark-ups on private AI investments, tax items, useful-life changes and one-time
    refunds inflate net income and EPS.
  Stance: Weakening (moved at the Q3 2025 10-Q). Non-operating gains, mostly Anthropic: $2.7B (Q1 2025),
    $10.2B (Q3 2025), $15.2B (FY2025), $15.6B (Q1 2026), $53.4B (Q2 2026), then $(703)M (Q3 2026), when net
    income ($20.7B) fell back below operating income ($28.1B). One filing does not move the stance: the Q3
    2026 Anthropic gain ($48.3B on the notes) went to other comprehensive income instead, and energy-contract
    volumes marked through operating income rose to 690M MWh. The FY2024 server-life shortening (6 to 5
    years) was a conservative move.
  Tests: other income vs pre-tax income; discrete tax items; one-offs inside operating income.

P6 AI-lab investments and customer concentration
  Bull assumes: stakes in Anthropic and OpenAI gain in value and anchor multi-year AWS contracts.
  Bear fears: Amazon funds its own customers (vendor financing in effect); a large share of the backlog
    and of the balance sheet depends on two cash-burning private companies.
  Stance: Weakening (moved at the Q2 2026 10-Q). Q1 2026: $15.0B into OpenAI plus a $35.0B commitment,
    alongside a $100.0B expansion of OpenAI's AWS commitment. Q2 2026: $10.0B into Anthropic, a $20.0B
    financing facility, and a more-than-$100.0B Anthropic AWS commitment. Q3 2026: OpenAI's remaining $21.3B
    paid ($50.0B total); Anthropic "12% ownership cap" (waivable) and share counts disclosed. Private equity
    holdings carried at $143.7B and Anthropic notes at $146.2B (September 30, 2026).
  Tests: further funding commitments, facility draws, any impairment or down-round, share of backlog
    from investees (not disclosed).

P7 Legal and regulatory
  Bull assumes: antitrust and privacy cases resolve with fines that are small relative to cash flow and
    no structural remedy.
  Bear fears: the FTC and state antitrust cases force changes to Prime, Featured Offer or seller
    practices; patent cases (Kove, Xockets) hit AWS hardware.
  Stance: Weakening (moved at the Q3 2026 10-Q). The FTC Prime case settled for $2.5B (Q3 2025); Italy's
    fine was cut to EUR 752M; the RPI Alexa patent ruling was affirmed for Amazon. Against that, antitrust
    cases seek structural relief, the Kove $525M verdict plus $148M interest is on appeal, the ITC opened an
    investigation into GB200 servers in June 2026 (widened to GB300 in Q3 2026), and in August 2026 the FTC
    and 22 states sued over reserve prices in sponsored-ad auctions. Two filings in a row added proceedings
    aimed at current growth lines (AI servers, advertising), which moves the stance.
  Tests: antitrust trial dates and rulings, the advertising case's schedule and remedies sought, ITC outcome
    on Xockets, Kove appeal.
