Amazon.com 10-K Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of SEC filings reveals the transformation from e-commerce retailer to AI infrastructure colossus -- tracked through language shifts, category additions, and financial inflection points invisible to surface-level reading.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001018724) | Fiscal Years 2018-2025 | Analysis: March 2026; FY2024-FY2025 transitions re-analyzed October 2026 (sentence-level shift and history checks)
FY2018 → FY2019

Advertising Formalized

Ad business specified for first time. Ring added to devices. "Lowest prices possible" dropped. Grocery competitors added. Employees +23% to 798K.

FY2019 → FY2020

COVID Surge

Revenue +38%. Employees +63% to 1.3M. Capex doubled to $35B. Human Capital section added (SEC mandate). ML added to AWS description. Advertising category (9) added to competition.

FY2020 → FY2021

Scale Peak & Jassy Era

Revenue $470B. Employees peak at 1.6M. Advertisers elevated to primary customer set. Bezos→Jassy transition. "Earth's best employer." Capex $55B.

FY2021 → FY2022

Post-COVID Reckoning

Operating income -51%. NA segment turns negative. Employees decline first time (-4%). Safety narrative added. Climate change first mentioned. Blink/eero added.

FY2022 → FY2023

Efficiency & AI Inflection

OI triples to $36.9B. AI enters risk factors (competition, IP, reputation). FTC litigation named. "Gatekeeper" language. Capex declines to $48B. Anthropic $1.25B.

FY2023 → FY2024

Capex Re-Commits

AWS re-accelerates to 19% and its cost-optimization warning is deleted. Capex outlook pinned to technology infrastructure ($77.7B). Server life extended, then cut back to five years "due to" AI. Kuiper costs expensed. Seller lending wound down. DEI language removed. "Tariff policy changes" first appears.

FY2024 → FY2025

The AI Infrastructure Bet

Capex $128.3B; FCF falls to $11.2B and two of three FCF measures are deleted. First forward AI-investment sentence. AWS margin slips to 35.4%. $2.5B FTC settlement, $2.7B severance. Anthropic re-marks drive $15.2B other income, and about $15B more is pre-announced for Q1 2026. "Tariff policy changes" spreads to six places.

FY2018 → FY2019: Advertising Formalized

Filed Feb 2020 | Employees: 647K → 798K (+23%) | Revenue: $232.9B → $280.5B (+20%) | AWS: ~$25.7B → ~$35.0B

Business

▶Advertising clients and formats specified for first timeNew
FY2018 filing referenced advertising only in passing. FY2019 introduced specific client types and ad formats:
"sellers, vendors, publishers, authors" + "sponsored ads, display, video"
This was the first time Amazon formally described its advertising business with specificity -- signaling its emergence as a material revenue stream.
▶"Lowest prices possible" dropped; strategic reframing to experienceChanged
FY2018: "We strive to offer our customers the lowest prices possible"
FY2019: "We seek to offer our customers low prices, fast and free delivery, easy-to-use functionality"
Dropped "lowest...possible" -- a subtle but significant strategic reframing from price-only competition to a broader value proposition encompassing delivery speed and UX.
▶Ring added to devices listNew
Ring (acquired April 2018) added to the Amazon devices list alongside Kindle, Fire, and Echo. First post-acquisition disclosure in the 10-K, reflecting Amazon's push into home security and smart home ecosystem.
▶Grocery competitors added as new competition categoryNew
Competition section expanded from 7 to 8 categories with the addition of:
"(8) companies that sell grocery products online and in physical stores"
Reflects Whole Foods integration maturing and Amazon Fresh expansion. First time grocery was called out as a distinct competitive battleground.

FY2019 → FY2020: COVID Surge

Filed Feb 2021 | Employees: 798K → 1,298K (+63%) | Revenue: $280.5B → $386.1B (+38%) | AWS: ~$35.0B → $45.4B

Business

▶"Machine learning" and "analytics" added to core AWS descriptionChanged
AWS service description expanded to include "machine learning" and "analytics" as named capabilities. Previously AWS was described primarily around compute, storage, and database. This marked the first time ML was embedded in the core Item 1 AWS description -- a precursor to the AI transformation that would follow.
▶Human Capital section created (SEC mandate)New
In response to the SEC's November 2020 mandate, Amazon created a dedicated Human Capital section covering:
"investment and innovation, inclusion and diversity, safety, and engagement"
Prior filings had minimal employee disclosure. This section would become the locus for DEI language evolution and safety narratives in subsequent years.
▶Advertising services added as 9th competition categoryNew
Competition section expanded from 8 to 9 categories:
"(9) providers of advertising services"
Advertising was now large enough to warrant its own competitive category -- Amazon was officially competing with Google and Meta for ad dollars.
▶Capex doubled from ~$16.9B to $35.0BSignal
Capital expenditures more than doubled in a single year, driven by COVID-era fulfillment and logistics buildout. This set the trajectory for the massive infrastructure investments that would define the next five years -- peaking at $128.3B in FY2025.

FY2020 → FY2021: Scale Peak & Jassy Era

Filed Feb 2022 | Employees: 1,298K → 1,608K (+24%) | Revenue: $386.1B → $469.8B (+22%) | AWS: $45.4B → $62.2B

Business

▶"Advertisers" elevated to primary customer set with own sectionChanged
Advertisers were elevated from a passing mention to a primary customer set alongside consumers, sellers, developers, and enterprises. Advertising received its own descriptive section in Item 1 for the first time -- a structural promotion reflecting its growing revenue contribution.
▶Prime description softened: "100 million items" droppedChanged
FY2020: "unlimited free shipping on over 100 million items"
FY2021: "fast, free shipping on millions of items"
Dropped "unlimited" and the specific "100 million" claim. The shift from precision to vagueness suggests either catalog changes or legal caution about quantified promises.
▶CEO transition: Bezos to Jassy; Selipsky becomes AWS CEOChanged
Andy Jassy succeeded Jeff Bezos as CEO in July 2021. Adam Selipsky became AWS CEO. The filing reflected the most significant leadership transition in Amazon's history -- the founder stepping back after 27 years.
▶Human Capital: "Earth's best employer"; constrained labor marketsNew
The Human Capital section added the aspiration to be "Earth's best employer" and acknowledged constrained labor markets for the first time. With 1.6M employees -- the peak headcount -- Amazon was the second-largest private employer in the US and labor scarcity was becoming a material risk.

FY2021 → FY2022: Post-COVID Reckoning

Filed Feb 2023 | Employees: 1,608K → 1,541K (-4%) | Revenue: $469.8B → $514.0B (+9%) | AWS: $62.2B → $80.1B

Business

▶Blink and eero added to devicesNew
Blink (smart cameras) and eero (mesh networking) added to the Amazon devices list. Both were acquisitions being formally recognized in the 10-K for the first time, extending the smart home ecosystem beyond Ring, Kindle, Fire, and Echo.
▶Safety narrative added to Human Capital; Career Choice at 100K+New
A dedicated safety narrative was added to the Human Capital section for the first time, reflecting increased public scrutiny of warehouse working conditions. Career Choice program disclosed as serving 100,000+ employees -- a scale metric intended to demonstrate worker investment.
▶"Extreme weather (including as a result of climate change)" first mentionedNew
Climate change appeared in risk factors for the first time:
"extreme weather (including as a result of climate change)"
This was Amazon's first acknowledgment of climate as a business risk in the 10-K, reflecting both physical supply chain risks and regulatory pressure.

Risk Factors

▶Sustainability initiative failure risk addedNew
New risk factor acknowledging that Amazon's sustainability commitments (Climate Pledge, net-zero by 2040) could fail to be achieved, exposing the company to reputational harm and regulatory consequences. First time sustainability appeared as a standalone risk.
▶Operating income collapsed -51%; NA segment turned negativeSignal
Operating income fell from $24.9B to $12.2B (-51%). The North America segment turned negative for the first time as the post-COVID demand hangover collided with the infrastructure buildout. This was the financial reckoning for the pandemic-era expansion.

FY2022 → FY2023: Efficiency & AI Inflection

Filed Feb 2024 | Employees: 1,541K → 1,525K (-1%) | Revenue: $514.0B → $574.8B (+12%) | AWS: $80.1B → $90.8B

Business

▶GC title changed: "General Counsel" to "Global Public Policy and General Counsel"Changed
The General Counsel's title expanded to "SVP, Global Public Policy and General Counsel" -- reflecting the growing importance of government affairs and public policy in Amazon's operations. The legal function was formally absorbing the policy function.
▶Prime: "millions" upgraded to "tens of millions" of itemsChanged
FY2022: "fast, free shipping on millions of items"
FY2023: "fast, free shipping on tens of millions of items"
A 10x upgrade in the catalog claim, reflecting continued marketplace expansion and same-day delivery buildout.

Risk Factors

▶AI added to competition risk: "practical applications of artificial intelligence"New
AI appeared in competition risk factors for the first time:
"practical applications of artificial intelligence and machine learning"
This was the inflection year -- AI went from absent in risk factors to present across multiple dimensions in a single filing cycle.
▶AI reputation risk: social/ethical issues from AI developmentNew
New risk factor addressing:
"social or ethical issues related to our development and use of artificial intelligence"
Amazon acknowledged that AI could create reputational harm -- a risk that did not exist in any prior filing.
▶AI IP risk: AI use may result in increased infringement claimsNew
New risk factor:
"use of artificial intelligence may result in increased claims of infringement"
Amazon flagged AI-generated content and AI training data as sources of potential IP litigation -- the same risk that would later manifest in copyright lawsuits across the industry.
▶FTC litigation named; "gatekeeper" language first appearsNew
The FTC's September 2023 antitrust lawsuit was explicitly named in the filing for the first time. "Gatekeeper" language appeared -- reflecting both US enforcement rhetoric and the EU's Digital Markets Act designation. Amazon was now formally identified as a platform gatekeeper by regulators.

Financial

▶OI tripled to $36.9B; Capex declined; Anthropic $1.25B investmentSignal
Operating income tripled from $12.2B to $36.9B as cost discipline took hold. Capex declined to $48.1B (from $58.3B) -- the only year of capex decline in the period. Amazon disclosed a $1.25B convertible note investment in Anthropic, marking its entry into the foundation model race.

FY2023 → FY2024: Capex Re-Commits, the AWS Warning Retires

Filed Feb 7, 2025 | Revenue: $574.78B → $637.96B (+11%) | Operating income: $36.85B → $68.59B | Net income: $30.43B → $59.25B | Operating cash flow: $84.95B → $115.88B | Purchases of property and equipment: $52.73B → $83.00B (Amazon's net "cash capital expenditures": $48.1B → $77.7B) | FCF (OCF less purchases): $32.22B → $32.88B | Employees: 1,525K → 1,556K

MD&A

▶Capex outlook becomes its own sentence with one named driver, technology infrastructure; the 2024 10-Qs' "meaningfully increase" is not carried into the 2025 outlookChanged

Shift: form: trailing clause → standalone sentence (+); scope: two drivers → one ("primarily driven by investments in technology infrastructure"); degree: "meaningfully increase" (Q1–Q3 2024) → "increase" (−)

FY2023: "...and in additional capacity to support our fulfillment network, which investments we expect to increase in 2024."
FY2024: "We expect cash capital expenditures to increase in 2025, primarily driven by investments in technology infrastructure."

History (phrase_history): "which investments we expect to increase in 2024" appears in the FY2023 10-K only; "meaningfully increase" in Q1, Q2 and Q3 2024 only; the FY2024 sentence is repeated in Q1 2025 and then folded back into a trailing clause in Q2 2025.

Amazon's net cash capex rose 62% to $77.7B (gross purchases of property and equipment $83.00B). The 10-K drops the "meaningfully" the 10-Qs used all year, even though 2025 capex turned out 65% higher. AI is not named in the capex sentence; it appears instead in the macro paragraph (next finding).

▶AWS cost-optimization warning gone from the annual report; AI added to the macro paragraph as an outside forceRemoved

Shift: hedge dropped (+): the only AWS-specific growth caution removed; named: + artificial intelligence as a macro factor

FY2023: "We also expect the current macroeconomic environment and enterprise customer cost optimization efforts to impact our AWS revenue growth rates."
FY2024: "Macroeconomic factors, including changes in inflation and interest rates, global economic and geopolitical developments, and the development and adoption of technologies and services, including artificial intelligence, have direct and indirect impacts on our results of operations that are difficult to isolate and quantify."

History (phrase_history): "enterprise customer cost optimization efforts" appears in Q2 2023, Q3 2023 and the FY2023 10-K, and in no filing from Q1 2024 on, so the deletion was made in the first 2024 10-Q. "including artificial intelligence, have direct and indirect impacts" first appears in the FY2024 10-K and is in every filing since. "significant capital market and supply chain volatility" was last used in Q2 2024.

AWS growth recovered from 13% to 19%. The new sentence treats AI as something acting on results, not as a spending commitment; a forward AI-investment sentence does not appear until FY2025.

▶AWS operating income +62% credited partly to lower payroll and longer server lives; AWS margin 27.1% → 37.0%Signal

Shift: direction: "increased payroll" → "decreased payroll" (cost pressure → saving); named: + server useful-life change as a driver

FY2023: "...primarily due to increased sales, partially offset by increased payroll and related expenses and spending on technology infrastructure..."
FY2024: "...primarily due to increased sales, decreased payroll and related expenses, and a reduction in depreciation and amortization expense from our change in the estimated useful lives of our servers, partially offset by spending on technology infrastructure..."

History (phrase_history): "decreased payroll and related expenses" first appears in Q1 2024 and runs through Q2 2025; the server-life clause appears in Q1 2024 through the FY2024 10-K and in no filing after.

G&A and sales and marketing give the same payroll reason. Note 1 puts the 2024 benefit of the five-to-six-year server life at $3.2B of lower depreciation, about a fifth of AWS's $15.2B operating income increase. The same filing reverses part of that estimate (see Notes).

▶Anthropic: an "up to" $2.75B option becomes a third note plus a $2.7B "will invest" commitment; notes marked at ~$13.8BChanged

Shift: certainty: "up to" option → "will invest" by a date (+); quant added: fair value ~$13.8B

FY2023: "We have an agreement that expires in Q1 2024 to invest up to an additional $2.75 billion in a second convertible note."
FY2024: "In Q4 2024, we entered into an agreement and invested $1.3 billion in a third convertible note, and will invest an additional $2.7 billion by Q4 2025."
FY2024: "The notes are classified as available-for-sale and are classified as Level 3 assets, and as of December 31, 2024 had an estimated fair value of approximately $13.8 billion."

History (phrase_history): "invest up to an additional $2.75 billion" appears in Q3 2023 and the FY2023 10-K; "will invest an additional $2.7 billion" in the FY2024 10-K and Q1 2025 only (Q2 2025 restates the remainder as $1.4B after funding $1.3B).

The $5.3B invested so far carries a fair value of about $13.8B. Market risk raises recorded equity and convertible investments from $9.6B to $22.1B and says the available-for-sale convertible notes "primarily relate to Anthropic".

▶New policy: satellite network costs are expensed until the service has paying customersNew

Shift: condition added: capitalization deferred until "commercial viability, including sales to customers" (+ disclosure)

FY2024: "We currently expense the majority of the costs associated with the development of our satellite network for global broadband service (including production, launch, and payroll costs, and launch services deposits upon launch). We will capitalize certain of these costs once the service achieves commercial viability, including sales to customers."

History (phrase_history): first appears in the Q3 2024 10-Q; no FY2023 10-K predecessor; unchanged in every filing through Q2 2026.

Kuiper production and launch costs go through technology and infrastructure expense rather than capex, so the program lowers operating income now, and that drag should ease once capitalization begins. The risk factors add "satellites" to competition and "satellite communications services" to regulation in the same filing. FY2025's guidance gives the first cost figure: about $1B of higher year-over-year Amazon Leo costs in Q1 2026.

Notes & Properties

▶Server life cut from six to five years, a year after it was extended; $920M of accelerated depreciation; both blamed on the pace of AINew

Shift: direction reversed (−): useful life lengthened → shortened; quant added: −$0.7B and −$0.6B to 2025 operating income; named: + "artificial intelligence and machine learning" as the cause

FY2023: "This guidance includes approximately $0.9 billion lower depreciation expense due to an increase in the estimated useful life of our servers beginning on January 1, 2024."
FY2024: "We completed our most recent servers and networking equipment useful life study in Q4 2024, and are changing the useful lives of a subset of our servers and networking equipment, effective January 1, 2025, from six years to five years." ... "These two changes above are due to an increased pace of technology development, particularly in the area of artificial intelligence and machine learning."

History (phrase_history): "from six years to five years" and "increased pace of technology development" first appear in the FY2024 10-K and run through the FY2025 10-K; neither appears in any MD&A.

The shorter life is expected to cut 2025 operating income by about $0.7B and early retirements by about another $0.6B, partly offset by about $0.9B from extending heavy equipment from ten to thirteen years. Amazon now says AI is shortening the economic life of the hardware it is buying in record amounts, which changes the return math behind the capex outlook. MD&A still lists the 2024 extension as a tailwind.

▶Seller lending wound down: the $1.5B facility secured by seller receivables is terminated and "seller lending" disappears from the 10-KRemoved

Shift: realized (+): an expandable facility ("may from time to time increase") → terminated; the program description deleted

FY2023: "We have a $1.5 billion secured revolving credit facility with a lender that is secured by certain seller receivables, which we may from time to time increase in the future subject to lender approval (the “Credit Facility”)."
FY2024: "As of September 30, 2024, we had repaid outstanding borrowings and terminated the secured revolving credit facility with a lender that was secured by certain seller receivables (the “Credit Facility”)."
FY2023: "Seller receivables are amounts due from sellers related to our seller lending program, which provides funding to sellers primarily to procure inventory."

History (phrase_history): "seller lending" appears in every filing from Q2 2023 through Q3 2024 and in none from the FY2024 10-K on; the Q3 2024 10-Q had switched "provides" to "provided".

Amazon ended its marketplace lending without a sentence in MD&A. Seller receivables went from $1.0B to nearly nothing during 2024, and the consolidation note drops "seller lending financing activities".

▶Owned square footage +29% against leased +6%; AWS owned space 17.8M → 24.1M sq ftSignal

Shift: quant only (Item 2 tables); no wording change

History: Item 2 totals compared across the FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks (thousands of sq ft): leased 664,091 → 701,974; owned 52,163 → 67,440 (FY2025: about 78.1M sq ft).

Owned "Fulfillment, data centers, and other" in North America rose from 25,630 to 36,869 (+44%). AWS owned space rose from 17,770 to 24,052 (+35%) and AWS leased space from 20,434 to 24,875. Owned space grew about five times faster than leased space, which suggests more of the buildout is being paid for through capex than through lease commitments.

Business

▶Human Capital drops "inclusion and diversity", "equity for all candidates" and the Technical Academy; Career Choice 175K → 240KRemoved

Shift: deleted (−): the four-part focus list, the equity clause and a named training program

FY2023: "As we strive to be Earth's best employer, we focus on investment and innovation, inclusion and diversity, safety, and engagement to hire and develop the best talent."
FY2024: "We strive to be Earth's best employer."

History (phrase_history): "inclusion and diversity", "promote equity for all candidates" and "Amazon Technical Academy" appear in the FY2023 10-K and in no later filing (Human Capital is a 10-K-only section).

The whole focus list goes, not only the diversity item. The safety paragraph survives intact, so the removals fall on the DEI wording and an engineering-training program.

▶Competitor list: AI tools folded into IT services, healthcare added as category (10), "virtual assistants" added to shopping-discovery rivalsNew

Shift: named: + AI tools, + healthcare providers, + virtual assistants (+ disclosure)

FY2024: "(6) companies that provide information technology services or products, including on-premises or cloud-based infrastructure, tools and services relating to artificial intelligence, and other services" ... "(10) providers of virtual or in-person healthcare services"

History (phrase_history): "virtual assistants" and "virtual or in-person healthcare services" first appear in the FY2024 10-K and are repeated in the FY2025 10-K.

AI is added to an existing category rather than given its own number; healthcare is the only new category (9 → 10). Naming virtual assistants as a way customers find goods is the first sign of the AI-intermediary risk FY2025 builds out.

Risk Factors

▶"Tariff policy changes" enters the risk factors, in one bulletNew

Shift: named: + tariff policy changes as a disruption risk in its own right

FY2023: "...labor or trade disputes (including restrictive governmental actions impacting us, our customers, and our third-party sellers and suppliers in China or other foreign countries), and similar events"
FY2024: "...our third-party sellers and suppliers in China or other foreign countries), tariff policy changes, and similar events"

History (phrase_history): "tariff policy changes" first appears in the FY2024 10-K and is in every filing since; "retaliatory" is added in Q1 2025.

Before this, tariffs appeared only inside the generic "trade protection measures" list. One quarter later, Q1 2025 spreads the wording through six places in the section.

▶Antitrust scrutiny names Prime and consumer-protection rules; "Amazon's acquisitions" droppedChanged

Shift: scope: + consumer protection, + Prime; de-named: "Amazon's acquisitions" removed (− on that target)

FY2023: "...infringe competition rules, including aspects of Amazon's operation of its stores including its fulfillment network, Amazon's acquisitions, and certain aspects of AWS's offering of cloud services."
FY2024: "...infringe competition or consumer protection rules, including aspects of Amazon's operation of its stores, including its fulfillment network and Prime, and certain aspects of AWS's offering of cloud services."

History (phrase_history): "competition or consumer protection rules" first appears in Q1 2024 (so the widening was made in the 10-Qs); "Amazon's acquisitions" stands in the FY2023 10-K and Q1–Q3 2024 and is dropped in the FY2024 10-K; the FY2023 notes record that the iRobot acquisition was terminated in January 2024, which may explain the removal (an inference).

Healthcare and satellite communications are added to the regulated product lines in the same filing. The FY2025 10-K records a $2.5B charge to settle a lawsuit with the FTC.

▶AI reputation risk widened: "development", "automation" and Amazon's own "positions" on social issuesChanged

Shift: scope widened: AI-related issues → Amazon's positions on social issues generally; named: + automation

FY2023: "...public perceptions regarding social or ethical issues related to our development and use of artificial intelligence and machine learning technologies, products, and services"
FY2024: "...public perceptions regarding our positions on social or ethical issues and our development and use of artificial intelligence, machine learning, and automation technologies, products, and services"

History (phrase_history): "our positions on social or ethical issues" and "machine learning, and automation" first appear in the FY2024 10-K and are in every filing since.

Reputation risk now covers Amazon's stances on social issues in general and adds automation, a word that covers warehouse robotics as well as software. The adoption-rate bullet adds AI technologies, making customer uptake of AI a stated revenue risk. Section mentions of "artificial intelligence": 8 → 9.

FY2024 → FY2025: Free Cash Flow Squeezed, Disclosure Trimmed

Filed Feb 6, 2026 | Revenue: $637.96B → $716.92B (+12%) | Operating income: $68.59B → $79.97B | Net income: $59.25B → $77.67B | Operating cash flow: $115.88B → $139.51B | Purchases of property and equipment: $83.00B → $131.82B (Amazon's net "cash capital expenditures": $77.7B → $128.3B) | FCF (OCF less purchases): $32.88B → $7.70B | Long-term debt: $52.62B → $65.65B | Employees: 1,556K → 1,576K

MD&A

▶Two of three free-cash-flow measures deleted in the year Amazon's FCF fell 71% to $11.2BRemoved

Shift: quant removed (−): two lease-adjusted measures and their reconciliations dropped; "multiple measures" → "a free cash flow measure"

FY2024: "We provide multiple measures of free cash flows because we believe these measures provide additional perspective on the impact of acquiring property and equipment with cash and through finance leases and financing obligations."
FY2025: "We provide a free cash flow measure because we believe it provides additional perspective on the impact of acquiring property and equipment with cash."

History (phrase_history): "multiple measures of free cash flows" stood in seven filings (Q2 2023 through the FY2024 10-K); "We provide a free cash flow measure" first appears in the Q1 2025 10-Q, so the cut was made in the first quarter of the year and carried into the 10-K.

The dropped measures subtracted finance-lease and financing-obligation repayments ($35.5B and $36.2B for 2024). The kept measure is by construction the highest of the three. Property acquired under finance leases rose from $854M to $2.9B, which is what the deleted measures would have deducted.

▶Spending outlook adds "can negatively impact short-term free cash flow"; named engineering hires replaced by "infrastructure"; first forward AI-investment sentenceNew

Shift: hedge/caveat added: FCF warning (+ disclosure); de-named: four job categories → "employees"; named: + "artificial intelligence initiatives" in a forward statement

FY2024: "We expect spending in technology and infrastructure will increase over time as we add computer scientists, designers, software and hardware engineers, and merchandising employees."
FY2025: "We expect spending in technology and infrastructure will increase over time, which can negatively impact short-term free cash flow, as we add infrastructure and employees, including to support our artificial intelligence and machine learning initiatives, to support long-term growth." ... "Further, we expect to continue making additional investments in our artificial intelligence initiatives."

History (phrase_history): the four-job-category sentence appears in the FY2023 and FY2024 10-Ks; "can negatively impact short-term free cash flow" appears in the FY2025 10-K only (the 10-Qs carry no Overview section); "additional investments in our artificial intelligence initiatives" first appears in the FY2025 10-K and is repeated in Q1 and Q2 2026.

This is the first sentence in the dataset that ties the spending outlook to free cash flow. Infrastructure now comes before employees. FY2024 named AI only as a macro factor; FY2025 commits to more AI spending. Technology and infrastructure expense rose 23% to $108.5B.

▶Capex outlook loses its "technology infrastructure" driver and absorbs fulfillment again; cash capex $77.7B → $128.3BChanged

Shift: scope: one named driver → "both of which" (−, less specific); form: standalone sentence → trailing clause

FY2024: "We expect cash capital expenditures to increase in 2025, primarily driven by investments in technology infrastructure."
FY2025: "...and in additional capacity to support our fulfillment network, both of which we expect to increase in 2026."

History (phrase_history): the standalone sentence appears in the FY2024 10-K and Q1 2025 only; the trailing-clause form starts in Q2 2025 ("increase in 2025"), Q3 2025 says "2025 and 2026", and "both of which we expect to increase in 2026" appears in the FY2025 10-K, Q1 and Q2 2026.

No size is given for 2026. Gross purchases of property and equipment were $131.82B. The parenthetical "(the majority of which is to support AWS business growth)" is unchanged, and AI is still not named in the capex sentence.

▶$2.5B FTC settlement and $2.7B severance; other operating expense $763M → $4.6B with an Italy tax resolutionNew

Shift: realized (+): litigation and workforce risk → recorded charges; direction: payroll "decrease" (FY2024) → severance cost

FY2025: "Operating income for 2025 includes charges of $2.5 billion we recorded in Q3 2025 related to the settlement of a lawsuit with the FTC and $2.7 billion, of which $1.8 billion was recorded in Q3 2025, of estimated severance costs primarily related to planned role eliminations."

History (phrase_history): "settlement of a lawsuit with the FTC" appears in Q3 2025 and the FY2025 10-K; "tax disputes associated with our stores business in Italy" in the FY2025 10-K only. "estimated severance costs ... planned role eliminations" is not new: the FY2023 and FY2024 10-K notes report $720M for 2022 and charges in 2023; it was absent from all 2024 and H1 2025 10-Qs.

FY2024 credited lower payroll in three expense lines; FY2025 credits it nowhere. This is the first severance charge to reach MD&A, and the largest in the dataset. The 10-K does not say which FTC lawsuit was settled, and the FTC is still a named plaintiff in the antitrust litigation in Note 7, so the settlement did not end the FTC's antitrust case.

▶AWS margin 37.0% → 35.4% as the payroll and server-life tailwinds drop out; the shorter server life cost $1.4B, double the $0.7B estimateSignal

Shift: named drivers removed: "decreased payroll" and the server-life benefit; quant: estimate ~$0.7B → actual $1.4B

FY2025: "The increase in AWS operating income in 2025, compared to the prior year, is primarily due to increased sales, partially offset by spending on technology infrastructure that was primarily driven by additional investments to support AWS business growth."
FY2025: "...was an increase in depreciation and amortization expense of $1.4 billion and a reduction in net income of $1.0 billion"

Themes

AI: From Zero to $128B Capex

Complete saturation of AI language from nothing (FY2018) to every section (FY2025). Capex 10x'd over the period. The AWS description itself was changed to include AI -- redefining the company's core cloud business at the SEC filing level.

  • FY2020: ML first appears in AWS description
  • FY2023: AI inflection -- enters risk factors, competition, IP
  • FY2024: AI named as a macro factor; server life cut back "due to" the pace of AI
  • FY2025: AI added to core Item 1 AWS description; MD&A forward commitment; AI framed as a channel that can "divert customers"

AWS as Profit Engine

57% of operating income. Growth re-accelerated from 13% to 20%. The filing says "the majority" of technology-infrastructure capex supports "AWS business growth". Margin is no longer rising in a straight line, though: 27.1% (FY2023) → 37.0% (FY2024, helped by a $3.2B server-life benefit and lower payroll) → 35.4% (FY2025, after the server life was cut back "due to" AI at a $1.4B cost). AWS is where both the returns and the costs of the AI build show up.

The Post-COVID Right-Sizing

1.6M employees → 1.5M. Operating income collapsed -51% in FY2022, then tripled in FY2023. The pandemic buildout (adding 961K employees in two years) was systematically unwound. Revenue is now 53% higher than FY2021 peak with fewer employees.

Advertising as Third Pillar

From a passing mention ("we provide services, such as advertising") to its own business line with named customer types and formats in three filings. Advertising is now consistently cited as a growth driver and has its own competition category. The third revenue pillar after retail and AWS.

FCF Under Siege

$128.3B of cash capex against $139.5B of OCF leaves $11.2B of FCF, down 71% while revenue grew 12%. The FY2025 10-K now says technology spending "can negatively impact short-term free cash flow", adds a forward AI-investment sentence, deletes two of its three FCF measures, and opens the door to "other financing arrangements". Debt proceeds went from $5.1B to $25.0B, and the 2025 Tax Act's cash-tax cut ($12.3B → $8.3B) is the main offset.

Regulatory Complexity Compounding

A $2.5B FTC lawsuit settlement (the FTC remains a plaintiff in the antitrust omnibus), Prime and consumer-protection rules named in antitrust scrutiny, "gatekeeper" laws, AI, healthcare and satellite regulation, Italy tax disputes, and tariffs and trade disputes. The General Counsel title was elevated twice in three years -- from "General Counsel" to "Chief Global Affairs & Legal Officer." The legal function is being restructured to match the regulatory surface area.
