APO 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of eight Apollo Global Management 10-Qs, Q1 2024 (quarter ended Mar 31, 2024) through Q2 2026 (Jun 30, 2026). The MD&A chain uses seven transitions: five adjacent-quarter diffs plus two year-boundary transitions, each read through the intervening 10-K (Q3→10-K and 10-K→Q1), with each finding saying which filing introduced the change. From Q3 2024 on, every finding carries a shift label (how the wording moved) and a history line (how long the old wording had stood, checked across all 13 filings). The annual risk-factor signal is a separate direct FY2024-vs-FY2025 10-K comparison.

▶ Athene PGA/ERISA litigation factor (born Q1 2024) softened: "four putative class actions" → "a number of" Changed

The Q1 2024 Item 1A carried one material-change factor — third-party litigation against Athene's pension-group-annuity customers:

"In addition, our business has been, and may continue to be, the subject of litigation between third parties that could negatively impact us. For example, beginning in March 2024, four putative class actions were filed in federal courts in the U.S. against certain customers of Athene, in their respective capacities as plan sponsors, alleging violations of the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 ("ERISA") in connection with their transfer of pension obligations under defined benefit plans governed under ERISA and their purchase of pension group annuity ("PGA") contracts from Athene."
"Although Athene is not a named defendant, the lawsuits make several negative allegations about Athene and its business, which we believe to be untrue. Negative public perceptions of Athene and its business could adversely affect (and may have already adversely affected) its ability to attract and retain customers... These lawsuits could also spur similar copycat lawsuits, which could further impact Athene's PGA business."

Q2 2024 changed the case count:

"beginning in March 2024, a number of putative class actions were filed..."

The factor also covers DGCL Section 220 books-and-records demands, indemnification limits, and ESG-related litigation risk.

▶ Second Item 1A factor added: extensive regulation, including the IAIG designation (section 7K → 16K chars) New

Q2 2024 added a second material-change factor under the heading "Extensive regulation of our businesses affects our activities and creates the potential for significant liabilities and penalties. The possibility of increased regulatory focus could result in additional burdens on our businesses." Five paragraphs: a regulatory-regime catalog (Advisers Act, Investment Company Act, Dodd-Frank, AIFMD/MiFID, GDPR, FCPA/OFAC, SFDR/SDR), investigation powers and CFIUS, the private-fund regulation trend, state guaranty-association assessment risk, and:

"On February 6, 2024, the IID identified AGM as meeting the criteria as an IAIG and further identified Athene as the Head of the IAIG. As a result of these identifications, we expect Athene to be subject to the relevant capital standard that the U.S. applies to IAIGs. At this time, we do not expect a significant impact on Athene's capital position or capital structure..."
▶ ADIP sidecar economics surface throughout the MD&A New

ADIP II (vintage 2024, $6,556M committed) and ADIP I (2020, 24%/20% IRR) were added to the investment record, with a new SRE driver sentence:

"Strategic capital management fees increased $8 million due to additional fees received from ADIP II as a result of the sale of a 50% interest in ACRA 2 to ADIP II effective July 1, 2023 and the subsequent increase in the ADIP II ownership of ACRA 2 to 60% effective December 31, 2023."

Cost-of-funds drivers gained the ACRA non-controlling-interest offset and new hedging language:

"including the additional costs of swapping to or issuing funding agreements as floating rate to mitigate SRE sensitivity to floating rate assets."
▶ New regulatory-matter accrual in the non-GAAP reconciliation New

A new footnote appears in the non-GAAP reconciliation:

▶ Capital solutions record quarter; Q1's FRE-margin expansion sentence dropped Changed

Q2 2024 added:

"Capital solutions fees in 2024 reflect growth across the broader debt origination ecosystem, including a record quarter of high-grade corporate solutions activity."

FRE was framed as "record quarterly fee related revenues." The Q1 2024 sentence guiding "more than 150 basis points of FRE margin expansion in 2024" was dropped. Fee-related performance fees from ADS ($10M) and MidCap Financial ($7M) were attributed to wealth:

"reflecting the growing contribution from Apollo's diversified suite of wealth-focused products."
▶ AHL liquidity facility replaced; ALRe net-worth covenant raised $8.8B → $10.2B Changed

The AHL liquidity facility was replaced June 28, 2024 ($2.6B capacity), with the ALRe minimum net worth covenant raised from $8.8B to $10.2B. The 2024 notes were retired and new 2054 Senior Notes entered the maturity list. Short-term repo went from zero to $1.2B, with a new "based on original issuance" qualifier.

▶ HVF II crosses hurdle; fund merger costs; Athora driver re-attributed; tax modal change Signal

HVF II entered the performance-fee tables ("the fund achieved its annualized hurdle rate in 2024"). Fund merger-related costs ($15–18M) were first mentioned. Athene alternative-investment drivers were re-attributed: Athora moved from "higher debt costs and operating expenses" to "continued inflationary pressures impacting the valuation"; Catalina was newly named. The income-tax outlook sentence shifted from "does not expect material impacts" to "does not expect a material increase." Fund VIII escrow ratio: 104% → 102% of unreturned capital.

▶ Origination becomes a defined metric, including "net purchases" of "hard to access assets" New

▶ Fund VIII escrow ratio falls from 102% to 90% of unreturned capital Signal

Quant changed (-): 102% → 90%, against a 115% requirement

Q3 2024: "As of September 30, 2024, the remaining investments and escrow cash of Fund VIII was valued at 90% of the fund’s unreturned capital, which was below the required escrow ratio of 115%."

History: the ratio was 104% in Q1 2024 and 102% in Q2 2024 ("valued at 102% of the fund’s unreturned capital" recurs only in Q2 2026). Q3 2024 is the first reading below 100% in the scanner, and it stays below 100% through Q1 2026.

Below 100%, the fund's remaining value no longer covers the capital investors are owed back, so carry already paid out is at risk. The nine-month MD&A also shows Fund VIII performance allocation losses of $117 million.

Themes

1. The Q3 2024 Re-Segmentation Was a Single-Quarter Event

In one filing (filed Nov 6, 2024, after the Oct 1, 2024 Investor Day), Apollo collapsed yield/hybrid/equity into credit/equity, deleted every target-return range (4–10%, 8–15%, >15%) and the target-return disclaimer block, and introduced the Origination operating metric. By Q1 2025 the transition sentence was already gone. An annual comparison would compress this into a generic year-over-year difference; the quarterly chain dates it to a single quarter.

2. AUM Grew $671B → $1.05T, With Inorganic Step-Ups (and Step-Downs) Dated to the Quarter

Each filing's AUM flows attribute the step-ups: Redding Ridge/Irradiant +$9.3B (Q2 2025), Bridge +$34.2B equity AUM (Q3 2025), Athora/PIC +$65.3B (Q1 2026). The $1.03 trillion total in Q1 2026 splits $834B credit / $192B equity. The employee-count sentence was deleted in the same filing the $1T threshold was crossed and has not returned. Q2 2026 added only $20.9B (to $1.05T: $849B / $198B), because the roll-forward now nets out moves between Apollo entities: $(7.8)B when ARI sold its loan book to Athene, a $(5.0)B AP Grange prepayment, and $(9.9)B of AMAPS-related "Other, net" adjustments.

3. The Tax Arc Ran Through Three Filings

Q2 2025 (first post-OBBBA filing): the act appears only in the tax note, which says the Company is "currently evaluating the impact"; the MD&A is silent. FY2025 10-K: new OBBBA paragraph, BEAT rate correction, Pillar Two "SbS Safe Harbor" paragraph (Jan 5, 2026 Inclusive Framework agreement), and the January 2026 revocation of ACRA's Bermuda CIT election with the $1.7B valuation allowance announced in advance for Q1. Q1 2026: the allowance is booked, producing a 598.6% quarterly effective tax rate, while new IRS (draft NOPA on 2019–2021) and German (assessments received) disputes appear in the notes — with the 10-K stating the developments "will have favorable implications for the Company's overall tax position over the longer term." Q2 2026 is the first test: a 15.9% quarterly effective rate, leaving 75.5% for the six months.

4. Quarterly Item 1A Carried Signal Only in FY2024; the Litigation Note Carries It Now

The Athene PGA/ERISA litigation factor (born Q1 2024 with "four putative class actions," softened to "a number of" in Q2 2024) and the regulation/IAIG factor (added Q2 2024) lived for three quarters, then were absorbed into the 2024 Annual Report, where the ERISA wording hardened from "could adversely affect (and may have already adversely affected)" to "have adversely affected." From Q1 2025 through Q2 2026 the quarterly Item 1A is a stub (Q1→Q2 2026 character-identical), leaving the FY2024-vs-FY2025 10-K comparison as the sole risk-factor delta — where the AI factor tripled, competition expanded to four paragraphs, and crypto/digital-asset, anti-DEI, and OBBBA/Pillar Two/Bermuda CIT blocks were added. Quarterly legal signal now sits in note 17: in Q2 2026 the Anguilla derivative suit moved to mediation, Harbinger dropped out, and the Epstein securities actions were consolidated.

5. AI Shows Up on Both Sides of the Ledger (Q2 2026)

In a single filing, AI is a growth driver in the macro paragraph ("investment in artificial intelligence infrastructure"), a credit risk ("Current stresses in software and artificial intelligence related credit appear concentrated"), and an exposure: a $35B Broadcom capital solution for Anthropic's compute, with Athene (AHL) guaranteeing 15% of the purchase obligation. The guarantee appears only in the notes. This follows the FY2025 10-K's warning that AI efficiency gains "could also negatively impact demand for, and the valuation of, digital infrastructure assets." Over the same quarter, AUM below its performance-fee hurdle rose $50.5B → $70.9B. The filing does not tie that rise to any one sector.
