APO 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of eight Apollo Global Management 10-Qs, Q1 2024 (quarter ended Mar 31, 2024) through Q2 2026 (Jun 30, 2026). The MD&A chain uses seven transitions: five adjacent-quarter diffs plus two year-boundary transitions. Q3 2024→Q1 2025 is a direct diff, so changes there may have originated in either the FY2024 10-K or the Q1 filing. Q3 2025→Q1 2026 is read through the FY2025 10-K, and each finding says which filing introduced the change. The annual risk-factor signal is a separate direct FY2024-vs-FY2025 10-K comparison.

Q1 2024 → Q2 2024

Total AUM: $671B → $696B | Employees: 4,945 → 5,009 | Dry powder: $65B → $68B
▶ Athene PGA/ERISA litigation factor (born Q1 2024) softened: "four putative class actions" → "a number of" Changed

The Q1 2024 Item 1A carried one material-change factor — third-party litigation against Athene's pension-group-annuity customers:

"In addition, our business has been, and may continue to be, the subject of litigation between third parties that could negatively impact us. For example, beginning in March 2024, four putative class actions were filed in federal courts in the U.S. against certain customers of Athene, in their respective capacities as plan sponsors, alleging violations of the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 ("ERISA") in connection with their transfer of pension obligations under defined benefit plans governed under ERISA and their purchase of pension group annuity ("PGA") contracts from Athene."
"Although Athene is not a named defendant, the lawsuits make several negative allegations about Athene and its business, which we believe to be untrue. Negative public perceptions of Athene and its business could adversely affect (and may have already adversely affected) its ability to attract and retain customers... These lawsuits could also spur similar copycat lawsuits, which could further impact Athene's PGA business."

Q2 2024 changed the case count:

"beginning in March 2024, a number of putative class actions were filed..."

The factor also covers DGCL Section 220 books-and-records demands, indemnification limits, and ESG-related litigation risk.

▶ Second Item 1A factor added: extensive regulation, including the IAIG designation (section 7K → 16K chars) New

Q2 2024 added a second material-change factor under the heading "Extensive regulation of our businesses affects our activities and creates the potential for significant liabilities and penalties. The possibility of increased regulatory focus could result in additional burdens on our businesses." Five paragraphs: a regulatory-regime catalog (Advisers Act, Investment Company Act, Dodd-Frank, AIFMD/MiFID, GDPR, FCPA/OFAC, SFDR/SDR), investigation powers and CFIUS, the private-fund regulation trend, state guaranty-association assessment risk, and:

"On February 6, 2024, the IID identified AGM as meeting the criteria as an IAIG and further identified Athene as the Head of the IAIG. As a result of these identifications, we expect Athene to be subject to the relevant capital standard that the U.S. applies to IAIGs. At this time, we do not expect a significant impact on Athene's capital position or capital structure..."
▶ ADIP sidecar economics surface throughout the MD&A New

ADIP II (vintage 2024, $6,556M committed) and ADIP I (2020, 24%/20% IRR) were added to the investment record, with a new SRE driver sentence:

"Strategic capital management fees increased $8 million due to additional fees received from ADIP II as a result of the sale of a 50% interest in ACRA 2 to ADIP II effective July 1, 2023 and the subsequent increase in the ADIP II ownership of ACRA 2 to 60% effective December 31, 2023."

Cost-of-funds drivers gained the ACRA non-controlling-interest offset and new hedging language:

"including the additional costs of swapping to or issuing funding agreements as floating rate to mitigate SRE sensitivity to floating rate assets."
▶ New regulatory-matter accrual in the non-GAAP reconciliation New

A new footnote appears in the non-GAAP reconciliation:

"Three and six months ended June 30, 2024 includes an accrual related to an estimated liability associated with a regulatory matter."
▶ Capital solutions record quarter; Q1's FRE-margin expansion sentence dropped Changed

Q2 2024 added:

"Capital solutions fees in 2024 reflect growth across the broader debt origination ecosystem, including a record quarter of high-grade corporate solutions activity."

FRE was framed as "record quarterly fee related revenues." The Q1 2024 sentence guiding "more than 150 basis points of FRE margin expansion in 2024" was dropped. Fee-related performance fees from ADS ($10M) and MidCap Financial ($7M) were attributed to wealth:

"reflecting the growing contribution from Apollo's diversified suite of wealth-focused products."
▶ AHL liquidity facility replaced; ALRe net-worth covenant raised $8.8B → $10.2B Changed

The AHL liquidity facility was replaced June 28, 2024 ($2.6B capacity), with the ALRe minimum net worth covenant raised from $8.8B to $10.2B. The 2024 notes were retired and new 2054 Senior Notes entered the maturity list. Short-term repo went from zero to $1.2B, with a new "based on original issuance" qualifier.

▶ HVF II crosses hurdle; fund merger costs; Athora driver re-attributed; tax modal change Signal

Athene alternative-investment drivers were re-attributed: Athora moved from "higher debt costs and operating expenses" to "continued inflationary pressures impacting the valuation"; Catalina was newly named. The income-tax outlook sentence shifted from "does not expect material impacts" to "does not expect a material increase." Fund VIII escrow ratio: 104% → 102% of unreturned capital.

"During the third quarter ended September 30, 2024, we updated our Asset Management strategies to credit and equity, from the previously reported yield, hybrid and equity, which, we believe, better reflects our Asset Management investment strategies. The credit and equity investing strategies of our Asset Management segment reflect the range of investment capabilities across our platform, from investment grade to private equity."

The Yield (4–10% target) and Hybrid (8–15% target) descriptions — including the total return yield fund 6%/5% ROE and hybrid fund 11%/7% ROE / 20%/15% IRR track records — were replaced by a single Credit description:

▶ $1.7B Bermuda tax asset written off: pre-announced in the 10-K, booked in Q1 (598.6% tax rate, $(1.91)B net loss) New

The Q3 2025 10-Q mentions the Bermuda CIT but says nothing about revoking the election or writing off the asset. The 10-K added a "Bermuda Corporate Income Tax" subsection that gave the Q1 charge in advance:

FY2025 10-K: "As of December 31, 2025, the Company had $1.7 billion of net Bermuda deferred tax assets and concluded that it was more likely than not that sufficient future taxable income would be generated to realize these deferred tax assets."
FY2025 10-K: "As a result of the foregoing, in the first quarter of 2026, the Company will record a full valuation allowance against its Bermuda deferred tax assets, as the Company no longer expects Athene or ACRA to incur Bermuda CIT or Pillar Two tax expense against which such deferred tax assets could be utilized."
Q1 2026: "The Company’s effective income tax rate was approximately 598.6% and 20.6% for the three months ended March 31, 2026 and 2025, respectively."

The ACRA election was revoked in January 2026, after the January 5 OECD guidance exempting U.S.-parented groups. The 10-K therefore carried a "realizable" asset at year end and announced in the same filing that it would be written off weeks later. The Q1 net loss of $(1.91)B and diluted EPS of $(3.27) follow from this non-cash charge. They do not reflect an operating loss.

▶ Securities class actions against AGM, Rowan and Black over 2021–2022 statements that Apollo "never did any business with Jeffrey Epstein" New

New in the Q1 2026 litigation note (the 10-K has no such case). Two suits were filed in the Southern District of New York on March 2 and April 29, 2026:

Q1 2026: "Both complaints challenge, among other things, certain of the defendants’ public statements in 2021 and 2022 that AGM never did any business with Jeffrey Epstein."
Q1 2026: "The class period alleged in both complaints is May 10, 2021 through February 21, 2026, inclusive."

The current CEO is named as a defendant alongside the former chairman whose name this dashboard's 10-K page tracks as having been removed from the filings by FY2021. The class period ends February 21, 2026. The filing gives "No reasonable estimate of possible loss." In Q2 2026 the two cases were consolidated.

▶ Two tax disputes escalate: IRS draft NOPA on 2019–2021 foreign minority stakes; German "may seek assessments" (10-K) becomes "has received certain assessments" (Q1) New

Neither matter appears in the Q3 2025 10-Q. The IRS issue concerns income from minority interests in foreign entities, and 2022–2023 are "under examination by the IRS on the same issue." Both carry "could be material" language with no estimate. Neither is mentioned in the MD&A.

▶ New private-credit paragraph concedes "increased redemption requests" at non-traded BDCs New

Two paragraphs appear in Q1 2026 Business Environment, with no counterpart in Q3 or the 10-K:

▶ Share of fund investments valued off market quotes falls 39% → 36% → 32%; model-based share reaches 68% Signal
Q3 2025: "As of September 30, 2025, approximately 39% of the value of the investments of the funds we manage, on a gross basis, was determined using market-based valuation methods"
FY2025 10-K: "As of December 31, 2025, approximately 36% of the value of our funds’ investments on a gross basis was determined using market-based valuation methods"
Q1 2026: "As of March 31, 2026, approximately 32% of the value of the investments of the funds we manage, on a gross basis, was determined using market-based valuation methods (i.e., reliance on broker or listed exchange quotes) and the remaining 68% was determined primarily by comparable company and industry multiples or discounted cash flow models."

The figure was 41% in Q1 and Q2 2025 (and 40% in the FY2024 10-K), so this is a third straight decline and the lowest of the filings checked from FY2024 on (Q2 2026: 31%). It fell during the same quarter in which the new private-credit paragraph mentions "industry inquiries around ... valuation methodologies." The filing does not explain the shift. Possible causes include the Bridge real-estate funds and growth in private credit, but that is an inference.

▶ Macro turns: "stagflationary" risk dropped, "credit fundamentals are improving" added, and Athene's written high-yield CDS rises $510M → $985M Changed
Q3 2025: "The ongoing uncertainty regarding trade policy poses a downside risk to the current economic outlook, with lower growth and higher inflationary pressures increasing the risk of a stagflationary environment."
Q1 2026: "Credit fundamentals are improving: default rates in both high yield and leveraged loans are declining, distressed exchanges are easing, and at this time a broad credit cycle deterioration does not appear likely."
Q1 2026: "As of March 31, 2026 and December 31, 2025, the maximum amount of potential future payments on the credit default swaps was $985 million and $510 million, respectively."

The stagflation sentence ran through Q3 and the 10-K. Q1 replaces it with "the conflict with Iran" and "the risk of a recession remaining modest," even though CPI rose from 2.7% to 3.3% and oil rose 76.6%. That position is consistent with the "improving" view, which the Q2 2026 10-Q softened to "generally constructive."

▶ Athene alternatives yield halves, 10.08% → 5.79%; Atlas takes an asset impairment; SRE down 11% Signal
Q1 2026: "Alternative net investment earned rate was 5.79% in 2026, a decrease from 10.08% in 2025, primarily driven by lower returns within origination platforms and credit funds, partially offset by higher returns within retirement services platforms."
Q1 2026: "The decrease in income from origination platforms was mainly attributable to outsized performance from MidCap FinCo and Redding Ridge in 2025, as well as market headwinds impacting the valuation of each in 2026, a valuation increase related to strong performance from Wheels in 2025 and a valuation decrease on Atlas resulting from an underlying asset impairment in 2026."

The origination platforms that the 10-K's new Origination section presents as a core engine are what pulled Athene's alternative returns down in Q1. Atlas, the warehouse-finance platform, recorded an impairment. Cost of funds rose to 3.79% from 3.46%. The total net investment earned rate held at 5.08% only because fixed-income yields rose.

▶ Fund VIII escrow 85% → 87% → 81%; GP clawback obligations $200M → $212M → $334M; "cyclically light" sentence dropped Signal

#8: Legal Proceedings Stub

8 One-Line Incorporation by Reference, Every Quarter

Every 10-Q in the set carries a one-line incorporation by reference into the financial-statement notes:

"ITEM 1. LEGAL PROCEEDINGS — See a summary of the Company's legal proceedings set forth in note 16 to our condensed consolidated financial statements, which is incorporated by reference herein."

The only change across the quarters is the note number: note 16 through Q2 2025, then note 17 from Q3 2025 onward (a renumbering of the notes, not a change in disclosure approach). Q1→Q2 2024, Q2→Q3 2024, Q1→Q2 2025, and Q1→Q2 2026 are zero-length diffs (verbatim stub). The substantive quarterly litigation signal lives in the Litigation note inside the full filing, not in Item 1. Q2 2026 example: the note recast the Anguilla derivative-suit stay as mediation toward "a potential negotiated resolution," dropped the concluded Harbinger case, and recorded consolidation of the Epstein-related securities class actions.

Themes

1. The Q3 2024 Re-Segmentation Was a Single-Quarter Event

In one filing (filed Nov 6, 2024, after the Oct 1, 2024 Investor Day), Apollo collapsed yield/hybrid/equity into credit/equity, deleted every target-return range (4–10%, 8–15%, >15%) and the target-return disclaimer block, and introduced the Origination operating metric. By Q1 2025 the transition sentence was already gone. An annual comparison would compress this into a generic year-over-year difference; the quarterly chain dates it to a single quarter.

2. AUM Grew $671B → $1.05T, With Inorganic Step-Ups (and Step-Downs) Dated to the Quarter

Each filing's AUM flows attribute the step-ups: Redding Ridge/Irradiant +$9.3B (Q2 2025), Bridge +$34.2B equity AUM (Q3 2025), Athora/PIC +$65.3B (Q1 2026). The $1.03 trillion total in Q1 2026 splits $834B credit / $192B equity. The employee-count sentence was deleted in the same filing the $1T threshold was crossed and has not returned. Q2 2026 added only $20.9B (to $1.05T: $849B / $198B), because the roll-forward now nets out moves between Apollo entities: $(7.8)B when ARI sold its loan book to Athene, a $(5.0)B AP Grange prepayment, and $(9.9)B of AMAPS-related "Other, net" adjustments.

3. The Tax Arc Ran Through Three Filings

Q2 2025 (first post-OBBBA filing): no OBBBA mention in the MD&A. FY2025 10-K: new OBBBA paragraph, BEAT rate correction, Pillar Two "SbS Safe Harbor" paragraph (Jan 5, 2026 Inclusive Framework agreement), and the January 2026 revocation of ACRA's Bermuda CIT election with the $1.7B valuation allowance announced in advance for Q1. Q1 2026: the allowance is booked, producing a 598.6% quarterly effective tax rate, while new IRS (draft NOPA on 2019–2021) and German (assessments received) disputes appear in the notes — with the 10-K stating the developments "will have favorable implications for the Company's overall tax position over the longer term." Q2 2026 is the first test: a 15.9% quarterly effective rate, leaving 75.5% for the six months.

4. Quarterly Item 1A Carried Signal Only in FY2024; the Litigation Note Carries It Now

The Athene PGA/ERISA litigation factor (born Q1 2024 with "four putative class actions," softened to "a number of" in Q2 2024) and the regulation/IAIG factor (added Q2 2024) lived for three quarters, then were absorbed into the 2024 Annual Report. From Q1 2025 through Q2 2026 the quarterly Item 1A is a stub (Q1→Q2 2026 character-identical), leaving the FY2024-vs-FY2025 10-K comparison as the sole risk-factor delta — where the AI factor tripled, competition expanded to four paragraphs, and crypto/digital-asset, anti-DEI, and OBBBA/Pillar Two/Bermuda CIT blocks were added. Quarterly legal signal now sits in note 17: in Q2 2026 the Anguilla derivative suit moved to mediation, Harbinger dropped out, and the Epstein securities actions were consolidated.

5. AI Shows Up on Both Sides of the Ledger (Q2 2026)

In a single filing, AI is a growth driver in the macro paragraph ("investment in artificial intelligence infrastructure"), a credit risk ("Current stresses in software and artificial intelligence related credit appear concentrated"), and an exposure: a $35B Broadcom capital solution for Anthropic's compute, with Athene (AHL) guaranteeing 15% of the purchase obligation. The guarantee appears only in the notes. This follows the FY2025 10-K's warning that AI efficiency gains "could also negatively impact demand for, and the valuation of, digital infrastructure assets." Over the same quarter, AUM below its performance-fee hurdle rose $50.5B → $70.9B. The filing does not tie that rise to any one sector.
