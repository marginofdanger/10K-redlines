APO 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of seven Apollo Global Management 10-Qs, Q1 2024 (quarter ended Mar 31, 2024) through Q1 2026 (Mar 31, 2026). The MD&A chain uses six transitions: four adjacent-quarter diffs plus two direct year-boundary diffs (Q3 2024→Q1 2025 and Q3 2025→Q1 2026, skipping the 10-Ks whose MD&A extracts were unusable) — changes at those two boundaries may have originated in either the 10-K or the Q1 filing. The annual risk-factor signal is a separate direct FY2024-vs-FY2025 10-K comparison.

Q1 2024 → Q2 2024

Total AUM: $671B → $696B | Employees: 4,945 → 5,009 | Dry powder: $65B → $68B
▶ Athene PGA/ERISA litigation factor (born Q1 2024) softened: "four putative class actions" → "a number of" Changed

The Q1 2024 Item 1A carried one material-change factor — third-party litigation against Athene's pension-group-annuity customers:

"In addition, our business has been, and may continue to be, the subject of litigation between third parties that could negatively impact us. For example, beginning in March 2024, four putative class actions were filed in federal courts in the U.S. against certain customers of Athene, in their respective capacities as plan sponsors, alleging violations of the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 ("ERISA") in connection with their transfer of pension obligations under defined benefit plans governed under ERISA and their purchase of pension group annuity ("PGA") contracts from Athene."
"Although Athene is not a named defendant, the lawsuits make several negative allegations about Athene and its business, which we believe to be untrue. Negative public perceptions of Athene and its business could adversely affect (and may have already adversely affected) its ability to attract and retain customers... These lawsuits could also spur similar copycat lawsuits, which could further impact Athene's PGA business."

Q2 2024 changed the case count:

"beginning in March 2024, a number of putative class actions were filed..."

The factor also covers DGCL Section 220 books-and-records demands, indemnification limits, and ESG-related litigation risk.

▶ Second Item 1A factor added: extensive regulation, including the IAIG designation (section 7K → 16K chars) New

Q2 2024 added a second material-change factor under the heading "Extensive regulation of our businesses affects our activities and creates the potential for significant liabilities and penalties. The possibility of increased regulatory focus could result in additional burdens on our businesses." Five paragraphs: a regulatory-regime catalog (Advisers Act, Investment Company Act, Dodd-Frank, AIFMD/MiFID, GDPR, FCPA/OFAC, SFDR/SDR), investigation powers and CFIUS, the private-fund regulation trend, state guaranty-association assessment risk, and:

"On February 6, 2024, the IID identified AGM as meeting the criteria as an IAIG and further identified Athene as the Head of the IAIG. As a result of these identifications, we expect Athene to be subject to the relevant capital standard that the U.S. applies to IAIGs. At this time, we do not expect a significant impact on Athene's capital position or capital structure..."
▶ ADIP sidecar economics surface throughout the MD&A New

ADIP II (vintage 2024, $6,556M committed) and ADIP I (2020, 24%/20% IRR) were added to the investment record, with a new SRE driver sentence:

"Strategic capital management fees increased $8 million due to additional fees received from ADIP II as a result of the sale of a 50% interest in ACRA 2 to ADIP II effective July 1, 2023 and the subsequent increase in the ADIP II ownership of ACRA 2 to 60% effective December 31, 2023."

Cost-of-funds drivers gained the ACRA non-controlling-interest offset and new hedging language:

"including the additional costs of swapping to or issuing funding agreements as floating rate to mitigate SRE sensitivity to floating rate assets."
▶ New regulatory-matter accrual in the non-GAAP reconciliation New

A new footnote appears in the non-GAAP reconciliation:

"Three and six months ended June 30, 2024 includes an accrual related to an estimated liability associated with a regulatory matter."
▶ Capital solutions record quarter; Q1's FRE-margin expansion sentence dropped Changed

Q2 2024 added:

"Capital solutions fees in 2024 reflect growth across the broader debt origination ecosystem, including a record quarter of high-grade corporate solutions activity."

FRE was framed as "record quarterly fee related revenues." The Q1 2024 sentence guiding "more than 150 basis points of FRE margin expansion in 2024" was dropped. Fee-related performance fees from ADS ($10M) and MidCap Financial ($7M) were attributed to wealth:

"reflecting the growing contribution from Apollo's diversified suite of wealth-focused products."
▶ AHL liquidity facility replaced; ALRe net-worth covenant raised $8.8B → $10.2B Changed

The AHL liquidity facility was replaced June 28, 2024 ($2.6B capacity), with the ALRe minimum net worth covenant raised from $8.8B to $10.2B. The 2024 notes were retired and new 2054 Senior Notes entered the maturity list. Short-term repo went from zero to $1.2B, with a new "based on original issuance" qualifier.

▶ HVF II crosses hurdle; fund merger costs; Athora driver re-attributed; tax modal change Signal

Athene alternative-investment drivers were re-attributed: Athora moved from "higher debt costs and operating expenses" to "continued inflationary pressures impacting the valuation"; Catalina was newly named. The income-tax outlook sentence shifted from "does not expect material impacts" to "does not expect a material increase." Fund VIII escrow ratio: 104% → 102% of unreturned capital.

▶ Retitled: political/market conditions; misconduct broadened; regulation "could" → "would"; climate relocated Changed

"Difficult political, market or economic conditions..." becomes "Evolving political, market and economic conditions, including increased policy uncertainty and market volatility, may adversely affect our businesses and financial results." "Governments have increasingly relied on tariffs, export controls, investment screening and other policy tools to advance domestic economic and national security objectives..."

▶ AI factor retitled "AI Technologies" and roughly tripled in length Changed

"Artificial intelligence could increase competitive, operational, legal and regulatory risks..." becomes "AI Technologies could increase..." — and the body roughly tripled, adding digital-infrastructure valuation risk, internal generative-AI use, AI-washing exposure, competitive-lag risk, and AI-regulation compliance costs:

"Advancements in computing and AI Technologies, including efficiency improvements, without related increases in the adoption and development of such technologies, could also negatively impact demand for, and the valuation of, digital infrastructure assets, a sector to which certain of our investment strategies have significant exposure."
"We may also communicate externally regarding AI Technology-related initiatives... which subjects us to the risk of being accused of making inaccurate or misleading statements"

The expanded factor names competition "to source, originate, structure, finance and syndicate investments," retirement-services competition from "alternative capital providers seeking exposure to similar long-duration liabilities," competitors that "may be willing or able to pursue opportunities or strategies or retirement-related transactions on more aggressive terms, including by accepting lower returns or greater risk," and AI/digital-asset disruption pressure.

A new paragraph in the technology factor covers blockchain/cryptocurrency/DeFi exposure and crypto collateral, plus a new regulatory paragraph:

"As we and the funds we manage become more actively involved in digital asset markets, the regulatory risks described herein will be heightened..."

"ESG" was replaced with "sustainability-related" terminology throughout, and a long new anti-DEI paragraph was added covering executive orders terminating DEI programs, threatened investigations/enforcement, debarment risk, and investor exclusion both for and against.

New OBBBA paragraph (TCJA permanence, GILTI/FDII/BEAT changes, 163(j) restoration) and OBBBA CFC-attribution changes; the BEAT rate corrected from 12.5% to 10.5% post-2025; a new Pillar Two "SbS Safe Harbor" paragraph (January 5, 2026 Inclusive Framework agreement; UK implementation pending); and the ACRA Bermuda CIT election revocation with the quantified $1.7B equity hit — matching the Q1 2026 MD&A disclosure.

#8: Legal Proceedings Stub

8 One-Line Incorporation by Reference, Every Quarter

Every 10-Q in the set carries a one-line incorporation by reference into the financial-statement notes:

"ITEM 1. LEGAL PROCEEDINGS — See a summary of the Company's legal proceedings set forth in note 16 to our condensed consolidated financial statements, which is incorporated by reference herein."

The only change across the quarters is the note number: note 16 through Q2 2025, then note 17 from Q3 2025 onward (a renumbering of the notes, not a change in disclosure approach). Q1→Q2 2024, Q2→Q3 2024, and Q1→Q2 2025 are zero-length diffs (verbatim stub). The substantive quarterly litigation signal lives in the Litigation note inside the full filing, not in Item 1.

Themes

1. The Q3 2024 Re-Segmentation Was a Single-Quarter Event

In one filing (filed Nov 6, 2024, after the Oct 1, 2024 Investor Day), Apollo collapsed yield/hybrid/equity into credit/equity, deleted every target-return range (4–10%, 8–15%, >15%) and the target-return disclaimer block, and introduced the Origination operating metric. By Q1 2025 the transition sentence was already gone. An annual comparison would compress this into a generic year-over-year difference; the quarterly chain dates it to a single quarter.

2. AUM Grew $671B → $1.03T, With Inorganic Step-Ups Dated to the Quarter

Each filing's AUM flows attribute the step-ups: Redding Ridge/Irradiant +$9.3B (Q2 2025), Bridge +$34.2B equity AUM (Q3 2025), Athora/PIC +$65.3B (Q1 2026). The $1.03 trillion total in Q1 2026 splits $834B credit / $192B equity. The employee-count sentence was deleted in the same filing the $1T threshold was crossed.

3. The Tax Arc Ran Through Three Filings

Q2 2025 (first post-OBBBA filing): no OBBBA mention in the MD&A. FY2025 10-K: new OBBBA paragraph, BEAT rate correction, and Pillar Two "SbS Safe Harbor" paragraph (Jan 5, 2026 Inclusive Framework agreement). Q1 2026: ACRA's Bermuda CIT election revoked, a full $1.7B valuation allowance against Bermuda deferred tax assets, and a 598.6% quarterly effective tax rate — with the filing stating the developments "will have favorable implications for the Company's overall tax position over the longer term."

4. Quarterly Item 1A Carried Signal Only in FY2024

The Athene PGA/ERISA litigation factor (born Q1 2024 with "four putative class actions," softened to "a number of" in Q2 2024) and the regulation/IAIG factor (added Q2 2024) lived for three quarters, then were absorbed into the 2024 Annual Report. From Q1 2025 through Q1 2026 the quarterly Item 1A is a stub, leaving the FY2024-vs-FY2025 10-K comparison as the sole risk-factor delta — where the AI factor tripled, competition expanded to four paragraphs, and crypto/digital-asset, anti-DEI, and OBBBA/Pillar Two/Bermuda CIT blocks were added.
