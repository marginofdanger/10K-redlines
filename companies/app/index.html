APP 10-K Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of AppLovin's SEC filings reveals the cleanest pivot in recent tech history: from mobile gaming company to pure-play AI advertising platform in four filings.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001751008) | Fiscal Years 2021-2025 | Analysis: March 2026; FY2024 and FY2025 transitions re-analyzed October 2026 with sentence-level shift labels and phrase histories checked across all 10-Ks and 10-Qs
FY2021 → FY2022

The Pivot Begins

Mission broadened from "developers" to "companies." Apps strategic review commenced: "no longer required." Flywheel language gutted. Studios 19→11. Segment reporting created. Wurl (CTV) acquired.

FY2022 → FY2023

The AI Rebranding

Mission: "meaningful connections between companies and ideal customers." AXON: "machine-learning" → "AI-powered." "Mobile app ecosystem" → "advertising ecosystem." Values rewritten to performance culture. 1.4B DAUs disclosed. GenAI risk factor added.

FY2023 → FY2024

The Harvest

Segment renamed "Software Platform" → "Advertising" (68% of revenue). Revenue tied to client ROAS. Apps term sheet ($900M, part possibly seller-financed). E-commerce "positive results". Values section deleted. Unsecured investment-grade notes; KKR exits Voting Agreement. Installs +50%, revenue/install +22%.

FY2024 → FY2025

Pure-Play AI Advertising

Apps sold for $715.6M (vs $900M term sheet). Single segment; Axon Ads Manager is "substantially all" revenue. Pricing now on "campaign goals", ROAS may be "self-attribution". "Apps and websites." 898 employees, $5.5B revenue (+70%), 82% EBITDA margin. Securities litigation. Installs +3%, rev/install +72%.

FY2021 → FY2022: The Pivot Begins

Filed Mar 2023 | Employees: 1,594 → 1,707 | Revenue: $2.79B → $2.82B (flat) | Ad Revenue: 24% → 37%

Business

▶Mission: "enabling success of mobile app developers" → "help companies grow their apps"Changed
The mission statement broadened from serving a narrow constituency ("mobile app developers") to an expansive one ("companies"). This single word change telegraphed the entire strategic pivot -- AppLovin was no longer a developer tools company but an advertising platform serving any business.
FY2021: "Our mission is enabling the success of mobile app developers"
FY2022: "Our mission is to help companies grow their apps"
▶Apps strategic review commenced: "no longer required access to a first-party portfolio of Apps"New
Management disclosed for the first time that the Apps business was under strategic review, with the extraordinary statement that access to first-party apps was no longer necessary for the core software platform business.
"We no longer required access to a first-party portfolio of Apps to support the growth and development of our Software Platform"
This sentence is the kill shot for the apps business, appearing two years before the actual sale.
▶Flywheel language reduced from 4 paragraphs to 1 sentence; studios 19 → 11Removed
FY2021 devoted four full paragraphs to the "flywheel" concept -- how first-party apps fed data into the advertising engine. FY2022 compressed this to a single sentence. The studio count dropped from 19 to 11. The narrative that apps were strategically essential to the ad platform was being dismantled in real time.
▶Wurl (CTV) added as 4th Software Platform componentNew
The acquisition of Wurl was disclosed as adding a connected TV (CTV) capability, expanding the Software Platform from three components to four. This represented an attempt to diversify the advertising platform beyond mobile -- a precursor to the later web expansion language.

Risk / Financial

▶Growth strategy: "Grow AppLovin Apps" → "Optimize AppLovin Apps"Changed
A subtle but telling language shift in the growth strategy section. "Grow" implies investment and expansion; "Optimize" implies extraction and efficiency. The Apps business had been reclassified from a growth engine to a cost-optimization target.
FY2021: "Grow AppLovin Apps"
FY2022: "Optimize AppLovin Apps"
▶Net income: +$35M → -$193M (Apps impairments $128M)Signal
The company swung from $35M net income to a $193M net loss, driven in part by $128M in Apps-related asset impairments. The financial statements were confirming what the language changes had already signaled: the Apps business was being written down while the Software Platform was being elevated.

FY2022 → FY2023: The AI Rebranding

Filed Feb 2024 | Employees: 1,707 → 1,745 | Revenue: $2.82B → $3.28B (+16%) | Ad Revenue: 37% → 56%

Business

▶Mission: "help companies grow their apps" → "create meaningful connections between companies and their ideal customers"Changed
The second mission rewrite in two years removed all references to "apps" from the mission itself. The company now described its purpose in pure advertising-platform language -- connecting companies to customers. The word "apps" in the mission was eliminated.
FY2022: "Our mission is to help companies grow their apps"
FY2023: "Our mission is to create meaningful connections between companies and their ideal customers"
▶AXON: "machine-learning recommendation engine" → "AI-powered advertising engine"; "mobile app ecosystem" → "advertising ecosystem"Changed
Two critical rebrandings in one filing. "Machine-learning" became "AI-powered" -- perfectly timed with the generative AI wave. And "mobile app ecosystem" became simply "advertising ecosystem," dropping both "mobile" and "app" as scope limiters.
FY2022: "AXON, our machine-learning recommendation engine... within the mobile app ecosystem"
FY2023: "AXON, our AI-powered advertising engine... within the advertising ecosystem"
▶Values completely rewritten: DEI ("culture of equality") → performance culture ("Hire and Reward Doers")Changed
The corporate values section was gutted and rewritten from a standard corporate DEI framework to an aggressive performance culture manifesto. Key new phrases included:
"Think Like an Entrepreneur," "Hire and Reward Doers," "not afraid to part ways with underperformers"
The previous "culture of equality" language was entirely removed. This is among the most dramatic values rewrites in public company filing history.
▶1.4B daily active users disclosed for first time; SparkLabs creative team addedNew
For the first time, AppLovin quantified the reach of its advertising platform: 1.4 billion daily active users. This placed AppLovin's reach among the largest advertising platforms globally. SparkLabs, the in-house creative team, was also disclosed as a new capability for the first time.

Risk Factors

▶GenAI risk factor added: "uncertain regulatory environment"New
A new risk factor specifically addressing generative AI was added for the first time, citing the "uncertain regulatory environment" around AI technologies. This reflected both the company's increasing reliance on AI-powered advertising technology and the broader regulatory concerns emerging around generative AI in 2023.
▶"Core Technologies" section eliminated; App Graph concept disappearedRemoved
The "Core Technologies" section that had described the company's technical architecture -- including the "App Graph" concept of interconnected app data -- was entirely removed. This reflected the shift from a data-network-effects narrative to a pure AI-engine narrative. AXON replaced everything.

FY2023 → FY2024: The Harvest

Filed Feb 2025 | Revenue: $3.28B → $4.71B (+43%) | Advertising: $1.84B → $3.22B (+75%), 56% → 68% of revenue | Net income: $357M → $1.58B | Employees: 1,745 → 1,563 (-10%)

Business

▶Revenue tied to client returns for the first time: "We generate revenue when our advertisers achieve their return on spend targets"; price set by the revenue advertisers get from acquired usersChanged

Shift: named, pricing mechanism disclosed (+ disclosure); realized, growth tied to client ROAS (+)

FY2023: "Our scaled business model sits at the nexus of the advertising ecosystem, which creates a durable competitive advantage that has fueled our clients' success and our strong growth."
FY2024: "We generate revenue when our advertisers achieve their return on spend targets with our Advertising solutions, ensuring that their success directly fuels our growth."
FY2024 (AppDiscovery, new): "Advertisers set return goals for their campaigns and AppDiscovery targets users to match those goals. ... Advertisers are charged for advertising dynamically based on the revenue they receive from acquired users, rather than a simple fixed price per impression or per action (click or installation)."

History: "return on spend targets" and "revenue they receive from acquired users" appear in the FY2024 10-K only; no 10-Q before or after uses either phrase. The FY2025 10-K replaces the pricing sentence with "campaign goals".

The first time the 10-K explains how AppLovin is paid: on a price tied to what each acquired user is worth to the advertiser, not a fixed CPI or CPM. The FY2025 10-K rewrites this same sentence (see next transition), so this is the baseline the later change is measured against.
▶E-commerce moves from a "long-term objective" to claimed "positive results"; the risk factors call it "still in its early stages"New

Shift: named +e-commerce; realized claim ("positive results") (+) beside a hedge ("still in its early stages") (−)

FY2024 (Business): "We have made our Advertising solutions available to e-commerce advertisers, and while we are early in this market expansion, our e-commerce customers to date have experienced positive results, demonstrating the flexibility and future growth potential of our Advertising solutions."
FY2024 (Risk Factors): "The expansion of our services to this market is still in its early stages. ... Further, there can be no assurance that we will achieve broader adoption among e-commerce advertisers or that we will effectively develop technology for our AXON platform."
FY2024 (Risk Factors, new): "Our ability to improve the effectiveness and predictability of our advertising recommendations through improvements to our AI-powered advertising engine AXON is critical to our continuing success and future growth."

History: "positive results" appears in the FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks (Business). "still in its early stages" runs FY2024 10-K through Q2 2025 and is gone from Q3 2025 on.

FY2023 named e-commerce only as a target vertical ("multiple verticals, including, for example, e-commerce and social media"). The business section now claims results, but the risk factors hedge them. A new summary bullet, "our ability to attract and retain clients, including, for example, in new markets such as e-commerce and social", makes e-commerce adoption a named risk from the start. AXON is now called "critical" in both sections.
▶"Our Values" section deleted one year after it was written; a "lean organizational structure" risk factor replaces itRemoved

Shift: removed (−); culture restated as a risk (realized, "lean workforce could limit")

FY2023: "We are not afraid to part ways with underperformers or those satisfied with the bare minimum and will enable them to follow their passions towards a better path."
FY2024 (new risk factor): "While we believe our lean culture allows us to move faster than other companies our size, a lean workforce could limit our ability to scale operations quickly in response to increased demand, develop new products or services in a timely manner, or effectively manage multiple initiatives simultaneously."

History: "part ways with underperformers" appears in the FY2023 10-K only. "a lean workforce could limit" runs FY2024 10-K through Q3 2025; the FY2025 10-K keeps the lean-structure paragraph but not that sentence.

All six values ("Think Like an Entrepreneur", "Hire and Reward Doers", "Move Fast" and the rest) and the workplace-awards paragraph (Great Place to Work, the 2023 Q+ Workplace certification) were deleted. The thin headcount now appears as a risk, not as culture. Headcount fell 1,745 → 1,563, and R&D staff fell from about 950 (54%) to 788 (50%).
▶Reach claim raised from 1.4B "users per day" to 1.6B "daily active users", with a new footnote that excludes ATT opt-outsChanged

Shift: quant changed, 1.4B → 1.6B (+); definition added (+ disclosure)

FY2023: "Our Software Platform reaches approximately 1.4 billion users per day, enabling developers to target and find the right users for their content worldwide."
FY2024: "We provide advertisers with access to approximately 1.6 billion daily active users ... This figure does not include any users who have opted out of allowing apps to track on their mobile phone."

History: "1.6 billion daily active users" appears in the FY2024 10-K only. The Q3 2024 10-Q had "over 1.4 billion daily active users" with the same footnote; no other filing carries a reach figure.

For the first time the filing defines the number: unique device identities opening an app with AppLovin's SDK, counted through the SDK. The FY2025 10-K deletes the figure and its footnote entirely. That makes FY2024 the only 10-K with a defined reach metric.
▶Patent count cut from 28 issued U.S. patents to seven for Advertising; "speed of our technological development" now named as the moatChanged

Shift: quant changed, 28 → 7 (−); named +"speed of our technological development" as the advantage

FY2023: "we owned the following patents related to the business: 28 issued U.S. patents and 1 U.S. patent application"
FY2024: "As of December 31, 2024, we owned seven patents related to our Advertising solutions and four related to our Apps business."
FY2024: "the speed of our technological development and the functionality and flexibility of our Advertising solutions in an ever-evolving industry, are critical contributors to our success"

History: "28 issued U.S. patents" in FY2023 only; "seven patents" in FY2024 only. "speed of our technological development" appears in FY2024 and FY2025.

Patents are no longer where the company says its advantage lies. The FY2023 sentence had said those factors were "also key contributors". The FY2025 10-K drops the patent and trademark counts altogether.

MD&A

▶Advertising +75% was volume-led: installs +17% → +50%, net revenue per installation +35% → +22%Signal

Shift: quant changed, installs +17% → +50%, price +35% → +22%

FY2023: "The increase in Software Platform revenue was also due to improved AppDiscovery performance, where installations increased 17% and net revenue per installation increased 35% compared to the prior year period."
FY2024: "primarily due to improved AppDiscovery performance, where the volume of installations increased 50% and net revenue per installation increased 22% compared to the prior year period."

History: the installs/price split is given in every 10-K and in most 10-Qs from Q3 2023 on; FY2024 is the highest annual volume figure in the series (FY2023 +17%, FY2025 +3%).

This was the most volume-driven year in the series. FY2025 reverses it (+3% volume, +72% price), so the two years together show the shift from more installs to higher prices. The segment was renamed in the same filing: "Software Platform Revenue represented 56% of total revenue" became "Advertising Revenue represented 68% of total revenue".
▶Apps sale term sheet: $900M = $500M cash + $400M buyer equity, with up to $250M of the cash possibly financed by AppLovinNew

Shift: named +Term Sheet; quant added, $900M, $250M possible seller financing (+ disclosure)

FY2024: "The Term Sheet provides for the total consideration to consist of $400.0 million in shares of the Acquirer's common equity and $500.0 million in cash, subject to customary purchase price adjustments. The Term Sheet also provides that the Acquirer will borrow up to $250.0 million of the cash portion of the total consideration and that, if the Acquirer is unable to obtain such financing, we agree to provide financing in such amount to the Acquirer through the issuance of a promissory note."

History: "Term Sheet" first appears in the FY2024 10-K; later filings mention it only in the notes. The Q1 2025 10-Q replaces it with a signed agreement on different terms.

Only the MD&A gives the seller-financing term. The business section mentions the $900M headline alone. The term sheet was non-binding. The sale closed in June 2025 for $430.6M cash plus Tripledot shares valued at $285.0M (see FY2025).
▶Cloud purchase obligations up from $252.0M to $1.2B ($438.9M due within 12 months); new risk on securing compute "at a reasonable cost"Signal

Shift: quant up, $252.0M → $1.2B (+ obligation); certainty, capacity risk stated ("secure sufficient capacity at a reasonable cost")

FY2023: "we have non-cancellable purchase obligations, which primarily consist of a certain arrangement related to cloud platform services, of $252.0 million, with $160.2 million payable within twelve months."
FY2024: "we had non-cancellable purchase obligations primarily related to an agreement for third-party cloud computing services of $1.2 billion, with $438.9 million payable within twelve months."
FY2024 (Risk Factors, new): "Additionally, we rely on certain third-party providers for our increasing network capacity and computing power needs, and if we fail to properly anticipate our needs or secure sufficient capacity at a reasonable cost, our ability to scale and grow our business, or our profitability, could be negatively impacted."

History: the capacity sentence first appeared in the Q2 2024 10-Q, not the 10-K, and runs in every filing since. The $1.26B minimum was first disclosed in the Q3 2024 10-Q.

The notes say the August 2024 amendment committed AppLovin to spend at least $1.3 billion over three years. That compute commitment is about five times the prior one. The new risk factor treats capacity as a constraint on growth, not only on cost.
▶Buybacks capped at the prior quarter's Free Cash Flow; the option to fund them by "accessing the capital markets" is deletedChanged

Shift: condition added, FCF cap (− flexibility); funding option "accessing the capital markets" removed

FY2023: "We will continuously evaluate efficient alternatives to using cash on hand to fund the program, including accessing the capital markets, subject to market conditions."
FY2024: "During 2024, our board of directors authorized increases to our stock repurchase program of an aggregate amount of $3.3 billion, which additional amount may be repurchased from time to time subject to a limitation in any fiscal quarter of the amount of our Free Cash Flow in the preceding fiscal quarter"

History: "accessing the capital markets" appears in the FY2023 10-K only. "Free Cash Flow in the preceding fiscal quarter" runs Q3 2024 through Q2 2025.

The authorization grew, but repurchases became limited by cash generation. The board loosened the cap in February 2025 and removed it in October 2025 (see FY2025).

Risk Factors

▶Secured term loans ($3.2B) replaced by $3.6B of unsecured notes "rated investment-grade"; collateral language deletedChanged

Shift: named +Senior Notes, investment-grade rating (+); collateral language removed

FY2023: "In addition, we have granted a security interest in a significant portion of our assets to secure our obligations under our credit facility."
FY2024: "As of December 31, 2024, we had a total of $3.6 billion in aggregate principal amount of senior unsecured notes outstanding ... Moreover, our Senior Notes are currently rated investment-grade by various rating agencies."

History: "security interest in a significant portion" ran Q2 2023 through Q3 2024 and is absent from the FY2024 10-K on. "rated investment-grade" runs from the FY2024 10-K through Q2 2026.

A new paragraph on credit ratings and a change-of-control put on the notes replace the foreclosure language. The heading still reads "We have a substantial amount of indebtedness"; "substantial" was dropped in the Q3 2025 10-Q and the FY2025 10-K.
▶KKR leaves the Voting Agreement: insider voting power 85% → 68%; controlled-company exemptions no longer usedChanged

Shift: quant changed, 85% → 68% (− insider control); de-named, KKR Denali removed

FY2023: "As of December 31, 2023, the Voting Agreement Parties collectively held approximately 85% of the voting power of our outstanding capital stock in the aggregate."
FY2024: "As of December 31, 2024, the Voting Agreement Parties collectively held approximately 68% of the voting power of our outstanding capital stock in the aggregate."
FY2023: "we qualify for, and currently rely on, exemptions from certain corporate governance requirements"
FY2024: "We do not currently avail ourselves of any of these corporate governance accommodations, though we may do so in the future."

History: the Q1 2024 10-Q first said "We do not currently avail ourselves"; the 10-Qs gave about 81% (Q1 2024) and 73% (Q2–Q3 2024) before the 10-K's 68%. The percentage is stated in every filing in the dataset.

Votes are now decided by Foroughi and Chen alone; previously two of three parties decided, one of whom had to be Foroughi. The sentence saying the compensation committee was not fully independent was deleted.
▶"Publication of short seller reports" added to the stock-price factors, which now "have in the past caused" fluctuationsNew

Shift: named +short seller reports; realized, "have in the past caused" (+)

FY2023: "short selling of our Class A common stock or related derivative securities;"
FY2024: "short selling of our Class A common stock or related derivative securities, and the publication of short seller reports"
FY2024: "Factors that have in the past caused and could in the future cause fluctuations in the market price of our Class A common stock include the following"

History: "publication of short seller reports" first appears in the FY2024 10-K and runs in every filing since.

The first reference to short-seller reports in the 10-K. It names no report. The securities class actions that followed in March 2025 are first disclosed in the FY2025 10-K.
▶Concentration risk redefined from "various ways" to "the mobile app ecosystem"; the three-games-equal-15% disclosure is deletedChanged

Shift: scope narrowed, "various ways" → "the mobile app ecosystem"; quant removed (the 15% three-game figure) (−)

FY2023: "Our revenue has been concentrated in various ways and the loss of, or a significant reduction in, any such revenue source, or our failure to successfully expand and diversify our revenue sources could adversely affect our business"
FY2024: "Our revenue has been concentrated in the mobile app ecosystem and any failure to successfully expand and diversify our revenue sources beyond the mobile ecosystem could adversely affect our business"

History: "concentrated in various ways" stood Q2 2023 through Q3 2024; "concentrated in the mobile app ecosystem" runs from the FY2024 10-K through Q2 2026. "Project Makeover" last appears in the Q3 2024 10-Q.

FY2023 said Project Makeover, Matchington Mansion and Wordscapes made up "approximately 15% of our revenue". With Apps up for sale, that paragraph was cut. The named concentration risk is now dependence on mobile, which is the risk the e-commerce push is meant to address.

FY2024 → FY2025: Pure-Play AI Advertising

Filed Feb 2026 | Revenue (continuing operations, Apps restated out): $3.22B → $5.48B (+70%) | Installs +3%, net revenue per installation +72% | Net income: $1.58B → $3.33B | Adj. EBITDA margin 74.8% → 82.3% | Employees: 1,563 → 898 (Apps staff left with the sale)

Business

▶Pricing basis rewritten from "the revenue they receive from acquired users" to "their campaign goals"; ROAS may now be measured by "self-attribution"Changed

Shift: named, "revenue they receive from acquired users" → "campaign goals" (− specificity); measurement widened to "self-attribution" (−)

FY2024: "Return on advertising spend is measured based on third-party attribution, Advertisers are charged for advertising dynamically based on the revenue they receive from acquired users"
FY2025: "Return on advertising spend is measured based on either third-party or self-attribution. Advertisers are charged dynamically based on their campaign goals, rather than a simple fixed price per impression or per action (click or installation)."
FY2024 (Risk Factors, deleted in FY2025): "Advertising Revenue is mostly from AppDiscovery and is generated from our advertisers, typically on a performance-based, cost-per-install basis, then shared with our advertising publishers, typically on a cost per impression model."

History: "revenue they receive from acquired users" appears in the FY2024 10-K only; "their campaign goals" first appears in the FY2025 10-K and is carried into the Q1 and Q2 2026 MD&A. "self-attribution" appears in the FY2025 10-K only. "performance-based, cost-per-install basis" was already gone from the Q1 2025 10-Q.

The two anchors of the FY2024 description are both gone. The price is no longer tied to downstream revenue, and the return on spend can now be measured by the advertiser itself rather than only by a third party. The MD&A drops the cost-per-impression revenue-share sentence too. The new wording fits non-install (web) campaigns. It also leaves less outside measurement in the revenue model, which matters because the securities suits concern statements about the advertising solutions.
▶One product is now the company: Axon Ads Manager "comprises substantially all of our revenue" (AppDiscovery was "a vast majority of our Advertising Revenue")Changed

Shift: scope widened, "a vast majority of our Advertising Revenue" → "substantially all of our revenue" (+)

FY2024: "Revenue from AppDiscovery comprises a vast majority of our Advertising Revenue."
FY2025: "Revenue from Axon Ads Manager comprises substantially all of our revenue."

History: "a vast majority of our Advertising Revenue" in FY2024 only; "comprises substantially all of our revenue" in FY2025 only.

The denominator widens from one segment to the whole company and the qualifier strengthens. MAX, Adjust and Wurl together are now immaterial; the MD&A says "Revenue from other services was not material."
▶AppDiscovery → Axon Ads Manager, matching "apps and websites" to "engaged users"; seasonality now names "large e-commerce advertisers"Changed

Shift: named, AppDiscovery → Axon Ads Manager; scope widened, "apps" → "apps and websites"; named +large e-commerce advertisers

FY2024: "AppDiscovery is powered by AXON's predictive algorithms to enable advertisers to match their apps to users that are more likely to download them."
FY2025: "Axon Ads Manager is powered by Axon AI's predictive algorithms to enable advertisers to match their apps and websites to engaged users, delivering more of what they are likely to be interested in."
FY2025 (Seasonality): "As the breadth and scale of advertisers using our platform continues to expand, including increased participation from large e-commerce advertisers, the magnitude and impact of these seasonal trends may become more pronounced over time."

History: "apps and websites to engaged users" and "large e-commerce advertisers" appear in the FY2025 10-K only. "Axon Ads Manager" first appeared in the Q3 2025 10-Q and was renamed AppLovin Ads in Q2 2026.

"Download" is gone from the product description and the corporate blog moved to axon.ai/blog. The FY2024 seasonality note named only the fourth quarter. The new one adds "promotional events" and "school-related cycles". It says the impact "may become" more pronounced, so it does not yet claim e-commerce moves the numbers.
▶Reach and product claims removed: the 1.6B DAU figure, SparkLabs, three Wurl products, the Array OEM/carrier initiative, and patent and trademark countsRemoved

Shift: quant removed, 1.6B DAU (−); de-named, SparkLabs, Array, three Wurl products

FY2024: "We provide advertisers with access to approximately 1.6 billion daily active users"
FY2025: "We enable advertisers to target and find the right users for their content and products worldwide"
FY2024 (deleted): "including with mobile OEMs and carriers through our Array product initiative, as well as the growing CTV industry through Wurl"

History: "1.6 billion daily active users" appears in FY2024 only; "SparkLabs" in FY2023 and FY2024 only; "through our Array product initiative" last appears in the Q2 2025 10-Q.

The only reach metric the 10-K ever defined is gone, a year after it was introduced. So are the in-house creative team ("SparkLabs"), TVBits, BrandDiscovery and ContentDiscovery, Array, and every patent and trademark count. What remains describes one product.
▶R&D staff 788 → 380 (50% → 42% of headcount); the "approximately 2,000 additional team members" from acquired studios are deletedChanged

Shift: quant changed, 788 → 380 R&D staff; quant removed, "approximately 2,000 additional team members" (−)

FY2024: "As of December 31, 2024, we had approximately 788 employees, or 50% of our total headcount, involved in research and development and related activities."
FY2025: "As of December 31, 2025, we had approximately 380 employees, or 42% of our total headcount, involved in research and development and related activities."
FY2024 (deleted): "As a result of our historical strategic acquisition and partnership activity, we also have access to approximately 2,000 additional team members, a majority of whom are research and development resources"

History: "approximately 2,000 additional team members" stood in FY2023 and FY2024; "380 employees" in FY2025 only.

The filing does not split the cut between Apps staff transferred to Tripledot and the continuing business. Total headcount fell from 1,563 to 898, and R&D expense fell 40%, which the MD&A attributes to lower stock-based compensation.
▶Adjust data firewall gains an exception: "unless directed by a customer"Changed

Shift: condition added, "unless directed by a customer" (− firewall)

FY2025: "data generated by Adjust's services is not shared with AppLovin or incorporated into or used to optimize its recommendation engine or other technologies unless directed by a customer."

History: "unless directed by a customer" appears in the FY2025 10-K only; the sentence without it stood in FY2023 and FY2024.

For three filings the sentence ended at "technologies". The added clause allows Adjust measurement data to reach Axon AI if the customer chooses. The filing does not say whether any customer has done so.

MD&A

▶Growth came almost entirely from price: revenue +70%, installs +3% (was +50%), net revenue per installation +72% (was +22%)Signal

Shift: quant changed, installs +50% → +3%, price +22% → +72%

FY2024: "where the volume of installations increased 50% and net revenue per installation increased 22% compared to the prior year period."
FY2025: "primarily due to improved Axon Ads Manager performance, where the volume of installations increased 3% and net revenue per installation increased 72% compared to the prior year period."

History: "net revenue per installation increased 72%" in FY2025 only. Price led in every quarterly print from Q1 2025 on.

This is the restated continuing-operations basis: $3.22B → $5.48B. The $4.71B FY2024 figure included Apps. Install growth fell from +50% to +3% in one year. The 10-Qs show the quarterly volume turning negative (−1% in Q3 2025, −18% in Q1 2026).
▶Apps sale closed for $715.6M against the $900M term sheet: $430.6M cash plus Tripledot shares valued at $285.0M; no seller noteChanged

Shift: quant changed, $900M term sheet → $715.6M received (−); realized, "No promissory note was issued"

FY2024: "The Term Sheet provides for the total consideration to consist of $400.0 million in shares of the Acquirer's common equity and $500.0 million in cash"
FY2025: "the Company received $715.6 million in total consideration, consisting of $430.6 million in cash and 596.9 million ordinary shares of Tripledot, valued at $285.0 million."
FY2025: "No promissory note was issued as part of the transaction."

History: "715.6 million" first appears in the Q2 2025 10-Q notes and is carried in every filing since; the FY2025 10-K is the first 10-K to state it.

Themes

The Cleanest Pivot in Tech

From mobile gaming company (76% Apps) to pure AI advertising platform (100% Advertising) in 4 filings. Every non-core element stripped: studios from 19 to 0, flywheel narrative gutted, Apps sold to Tripledot. The language preceded the financials at every turn -- "no longer required" appeared two years before the sale closed.

898 People, $5.5B Revenue

Revenue per employee 3.5x'd in 4 years ($1.75M → $6.10M). Headcount fell 43% in FY2025, mostly because Apps staff left with the sale (R&D staff 788 → 380), while continuing-operations revenue grew 70% ($3.22B → $5.48B). The FY2024 10-K turned the thin headcount into a disclosed risk ("a lean workforce could limit our ability to scale operations quickly").

AXON Becomes the Identity

From buried technical component ("machine-learning recommendation engine") to company brand. Blog at axon.ai. Product named Axon Ads Manager. Engine renamed Axon AI. The ML → AI rebranding was perfectly timed with the generative AI wave, even though AXON's core technology predated it. The 10-Qs since have partly reversed this: the Q2 2026 10-Q renames Axon Ads Manager "AppLovin Ads" and deletes the Axon brand-awareness sentences, while keeping "Axon AI" for the engine (see the 10-Q scanner).

From Mobile to Web

"Apps" → "apps and websites." E-commerce from aspiration to deployment. Campaign goals broadened beyond install-based revenue. Revenue model language shifted from "revenue from acquired users" to "campaign goals" -- reflecting the expansion to web-based advertisers and non-install objectives.

82% EBITDA Margins on $5.5B

The operating leverage of an AI advertising engine with minimal variable costs. From 26% to 82% in four years. Among the highest margins in public software or adtech at any revenue scale. Once the AI model works, incremental revenue drops almost entirely to profit -- the economics of a pure software business with no content costs, no hardware, and a shrinking workforce.
