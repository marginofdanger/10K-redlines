AVGO 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of Broadcom's 10-Q filings, FY2024 Q1 (quarter ended February 4, 2024 — the first full VMware quarter) through FY2026 Q2 (quarter ended May 3, 2026, filed June 9, 2026), with the FY2024 and FY2025 10-K boundary transitions included. Broadcom restates its full risk factors every quarter, so the quarterly redline chain is unusually rich: language changes surface here 3–9 months before annual comparisons would catch them.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001730168) | 8 quarterly 10-Qs + 2 annual 10-Ks | FY2024 Q1 – FY2026 Q2 | Analysis: June 2026

Transition Overview

FY24 Q1 → Q2

Apple's 17% disclosure and the WT Microelectronics name are both removed and never restored. The first AI revenue attribution ("products in AI networking") appears in MD&A, and AI enters the demand-forecasting, stock-price and cyber-threat risk language. VMware integration statements shift from future to present tense.

FY24 Q2 → Q3

The EUC business is sold to KKR for $3.5B, $5.0B of notes are issued to repay the $9,195M term A-2 facility, and a 10-for-1 stock split completes. AI attribution strengthens from "including" to "primarily," and a $4,238M tax provision lands from onshoring IP to the U.S.

FY24 Q3 → FY2024 10-K

"Custom AI accelerators or XPUs" appears for the first time anywhere, alongside hyperscale-customer concentration, design-win strain language and a "record revenue, which may not be sustainable" caveat. The Huawei example is deleted and the VMware risk is reframed around the subscription-licensing transition.

FY2024 10-K → FY25 Q1

"VMware Merger" becomes "acquisition of VMware" throughout, and MD&A attribution moves from VMware contributions to organic VCF demand. A standing Macroeconomic Factors section debuts citing tariffs, export-control language hardens to present tense, and commercial paper debuts at $4.0B.

FY25 Q1 → Q2

Tariffs get a dedicated risk paragraph after the April 2025 escalation. Buybacks resume under a new $10B authorization ($2,450M in the quarter) and the buyback risk factor returns. Two-Year Equity Awards replace annual grants, lifting unrecognized compensation from $10.7B to $27.0B.

FY25 Q2 → Q3

The largest single-quarter risk rewrite of the set: "AI racks based on our XPUs," the sale-or-lease model and AI-customer credit risk all arrive. "Custom AI accelerators" is first named in revenue attribution, OBBBA drives a $1,058M CAMT valuation allowance, and floating-rate debt is eliminated.

FY25 Q3 → FY2025 10-K

A new lead risk factor states the industry is "undergoing profound change due to AI" and that AI customers "may be unable to pay." The business section is rewritten around AI with "AI frontier models" customers named, the first tariff materiality statement appears, $7.8B of upfront license revenue is reported within products, and the Seasonality section is deleted.

FY2025 10-K → FY26 Q1

Customer concentration step-changes: top five end customers ~40%→~50%, largest distributor 29%→42%, distributor channel 48%→55%. Guarantees enter the financing risk language, memory chips are named a supply constraint, Pillar Two moves to "have materially increased," and $7,850M of stock is repurchased in one quarter.

FY26 Q1 → Q2

A June 8, 2026 subsequent event discloses a $29B backstop of a customer's AI-rack lease obligations arranged with Apollo. "Backstops" enters the risk factors, purchase commitments jump $54M→$128.1B, RPO jumps $45.0B→$164.6B on a long-term custom AI accelerator contract, buybacks throttle $7,850M→$600M, and inventory builds to $4,328M for custom AI accelerators.

Risk Factors

▶ Apple percentage disclosure removed Removed

Q1 was the only 10-Q in the set to name Apple and quantify its share. The MD&A carried a parallel sentence ("We believe aggregate sales to Apple Inc., through all channels, accounted for approximately 17% and 20% of our net revenue…") that also does not appear in Q2 or any later filing.

Q1 FY2024 (prior)

"We believe aggregate sales, through all channels, to Apple and our top five end customers, accounted for approximately 17% and 40%, respectively, of our net revenue for the fiscal quarter ended February 4, 2024."

Q2 FY2024 (current)

"We believe aggregate sales, through all channels, to our top five end customers, accounted for approximately 40% of our net revenue for the two fiscal quarters ended May 5, 2024."
▶ AI added to demand-forecasting and stock-volatility risks New

Q2 inserted "demand for AI-related products" in two places where Q1 had no AI references at all (zero instances of "AI-related" in Q1 risk factors): the customer-demand-estimate factor adds "…new or unexpected end-user products, such as demand for AI-related products…" and the stock-price factor list gains a new bullet:

Q2 FY2024 (new stock-price factor)

"demand for AI-related products by our customers or perceived by investors"
▶ Generative-AI cyber threat language added New

The cybersecurity risk factor gains a sentence on AI-enabled attack methods that did not exist in Q1; this sentence later shifts to past tense in FY2026 Q2.

Q2 FY2024 (new)

"The emergence and maturation of AI capabilities may also lead to new and/or more sophisticated methods of attack, including fraud that relies upon “deep fake” impersonation technology or other forms of generative automation that may scale up the efficiency or effectiveness of cyber threat activity."
▶ Semiconductor down-cycle moved to past tense Changed

The cyclical-industry factor stops describing the downturn as current.

Q1 FY2024 (prior)

"The industry, however is currently experiencing a downturn…"

Q2 FY2024 (current)

"…which was followed by a down-cycle resulting in diminished demand for end-user products, high inventory levels…"
▶ VMware channel dependence: expectation becomes fact Changed

The same quarter, "we plan to focus on VMware's core business" became "we are focusing on VMware's core business" — the integration statements moved from forward-looking to present tense across the factor.

Q1 FY2024 (prior)

"We expect our dependence on channel partners will increase following the VMware Merger."

Q2 FY2024 (current)

"Our dependence on channel partners has increased following the VMware Merger."
▶ April 2024 Taiwan earthquake cited as TSMC concentration example New

The supplier-concentration factor adds a current-events example tied directly to the foundry that produces ~95% of Broadcom's CM wafers. The same quarter also added "A reduction in our cash dividend payments could have a negative effect on our stock price" and deleted COVID-19 references throughout.

Q2 FY2024 (new)

"For example, in April 2024, Taiwan experienced an earthquake, which is where TSMC is located."

MD&A

▶ First AI revenue attribution: "products in AI networking" Changed

The Q1 MD&A contained zero instances of "AI." Q2 inserts the first AI attribution into the networking revenue sentence, and reorders the offset clause ("server storage and broadband" becomes "broadband and server storage").

Q1 FY2024 (prior)

"…due to strong product demand for networking products, partially offset by lower demand for our server storage and broadband products."

Q2 FY2024 (current)

"…due to strong product demand for our networking products, including products in AI networking, partially offset by lower demand for our broadband and server storage products."
▶ Largest distributor de-named (WT Microelectronics) Changed

Q1 named the largest direct customer; from Q2 onward it is an anonymous "one customer, which is a distributor" (renamed "one semiconductor solutions customer" in FY2025 Q3). The name never returns.

Q1 FY2024 (prior)

"Direct sales to WT Microelectronics Co., Ltd., a distributor, accounted for 27% and 22% of our net revenue for the fiscal quarters ended February 4, 2024 and January 29, 2023, respectively."

Q2 FY2024 (current)

"Direct sales to one customer, which is a distributor, accounted for 29% and 28% of our net revenue for the fiscal quarter and two fiscal quarters ended May 5, 2024, respectively…"

Notes / Financial

▶ Quarter housekeeping: $2.0B term-loan repayment, Seagate SoC deal, debt step-down Signal

Q2 highlights a $2.0B repayment on the unsecured term A-2 facility; senior-note indebtedness cited in the risk factors steps down $78,521M → $76,521M. The Seagate SoC asset acquisition (April 23, 2024, $600M) is first disclosed. Distributor channel share moves 51% → 50%, and the floating-rate debt bullet drops the clause "which we do not typically hedge against."

The export-controls factor recasts the Huawei situation: sales are no longer suspended pending licenses; the risk is now failing to maintain licenses already issued.

Q3 FY2024 (current)

"…that require us to suspend sales to Huawei if we fail to maintain licenses issued by the U.S. Department of Commerce."
▶ "Evolving markets such as AI" added to the cyclical-industry risk New

AI is inserted into the standing list of cyclical-industry characteristics.

Q3 FY2024 (new)

"The semiconductor industry is highly cyclical and is characterized by price erosion, wide fluctuations in product supply and demand, constant and rapid technological change, evolving technical standards, evolving markets such as AI, frequent new product introductions, and short product life cycles…"
▶ New distributor go-to-market risk: Accelerate Program and Catalyst Initiative New

First disclosure that distributors lead go-to-market and customer relationships for certain software products, with sole distribution relationships by region.

Q3 FY2024 (new)

"From time to time, we enlist our distributors and channel partners to lead go-to-market and customer relationships for certain products, such as our Accelerate Program and Catalyst Initiative for certain infrastructure software products, with certain sole distribution relationships by region."
▶ Geographic concentration intensified: "located in" becomes "concentrated in the same geographic regions" Changed

Q2 FY2024 (prior)

"Many of our facilities, and those of our CMs and suppliers, are located in California and the Pacific Rim region, which have above average seismic activity and severe weather activity."

Q3 FY2024 (current)

"Many of our facilities, and those of our CMs and suppliers, are concentrated in the same geographic regions of California and the Pacific Rim, which have above average seismic activity and severe weather activity, and increases the risk of natural disasters impacting multiple suppliers."

Themes

1. The AI Buildout Moved Onto the Balance Sheet in FY2026 Q2

Four data points landed in the same filing: purchase commitments $54M → $128,110M, RPO $45.0B → $164.6B (with a new long-term custom AI accelerator contract sentence), inventory $2,270M → $4,328M attributed to custom AI accelerators, and a $29B Apollo lease backstop dated the day before filing. Buybacks fell from $7,850M to $600M in the same quarter while cash built to $19,628M.

2. Risk Language Preceded the Numbers by 1–3 Quarters

"Custom AI accelerators or XPUs" appeared in risk factors (FY2024 10-K) three quarters before entering revenue attribution (FY25Q3). The sale-or-lease model appeared as a hypothetical (FY25Q3) three quarters before the backstop and purchase-commitment numbers (FY26Q2). "Guarantees" entered the financing factor (FY26Q1) one quarter before "backstops" and the executed arrangement.

3. Concentration Disclosure Narrowed as Concentration Rose

Apple (17%) and WT Microelectronics (27%) were named in FY24Q1; both names were removed at FY24Q2 and never restored. By FY26Q1 the anonymous largest distributor was 42% of revenue, top five end customers ~50%, and the distributor channel 55% — all-time highs for the series, disclosed with unchanged sentence structures.

4. The Liability Structure Was De-Risked Before New Obligations Arrived

$30.4B of floating-rate VMware term loans (7.0–7.2% effective) were fully replaced by fixed-rate debt over seven quarters ("no floating-rate term loans outstanding" at FY25Q3), a first 30-year tranche was issued January 2026, and the two newest note series dropped the change-of-control put — followed within two quarters by backstop and guarantee obligations entering the same risk factor.

5. The Tax Regime Shifted From Near-Zero to Structural

Implied quarterly rates ran ~0–5% through FY25H1 (excluding the one-time $4,238M IP-onshoring charge).
