AVGO 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of Broadcom's 10-Q filings, FY2024 Q1 (quarter ended February 4, 2024 — the first full VMware quarter) through FY2026 Q3 (quarter ended August 2, 2026, filed September 10, 2026), with the FY2024 and FY2025 10-K boundary transitions included. Broadcom restates its full risk factors every quarter, so the quarterly redline chain is unusually rich: language changes surface here 3–9 months before annual comparisons would catch them.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001730168) | 9 quarterly 10-Qs + 2 annual 10-Ks | FY2024 Q1 – FY2026 Q3 | Analysis: June 2026, extended October 2026; every transition from the FY2024 10-K onward re-done with sentence signals October 2026

Transition Overview

FY24 Q1 → Q2

Apple's 17% disclosure and the WT Microelectronics name are both removed and never restored. The first AI revenue attribution ("products in AI networking") appears in MD&A, and AI enters the demand-forecasting, stock-price and cyber-threat risk language. VMware integration statements shift from future to present tense.

FY24 Q2 → Q3

The EUC business is sold to KKR for $3.5B, $5.0B of notes are issued to repay the $9,195M term A-2 facility, and a 10-for-1 stock split completes. AI attribution strengthens from "including" to "primarily," and a $4,238M tax provision lands from onshoring IP to the U.S.

FY24 Q3 → FY2024 10-K

"Custom AI accelerators or XPUs" appears for the first time anywhere, with design-win costs "which we expect to continue," hyperscale-customer concentration and a "record revenue, which may not be sustainable" caveat. The Huawei example is deleted (China revenue fell only 9% in dollars), the VMware risk is reframed around the subscription-licensing transition, and the one-quarter-old "ratable revenue recognition" clause is dropped.

FY2024 10-K → FY25 Q1

Software growth is credited to VCF demand and "license revenue recognized on contracts where customers do not have the right to terminate" instead of "contributions from VMware," and semiconductors are split into AI and shrinking "non-AI solutions." A Macroeconomic Factors section debuts citing tariffs, the U.S. "continues to add" companies to its restricted list, trade tensions widen from China to "U.S. trading partners," and $4.0B of commercial paper is outstanding.

FY25 Q1 → Q2

Product lifecycle management "have previously led to" customer dissatisfaction, the quarter's only "realized" change. The U.S. tax-reform sentence drops "we expect an increase in our effective tax rate." Tariffs get their own risk sentences, the buyback factor returns with a $10B program ($2,450M bought), and Two-Year Equity Awards lift unrecognized compensation from $10.7B to $27.0B.

FY25 Q2 → Q3

The largest single-quarter risk rewrite of the set: "AI racks based on our XPUs," the sale-or-lease model and AI-customer credit risk all arrive. "Custom AI accelerators" is first named in revenue attribution, OBBBA drives a $1,058M CAMT valuation allowance, and floating-rate debt is eliminated.

FY25 Q3 → FY2025 10-K

The lead risk factor states the industry is "undergoing profound change due to AI" and that AI customers "may be unable to pay." Customers "have made" rack-lease demands and Broadcom "may offer and have offered" financing; racks "will likely" compress gross margin. "The majority of our sales" concentration sentence is deleted, factoring enters the risk factors, and $7.8B of upfront license revenue is reported within products.

FY2025 10-K → FY26 Q1

Concentration steps up: top five end customers ~40%→~50%, distributor channel 48%→55%, and one distributor at 42% of the quarter. "Guarantees" appear in three places one quarter before the backstop, and the AI-rack model moves to "We have dedicated." RPO rises $33.3B→$45.0B. Software grows 1%, and the licensing risk is corrected from "perpetual" to "subscription." Pillar Two minimum taxes "have materially increased," and $7,850M of stock is repurchased.

FY26 Q1 → Q2

A June 8, 2026 subsequent event discloses a $29B backstop of a customer's AI-rack lease obligations, arranged with Apollo. "Backstops" enters the risk factors. Customers are now "requiring us to purchase and then lease" racks. Purchase commitments jump $54M→$128.1B and RPO $45.0B→$164.6B on one custom AI accelerator contract. Buybacks fall $7,850M→$600M and inventory builds to $4,328M. Top five end customers dip to ~45%, and the export-control factor softens "will likely limit" to "can limit."

FY26 Q2 → Q3

The backstop becomes the first $35B tranche of an "AI XPV platform" covering more than 20 GW through 2028, and a new note lets the lease customer issue Broadcom up to $42B of convertible notes to fund its lease payments. Risk factors name an AI spending "bubble" while MD&A cites the frontier labs' "strong profitability trajectory." Termination for convenience is dropped from most new software contracts (upfront license $1,964M→$3,465M). One distributor reaches 50% of revenue. Stock buybacks go to zero while $5,641M of notes are retired.

Themes

1. The AI Buildout Moved Onto the Balance Sheet (FY26Q2), Then Became a Financing Platform (FY26Q3)

FY26Q2 brought four balance-sheet data points in one filing: purchase commitments $54M → $128,110M, RPO $45.0B → $164.6B, inventory $2,270M → $4,328M, and a $29B Apollo lease backstop. In FY26Q3 the single arrangement became the $35B first tranche of an "AI XPV platform" aimed at more than 20 GW through 2028. A new note lets the lease customer issue Broadcom up to $42B of convertible notes to fund its lease payments, and RPO reached $179.2B. Stock buybacks went $7,850M → $600M → $0 over the three quarters.

2. Risk Language Preceded the Numbers by 1–3 Quarters

"Custom AI accelerators or XPUs" appeared in risk factors (FY2024 10-K) three quarters before entering revenue attribution (FY25Q3). The sale-or-lease model appeared as a hypothetical (FY25Q3) three quarters before the backstop and purchase-commitment numbers (FY26Q2). "Guarantees" entered the financing factor (FY26Q1) one quarter before "backstops" and the executed arrangement. In FY26Q3 the two sections diverged instead: MD&A called residual value guarantees "low probability" because of the frontier labs' "strong profitability trajectory," while the risk factors added an AI spending "bubble" and customers "unable to generate a profit required to support their expenditures."

3. Concentration Disclosure Narrowed as Concentration Rose

Apple (17%) and WT Microelectronics (27%) were named in FY24Q1; both names were removed at FY24Q2 and never restored. By FY26Q1 the anonymous largest distributor was 42% of revenue, top five end customers ~50%, and the distributor channel 55% — all-time highs for the series at the time, disclosed with unchanged sentence structures, one filing after the FY2025 10-K deleted "The majority of our sales have historically come from a small number of customers." After a dip to ~45% in FY26Q2, FY26Q3 set new highs: one distributor at 50% of quarterly revenue and the top five end customers at ~55%.

4. Funded Debt Fell as Contingent Obligations Rose

$30.4B of floating-rate VMware term loans (7.0–7.2% effective) were fully replaced by fixed-rate debt over seven quarters ("no floating-rate term loans outstanding" at FY25Q3), a first 30-year tranche was issued January 2026, and the two newest note series dropped the change-of-control put — followed within two quarters by backstop and guarantee obligations entering the same risk factor. In FY26Q3, $5,641M of notes were retired and aggregate indebtedness fell to $61,079M, the series low. The off-balance-sheet $29B Backstop and possible $42B of customer notes grew alongside, and the credit-rating factor now names backstops as a possible cause of a downgrade.

5. The Tax Regime Shifted From Near-Zero to Structural

Implied quarterly rates ran ~0–5% through FY25H1 (excluding the one-time $4,238M IP-onshoring charge). In FY25Q2 the risk factors quietly dropped "we expect an increase in our effective tax rate and our cash tax costs" from U.S. tax reform, one quarter before the tax law changed. From FY25Q3: OBBBA produced a $1,058M CAMT valuation allowance, Pillar Two language escalated from "may materially increase" to "have materially increased," and FY26Q1–Q2 implied rates settled at 8–10% with a full CAMT allowance maintained. FY26Q3 broke that range at 14.3%, and the CAMT expectation sentences were deleted without explanation.

6. VMware Was Fully Absorbed, Then Its Contract Terms Became a Revenue Lever

The integration arc ran from "we plan to focus" (FY24Q1) through tense shifts (FY24Q2), the EUC sale (FY24Q3), the "acquisition of VMware" rename and VCF attribution (FY25Q1), the admission that lifecycle changes "have previously led to" customer dissatisfaction (FY25Q2), the liquidity sentence dropping "assumed" (FY25Q3), the $7.8B upfront license reclassification (FY2025 10-K), the Backlog lawsuit's removal (FY26Q1), and deletion of the last channel-dependence sentence (FY26Q2). In FY26Q3, termination for convenience was dropped from "the majority of new software contracts." This let license revenue be recognized upfront ($3,465M vs $1,964M in Q2) and lifted software growth from 9% to 29% year over year. Part of that jump reflects when revenue is recognized rather than demand alone.
