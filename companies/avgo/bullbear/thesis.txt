AVGO (Broadcom) INVESTMENT THESIS: PILLARS
==========================================
Written October 2026 from the 10-K dashboard themes and tracked signals (AI
language, VMware arc, customer concentration, AI-rack purchase and lease arc,
debt and capital returns, tax arc). Extended the same month to cover every
10-K and 10-Q filed since October 2024: FY2024 10-K, Q1-Q3 FY2025 10-Qs,
FY2025 10-K, Q1-Q3 FY2026 10-Qs. Stances follow BULLBEAR.txt: a stance moves
only when findings point the same way across two or more filings. Starting
stance for every pillar at the FY2024 10-K: Intact.

P1 AI semiconductor growth
  Bull assumes: custom AI accelerators (XPUs) and AI networking keep growing
    for years on multi-year contracts with hyperscalers and frontier AI labs;
    backlog (RPO) converts to revenue.
  Bear fears: AI spending is a cycle or a "bubble"; customers design their own
    silicon ("customer-owned tooling"); growth depends on financing Broadcom
    itself arranges.
  Stance: Strengthening, moved at the Q3 FY2025 10-Q. Semiconductor growth
    went +7% (FY2024), +11% (Q1 FY2025), +17% (Q2), +26% (Q3, the first
    filing to name custom AI accelerators as the driver), then +52%, +79% and
    +127% in FY2026. RPO $20.5B -> $179.2B over the window. Against it: the
    next-12-month share of RPO fell from 43% to 25%, and the "may not be
    sustainable" language (FY2024) became an AI "bubble" (Q3 FY2026).
  Tested by: semiconductor segment revenue and operating income, RPO and its
    next-12-month share, customer-count language, the "bubble" risk factor.

P2 Customer concentration and counterparty credit
  Bull assumes: the few large customers are creditworthy hyperscalers, and
    concentration is the price of winning the biggest AI programs.
  Bear fears: one distributor and five end customers are most of revenue; some
    AI customers "may be unable to pay"; Broadcom takes their credit risk.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the FY2025 10-K (after Q3 FY2025 and FY2025
    both showed the distributor share rising to 32% and new wording on AI
    customers' ability to pay). It kept deteriorating: distributor 42%, 42%
    and 50% of quarterly revenue in FY2026; top five ~50%, ~45%, ~55%.
  Tested by: distributor and top-five percentages, credit-risk wording,
    backstop payments, receivable allowances, DSO.

P3 Infrastructure software (VMware) franchise
  Bull assumes: VCF subscriptions give durable, high-margin, growing revenue
    with integration costs behind it.
  Bear fears: growth is pulled forward by upfront license recognition on
    non-terminable contracts; customer pushback and regulators (Korea, Japan,
    EU) constrain pricing.
  Stance: Intact (never moved). Software margin rose from 65% (FY2024) to
    84% (Q3 FY2026) and restructuring finished on schedule, but growth swung
    with recognition timing: +47% (Q1 FY2025) down to +1% (Q1 FY2026), back
    to +29% (Q3 FY2026) with upfront license 40% of software revenue. The
    findings have not pointed one way for two filings running.
  Tested by: software segment revenue and margin, upfront license revenue,
    contract assets, termination-for-convenience share, regulatory language.

P4 Margins and operating leverage
  Bull assumes: revenue grows far faster than opex; gross margin holds near
    two-thirds.
  Bear fears: rack and system sales "will likely ... compress or lower future
    gross margin"; semiconductor mix lowers gross margin.
  Stance: Strengthening, moved at the Q2 FY2025 10-Q (Q1 and Q2 FY2025 both
    showed gross margin 68% vs 62% and operating margin roughly doubled on
    lower VMware headcount). Operating margin went 26% (FY2024) -> 40%
    (FY2025) -> 54% (Q3 FY2026). The compression warning widened in Q2 FY2026
    to all AI product sales, but gross margin has held at 68-69%.
  Tested by: gross and operating margin, segment margins, R&D and SG&A trend.

P5 Cash conversion and capital returns
  Bull assumes: free cash flow at or above net income funds a rising dividend
    and buybacks without new debt.
  Bear fears: receivables and contract assets grow faster than revenue;
    factoring and sell-to-cover flatter cash flow; buybacks stop or are
    debt-funded; stock compensation dilutes.
  Stance: Intact (never moved). FCF exceeded net income in every filing
    (104%-170% of NI in the 10-Qs), and the dividend rose from $0.59 to $0.65.
    Against it: DSO rose from 30 to 44 days, FY2024 payouts were 114% of FCF,
    the Q1 FY2026 buyback took payouts to 137% of FCF while debt rose, and
    the Two-Year Equity Awards (Q2 FY2025) nearly tripled unrecognized SBC.
  Tested by: OCF / net income, FCF, DSO, factoring volumes, buybacks,
    dividends, SBC / OCF, diluted share count.

P6 Balance sheet and contingent exposure
  Bull assumes: VMware debt is paid down from cash flow; liquidity is ample.
  Bear fears: off-balance-sheet obligations (lease backstops, customer
    convertible notes, $100B+ purchase commitments) replace on-balance-sheet
    debt as the risk.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q2 FY2026 10-Q (Q1 FY2026 added guarantee
    language to the risk factors and liquidity section; Q2 FY2026 quantified
    a $29B backstop and $128.1B of purchase commitments; Q3 FY2026 added a
    $42B convertible-note facility). Reported debt did the opposite:
    $69,847M (FY2024) -> $61,079M (Q3 FY2026), with every floating-rate term
    loan refinanced or repaid by Q3 FY2025.
  Tested by: total debt, backstop exposure and payments, notes issued,
    purchase commitments, rating language.

P7 Tax rate
  Bull assumes: Singapore incentives and U.S. planning keep the effective
    rate low.
  Bear fears: Pillar Two and CAMT push the rate up materially from FY2026.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q1 FY2026 10-Q (the FY2025 10-K expected a
    material increase; Q1 FY2026 says the global minimum tax "has materially
    increased" the provision). Earlier: a one-off 37.8% FY2024 rate (IP
    transfer), a $1,058M CAMT allowance in Q3 FY2025, then a -1.7% FY2025 rate
    on statute expirations. Quarterly rates in FY2026: 10.3%, 8.1%, 14.3%;
    $2,186M global minimum tax payable at Q3 FY2026.
  Tested by: effective tax rate, global minimum tax payable, CAMT valuation
    allowance, incentive value.
