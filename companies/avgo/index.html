FY2018 → FY2019: CA Integration

Filed Dec 2019 | Employees: ~15,000 → ~21,000 (+40%) | Revenue: $20.8B → $22.6B (+9%) | Segments: 4 → 3

Business Overview

▶Business Strategy section added for the first timeNew
FY2018 had no dedicated strategy section. FY2019 introduced the formulation that would persist for five years:
"Our strategy is to combine best-of-breed technology leadership in semiconductor and infrastructure software solutions, with unmatched scale, on a common sales and administrative platform to deliver a comprehensive suite of infrastructure technology products to the world's leading business and government customers. We seek to achieve this through responsibly financed acquisitions of category-leading businesses and technologies, as well as investing extensively in research and development, to ensure our products retain their technology leadership."
Acquisitions listed first, R&D second. This ordering would reverse in FY2025.
▶Segments consolidated from 4 to 3; CA software integratedChanged
FY2018 reported four segments: Wired Infrastructure, Wireless Communications, Enterprise Storage, and Industrial & Other. FY2019 collapsed these into three: Semiconductor Solutions, Infrastructure Software, and IP Licensing. The CA mainframe and enterprise software businesses were folded into Infrastructure Software alongside the Brocade FC SAN business.
▶Symantec enterprise security acquisition announced ($10.7B)New
FY2019 disclosed the pending acquisition of Symantec's Enterprise Security business for approximately $10.7 billion in cash. The company origin history expanded to include "Symantec Corporation" alongside HP, AT&T, LSI, BRCM, Brocade, and CA.
▶Enterprise-wide license model replacing perpetual per-seatChanged
FY2019 described the shift in CA's contracting model from a perpetual per-seat license to an enterprise-wide license model with at-will termination. Revenue recognized ratably over time rather than upfront. This became Broadcom's standard software licensing approach for all acquired businesses.

FY2019 → FY2020: Symantec Added

Filed Dec 2020 | Employees: ~21,000 → ~21,000 (flat) | Revenue: $22.6B → $23.9B (+6%) | Segments: 3 → 2

Business Overview

▶Segments consolidated from 3 to 2; IP licensing absorbedChanged
FY2019 had three segments: Semiconductor Solutions, Infrastructure Software, and IP Licensing. FY2020 eliminated IP Licensing as a standalone segment, folding it into Semiconductor Solutions. The company now reported two segments that would persist through FY2025.
▶BizOps and AIOps terminology introducedNew
FY2020 introduced "BizOps" as a software portfolio category and "AIOps" as a solution category within the infrastructure software segment:
"Our infrastructure software segment includes our mainframe, BizOps and cyber security software solutions, and our FC SAN business."
These terms reflected Broadcom's rebranding of acquired CA and Symantec capabilities into enterprise-oriented product lines.
▶Foundry list reduced to TSMC onlyChanged
FY2019: "...outsourced to external foundries, including Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited ('TSMC'), primarily, as well as United Microelectronics Corporation, GlobalFoundries, Silicon Manufacturing Partners Pte. Ltd., Tower Semiconductor Ltd. and WIN Semiconductors Corp."
FY2020: "...outsourced to external foundries, including Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited ('TSMC')."
Five additional foundry names removed in a single year. SMIC had already been dropped between FY2018 and FY2019.
▶China trade language added to Governmental RegulationNew
FY2020 added specific language about trade tensions:
"These laws, regulations and orders are complex, may change frequently and with limited notice, have generally become more stringent over time, and have intensified under the current U.S. administration, especially in light of ongoing trade tensions with China."
This language did not appear in FY2019.

FY2020 → FY2021: Flat Efficiency

Filed Dec 2021 | Employees: ~21,000 → ~20,000 (-5%) | Revenue: $23.9B → $27.5B (+15%) | Voluntary Attrition: 7%

Business Overview

▶Headcount declined while revenue grew 15%Signal
Broadcom grew revenue from $23.9B to $27.5B while reducing headcount by approximately 1,000 employees. This established the operating model of flat headcount with rising revenue that would persist through FY2023. R&D workforce remained at approximately 63% of total headcount.
▶China trade language removed from Governmental RegulationRemoved
FY2020: "...have generally become more stringent over time, and have intensified under the current U.S. administration, especially in light of ongoing trade tensions with China."
FY2021: "...have generally become more stringent over time."
The explicit China reference and "current U.S. administration" language were removed, coinciding with the change in U.S. presidential administration.
▶Patent portfolio pruned from ~23K to ~19KChanged
FY2020 reported 22,774 U.S. and other patents. FY2021 reported 19,170 -- a reduction of approximately 3,600 patents. Pending applications also declined from 1,018 to 511. This represented an active pruning of the IP portfolio, shedding lower-value patents acquired through acquisitions.

FY2021 → FY2022: VMware Announced

Filed Dec 2022 | Employees: ~20,000 → ~20,000 (flat) | Revenue: $27.5B → $33.2B (+21%) | Voluntary Attrition: 7% → 6.5%

Business Overview

▶VMware acquisition announced at $61BNew
FY2022 disclosed the pending acquisition of VMware for approximately $61 billion in cash and stock. The filing described VMware as a provider of multi-cloud services for all apps. This dwarfed the prior CA ($18.8B) and Symantec ($10.7B) deals combined.
▶First AI mention in filing history -- Symantec contextNew
FY2022 contained the first use of "artificial intelligence" in a Broadcom 10-K Business section:
"Our Symantec cyber security software solutions help organizations and governments secure against threats and compliance risks... Our Symantec solutions utilize rich threat intelligence from a global network of security engineers, threat analyst and researchers, as well as advanced artificial intelligence ('AI') and machine-learning engines"
AI appeared only in the cybersecurity context, not as a standalone strategic theme.
▶Patent portfolio at trough: 17KChanged
FY2022 reported 17,035 patents, down from 19,170 in FY2021. The portfolio was at its lowest point in the 8-year period. Pending applications increased from 511 to 618, indicating new filings were underway even as legacy patents were shed.
▶Revenue grew 21% with flat headcount; gross margin expanded to 67%Signal
Revenue increased from $27.5B to $33.2B while headcount remained at approximately 20,000. Gross margin expanded from approximately 61% to 67%. This was the third consecutive year of flat headcount with double-digit revenue growth.

FY2022 → FY2023: Pre-VMware Peak

Filed Dec 2023 | Employees: ~20,000 → ~20,000 (flat) | Revenue: $33.2B → $35.8B (+8%) | Voluntary Attrition: 6.5% → 3.3%

Business Overview

▶VMware closes post fiscal year-end; integration risks disclosedNew
VMware acquisition closed on November 22, 2023, after the FY2023 fiscal year-end (October 29, 2023). The FY2023 filing disclosed extensive VMware integration risks. The FY2023 financials do not include VMware revenue.
▶"Beyond Code" programs added to mainframe softwareNew
FY2023 introduced "Beyond Code" programs within the mainframe software portfolio, described as helping customers get the most out of their mainframe investments through education, expert guidance, and efficiency improvements. This reflected a services-oriented approach to mainframe customer retention.
▶Voluntary attrition dropped to 3.3%Signal
"Our global voluntary attrition rate in fiscal year 2023 was approximately 3.3%, well below the technology industry benchmark"
Down from 6.5% in FY2022. The qualifier changed from "below" to "well below" the industry benchmark. This was the lowest attrition rate in the filing history.
▶Apple disclosed at ~20% for the last timeSignal
"We believe aggregate sales to Apple Inc., through all channels, accounted for approximately 20% of our net revenue for each of fiscal years 2023 and 2022."
Apple had been disclosed at approximately 25% in FY2018, then ~20% in subsequent years. This was the last year Apple would be named in a Broadcom filing.

FY2023 → FY2024: VMware Lands, AI Enters the Risk Factors

Filed Dec 20, 2024 | Revenue: $35.8B → $51.6B (+44%) | Operating income: $16.2B → $13.5B | Net income: $14.1B → $5.9B | Employees: ~20,000 → ~37,000 | Semi/SW Mix: 79%/21% → 58%/42%

Business

▶Strategy drops "responsibly financed" and the "unmatched scale, on a common sales and administrative platform" model; acquisitions still listed firstChanged
FY2023: "Our strategy is to combine best-of-breed technology leadership in semiconductor and infrastructure software solutions, with unmatched scale, on a common sales and administrative platform ... We seek to achieve this through responsibly financed acquisitions of category-leading businesses and technologies, as well as investing extensively in research and development"
FY2024: "Our strategy is focused on technology leadership and category-leading semiconductor and infrastructure software solutions delivering a comprehensive suite of innovative infrastructure technology products ... We seek to achieve this through strategic acquisitions of businesses and technologies, as well as extensive internal research and development"
The financing qualifier was removed in the year Broadcom borrowed $30,390M of term loans for VMware. Aggregate indebtedness went from $40,815M (senior notes only) to $69,847M. "Category-leading" now describes Broadcom's own products rather than the companies it buys. The shared-platform description of the acquisition model, which had stood since FY2019, is gone.
▶Apple's ~20% disclosure deleted; top five end customers ~35% → ~40%; largest distributor 21% → 28% and no longer namedRemoved
FY2023: "We believe aggregate sales to Apple Inc., through all channels, accounted for approximately 20% of our net revenue for each of fiscal years 2023 and 2022."
FY2024: "We believe aggregate sales to our top five end customers, through all channels, accounted for approximately 40% and 35% of our net revenue for fiscal years 2024 and 2023, respectively."
MD&A also stops naming WT Microelectronics. It now says "one customer, which is a distributor" accounted for 28% of revenue, against 21% in FY2023. The top-customer risk factor adds "including our hyperscale customers." Concentration rose while the named disclosures went away. The filing gives no reason for dropping Apple. Our arithmetic: if Apple sales had stayed at ~20% of FY2023 revenue (about $7.2B), they would still be about 14% of FY2024 revenue, so VMware's added revenue alone does not explain the omission.
▶Custom silicon retargeted from 5G and compute offload to "AI and high-performance computing"; AI mentions in Item 1 rise from 4 to 23Changed
FY2023: "...targeting data center compute offload, legacy and new 5G radio infrastructure, and wired communication networks."
FY2024: "...targeting AI and high-performance computing, networking and storage applications."
A new networking paragraph pitches Ethernet NICs and switching "to resolve connectivity bottlenecks in data centers, particularly in AI data centers where compute bandwidth and cluster sizes grow rapidly." AI is also added to the end-market list ("including artificial intelligence ... networking and connectivity"). The custom ASIC business, which became the XPU franchise, is described as AI-first for the first time.
▶VMware's portfolios added (Private Cloud, Tanzu, VeloCloud, Application Networking and Security); VCF sold as subscriptions with "license portability"New
FY2024: "...provides license portability, which enables customers to purchase subscriptions of VCF software and move their VCF environments between on-premises data centers and supported cloud endpoints."
The software section grows to nine portfolio headings. Four are new from VMware, alongside Mainframe, Distributed, Enterprise Security, FC SAN Management and Payment Security. The FY2023 sentence "All discussions ... relate solely to our operations prior to the VMware Merger" is deleted. The 10-K also discloses the $3.5B sale of VMware's end-user computing business to KKR.

Risk Factors

▶"Currently experiencing a downturn" becomes an AI upturn with "record revenue, which may not be sustainable"Changed
FY2023: "The industry, however is currently experiencing a downturn, and historically, such down-cycles have been characterized by diminished demand for end-user products"
FY2024: "The market for AI-related products has resulted in a significant upturn in certain segments of the industry resulting in record revenue, which may not be sustainable."
The cyclicality factor goes from describing a downturn to describing an AI-led upturn in one year, and the first description of the boom already says it may not last. The same factor adds "evolving markets such as AI." A new demand-forecasting risk names "demand for AI-related products."
▶"Custom AI accelerators or XPUs" named for the first time; AI design wins "may strain our resources," with extra costs "which we expect to continue"New
FY2023: "...implementing production for a major design win, or multiple design wins at the same time, may strain our resources and those of our CMs."
FY2024: "...such as our design wins for our custom AI accelerators or XPUs and other AI-related products, may strain our resources and those of our CMs. In such event, we may be forced to dedicate significant additional resources such as product engineering and incur additional costs and expenses, which we expect to continue for our AI-related products."
"XPU" appears nowhere in the FY2023 10-K. The customer factor adds that customers may buy less "due to their internal development of the products." The stock-price factor adds "demand for AI-related products, including ASICs such as custom AI accelerators or XPUs" as a driver. A generic design-win risk now has a named product and a cost Broadcom expects to keep paying.
▶VMware risk names the "transition from a perpetual to a subscription licensing model"; "we may lose customers" becomes "significant customers"Changed
FY2023: "If VMware customers do not accept this plan, the investments we have made or may make to implement this plan may be of no or limited value, we may lose customers"
FY2024: "If VMware customers do not accept our business strategy, including our transition from a perpetual to a subscription licensing model and our simplified product portfolio, the investments we have made or may make to implement our strategy may be of no or limited value, we may lose significant customers"
The licensing change is acknowledged as a customer-retention risk, and the possible losses are upgraded to "significant." The seven-bullet integration checklist in FY2023 (preserving VMware relationships, integrating controls and so on) is deleted. The risk now centers on whether customers accept the new model, not on whether Broadcom can integrate VMware.
▶Termination-for-convenience clauses enter the risk factors, along with contracts that lack themNew
FY2024: "These customers often do not have a contractual obligation to purchase additional solutions and often have termination for convenience clauses without payment of a substantive penalty."
FY2024: "...or software license agreements without termination for convenience clauses could cause our operating results to fluctuate."
FY2023 mentioned these clauses only in the revenue note. Here the risk factors cover both sides: customers who can walk away, and contracts without that right, which shift revenue timing. This is the mechanism behind FY2025's $7,800M of upfront license revenue. The operating-results list also adds "the timing of software contracts that do not have termination for convenience clauses."
▶Huawei export-license example deleted as China's share of revenue falls from 32% to 20%Removed
FY2023: "For example, Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd., one of our customers, is subject to certain U.S. export restrictions, which has required us to suspend sales to Huawei until we obtain licenses from the U.S. Department of Commerce."
FY2024 (MD&A): "In fiscal years 2024 and 2023, 20% and 32%, respectively, of our net revenue came from shipments or deliveries to China (including Hong Kong)."
The only named export-control example is removed. The restricted-entity sentence changes from "may also add" to "may continue to add." China-bound shipments drop 12 points in a year when total revenue rose 44%, so part of the drop reflects VMware's revenue mix.
▶Debt-servicing, covenant and buyback risk factors deleted as indebtedness rises from $40,815M to $69,847MRemoved
FY2023: "Servicing our debt requires a significant amount of cash, and we may not have sufficient cash flow from our business to pay our substantial debt."
FY2023: "As of October 29, 2023, the aggregate indebtedness under our senior notes was $40,815 million."
FY2024: "As of November 3, 2024, the aggregate indebtedness was $69,847 million."
Three standalone factors are removed: debt servicing, indenture covenants, and "The amount and frequency of our stock repurchases may fluctuate." So is the large-holders factor. Their content is folded into a shorter indebtedness list, and the risk section shrinks from 16,411 to 12,744 words. Some of this is consolidation. Still, the factor saying Broadcom might not generate enough cash to pay its debt was dropped in the year debt rose 71%. The buyback factor returns in FY2025.
▶Singapore minimum tax dated to FY2026, while the Singapore incentive's expiry moves from November 2025 to November 2030Changed
FY2023 (MD&A): "These Singapore tax incentives are presently expected to expire in November 2025."
FY2024 (MD&A): "These Singapore tax incentives are scheduled to expire in November 2030."
FY2024: "For example, Singapore recently adopted the global minimum tax, which will be effective for our fiscal year 2026."
The incentive was extended five years in the same filing that says a minimum tax will cut into it from FY2026. The risk factor also drops the FY2023 detail that the Singapore rate without the incentive "would be 17%" and the Malaysia holiday's 2028 expiry, though both remain in MD&A. FY2025 escalates this to "we expect to materially increase," and Q1 FY2026 to "have materially increased."
▶TSMC's share of contract-manufactured wafers: ~90% → ~95%Changed
FY2023: "TSMC, one of our CMs, manufactured approximately 90% of the wafers manufactured by our CMs during fiscal year 2023."
FY2024: "TSMC, one of our CMs, manufactured approximately 95% of the wafers manufactured by our CMs during fiscal year 2024."
Single-foundry dependence rises as AI silicon grows. The CM factor adds that qualifying a new CM is "a lengthy and often expensive process." The disruption factor adds that suppliers are "concentrated in the same geographic regions." Every later filing in the set holds ~95%.

MD&A

▶Tax provision $1,015M → $3,748M from moving IP rights to the United States; net income falls $14.1B → $5.9B on 44% higher revenueSignal
FY2024: "The increase was primarily due to the impact of a non-recurring intra-group transfer of certain IP rights to the United States as a result of supply chain realignment and the resulting shift in the jurisdictional mix of income."
The IP onshoring is labeled "non-recurring," but it moves future income into the U.S. tax base. That fits the FY2025 risk factor stating that "Our global income is subject to tax in the U.S." Restructuring charges in operating expenses rose from $244M to $1,533M, "primarily ... employee termination costs" from VMware, and VMware amortization pushed gross margin from 69% to 63%.

FY2024 → FY2025: AI Customers Become a Credit Risk

Filed Dec 2025 | Revenue: $51.6B → $63.9B (+24%) | Operating income: $13.5B → $25.5B | Net income: $5.9B → $23.1B | Employees: ~37,000 → ~33,000 (-11%) | Semi/SW Mix: 58%/42% → 58%/42% | Voluntary Attrition: 2.9%/6.2% → 4.1%

Risk Factors

▶Lead risk rewritten: an industry "undergoing profound change due to AI" whose customers "may be unable to pay for their required AI infrastructure"New
FY2024: "We operate in the highly cyclical semiconductor industry."
FY2025: "We operate in a highly cyclical semiconductor industry that is undergoing profound change due to AI."
FY2025: "Some of these AI customers may have constrained resources or capital and may be unable to pay for their required AI infrastructure and/or seek alternative financings or novel or deferred payment models from their vendors and suppliers."
This is the first annual report to say Broadcom's AI customers may not be able to pay. The same paragraph adds that they may be "unable to generate the profit required to offset their spending." The warning arrives in the year semiconductor revenue grew 22% on "custom AI accelerators and AI networking products." The FY2024 sentence on AI "record revenue, which may not be sustainable" becomes an AI upturn "which may not be sustainable."
▶Selling or leasing "AI racks or systems based on our XPUs" with "novel or deferred payment models" enters the annual report; customers "have made" lease demandsNew
FY2024: "...our top customers' purchasing power has, in some cases, given them the ability to make greater demands on us with regard to pricing and contractual terms in general."
FY2025: "...our top customers, including our AI customers, may make and have made greater demands on us with regards to pricing and contractual terms, such as seeking to lease AI racks or systems based on our XPUs instead of purchasing"
FY2025: "In such event, we may dedicate significant additional resources or execute on new business strategies or models such as the sale or leasing of AI racks or systems based on our XPUs to our customers with alternative financings or novel or deferred payment models, which could result in additional costs, expenses, credit or customer default risks, reduced gross margin and cash flows."
The language first appeared in the Q3 FY2025 10-Q. This is its first appearance in a 10-K. "Have made" says customers have already asked. Within two quarters the 10-Qs report "We have dedicated" resources (Q1 FY2026) and a $29B lease backstop (Q2 FY2026). The R&D summary risk is retitled to cover "expansion of our business strategy or adoption of new business models."
▶Gross-margin factor: racks "will likely ... compress or lower future gross margin, which would adversely impact our stock price"; the "robust demand ... higher gross margins" sentence is deletedChanged
FY2024: "Conversely, periods of robust demand that create a supply imbalance can lead to higher gross margins that may not be sustainable over the longer term."
FY2025: "The sale or leasing of AI racks or systems based on our XPUs will likely increase our operating margin but compress or lower future gross margin, which would adversely impact our stock price."
A risk factor that predicts its own effect on the stock price is unusual. The factor also adds "adoption of a new business model" to the drivers of gross margin. It states that semiconductor gross margin "has typically been lower than our infrastructure software solutions." Reported gross margin rose to 68% from 63%, so the warning concerns a mix that had not yet shown up in the numbers.
▶First tariff materiality statement: tariffs "have not had a material impact"New
FY2025: "While U.S. tariffs and counter-tariffs, including semiconductor-related tariffs, have not had a material impact on our financial condition or results of operations, tariffs and other macroeconomic factors could materially increase costs and disrupt our supply chain."
The macro factor is rewritten around tariffs and "onshoring of semiconductor manufacturing." China-specific phrasing ("harm our ability to participate in Chinese markets") is generalized to "some markets." The export-control factor adds that Broadcom may have to suspend "purchasing from such suppliers" as well as selling to customers. China's share of revenue fell further, from 20% to 17%.
▶Antitrust exposure widens from one named Korean investigation to inquiries in Korea, Japan and the European UnionChanged
FY2024: "...such as the ongoing investigation by the Korean Fair Trade Commission into certain of our contracting and business practices"
FY2025: "...regulatory investigations or inquiries from regulatory authorities in Korea, Japan and the European Union into certain of our contracting and business practices, which have and may in the future evolve into legal or other administrative proceedings."
Two more jurisdictions are now looking at the same "contracting and business practices." "Anti-competition" becomes "antitrust," and the factor adds that the technology industry faces "intense media, political and regulatory scrutiny." Legal Proceedings still incorporates the notes by reference and quantifies nothing.
▶Pillar Two moves from "may materially increase" to "we expect to materially increase" in FY2026; OBBBA forces a $1,321M CAMT valuation allowanceChanged
FY2024: "Many countries have implemented or are in the process of implementing a global minimum tax, which may materially increase our effective tax rate and cash tax costs."
FY2025: "Many countries have implemented or are in the process of implementing a global minimum tax, which we expect to materially increase our effective tax rate and cash tax costs for our fiscal year ending November 1, 2026."
FY2025 (MD&A): "...it is no longer more-likely-than-not that we are able to utilize our federal corporate alternative minimum tax ... credits, and we established a $1,321 million valuation allowance against our CAMT credit carryforwards and CAMT credits generated in the current fiscal year."
A possibility becomes an expectation with a date. MD&A adds: "we expect a material impact ... for our fiscal year 2026." Even so, FY2025 shows a $397M tax benefit, driven by expiring statutes of limitations and stock-award deductions. Tax incentives were worth $2,709M, up from $2,261M. That figure is the one Q1 FY2026 deletes.

Business

▶Broadcom now sells "semiconductor-based solutions" up to "racks," to "hyperscalers and companies with AI frontier models"Changed
FY2024: "We are a global technology leader that designs, develops and supplies a broad range of semiconductor and infrastructure software solutions."
FY2025: "We are a global technology leader that designs, develops and supplies a broad range of semiconductor and semiconductor-based solutions and infrastructure software solutions."
FY2025: "Customers of these solutions are hyperscalers and companies with AI frontier models, as well as original equipment manufacturers ... and system integrators that develop servers, switches and racks deployed in large-scale AI data centers to run training and inference workloads. Our AI semiconductor solutions include custom accelerators or XPUs, Ethernet switching and routing silicon, Ethernet NICs, physical layer devices ... and optical components, as well as racks and systems based on our XPUs."
The opening sentence changes for the first time since FY2018. "Semiconductor-based solutions" is defined to include "network interface cards ... and other modules, switches, subsystems and, in some cases, racks." The ASIC paragraph adds that XPUs are built "for hyperscalers, companies with AI frontier models and system integrators, and in some cases used in racks or systems." Racks appear in the business description in the same filing whose risk factors cover leasing them.
▶Customers named as future competitors; all 56 named competitors deletedRemoved
FY2024: "In semiconductor solutions, we compete with integrated device manufacturers, fabless semiconductor companies and the internal resources of large integrated OEMs, such as Advanced Micro Devices, Inc., Amlogic Inc., Analog Devices, Inc., Cisco Systems, Inc."
FY2025: "Competitors in semiconductor solutions include integrated device manufacturers, fabless semiconductor companies and the internal resources of large integrated OEMs."
FY2025: "We also expect competition in these markets to continue to increase as existing competitors improve or expand their product offerings and as new companies, including some of our customers, enter the markets."
FY2024 named 33 semiconductor competitors (NVIDIA among them) and 23 software competitors. FY2025 names none. In their place the business section and the competition risk factor ("new competitors, including our customers") both warn about customers. That is consistent with hyperscalers developing their own silicon, the same risk Q1 FY2026 later calls "customer-owned tooling."
▶Strategy reordered: R&D listed before acquisitions for the first time since FY2019Changed
FY2024: "We seek to achieve this through strategic acquisitions of businesses and technologies, as well as extensive internal research and development, to ensure our products retain their technology market leadership."
FY2025: "We seek to achieve this through extensive internal research and development, as well as strategic acquisitions of businesses and technologies, to ensure our products retain their technology market leadership."
The strategy sentence also adds "sustained" technology leadership. The VMware deal paragraph is deleted from Item 1 entirely. The ordering had been the same since the strategy section began in FY2019. The acquisitions risk factor still says "we have pursued, and may in the future pursue," so acquisitions are listed second, not dropped.
▶Seasonality section deleted; "smartphones" becomes "wireless devices"Removed
FY2024: "Historically, our net revenue has typically been higher in the second half of the fiscal year than in the first half, primarily due to seasonality in our wireless communications products."
The whole section goes. MD&A's cross-reference changes from "the effects of seasonality ... under Seasonality" to Item 1A, and the gross-margin factor drops "susceptible to seasonal ... fluctuations." The phone-launch cycle that set the pattern in the Apple years no longer describes Broadcom's revenue timing. MD&A instead points to "customer deployments" and, for software, whether customers can terminate (our inference).
▶Nine software portfolio headings collapse to "five major" portfolios; VeloCloud and Payment Security disappearRemoved
FY2025: "We offer five major infrastructure software portfolios: Private Cloud, Mainframe Software, Cybersecurity, Enterprise Software and FC SAN Management."
Tanzu and Application Networking and Security are folded into Private Cloud as VCF "advanced services." VeloCloud and the Arcot payment-security portfolio are no longer mentioned anywhere in the 10-K. MD&A reports "a gain on the sale of a business" in other income without naming it. The filing does not say whether that business was either of these.
▶Headcount ~37,000 → ~33,000; Czech Republic and Israel dropped from the main R&D locationsChanged
FY2024: "...a significant majority of our research and development personnel are located in the Czech Republic, India, Israel, and the U.S., and our primary warehouse is in Malaysia."
FY2025: "...a significant majority of our research and development personnel are located in the U.S. and India, and our primary warehouse is in Malaysia."
R&D share went from 55% to 57%. By our arithmetic that is roughly 20,400 to 18,800 R&D staff and 16,700 to 14,200 others, so both groups shrank, non-R&D more. SG&A fell $748M on "lower compensation resulting from a decrease in headcount." Attrition is now reported as a single 4.1% rate, where FY2024 gave 2.9% excluding and 6.2% including VMware staff.

MD&A / Software Revenue

▶$7,800M of upfront license revenue now reported in products; $4,601M of FY2024 reclassifiedNew
FY2025: "In fiscal year 2025, we included upfront license revenue of $7,800 million within products revenue."

Themes

VMware Transformation

Revenue mix shifted from 79% semiconductor / 21% software (FY2023) to 58% / 42% (FY2024) and held there in FY2025, when AI semiconductors and software grew at similar rates (+22% and +26%). Software revenue grew from $7.6B to $27.0B in two years. By FY2025 VMware was fully absorbed: the integration risk factor and the deal paragraph are gone, and the nine FY2024 software portfolios are folded into five.

AI Networking

AI language went from 0 mentions in the Business section (FY2018-FY2021) to a central narrative (FY2024-FY2025). The risk factors set the pace. FY2024 named "custom AI accelerators or XPUs" and called AI-driven record revenue possibly unsustainable. FY2025 says AI customers "may be unable to pay," adds selling or leasing XPU-based racks with "novel or deferred payment models," and warns this would "compress or lower future gross margin." FY2025 also names hyperscalers and "companies with AI frontier models" as customers, and the same customers as future competitors.

Acquisition Playbook

Headcount held flat at ~20,000 for four years (FY2020-FY2023) while revenue grew 50%. VMware added ~17,000 employees. Within two years, ~4,000 were cut. R&D share of headcount increased from 55% to 57% during the reduction. Strategy language shifted from acquisitions-first (FY2019-FY2024) to R&D-first (FY2025).

Semiconductor to Systems

Self-description evolved from "semiconductor and infrastructure software solutions" (FY2018-FY2024) to "semiconductor and semiconductor-based solutions" (FY2025). Product portfolio expanded from discrete chips to include NICs, modules, switches, subsystems, and racks. XPUs and systems-level AI data center solutions became named product categories.

Software Licensing

Perpetual per-seat licensing (CA legacy) was converted to enterprise-wide at-will contracts (FY2019). VMware added subscription licensing with license portability across on-premises and cloud endpoints (FY2024). FY2024's risk factors also put termination-for-convenience clauses, and contracts without them, at the center of revenue timing. In FY2025 non-terminable contracts produced $7.8B of upfront license revenue, about 29% of software. That year's risk factor oddly referred to "our perpetual licensing model"; the next 10-Q corrected it to "subscription."

Apple Disappearance

Approximately 20-25% of revenue was attributed to Apple through all channels for six consecutive years (FY2018-FY2023). Apple was removed entirely from the FY2024 filing without explanation, and WT Microelectronics (the largest distributor) was de-named at the same time. Concentration rose anyway: top five end customers went from ~35% to ~40%, and the largest distributor from 21% (FY2023) to 28% (FY2024) to 32% (FY2025). No individual customer has been named since FY2023.
