CVNA 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of ten SEC filings — eight 10-Qs from Q1 2024 (quarter ended March 31, 2024) through Q2 2026 (June 30, 2026), with the FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks interleaved at the year boundaries. Nine transitions covering MD&A (including the forward-looking factor list), Item 1A, and legal proceedings and the Legal Matters note. The annual dashboard tracks the arc from near-death to record units; this scanner tracks the quarter-by-quarter wording behind it: growth displacing profitability in the priority language, a self-funding pledge followed by an equity raise, and unit-economics explanations thinning as gross profit per unit started falling.

MD&A & Strategy

▶ Investment-in-growth paragraph drops "Due to the current macroeconomic environment" and adds a plan to invest in "profitable expansion" Signal

Q1 2024:

"We have historically aggressively invested in the growth of our business. Due to the current macroeconomic environment, we are focused on driving fundamental gains in gross profit per unit and operational efficiency, flexibility, and scalability through process and technology improvements to increase profitability and provide a strong foundation for profitable growth."

Q2 2024:

"While we have historically aggressively invested in the growth of our business, for the past several quarters we have been and continue to be focused on driving fundamental gains in gross profit per unit ... As we continue targeting initiatives aimed at improving efficiencies, we simultaneously plan to invest in the profitable expansion of our business."

The efficiency focus is no longer tied to the macro environment; it is described as a past-tense phase ("for the past several quarters"). The plan to invest is new. Q3 2024 changes "plan to invest" to "are simultaneously investing" (see next transition).

▶ Priority sentence loses "During the current macroeconomic uncertainty"; strategies stop being "Secondarily" and are recast as "efficient growth initiatives" Changed

Q1 2024:

"During the current macroeconomic uncertainty, our highest priority will continue to be providing exceptional customer experiences while improving efficiency and utilizing our infrastructure to support efficient growth in retail units sold to help us move along the path to achieve sustained profitability. Secondarily, we plan to pursue several strategies designed to increase our brand awareness and total gross profit per unit. These strategies may include the following:"

Q2 2024:

"Our highest priority continues to be providing exceptional customer experiences while improving efficiency and utilizing our infrastructure to support efficient growth in retail units sold to help us move along the path to achieve sustained profitability. Efficient growth initiatives, which we may undertake from time to time, include the following:"

The macro qualifier is gone. The bullet list is no longer framed around brand awareness and gross profit per unit; it is framed as growth. "To help us move along the path to achieve sustained profitability" survives until Q2 2025, when it is deleted (tracked in Signal #1).

▶ "Reduce average days to sale" becomes "Optimize": the goal of growing sales faster than inventory is dropped; website units 30,694 → 37,299 Changed

Q1 2024:

"Reduce average days to sale. Our goal is generally to increase our sales at a faster rate than we increase our inventory size, which we believe would decrease average days to sale due to a relative increase in demand versus supply."

Q2 2024:

"Optimize average days to sale. Our goal is generally to optimize our inventory size relative to sales to achieve our desired average days to sale."

The deleted goal was a commitment to keep inventory growing slower than sales. The new wording leaves room for inventory to grow faster than sales. Inventory did: total website units rose from 30,694 (March 31, 2024) to 75,683 at the end of 2025, and 77,971 by June 30, 2026. The same quarter raised the stated reconditioning capacity from "approximately 1.3 million" to "approximately 1.35 million vehicles per year" (Signal #3).

▶ Unique-visitor metric redefined to include app users; prior periods recast upward by 4.2% to 9.6% Changed
"During the three months ended June 30, 2024, the methodology used by Google Analytics to count unique visitors changed to include individuals visiting our iOS/Android application, in addition to those visiting our website."

The recast added 9.6% to Q2 2023 and 4.2% to Q1 2024, so the reported growth rate of a key operating metric changed retroactively. Q2 2024 average monthly unique visitors were 18.6M against a recast 16.0M a year earlier.

The figure went from precise to a floor, and the floor is 350,000 units lower than the prior quarter's figure. No reason is given. Annualized retail sales (about 435,000 at the Q3 2024 run rate) were far below either number, which suggests the change is a restatement of how capacity is counted, not a loss of capacity. That is an inference; the filing does not say.

As of Q2 2026, the language says scale is self-reinforcing, while the reported unit margins have declined for three 10-Qs in a row.

2. Unit-Economics Explanations Thinned as GPU Declined

In six 10-Qs and the FY2024 10-K, every retail GPU change came with a dollar level and a "primarily driven by" cause. That included the first decline in Q3 2025 ("higher retail depreciation rates"). The FY2025 10-K gave no cause. The 2026 10-Qs give neither the level nor the cause, and Q2 2026 extends the same treatment to wholesale GPU. Over the same quarters, loan sale spreads turned from a tailwind into a headwind, and the stated reconditioning capacity disappeared. The disclosure got less specific in the areas where results got worse.

3. The Self-Funding Pledge Was Rewritten After the Raise

Q1 2025 replaced "financing ... has funded growth" with funding "primarily through cash generated from operating activities". The ATM cap was cut from 35M to 21M shares, and two quarters showed no activity. Q3 2025 then sold $539M of stock alongside a $559M secured-note redemption, with the sentence still reading "if necessary". The FY2025 10-K changed it to "if desired". The secured-debt paydown coincided with about $1.8B of gross equity sales in 2024–2025, which the funding sentence does not reflect.

4. Adverse Legal Facts Arrived Late, Then Eased

The January 2025 short-seller report is absent from two filings made after it (FY2024 10-K, Q1 2025 10-Q). It appears in Q2 2025 only together with the SEC subpoena, the same period in which Item 1A stopped asserting "no material changes" and the blanket "without merit" reassurance was deleted. Reserves went from none to $14M, and AG claims from "could" to "have resulted", with "lending practices" added. By Q2 2026, two cases had settled for immaterial amounts and the accrual was back to $10M. The SEC matter and the class action remain open without estimates.

5. Profitability Brought a $2.3B Bill to Insiders

The TRA was first named as a $19M expense in Q2 2024 (a $14M expense in Q3 2023 was disclosed only later), a recognized $82M (with $2.0B unrecorded) in the FY2024 10-K, and a $2.3B liability in the FY2025 10-K, $1.7B of it owed to related parties. The valuation-allowance release behind it added $2.2B to the tax line and raised the effective tax rate from about 0% to 8–11% in 2026. The Q3 2025 10-Q still called the deferred tax assets unlikely to be realized, so the reversal reached the income statement in one quarter. Most of the resulting tax value flows to the controlling holders.
