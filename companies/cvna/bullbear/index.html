Latest filing: 10-Q Q2 2026

What the Q2 2026 10-Q adds, for and against. Volume, SG&A leverage and a clean income statement on one side; a third straight decline in gross profit per unit, thinner loan-sale spreads and a large inventory build on the other.

Bull case

P1 Unit growth and demandStated
Retail units +37.7% to 197,325; revenue +52.4% to $7.38B; website inventory 77,971 units (+38%)
Q2 2026: "During the three months ended June 30, 2026, the number of vehicles we sold to retail customers increased by 37.7% to 197,325, compared to 143,280 in the three months ended June 30, 2025."

Size: 54,045 more units than a year earlier; annualized run rate about 790,000 units, against 596,641 sold in 2025. Average monthly unique visitors 21.8M (+20%).

Growth is still close to 40% at roughly double the 2024 base, which is the core of the bull case. Selection kept pace with sales, so volume was not bought by thinning inventory.

Next filing: Q3 2026 unit growth above 30% against the strong Q3 2025 comparison (155,941 units).

P2 Unit economicsStated
SG&A per retail unit $3,846 → $3,568 (−7%); operating income $511M → $680M (+33%)
Q2 2026: "Total SG&A expenses per retail unit | 3,568 | 3,846 | 3,623 | 3,918"

Size: $278 per unit on 197,325 units = about $55M of quarterly operating cost avoided at last year’s rate; operating income $680M = 9.2% of revenue.

Fixed-cost leverage is still working: SG&A rose 28% while units rose 38%. This is the offset to the gross-profit decline on the bear side, and it is why operating income still grew by a third.

Next filing: SG&A per unit below $3,816 (Q3 2025) while advertising ($121M, +44%) keeps growing.

P4 Earnings qualityStated
A clean quarter: interest expense −$42M, other expense zero (was $60M), no PIK; pre-tax income $308M → $579M
Q2 2026: "Interest expense, net decreased by $42 million to $101 million during the three months ended June 30, 2026 compared to $143 million during the three months ended June 30, 2025"
Q2 2026: "Other expense (income), net was zero during the three months ended June 30, 2026 compared to an expense of $60 million during the three months ended June 30, 2025"

Size: pre-tax income +88%; net income $513M after a $66M tax charge (11.4% rate) that the 2025 quarters did not carry. Root warrant and TRA items, which swung 2025 results by up to $158M a quarter, were near zero.

For the first time in the window, reported earnings are close to operating earnings: interest is paid in cash, the TRA is on the balance sheet, and taxes are booked. Net income growth (+67%) understates pre-tax growth because of the tax line.

Next filing: other expense near zero again and an effective tax rate near 10–12%.

P5 Cash conversion and inventoryStated
H1 operating cash flow $345M vs $261M, without the $146M of PIK interest that propped up H1 2025
Q2 2026: "Cash provided by operating activities was $345 million and $261 million during the six months ended June 30, 2026 and 2025, respectively, an increase in cash provided by operating activities of $84 million"
Q2 2026: "Payment-in-kind interest expense | — | 146"

Size: on a like-for-like cash-interest basis H1 2025 would have been about $115M, so H1 2026 is about 3x; capex $102M leaves free cash flow of about $243M.

Cash generation improved even while inventory absorbed $833M, which supports the FY2025 claim that growth can be funded from operations. Part of the gain is non-cash deferred tax ($98M) and payables (+$362M).

Next filing: nine-month operating cash flow above the $606M of 2025 with no equity issued.

P3 Loan sales and finance profitStated
Ally forward-flow commitment raised to $8.0B through July 2027; a seventh $500M receivables lender added
Q2 2026: "On July 27, 2026, the Company and Ally amended the Ally MPSA to, among other things, increase the commitment by Ally to purchase up to $8.0 billion of principal balances of finance receivables between July 27, 2026 and July 26, 2027"

Size: $8.0B = about 44% of annualized H1 2026 originations ($9.0B in six months); short-term revolving commitments $5.6B (December: $5.0B); fixed-pool capacity $8.7B unused.

Funding for originations is committed for a year at a larger size, on “substantially similar terms”, so the loan-sale engine that produces most of other gross profit is not capacity-constrained.

Next filing: loan-sale volume and gain per unit holding with Ally headroom above $4B.

Bear case

P2 Unit economicsStated
Total gross profit per unit $7,426 → $7,014 (−5.5%); retail −$89, wholesale −$67, with no cause given; Adjusted EBITDA margin 12.4% → 10.4%
Q2 2026: "This increase was driven primarily by an increase in the number of retail vehicles sold to 197,325 from 143,280 during the three months ended June 30, 2026 and 2025, respectively, partially offset by a $89 decrease in retail vehicle gross profit per unit."
Q2 2026: "Adjusted EBITDA margin | 10.4 | 12.4 | 10.4 | 12.0"

Size: $412 per unit on 197,325 units = about $81M of quarterly gross profit; 2 points of margin on $7.38B of revenue = about $148M of Adjusted EBITDA. Operating margin 9.2% vs 10.6%.

This is the third 10-Q in a row with lower total GPU (Q3 2025, Q1 2026, Q2 2026), and the MD&A no longer explains either the retail or the wholesale decline. Growth is now arriving at lower margin per car.

Next filing: total GPU at or above $7,362 (Q3 2025) would refute; a fourth decline confirms the trend.

P3 Loan sales and finance profitStated
Loan sale spreads lower for a second quarter; other gross profit per unit $2,869 → $2,666 (−7.1%)
Q2 2026: "The increase in revenue was primarily due to an increase in gain on loan sales as a result of increased retail units sold and loan sale volume, partially offset by lower loan sale spreads, and to higher VSC and GAP conversion rates during the three months ended June 30, 2026."

Size: gain on loan sales $349M on about $4.7B of principal sold (about 7.4%) vs $274M on about $3.1B (about 8.8%) a year earlier; $203 per unit less other gross profit = about $40M a quarter.

Other gross profit is 100% margin and makes up 38% of gross profit, so a lower premium on loans flows straight to earnings. Spreads were cited as a growth driver as late as the FY2025 10-K (full year).

Next filing: spreads described as stable or higher, or gain per principal dollar back above 8%.

P5 Cash conversion and inventoryStated
Inventory $2.41B → $3.26B in six months (+36%); it absorbed $833M of H1 cash
Q2 2026: "partially offset by an investment in vehicle inventory as we grow retail unit sales and acquire more vehicles from customers."
Q2 2026: "Vehicle inventory | 3,263 | 2,408"

Size: inventory +61% year over year ($2.02B at June 2025) vs units +38%; H1 operating cash flow $345M = 24% of H1 Adjusted EBITDA ($1.441B).

Inventory value is rising faster than both sales and website units (77,971, +38%), so dollars per car on hand are up about 17% (inference: higher prices, new-car dealerships, and more customer-sourced cars). If prices turn, this is the exposure.

Next filing: inventory growth back in line with unit growth, and Q3 operating cash flow above the $345M of Q3 2025.

P3 Loan sales and finance profitStated
Ally bought $2.1B of loans in the quarter (vs $1.2B), about 45% of all loans sold; headroom fell to $1.2B before the renewal
Q2 2026: "During the three months ended June 30, 2026 and 2025, the Company sold $2.1 billion and $1.2 billion, respectively, in principal balances of finance receivables under the Ally MPSA."
Q2 2026: "As of June 30, 2026 and December 31, 2025, we had $1.2 billion and $4.9 billion, respectively, of unused capacity under the Ally MPSA"

Size: Ally $2.1B of about $4.7B sold (securitizations $1.10B, fixed pool $1.5B); H1 Ally share about 42% vs 36% in H1 2025. Ally is also the floor-plan lender.

One buyer is taking a rising share at falling spreads, and the $6.0B commitment would have run out before its October 2026 end. The renewal was disclosed only in the subsequent-events note.

Next filing: Ally share of loan sales back below 40% and securitization volume growing.

P1 Unit growth and demandInferred
Revenue per unit +17.4% to $27,908 on mix and used-car price appreciation; gasoline prices “have pressured” consumers
Q2 2026: "an increase in retail revenue per retail unit sold to $27,908 from $23,765 in the prior year, primarily due to lower retail marketplace units sold as a share of total retail units sold and overall appreciation in the retail market."
Q2 2026: "sustained increases in gasoline prices, including as a result of the conflict in Iran, have pressured consumer disposable income and could reduce their ability to purchase vehicles"

Size: retail revenue +61.7% vs units +37.7%; unit growth has slowed from +43.5% (Q3 2025) to +40.0% and +37.7%.

Revenue growth now overstates volume growth, and price appreciation can reverse. Higher car prices and fuel costs act on the same customer, which is the first stated demand-side pressure in the window.

Next filing: unit growth holding above 35% if prices flatten.

P6 Balance sheet and fundingStated
TRA payments due within a year rise to $99M (December: $37M) on a $2.2B liability, $1.7B owed to related parties
Q2 2026: "As of June 30, 2026 and December 31, 2025, $99 million and $37 million, respectively, are included in other current liabilities"

Size: TRA liability $2.229B = 43% of total debt ($5.13B); the current portion equals about one quarter’s interest expense ($101M).

As the company uses its tax assets, 85% of the savings go to LLC unitholders, mostly the Garcia Parties. The cash cost begins to scale in 2026 and rises with profits.

Next filing: TRA payments in Q3 and Q4 against the $99M current portion.
