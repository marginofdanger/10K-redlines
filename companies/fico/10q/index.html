FICO 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of eight Fair Isaac 10-Q filings, FY2024 Q1 (December 2023 quarter) through FY2026 Q2 (March 2026 quarter), with the FY2024 and FY2025 10-K boundary transitions included. The value is speed: quarterly cadence catches language changes 3–9 months before annual filings would surface them.
Source: SEC EDGAR | 8 Quarterly Filings + FY2024/FY2025 10-K Baselines | FY2024 Q1 – FY2026 Q2 | Analysis: June 2026

FY24 Q1 → Q2

Mortgage origination volume enters the B2B Scores attribution for the first time — as a negative offset to pricing. DBNRR is redefined from a period metric ("during the quarter") to a point-in-time metric ("as of"), and ACV Bookings is downgraded from "a more meaningful measure" to "a useful supplemental measure." A new $500.0M January 2024 buyback program replaces the October 2022 program.

FY24 Q2 → Q3

A new $450M unsecured term loan raises credit agreement capacity to $1.35B, and the buyback authorization doubles to $1.0B in July 2024. Q3 repurchases of $255.5M are the third consecutive quarterly step-up.

FY24 Q3 → FY24 10-K

The FY2024 10-K adds the first FHFA reference in the data set, a bi-merge/tri-merge risk, and a new risk factor naming the CFPB's May 2024 mortgage-fee inquiry. The bureau "alliance" becomes a "joint venture." None of these terms appears in any 10-Q.

FY24 10-K → FY25 Q1

Mortgage volume flips from headwind to tailwind in the B2B driver sentence — the cleanest inflection in the data set. The consolidated antitrust class actions are disclosed for the first time, two months after the FY2024 10-K Item 3 said "Not applicable." The quarter's effective tax rate is (1.6)%.

FY25 Q1 → Q2

Mortgage drops out of the quarter-level attribution after one quarter (retained at six-month level), and B2C gains a bureau-royalty driver after five filings of myFICO.com declines. Software margin slips to 31% on data center hosting costs and the first headcount decline is referenced.

FY25 Q2 → Q3

$1.5B of 6.00% senior notes refinance both term loans; the amended credit agreement's covenant sentence no longer lists the 3.0x minimum interest coverage ratio. Buybacks hit a then-record $511.3M under a new $1.0B June 2025 program, and the legal proceedings item adds "vigorously defend."

FY25 Q3 → FY25 10-K

The FY2025 10-K rewrites the FHFA risk factor around the July 2025 announcement and adds the first language framing bureau pricing as a constraint; the CFPB is de-named. First restructuring disclosure: $10.9M for 226 eliminated positions. Tariffs, an India workforce concentration, and AI language are added throughout.

FY25 10-K → FY26 Q1

The forward-looking statements boilerplate is rewritten for the first time in nine filings. A new tax sentence attributes a smaller excess tax benefit to the December 2025 stock-price decline, and operating cash flow declines year-over-year for the first time in the data set.

FY26 Q1 → Q2

Scores revenue rises 60% with no change to the attribution wording; bureau concentration reaches 64% of quarterly revenue. $1.0B of 6.25% 2026 notes retire the 2018 notes early, and a record $611.3M is repurchased under a new $1.5B February 2026 authorization.

FY2024 Q1 → Q2

Dec 31, 2023 qtr → Mar 31, 2024 qtr | Revenue: $382.1M → $433.8M | Scores: $192.1M → $236.9M | Buybacks: $71.7M → $179.5M

MD&A

▶ Mortgage originations enters the B2B Scores attribution — as a negative offset New

FY2024 Q1 contains zero occurrences of the word "mortgage" anywhere in the MD&A. FY2024 Q2 introduces it as a volume offset to pricing:

FY2024 Q1:

"The increase in business-to-business scores revenue was primarily attributable to a higher unit price."

FY2024 Q2:

"The increase in business-to-business scores revenue was primarily attributable to a higher unit price, partially offset by a decrease in volume of mortgage originations."

"A higher unit price" is the only pricing language FICO uses in MD&A — the phrase "special pricing" never appears in any filing in this set.

▶ DBNRR reframed from a period metric to a point-in-time metric Changed

FY2024 Q1:

"Dollar-Based Net Retention Rate for our Software segment was 114% during the quarter ended December 31, 2023."

FY2024 Q2:

"Dollar-Based Net Retention Rate for our Software segment was 112% as of March 31, 2024."

The companion table headers changed the same way: the ARR table moved from "at each of the dates presented" to "exiting each of the dates presented," and the DBNRR table moved from "for each of the periods presented" to "exiting each of the dates presented."

▶ ACV Bookings downgraded from "more meaningful" to "useful supplemental" Changed

FY2024 Q1:

"…we believe ACV Bookings is a more meaningful measure of our business…"

FY2024 Q2:

"…we believe ACV Bookings is a useful supplemental measure of our business…"
▶ New ~30% usage-based-fee estimate disclosure added to the ACV definition New

FY2024 Q2 added a quantified estimate to the Key Performance Metrics subsection:

"For each of the periods presented, ACV Bookings related to estimates of future usage-based fees was approximately 30% of the total ACV Bookings amount. Differences between the initial estimates of future usage-based fees and actual results historically have not been material and we do not currently expect that they will be materially different in the future."

The FY2024 10-K later refined the 30% figure with "on an annualized basis."

▶ "Services" renamed "professional services" and given a strategy attribution Changed

FY2024 Q1:

"…partially offset by a $1.0 million decrease in services revenue."

FY2024 Q2:

"…partially offset by a $7.4 million decrease in professional services revenue. … The decrease in professional services revenue was primarily attributable to our strategy to emphasize higher-margin software over professional services."

This is the first appearance of the software-over-services strategy sentence; it persists through FY2025 Q1 and is dropped from the quarter discussion at FY2025 Q2.

Notes / Financial

▶ New $500.0M January 2024 repurchase program; quarterly buybacks step up to $179.5M Signal

The January 2024 program replaced the October 2022 $500.0M program, which had $48.8M remaining at December 31, 2023. It was disclosed in FY2024 Q1 as a subsequent event (one of only two subsequent-events notes across the eight quarters). Q2 repurchases were $179.5M versus $71.7M in Q1.

FY2024 Q2 → Q3

Mar 31, 2024 qtr → Jun 30, 2024 qtr | Revenue: $433.8M → $447.8M | Scores: $236.9M → $241.5M | Buybacks: $179.5M → $255.5M

MD&A

▶ New $450 Million Term Loan disclosed in Liquidity New

FY2024 Q3:

"On June 13, 2024, we amended our credit agreement to provide for the issuance of a new $450 million unsecured term loan (the “$450 Million Term Loan”) with a syndicate of banks, increasing the total capacity of the credit agreement to $1.35 billion. … We have no obligation to make scheduled principal payments on the $450 Million Term Loan prior to the maturity date…"

The section heading changed from "Revolving Line of Credit and Term Loan" to "Revolving Line of Credit and Term Loans." The new loan matures August 19, 2026, the same date as the existing $300M term loan.

▶ Highlights bullets switch to plural revenue phrasing Changed

FY2024 Q2:

"Total revenue was $433…" / "Total revenue for our Scores segment was $236…"

FY2024 Q3:

"Total revenues were $447…" / "Revenues for our Scores segment were $241…"

The plural phrasing persists in all later quarters in the data set.

▶ Software operating margin decline disclosed: 38% to 34% Signal

The stated cause is a prior-year comparison effect rather than a current-period cost increase:

"…decreased to 34% from 38%, primarily attributable to a prior-year quarter one-time reimbursement from a third-party data center provider for implementation costs previously incurred."

The B2B Scores attribution (higher unit price, partially offset by lower mortgage origination volume) was retained verbatim from Q2.

Notes / Financial

▶ Buyback authorization doubled to $1.0B (July 2024) New

Disclosed via the second (and last) subsequent-events note in the data set:

"In July 2024, our Board of Directors approved a new stock repurchase program, replacing the January 2024 program, which was terminated prior to its expiration and under which $29.6 million was remaining for repurchase at the time of termination."

The new program is open-ended with a $1.0B aggregate limit — double the size of the January 2024 and October 2022 programs. Q3 repurchases of $255.5M were the third consecutive quarterly step-up ($71.7M → $179.5M → $255.5M).

FY2024 Q3 → FY2024 10-K

Jun 30, 2024 qtr → FY2024 annual (Sep 30, 2024) | FY2024 revenue: $1.7B (+13%) | Scores: $919.7M (+19%) | FY2024 buybacks: $833.3M | ETR: 20.1%

MD&A / Business

▶ First FHFA mention in the data set — annual filing only New

FY2024 10-K business overview:

"The adoption of our most predictive scores, FICO Score 10 and 10 T, gained increased traction for non-conforming mortgages and will be implemented for conforming mortgages based on the timeline set forth by the Federal Housing Finance Agency for enterprise credit scoring requirements."

No 10-Q in this set ever mentions FHFA, VantageScore, or the CFPB — these terms appear only in the annual filings.

▶ Platform first named in the Software revenue driver sentence New

The FY2024 full-year Software driver attributes SaaS growth "for our Platform products" — the first time Platform is named in the revenue driver sentence. The full-year B2B Scores attribution matched the quarters verbatim ("a higher unit price, partially offset by a decrease in volume of mortgage originations"). The ACV usage-based estimate gained the qualifier "on an annualized basis."

▶ New SG&A item: transfer-pricing tax law change at a non-U.S. subsidiary New
"The increase in non-income tax costs was primarily attributable to a tax law change related to transfer pricing effective in fiscal 2024 that impacted a non-U.S. subsidiary."

A similar transfer-pricing sentence recurs in the FY2025 Q2 and Q3 10-Qs ("a foreign tax law change related to transfer pricing… that impacted a non-U.S. subsidiary").

Risk Factors (FY2023 10-K → FY2024 10-K)

▶ Bi-merge/tri-merge risk added to the FHFA risk factor New

FY2024 10-K:

"Other changes implemented by FHFA, Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac could also affect the demand for FICO Scores and thus could have similar adverse effects on our business, including, for example, a change permitting mortgage originators to underwrite loans using credit scores from only two of the three national consumer reporting agencies (a “bi-merge report”) rather than from all three (a “tri-merge report”)."

The defined terms "bi-merge report" and "tri-merge report" exist only in the FY2024 10-K; the FY2025 10-K replaced them with "fewer than three."

▶ Entirely new risk factor on mortgage-pricing regulation, naming the CFPB inquiry New

FY2024 10-K:

"Increased regulatory focus on U.S. residential mortgage closing costs may affect our ability to implement price changes for FICO Scores used in mortgage originations and thus limit the revenues and profitability of the FICO Score. … For example, in May 2024, the CFPB launched a public inquiry to obtain information on fees charged by providers of mortgages and related settlement services in the U.S. residential mortgage market, including fees for credit reports and credit scores."

This is the only place FICO links regulation to its mortgage-score pricing. A separate added sentence stated that the CFPB has indicated it intends to issue rules under the FCRA extending the statute to business practices not currently subject to it. Both CFPB references were deleted in the FY2025 10-K.

▶ Bureau "alliance" recharacterized as "joint venture" Changed

FY2023 10-K:

"Experian, TransUnion and Equifax have formed an alliance that is selling a credit scoring product competitive with our products…"

FY2024 10-K:

"…have formed a joint venture that is selling a credit scoring product…"

VantageScore is named — identically in FY2024 and FY2025 — only in the Item 1 competition discussion, as "a joint venture entity established by the three major U.S. consumer reporting agencies."

▶ Cybersecurity and technology risk language updated; banking concentration 92% Changed

FY2024 additions versus FY2023: "generative artificial intelligence" added to the technology-change risk; "social engineering attacks" added to the cybersecurity factor, while "highly obscure security vulnerabilities" became "security vulnerabilities." COVID-19 references and the remote-work risk factor were removed. The banking concentration figure updated to "92% of our revenues were derived from sales of products and services to the banking industry" (from 91% in fiscal 2023).

MD&A

▶ Mortgage volume flips from headwind to tailwind Changed

The single cleanest inflection in the data set, at quarterly resolution nine months before the FY2025 10-K framed the year:

FY2024 Q3:

"Scores segment revenues increased $39.7 million due to an increase of $40.6 million in our business-to-business scores revenue, partially offset by a decrease of $0.9 million in our business-to-consumer revenue. The increase in business-to-business scores revenue was primarily attributable to a higher unit price, partially offset by a decrease in volume of mortgage originations."

FY2025 Q1:

"Scores segment revenues increased $43.6 million due to an increase of $42.0 million in our business-to-business scores revenue and an increase of $1.6 million in our business-to-consumer revenue. The increase in business-to-business scores revenue was primarily attributable to a higher unit price and an increase in volume of mortgage originations."
▶ B2C myFICO.com decline sentence dropped Removed

Present in every FY2024 filing, this sentence disappears at FY2025 Q1:

FY2024 Q3 (removed thereafter):

"The decrease in business-to-consumer revenue was primarily attributable to a decrease in direct sales generated from the myFICO.com website."

FY2025 Q1 gives no attribution for the B2C increase. This is the last quarter myFICO.com is referenced in a revenue driver; the segment-description boilerplate still lists "our myFICO.com subscription offerings."

▶ New Software driver: large license renewal recognized at a point in time New

FY2025 Q1:

"…primarily attributable to an increase in license revenue recognized at a point in time due to a large license renewal and an increase in revenue recognized over time largely driven by SaaS growth for our Platform products."
▶ Scores margin dips to 86% on marketing spend Signal
"…decreased to 86% from 88%, primarily attributable to an increase in marketing expenses."

SG&A rose on a $10.2 million increase in advertising and other promotional expenses. The margin recovered to 88% by FY2026 Q1 and reached 91% in FY2026 Q2.

▶ Negative effective tax rate: (1.6)% Signal

The quarter's ETR was (1.6)% versus 7.3% a year earlier, favorably impacted by excess tax benefits relating to share-based compensation that vests in the December quarter. The same December-quarter effect reversed at FY2026 Q1, when a lower stock price cut the benefit and the ETR rose to 17.5%.

Legal Proceedings

▶ First-ever litigation disclosure: consolidated antitrust class actions New

FY2025 Q1, Part II Item 1:

"FICO is a defendant in consolidated putative class action lawsuits brought in the Northern District of Illinois against FICO and the credit bureaus, Equifax, Experian and TransUnion, alleging antitrust claims in connection with the distribution of FICO Scores. On November 24, 2024, the court ruled on FICO's and the credit bureaus' motions to dismiss the plaintiffs' amended complaints. The court dismissed with prejudice all claims in the lawsuit other than a Sherman Act Section 2 claim and accompanying state law claims against FICO, which were allowed to proceed through the discovery stage of the litigation."

The FY2024 10-K Item 3, filed about two months earlier, said "Not applicable." The surviving claims run against FICO only; the bureaus are described as co-defendants. The disclosure has never included a loss estimate or accrual, and the matter was never added to the Contingencies footnote.

Notes / Financial

▶ Segment note: "depreciation amounts" becomes "depreciation and amortization amounts" Changed

FY2024 Q3:

"…rather, depreciation amounts are allocated…"

FY2025 Q1:

"…rather, depreciation and amortization amounts are allocated…"

No change to the two-segment structure, allocation basis, or unallocated-items list. A second methodology-language change followed at FY2026 Q1 with the ASU 2023-07 interim adoption.

MD&A

▶ Mortgage volume drops out of the quarter-level attribution after one quarter Changed

FY2025 Q1 (quarter):

"…primarily attributable to a higher unit price and an increase in volume of mortgage originations."

FY2025 Q2 (quarter):

"The increase in business-to-business scores revenue was primarily attributable to a higher unit price."

The six-month sentence kept both drivers ("a higher unit price and an increase in volume of mortgage originations"). Mortgage returned to the quarter-level attribution at FY2025 Q3.

▶ B2C gains an explicit new driver: bureau royalties New

FY2025 Q2:

"The increase in business-to-consumer revenue was primarily attributable to an increase in royalties derived from scores sold indirectly to consumers through credit reporting agencies."

This replaces the myFICO.com direct-sales framing that ran through all of FY2024 and carries forward through FY2026 Q2. The FY2025 10-K version says "consumer reporting agencies."

▶ Software-over-services strategy sentence dropped from the quarter discussion Removed

FY2025 Q1 (removed at FY2025 Q2):

"The decrease in professional services revenue was primarily attributable to our strategy to emphasize higher-margin software over professional services."

The FY2025 Q2 quarter-level software driver narrowed to "an increase in license revenue recognized at a point in time" with no SaaS-growth clause at quarter level.

▶ Software margin pressure attributed to third-party hosting costs Signal
"…decreased to 31% from 33%, primarily attributable to the increase in third-party data center hosting costs."

The hosting-cost attribution recurs at FY2026 Q1, when the margin fell further to 28% with a second cause added.

▶ First headcount decline reference in the data set New

FY2025 Q2, cost of revenues:

"The decrease in personnel and labor costs was primarily attributable to decreased headcount."

Period-end employee counts kept rising through FY2025 Q3 (3,855) before the FY2025 10-K disclosed a Q4 restructuring; the count was 3,758 at FY2026 Q2.

Notes / Financial

▶ Receivables concentration: three customers at or above 10% Signal

The revenue note reported three customers each accounting for 10% or more of total consolidated receivables at March 31, 2025, versus one at September 30, 2024. Bureau revenue concentration reached 52% of quarterly revenue (47% a year earlier). The paragraph's wording is unchanged — the movement is entirely in the numbers.

FY2025 Q2 → Q3

Mar 31, 2025 qtr → Jun 30, 2025 qtr | Revenue: $498.7M → $536.4M | Scores: $297.0M → $324.3M | Buybacks: $207.0M → $511.3M

MD&A

▶ $1.5B senior note issuance and full debt refinancing New

New highlights bullet, FY2025 Q3:

"We issued $1.5 billion of senior notes and used the net proceeds to repay all the outstanding balances on our term loans and revolving line of credit. We also amended our credit agreement to increase our borrowing capacity under the unsecured revolving line of credit to $1.0 billion and extended its maturity. Total debt balance was $2.8 billion as of June 30, 2025, compared with $2.2 billion as of September 30, 2024."

Liquidity detail: the May 13, 2025 amendment raised the revolver from $600 million to $1.0 billion and extended maturity to May 13, 2030; the $300 million and $450 million term loans were repaid in full and terminated. The 2025 Senior Notes carry 6.00% and mature May 15, 2033. The 12-month obligations clause rotated from the term-loan principal payments to "the $400.0 million principal payment on the 2018 Senior Notes."

▶ B2B driver adds a one-time insurance score license renewal; mortgage returns Changed

FY2025 Q2 (quarter):

"The increase in business-to-business scores revenue was primarily attributable to a higher unit price."

FY2025 Q3 (quarter):

"The increase in business-to-business scores revenue was primarily attributable to a higher unit price, an increase in volume of mortgage originations and a multi-year license renewal in the U.S. recognized on our insurance score product during the quarter ended June 30, 2025."

The insurance-renewal clause appears only in FY2025 Q3 and the FY2025 10-K; it rolled off at FY2026 Q1. Professional services revenue also increased for the first time in the data set ("a $1.6 million increase in professional services revenue"), with no strategy attribution given.

Risk Factors

▶ Risk factor stub unchanged despite the July 2025 FHFA announcement Signal

FY2025 Q3 covers the period ended June 30, 2025 and was filed after the FHFA Director's July 2025 announcement on credit score choice at the GSEs. Its Item 1A nevertheless remained the standard one-paragraph stub:

"There have been no material changes from the risk factors disclosed in our Annual Report on Form 10-K…"

The referenced FY2024 10-K FHFA risk factor described only a hypothetical bi-merge change. The announcement entered SEC disclosure only in the FY2025 10-K, filed in November 2025.

Legal Proceedings

▶ One sentence added to the antitrust disclosure Changed

The litigation text — byte-identical between FY2025 Q1 and Q2 — gained one sentence at the end:

"FICO intends to vigorously defend against the remaining claims in this proceeding."

The text has been frozen verbatim from this quarter through FY2026 Q2 — no status update across four subsequent quarters of discovery.

Notes / Financial

▶ Minimum interest coverage covenant disappears from the credit agreement description Removed

FY2025 Q2:

"…restrictive covenants including a maximum consolidated leverage ratio of 3.5 to 1.0, subject to a step up to 4.0 to 1.0 following certain permitted acquisitions and subject to certain conditions, and a minimum interest coverage ratio of 3.0 to 1.0."

FY2025 Q3:

"The credit agreement contains certain restrictive covenants including a maximum consolidated leverage ratio of 3.5 to 1.0, subject to a step up to 4.0 to 1.0 following certain permitted acquisitions and subject to certain conditions, and contains other covenants typical of an unsecured credit facility."

The amended agreement's sentence lists only the leverage covenant. Compliance is asserted every quarter. The interest-rate mechanics also changed from one-month adjusted term SOFR to a three-option structure including Daily Simple SOFR.

▶ Record buyback quarter ($511.3M) and a new $1.0B June 2025 program Signal
"In June 2025, our Board of Directors approved a new stock repurchase program…"

The June 2025 program ($1.0B, open-ended) replaced the July 2024 program. The $511.3M repurchased in the quarter was the largest of the data set to that point, in the same quarter as the $1.5B note issuance; it was surpassed by the $611.3M of FY2026 Q2.

▶ OBBBA added to the tax note New

FY2025 Q3:

"The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (“OBBBA”) of 2025 was signed into law on July 4, 2025. We continue to monitor the impact of the tax provisions of the OBBBA, most of which are not effective for FICO until fiscal 2026 and after."

FY2025 quarters had replaced the FY2024 quarters' Inflation Reduction Act paragraph with OECD Pillar Two language; FY2026 quarters dropped Pillar Two and specified the OBBBA's R&E expensing effects. See the ETR signal section for the full rotation.

MD&A / Business

▶ FHFA language moves from future implementation to approval Changed

FY2024 10-K:

"…FICO Score 10 and 10 T, gained increased traction for non-conforming mortgages and will be implemented for conforming mortgages based on the timeline set forth by the Federal Housing Finance Agency for enterprise credit scoring requirements."

FY2025 10-K:

"Score 10 and 10 T" narrowed to "Score 10 T."

▶ First restructuring disclosure in the data set: $10.9M, 226 positions New

Appears only in the 10-K, never in a 10-Q:

"During the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, we incurred charges of $10.9 million in employee separation costs due to the elimination of 226 positions throughout the Company. Cash payments for all the employee separation costs will be paid by the end of our fiscal 2026."

Headcount at FY2025 year-end was 3,811, down from 3,855 at June 30, 2025. 10-Q text searches for restructuring charges and "severance" return zero hits in all eight quarters.

▶ New product language: BNPL scores, Kenya score, Mortgage Simulator, FICO Marketplace New

The FY2025 business overview added FICO Score 10 BNPL and FICO Score 10 T BNPL:

"…the first credit scores from a leading credit scoring provider to incorporate Buy Now, Pay Later (“BNPL”) data…"

Also added: a FICO Score in Kenya leveraging TransUnion data, the FICO Score Mortgage Simulator, and FICO Marketplace (access to data, AI models, optimization tools, decision rulesets, and machine learning models). The FY2024 overview's Ukrainian credit score, FICO Resilience Index, and UltraFICO Score XD references were dropped.

▶ "Credit bureau" / "credit reporting agencies" terminology shifts to "consumer reporting" Changed

10-Qs through FY2025 Q3, cost of revenues:

"…credit bureau data and processing services…"

FY2025 10-K:

"…consumer reporting agency data and processing services…"

The B2C revenue attribution similarly says "consumer reporting agencies" in the 10-K. The cost-of-revenues terminology carried into FY2026 Q1, but the B2C attribution there reverted to "credit reporting agencies."

Risk Factors (FY2024 10-K → FY2025 10-K)

▶ FHFA risk factor rewritten around the July 2025 announcement; bureau-pricing constraint added Changed

FY2024 10-K:

"…including, for example, a change permitting mortgage originators to underwrite loans using credit scores from only two of the three national consumer reporting agencies (a “bi-merge report”) rather than from all three (a “tri-merge report”)."

FY2025 10-K:

"…including, for example, the change announced by the FHFA Director in July 2025 permitting mortgage originators to choose the credit score they submit with mortgages delivered to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac or a potential future change permitting mortgage originators to underwrite loans using credit scores from fewer than three national consumer reporting agencies. The ability of our FICO Score to compete effectively in the U.S. mortgage market may be constrained by the pricing and other business practices of the consumer reporting agencies, which could have a material adverse effect on our revenues, results of operations and stock price."

The final sentence is the first risk-factor language framing the consumer reporting agencies' own pricing and business practices as a constraint on the FICO Score at the GSEs.

▶ CFPB de-named in the mortgage-fee risk factor Removed

FY2024 10-K (deleted in FY2025):

"For example, in May 2024, the CFPB launched a public inquiry to obtain information on fees charged by providers of mortgages and related settlement services in the U.S. residential mortgage market, including fees for credit reports and credit scores."

FY2025 10-K (replacement):

"There has been increased regulatory focus in the U.S. related to the transparency and fairness of certain fees charged to consumers in connection with the closing of a residential mortgage loan, including fees for credit reports and credit scores."

The FCRA-rulemaking sentence ("the CFPB has indicated that it intends to issue rules under the FCRA…") was also deleted. CFPB mentions in the full 10-K fell from 5 (FY2024) to 2 (FY2025), both in the Item 1 regulatory overview.

▶ Tariffs added in three places New

FY2025 10-K:

"…including any stress or negative volatility related to the imposition of, and threatened imposition of, tariffs and retaliatory tariffs, economic sanctions and increased trade tensions."

"Trade policies and tariffs" was also inserted into the credit/equity-market volatility clause and the geopolitical-tensions clause. The international risk bullet was trimmed from "unfavorable tax rules or changes in tariffs and other trade barriers" to "changes in tariffs and other trade barriers." No 10-Q mentions tariffs.

▶ New India workforce concentration disclosure New

FY2025 10-K:

"For example, approximately one-third of our workforce is located in India, which could be negatively impacted by heightened tensions between India and Pakistan."
▶ AI language woven throughout the risk factors New

FY2025 10-K, new product-development sentence:

"Our use of AI in the development of our products and our incorporation of AI features into some of our products could introduce errors, defects, or delays…"

Other FY2025 additions: a new summary bullet on "changes in technology, including increased use of artificial intelligence"; the competition source expanded to information sources "including those that use AI technologies"; the competitor category "neural network developers and artificial intelligence system builders" became "developers and providers of neural networks, machine learning, and AI systems"; the IP risk notes protection "is rapidly evolving with respect to AI technologies"; and the talent risk now lists data science, AI and cybersecurity roles. Ransomware language added "and may demand payment by us in order to restore access." Banking concentration stayed at 92%.

MD&A

Themes

1. Bureau Channel Concentration Compounds Silently

Bureau revenue concentration went from 39% of quarterly revenue (FY24 Q1) to 64% (FY26 Q2), and receivables-concentrated customers from one to three, with zero wording changes to the revenue-note paragraph across all eight quarters. B2B Scores revenue grew 72% year-over-year in the final quarter while B2C grew 5%.

2. The Mortgage-Volume Clause Is the Fastest GSE Signal

The B2B driver clause moved: absent (FY24 Q1) → "partially offset by a decrease" (FY24 Q2) → "and an increase" (FY25 Q1) → out at quarter level for one quarter (FY25 Q2) → back plus a one-time insurance renewal (FY25 Q3). The FY25 Q1 sign flip preceded the FY2025 10-K's framing of the year by nine months. Pricing is listed first in every period.

3. Balance Sheet Rotated to Long-Dated Fixed Debt While Buybacks Scaled

Total debt rose from $2.0B to $3.6B across the nine filings: floating-rate term loans and revolver balances were replaced by $2.5B of new fixed notes at 6.00% (May 2033) and 6.25% (Sep 2034). The May 2025 amendment's covenant sentence dropped the 3.0x minimum interest coverage ratio. Buyback authorizations stepped $500M → $1.0B → $1.0B → $1.5B, with record quarters of $511.3M (FY25 Q3) and $611.3M (FY26 Q2).

4. Regulatory Disclosure Lives Only in the 10-Ks

FHFA, VantageScore, CFPB, tariffs, restructuring, BNPL scores, and bi-merge/tri-merge appear exclusively in annual filings; every 10-Q risk-factor item is a verbatim "no material changes" stub — including FY25 Q3, filed after the July 2025 FHFA announcement. The antitrust class actions, by contrast, surfaced first in a 10-Q (FY25 Q1), two months after the FY2024 10-K said "Not applicable."

5. Software KPIs Decayed, Were Redefined, Then Recovered

Total DBNRR fell 114% → 102% (FY25 Q2 trough) → 109% (FY26 Q2); non-platform DBNRR ended at 90% with non-platform ARR down from $497.4M to $440.0M, while platform ARR rose from $190.3M to $348.8M. The definitions moved early: at FY24 Q2, DBNRR became a point-in-time metric and ACV Bookings was downgraded from "a more meaningful measure" to "a useful supplemental measure." Software segment margin declined 38% → 28% across three disclosed step-downs, with third-party data center hosting costs cited in the last two.

6. FY2026 Delivered Magnitude Without Narrative

FY26 Q2 posted Scores +60%, total revenue +39%, 91% Scores margin, a record buyback, and a new $1.5B authorization with zero change in attribution wording. The quarter's only new sentences concern financing (2026 notes, 2018 notes retirement) and the rewritten liquidity clause ("no significant debt obligations maturing over the next 12 months"). The FY26 Q1 tax note carried the set's first acknowledgment of the stock-price decline.
