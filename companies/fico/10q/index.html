FICO 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of nine Fair Isaac 10-Q filings, FY2024 Q1 (December 2023 quarter) through FY2026 Q3 (June 2026 quarter), with the FY2024 and FY2025 10-K boundary transitions included. The value is speed: quarterly cadence catches language changes 3–9 months before annual filings would surface them.
Source: SEC EDGAR | 9 Quarterly Filings + FY2024/FY2025 10-K Baselines | FY2024 Q1 – FY2026 Q3 | Analysis: June 2026, extended October 2026; FY2024 10-K onward revised October 2026

FY24 Q1 → Q2

Mortgage origination volume enters the B2B Scores attribution for the first time — as a negative offset to pricing. DBNRR is redefined from a period metric ("during the quarter") to a point-in-time metric ("as of"), and ACV Bookings is downgraded from "a more meaningful measure" to "a useful supplemental measure." A new $500.0M January 2024 buyback program replaces the October 2022 program.

FY24 Q2 → Q3

A new $450M unsecured term loan raises credit agreement capacity to $1.35B, and the buyback authorization doubles to $1.0B in July 2024. Q3 repurchases of $255.5M are the third consecutive quarterly step-up.

FY24 Q3 → FY24 10-K

The FY2024 10-K brings FHFA into the MD&A ("will be implemented" for conforming mortgages), adds a bi-merge/tri-merge risk and a new risk factor naming the CFPB's May 2024 mortgage-fee inquiry, and books a withholding-tax liability on foreign earnings no longer permanently reinvested. The bureau "alliance" becomes a "joint venture." None of the regulatory terms appears in any 10-Q.

FY24 10-K → FY25 Q1

Mortgage volume flips from headwind to tailwind in the B2B driver sentence — the cleanest inflection in the data set. The consolidated antitrust class actions are disclosed for the first time, three months after the FY2024 10-K Item 3 said "Not applicable." Note 1 now calls FICO "a global analytics software leader," nine months before the 10-K does. The quarter's effective tax rate is (1.6)%.

FY25 Q1 → Q2

Mortgage drops out of the quarter-level attribution after one quarter (retained at six-month level), and B2C gains a bureau-royalty driver after five filings of myFICO.com declines. Software operating income falls year over year, the first of five straight quarterly declines, and the margin slips to 31% on data center hosting costs; "decreased headcount" appears for the first time.

FY25 Q2 → Q3

$1.5B of 6.00% senior notes refinance both term loans; the amended credit agreement's covenant sentence no longer lists the 3.0x minimum interest coverage ratio. Buybacks hit a then-record $511.3M under a new $1.0B June 2025 program, and the legal proceedings item adds "vigorously defend."

FY25 Q3 → FY25 10-K

The FY2025 10-K rewrites the FHFA risk factor around the July 2025 announcement and adds the first language framing bureau pricing as a constraint; the CFPB is de-named. First restructuring disclosure: $10.9M for 226 eliminated positions. The cloud-migration cyber risk becomes "We are exposed." An India workforce concentration and more tariff language are added.

FY25 10-K → FY26 Q1

The CEO adopts his third November 10b5-1 sale plan in three years (up to 47,904 shares). For the first time the Scores margin rise (88% from 86%) is attributed to "increased unit pricing." Non-platform ARR shrinks 8% as platform ARR adds $39.0M in a quarter. The tax note cites the December stock-price decline: net income +4% against operating income +30%. Headcount falls after the 226-position cut, which the 10-Q never mentions.

FY26 Q1 → Q2

B2B Scores +72% and Scores margin 91% with unchanged attribution wording; bureau concentration 64%. A record $611.3M is repurchased under a new $1.5B program, mostly in March at $1,183.53 a share, and the CEO cancels his sale plan on March 26, the first such termination in the set. $1.0B of 6.25% notes retire the 2018 notes early. The 2025 notes fall below par (97.1).

FY26 Q2 → Q3

A $1.5B term loan funds a $1.5B accelerated share repurchase; quarterly buybacks hit $2.3B, total debt rises to $5.6B, and the leverage covenant is loosened to 4.5x through December 2026. The B2B driver narrows to "a higher mortgage origination scores unit price" as Scores revenue falls sequentially. RPO falls between 10-Qs for the first time, platform ARR overtakes non-platform ARR, and headcount (3,876) passes its pre-restructuring level.

MD&A

▶ Mortgage originations enters the B2B Scores attribution — as a negative offset New

FY2024 Q1 contains zero occurrences of the word "mortgage" anywhere in the MD&A. FY2024 Q2 introduces it as a volume offset to pricing:

FY2024 Q1:

"The increase in business-to-business scores revenue was primarily attributable to a higher unit price."

FY2024 Q2:

"The increase in business-to-business scores revenue was primarily attributable to a higher unit price, partially offset by a decrease in volume of mortgage originations."

"A higher unit price" is the only pricing language FICO uses in MD&A — the phrase "special pricing" never appears in any filing in this set.

▶ DBNRR reframed from a period metric to a point-in-time metric Changed

FY2024 Q1:

"Dollar-Based Net Retention Rate for our Software segment was 114% during the quarter ended December 31, 2023."

FY2024 Q2:

"Dollar-Based Net Retention Rate for our Software segment was 112% as of March 31, 2024."

The companion table headers changed the same way: the ARR table moved from "at each of the dates presented" to "exiting each of the dates presented," and the DBNRR table moved from "for each of the periods presented" to "exiting each of the dates presented."

▶ New ~30% usage-based-fee estimate disclosure added to the ACV definition New

FY2024 Q2 added a quantified estimate to the Key Performance Metrics subsection:

"For each of the periods presented, ACV Bookings related to estimates of future usage-based fees was approximately 30% of the total ACV Bookings amount. Differences between the initial estimates of future usage-based fees and actual results historically have not been material and we do not currently expect that they will be materially different in the future."

The FY2024 10-K later refined the 30% figure with "on an annualized basis."

FY2024 Q2:

"…partially offset by a $7.4 million decrease in professional services revenue. … The decrease in professional services revenue was primarily attributable to our strategy to emphasize higher-margin software over professional services."

This is the first appearance of the software-over-services strategy sentence; it persists through FY2025 Q1, is dropped at FY2025 Q2, and returns at FY2026 Q3.

Risk Factors (FY2023 10-K → FY2024 10-K)

▶ New risk factor ties mortgage-score pricing to regulation, naming the CFPB's May 2024 inquiry New

Shift: new risk (+ disclosure); named +CFPB inquiry; FICO's own price changes named as the exposure

FY2024 10-K: "Increased regulatory focus on U.S. residential mortgage closing costs may affect our ability to implement price changes for FICO® Scores used in mortgage originations and thus limit the revenues and profitability of the FICO Score."
FY2024 10-K: "For example, in May 2024, the CFPB launched a public inquiry to obtain information on fees charged by providers of mortgages and related settlement services in the U.S. residential mortgage market, including fees for credit reports and credit scores."

History: "CFPB launched a public inquiry" appears in the FY2024 10-K only. The CFPB is never named in any 10-Q in the set; the FY2025 10-K deletes the inquiry and keeps the heading.

This is the only place FICO links regulation to its mortgage-score pricing. The three FY2024 10-Qs that preceded it, filed while the inquiry was open, carried only the standard "no material changes" risk-factor stub.

▶ Bi-merge/tri-merge scenario added to the Fannie/Freddie risk factor New

Shift: condition added, a second route to lost mortgage volume (+ disclosure); risk factor moved up from paragraph 63 to 30

FY2024 10-K: "Other changes implemented by FHFA, Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac could also affect the demand for FICO Scores and thus could have similar adverse effects on our business, including, for example, a change permitting mortgage originators to underwrite loans using credit scores from only two of the three national consumer reporting agencies (a “bi-merge report”) rather than from all three (a “tri-merge report”)."

History: "bi-merge" appears in the FY2024 10-K only; the FY2025 10-K replaced it with "fewer than three." The abbreviation "FHFA" first appears in this filing; "Federal Housing Finance Agency" was already named in the FY2023 10-K's risk factors and Item 1.

One fewer bureau pull per loan file means one fewer score sold, even if the FICO Score keeps its GSE approval.

▶ Bureau "alliance" recharacterized as a "joint venture" Changed

Shift: degree, "alliance" → "joint venture" (+ firmer)

FY2023 10-K: "For example, Experian, TransUnion and Equifax have formed an alliance that is selling a credit scoring product competitive with our products."
FY2024 10-K: "For example, Experian, TransUnion and Equifax have formed a joint venture that is selling a credit scoring product competitive with our products."

History: "formed an alliance" appears in the FY2023 10-K only; "joint venture that is selling" in the FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks. VantageScore is never named in a 10-Q.

The bureaus, which the revenue note shows at 47% of revenue in the June 2024 quarter, are described in sharper terms as owners of a competing product.

MD&A / Notes

▶ FHFA enters the MD&A: Score 10 T "will be implemented" for conforming mortgages on FHFA's timeline New

Shift: certainty, "continued to promote adoption" → "will be implemented" (+); named +Federal Housing Finance Agency in MD&A

FY2023 10-K: "We continued to promote adoption of our most predictive scores, FICO® Score 10 and 10 T."
FY2024 10-K: "The adoption of our most predictive scores, FICO® Score 10 and 10 T, gained increased traction for non-conforming mortgages and will be implemented for conforming mortgages based on the timeline set forth by the Federal Housing Finance Agency for enterprise credit scoring requirements."

History: "Federal Housing Finance Agency" appears in an MD&A for the first time in the FY2024 10-K (it was in the FY2023 10-K's risk factors and Item 1); no 10-Q in the set mentions FHFA. "Will be implemented for conforming" became "was approved for conforming" in the FY2025 10-K.

FHFA timing now governs the rollout of FICO's newest score, while the same filing moves the GSE risk factor up and adds the bi-merge risk.

▶ Platform first named in the Software revenue driver New

Shift: named +Platform products (+ disclosure)

FY2024 Q3: "The increase in our on-premises and SaaS software revenue was primarily attributable to an increase in revenue recognized over time largely driven by SaaS growth."
FY2024 10-K: "The increase in our on-premises and SaaS software revenue was primarily attributable to an increase in revenue recognized over time largely driven by SaaS growth for our Platform products."

History: "SaaS growth for our Platform products" first appears in the FY2024 10-K and recurs in every later filing (at the year-to-date level in FY2025 Q2).

The attribution now credits the platform specifically. The ARR table already showed platform ARR growing while non-platform ARR was flat ($494.2M at September 30, 2024 against $494.5M at June 30).

▶ Some foreign earnings no longer "permanently reinvested": $2.8M withholding-tax liability booked Changed

Shift: realized, no deferred tax provided → $2.8 million provided (+ quant added)

FY2023 10-K: "The Company has not provided deferred tax liabilities for foreign withholding taxes and certain state income taxes on the undistributed earnings and profits from certain non-U.S. subsidiaries that will be permanently reinvested outside the United States."
FY2024 10-K: "The Company has provided $2.8 million of deferred tax liabilities for foreign withholding taxes on the undistributed earnings and profits from certain non-U.S. subsidiaries that are not permanently reinvested outside the U.S."

History: "will be permanently reinvested" appears in the FY2023 10-K only; the FY2025 10-K keeps the new wording with $3.2 million. Unremitted foreign earnings rose from $27.2 million to $82.6 million.

FICO now expects to bring some foreign earnings home. The sums are small next to the year's $833.3M of buybacks, but the change is consistent with cash being pulled to the parent in the year buybacks exceeded operating cash flow (an inference; the note gives no reason).

Legal Proceedings

▶ First litigation disclosure: a Sherman Act Section 2 claim against FICO survives dismissal, three months after the 10-K said "Not applicable" New

Shift: named +lawsuit, "Not applicable" → disclosed proceeding (+ disclosure)

FY2025 Q1, Part II Item 1: "FICO is a defendant in consolidated putative class action lawsuits brought in the Northern District of Illinois against FICO and the credit bureaus, Equifax, Experian and TransUnion, alleging antitrust claims in connection with the distribution of FICO Scores. On November 24, 2024, the court ruled on FICO's and the credit bureaus' motions to dismiss the plaintiffs' amended complaints. The court dismissed with prejudice all claims in the lawsuit other than a Sherman Act Section 2 claim and accompanying state law claims against FICO, which were allowed to proceed through the discovery stage of the litigation."

History: "Sherman Act" first appears here and in every filing since. The legal-proceedings item read "Not applicable" in every earlier filing in the set, including the FY2024 10-K filed November 6, 2024.

The ruling came 18 days after the FY2024 10-K was filed. The surviving claim runs against FICO alone; the bureaus are co-defendants. No loss estimate or accrual has ever been given, and the matter was never added to the contingencies note.

▶ B2C myFICO.com decline sentence dropped; B2C grows with no stated driver Removed

▶ New Software driver: a large license renewal recognized at a point in time New

Shift: named +one-off license renewal ahead of SaaS growth

FY2025 Q1: "The increase in our on-premises and SaaS software revenue was primarily attributable to an increase in license revenue recognized at a point in time due to a large license renewal and an increase in revenue recognized over time largely driven by SaaS growth for our Platform products."

History: "large license renewal" first appears here and recurs in FY2025 Q2, Q3 and the FY2025 10-K (nine-month and full-year sentences).

A single point-in-time renewal leads the Software driver. Its loss in the following year is what the FY2026 10-Qs cite as "a decrease in sales of higher-margin software recognized at a point in time."

▶ Scores margin dips to 86% from 88% on marketing spend Signal

Shift: quant down, 88% → 86% with a stated cause

FY2025 Q1: "Segment operating income as a percentage of segment revenue for Scores decreased to 86% from 88%, primarily attributable to an increase in marketing expenses."

History: "decreased to 86% from 88%" appears in this filing only; FY2025 Q2 reverted to "materially consistent."

SG&A rose on a $10.2 million increase in advertising and other promotional expenses. The margin recovered to 88% by FY2026 Q1 and reached 91% in FY2026 Q2.

Themes

1. Bureau Channel Concentration Compounds Silently

Bureau revenue concentration went from 39% of quarterly revenue (FY24 Q1) to 64% (FY26 Q2), and receivables-concentrated customers from one to three, with zero wording changes to the revenue-note paragraph through eight quarters. FY26 Q3 eased to 63% (nine months 60%) and brought the paragraph's first edit, a reordering of the bureau names. B2B Scores revenue still grew 49% year-over-year in the latest quarter while B2C grew 5%.

2. The Mortgage Clause Is the Fastest GSE Signal — and Now Names the Price

The B2B driver clause moved: absent (FY24 Q1) → "partially offset by a decrease" (FY24 Q2) → "and an increase" (FY25 Q1) → out at quarter level for one quarter (FY25 Q2) → back plus a one-time insurance renewal (FY25 Q3) → volume out of the quarter again and the price itself tied to mortgage ("a higher mortgage origination scores unit price," FY26 Q3). Pricing is listed first in every period; at FY26 Q3 it is the only quarterly driver as Scores revenue fell sequentially for the first time in a March-to-June quarter. The 10-Qs still never mention FHFA or direct licensing.

3. Buybacks Outgrew Cash Flow; Debt Went from Refinancing to Funding Them

Total debt rose from $2.0B to $5.6B across the nine 10-Qs. Through FY26 Q2 the story was a rotation into long-dated fixed notes ($2.5B at 6.00% and 6.25%). FY26 Q3 partly reverses it: a $1.5B floating term loan, borrowed explicitly to fund a $1.5B ASR, takes floating-rate debt back to $2.2B and brings $300.0M of principal due within 12 months. The covenant record loosened twice: the 3.0x interest coverage ratio dropped (May 2025), then the leverage ceiling raised to 4.5x through December 2026 (June 2026). Authorizations stepped $500M → $1.0B → $1.0B → $1.5B → $2.0B, and quarterly buybacks reached $2.3B against nine-month operating cash flow of $777.9M.

4. Regulatory Disclosure Lives Only in the 10-Ks

FHFA, VantageScore, CFPB, tariffs, restructuring, BNPL scores, and bi-merge/tri-merge appear exclusively in annual filings; every 10-Q risk-factor item is a verbatim "no material changes" stub — including FY25 Q3, filed after the July 2025 FHFA announcement, and FY26 Q3, which more than doubled floating-rate debt and loosened the leverage covenant. The antitrust class actions, by contrast, surfaced first in a 10-Q (FY25 Q1), two months after the FY2024 10-K said "Not applicable," and the item has now been unchanged for five filings.

5. Software Is Becoming a Platform Business While Its Margin Falls

Total DBNRR fell 114% → 102% (FY25 Q2 trough) → 109% (FY26 Q2–Q3). Underneath, platform ARR ($412.8M, DBNRR 148%) overtook non-platform ARR ($403.0M, DBNRR 82%) at FY26 Q3. The definitions moved early: at FY24 Q2, DBNRR became a point-in-time metric and ACV Bookings was downgraded from "a more meaningful measure" to "a useful supplemental measure." Software segment margin has fallen in repeated step-downs, from 38% to 26% (FY26 Q3, versus 32% a year earlier), and segment operating income has declined year over year in every 10-Q since FY25 Q2, with lost point-in-time license sales now cited ahead of hosting costs, and RPO declined between 10-Qs for the first time ($717.7M → $680.4M).

6. FY2026: Magnitude Without Narrative, Then Leverage Without Narrative

FY26 Q2 posted Scores +60%, total revenue +39% and a record buyback with zero change in attribution wording. FY26 Q3 followed with the largest capital-structure move in the set (a debt-funded $1.5B ASR, a $2.0B authorization, a looser leverage covenant), explained only in the highlights bullets and the debt and ASR notes; the MD&A gives no rationale for the timing. Growth slowed (revenue +26%, Scores +41%) and interest expense rose 82% to $59.9M, and the FY26 Q2 liquidity clause "no significant debt obligations" lasted one filing. Insider signals ran the same way as the buyback: the CEO adopted a 10b5-1 sale plan in November 2025 (FY26 Q1), as he had in November 2023 and 2024, and for the first time terminated one, on March 26, 2026, the month the company bought 358,865 shares. Headcount, cut by 226 positions in late FY2025, was back above its pre-cut level by June 2026.
