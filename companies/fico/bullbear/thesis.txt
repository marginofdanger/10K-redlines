FAIR ISAAC (FICO): INVESTMENT THESIS PILLARS
Written October 2026 from the 10-K dashboard themes and the 10-Q scanner's tracked signals, then tested
against the eight filings from the FY2024 10-K (filed November 2024) through the Q3 FY2026 10-Q (filed
July 2026). Updated November 2026 for the FY2026 10-K (filed November 6, 2026). Fiscal year ends
September 30. Stances move only when two or more filings point the same way; every pillar started the
window at Intact.

P1 Scores pricing power
  Bull assumes: the FICO Score stays the standard in U.S. lending, so B2B unit price increases (above all
    on mortgage origination scores) keep lifting Scores revenue 20%+ a year at a ~90% segment margin.
  Bear fears: growth rests on one lever (mortgage score price); volume is cyclical, and a sequential
    decline or a price rollback would show the lever is exhausted.
  Stance: Intact (moved twice: to Strengthening at the Q2 FY2026 10-Q, after Q1 FY2026 margin 88% from 86%
    on "increased unit pricing" and Q2 FY2026 Scores +60% at a 91% margin; back to Intact at the FY2026
    10-K, because two filings in a row show B2B Scores falling sequentially ($417.6M Q2, $400.0M Q3, a
    derived $355.9M Q4) and growth slowing (+72%, +49%, +39%), while the 10-K firms the pricing-limit risk
    ("may limit") and gives Score 10T free to lenders who buy the Classic score. Full year: Scores +41%,
    margin 90%, no one-off).
  Tests: B2B Scores revenue sequentially and year over year, the attribution sentence, Scores margin, any
    mortgage price change after VantageScore's GSE and FHA start dates.

P2 Mortgage regulation, competition and litigation
  Bull assumes: FHFA, the GSEs and the CFPB leave FICO's mortgage position and pricing alone; VantageScore
    stays marginal; the antitrust suit is contained.
  Bear fears: score choice at the GSEs, bi-merge, bureau-controlled pricing and the surviving Sherman Act
    Section 2 claim erode price or share.
  Stance: Weakening (moved at the Q1 FY2025 10-Q: the FY2024 10-K added a CFPB closing-cost risk factor
    and a bi-merge risk; Q1 FY2025 disclosed the antitrust class action, with the Section 2 claim allowed
    into discovery; the FY2025 10-K added FHFA score choice and bureau pricing as a constraint, while the
    CFPB inquiry dropped out of the risk factor). FY2026 10-K: one bull assumption has failed (FHFA accepted
    VantageScore from all lenders in September 2026; FHA follows from January 1, 2027; the risk heading
    says a competing score "has" hurt the stock price), another held (the suit is settling for $65.0M plus
    undisclosed compliance undertakings). Not yet Broken: the filing reports no revenue effect and calls
    VantageScore adoption "difficult to predict".
  Tests: VantageScore usage at the GSEs and FHA, any mortgage score price cut, Score 10T implementation
    date, court approval and the compliance undertakings, the FY2027 10-Q risk-factor stubs.

P3 Bureau channel concentration
  Bull assumes: the three bureaus are distributors, not counterparties with leverage; concentration is a
    by-product of score pricing.
  Bear fears: the bureaus (which own VantageScore) collect a rising share of FICO revenue and receivables
    and can constrain FICO's mortgage pricing.
  Stance: Weakening (moved at the Q1 FY2025 10-Q: 45% of FY2024 revenue (41%), then 44% of Q1 FY2025
    (39%); since then 52%, 54%, 51% for FY2025, 64% in Q2 FY2026, 63% in Q3 FY2026 and 60% for FY2026;
    three customers each 10%+ of receivables). The FY2026 10-K's Mortgage Direct License Program is the
    first stated route around the bureaus; it has no revenue yet.
  Tests: bureau share of revenue and receivables, bureau-pricing language in the 10-K, any revenue or
    adoption figure for the Mortgage Direct License Program.

P4 Software platform transition
  Bull assumes: FICO Platform replaces the legacy (non-platform) book and lifts ARR growth back to
    double digits with net retention above 110%.
  Bear fears: non-platform attrition and hosting costs keep compressing the segment margin while ARR
    growth stalls.
  Stance: Intact (moved twice: to Weakening at the Q3 FY2025 10-Q after DBNRR fell to 102% and margin
    slipped in Q2 and Q3 FY2025; back to Intact at the Q3 FY2026 10-Q after ARR growth of 10% and DBNRR
    109% in two filings. FY2026 10-K: ARR +14%, DBNRR 112%, platform 53% of ARR, meeting the bull's ARR and
    retention bar; but the margin fell for a third year, to 28%, hosting purchase obligations tripled to
    $292.6M, and the payoff claim is now "over time". Held at Intact while ARR and margin point opposite ways).
  Tests: platform vs non-platform ARR, DBNRR, Software segment margin, RPO, purchase obligations.

P5 Margins and earnings quality
  Bull assumes: operating margin keeps rising with the Scores mix, and reported EPS is recurring.
  Bear fears: tax benefits from equity awards, one-off licence renewals and rising interest flatter or
    hide the trend.
  Stance: Strengthening (moved at the Q3 FY2025 10-Q: operating margin 49.2% in Q2 FY2025 and 48.9% in
    Q3 FY2025, up 4-6 points; 58.2% in Q2 FY2026 and 53.8% in Q3 FY2026. Tax help shrank: nine-month
    ETR 23.5% vs 17.2%, so FY2026 growth is less tax-assisted than FY2025's; FY2026 10-K: operating margin
    49.9% after $92.3M of settlement and severance charges, ETR 24.1%; a non-operating equity-sale gain
    lifted other income to $57.7M).
  Tests: operating margin, ETR vs ~24%, one-off items in the Scores attribution, interest expense, other
    income back near its ~$10M run rate.

P6 Cash conversion and capital return
  Bull assumes: free cash flow tracks or beats net income and buybacks shrink the share count steadily.
  Bear fears: buybacks outrun free cash flow and are paid for with debt.
  Stance: Weakening (moved at the Q3 FY2025 10-Q: FY2024 buybacks were 132% of FCF; Q3 FY2025 buybacks
    $511.3M vs $284.4M FCF as notes were issued; then 183% of FY2025 FCF and $3.1B in nine months of
    FY2026 against $777.9M of operating cash flow. Conversion itself is healthy: FCF/net income 118%;
    FY2026 128%). FY2026 10-K: $3.1B of buybacks = 2.9x FCF for the year, but none in the fourth quarter,
    when $323M of debt was repaid; one filing, so no stance change.
  Tests: buybacks vs FCF, stockholders' deficit, diluted share count, whether buying resumes before the
    leverage ceiling steps down.

P7 Balance sheet and leverage
  Bull assumes: debt grows in step with EBITDA, maturities are termed out at fixed rates, covenants have
    room.
  Bear fears: debt-funded buybacks push leverage up, covenants are loosened to make room, and interest
    eats operating income growth.
  Stance: Weakening (moved at the Q3 FY2025 10-Q: debt $2.2B -> $2.5B over the first two FY2025 quarters,
    then the amended credit agreement dropped the 3.0x interest coverage covenant; since then debt $5.6B,
    a $1.5B term loan for an ASR, and the leverage ceiling lifted from 3.5x to 4.5x through December 2026;
    FY2026 10-K: debt $5.26B, down $323M from June, but $3.06B a year earlier; interest $223.3M = 17.6% of
    operating income; $1.75B of principal due in FY2028).
  Tests: total debt, covenant ceiling vs actual leverage, interest expense vs operating income,
    term-loan amortization ($337.5M due in FY2027), the FY2028 maturities.
