FY2018 → FY2019: Score Acceleration

Filed Nov 2019 | Employees: ~4,200 → ~4,100 | Revenue: $1.16B → $1.16B (flat) | Scores Rev: 33% of total

Score Pricing

▶Auto pricing added to MDA Score revenue driversChanged
FY2018 MDA attributed Scores growth to mortgage pricing alone. FY2019 expanded the attribution:
FY2018: "primarily driven by a higher unit price in mortgage activities"
FY2019: "primarily attributable to a higher unit price in mortgage and auto activities"
Adding auto as a named vertical signals FICO was expanding its pricing power beyond mortgage originations.
▶Bureau concentration increased from 25% to 29%Signal
Revenue attributable to the three major consumer reporting agencies rose from approximately 25% to 29% of total FICO revenue, reflecting growing dependency on the bureau distribution channel for score delivery.

Risk Factors

▶Competition risk factor title unchanged; "pricing strategies" not yet in titleSignal
FY2018-2019: "If our competitors introduce new products and pricing strategies, it could decrease our product sales and market share, or could pressure us to reduce our product prices in a manner that reduces our margins."
The risk factor title focused on competitors' pricing strategies, not FICO's own. This framing would change materially by FY2023.
▶Fannie/Freddie: FHFA validation regulation first citedNew
FY2019 added specific reference to FHFA's new rule:
"Laws and regulations applicable to secondary market participants (e.g., Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) that could have an impact on our scoring products, including 12 CFR Part 1254 (Validation and Approval of Credit Score Models) issued by the Federal Housing Finance Agency"
FY2018 had referenced the same statute but not the CFR citation. The regulatory risk was becoming more concrete.

FY2019 → FY2020: COVID & Score 10

Filed Nov 2020 | Employees: ~4,100 → ~4,000 | Revenue: $1.16B → $1.29B (+11%) | Scores Rev: 38% of total

Business Overview

▶"Purchase" replaced with "access" for consumer score framingChanged
The verb describing how consumers interact with FICO Scores shifted:
FY2019: "Most leading banks and credit card issuers rely on our solutions...consumers purchase and understand their FICO Scores"
FY2020: "Most leading banks and credit card issuers rely on our solutions...consumers access and understand their FICO Scores"
"Purchase" implies a direct transaction; "access" is more diffuse and accommodates CRA royalty-based delivery where consumers get FICO Scores through bureau products rather than buying directly from FICO.
▶FICO Score 10, Score 10 T, UltraFICO, and Resilience Index all namedNew
FY2020 added four new score products to the Business section: FICO Score 10 and FICO Score 10 T (incorporating trended data), the UltraFICO Score (consumer-permissioned banking data), and the FICO Resilience Index (stress-testing tool for economic downturns). This was the largest single-year expansion of the score product lineup.

Risk Factors & MDA

▶COVID-19 elevated to #1 risk factorNew
The COVID-19 pandemic was added as the first risk factor in the filing, displacing decision management strategy risk. COVID would remain the #1 risk factor through FY2021 before being demoted in FY2022 and deleted entirely in FY2023.
▶MDA pricing language adds unsecured originations and mortgage volumeChanged
FY2020: "primarily attributable to an increase in mortgage volumes, a higher unit price in auto and unsecured originations"
Three changes from FY2019: (1) mortgage attributed to volume not price, (2) "unsecured" added as a pricing vertical, (3) large royalty true-up and annual license deal disclosed. The pricing narrative was expanding across more product lines.

FY2020 → FY2021: Segment Restructure

Filed Nov 2021 | Employees: ~4,000 → ~3,800 | Revenue: $1.29B → $1.32B (+2%) | Scores Rev: 42% of total

Business Overview

▶Self-description rewritten: "provides products, solutions and services" → "is a leading applied analytics company"Changed
The opening sentence of the entire 10-K changed:
FY2020: "Fair Isaac Corporation...provides products, solutions and services that enable businesses to automate, improve and connect decisions to enhance business performance."
FY2021: "Fair Isaac Corporation...is a leading applied analytics company."
A functional description replaced by an identity claim. The paragraph-long explanation of predictive analytics and decision management systems was deleted entirely.
▶Three segments collapsed to two (Applications + DMS → Software)Changed
FICO operated three segments (Applications, Scores, Decision Management Software) through FY2020. FY2021 merged Applications and DMS into a single "Software" segment. The filing became Scores + Software, simplifying comparison but obscuring the relative performance of legacy applications vs. platform products.
▶All three bureaus individually disclosed above 10% of revenueNew
For the first time, each of the three major consumer reporting agencies (Experian, TransUnion, Equifax) individually exceeded 10% of total FICO revenue, triggering individual disclosure. Combined bureau concentration crossed approximately 33% of total revenue.
▶Stock buybacks quadrupled to $882MSignal
Buybacks escalated from approximately $200M in FY2020 to $882M in FY2021, the beginning of the aggressive capital return program that would drive stockholders' equity negative.

Risk Factors

▶Fannie/Freddie risk factor appears as standaloneNew
A dedicated risk factor for Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac score requirements appeared for the first time:
"A significant portion of our revenues in our Scores segment is attributable to the U.S. mortgage market, which includes, for conforming mortgages in that market, a requirement of...Fannie Mae...and...Freddie Mac...that U.S. lenders provide FICO Scores for each mortgage delivered to them. However, their continued use of the FICO Score is currently subject to validation and approval by those enterprises and the Federal Housing Finance Agency."
Previously, Fannie/Freddie appeared only in the general regulatory list. This elevation to a standalone risk factor signaled growing concern about score mandates.
▶MDA pricing language becomes generic: named verticals disappearChanged
FY2020: "a higher unit price in auto and unsecured originations"
FY2021: "a higher unit price across several business-to-business offerings"
Mortgage, auto, and unsecured lending were no longer individually named. The generic "several business-to-business offerings" phrase obscured which verticals were experiencing price increases and by how much.

FY2021 → FY2022: Platform Focus

Filed Nov 2022 | Employees: ~3,800 → ~3,600 | Revenue: $1.32B → $1.38B (+5%) | Scores Rev: 46% of total

Business Overview

▶Software strategy risk elevated to #1 (COVID demoted)Changed
COVID-19 was demoted from the #1 operational risk factor. The risk that FICO's software platform transition might fail replaced it at the top of the risk factors section. This signaled management's view that the platform bet was the company's most material strategic uncertainty.
▶CRAs explicitly listed as customers for the first timeNew
The three major consumer reporting agencies were described as customers of FICO's scoring products, not just distribution partners. This dual role (customer and partner) would become central to the competition narrative by FY2025.
▶EU AI Act first mentioned in risk factorsNew
"The European Commission has also released draft proposed regulations (i.e., the EU AI Act) that would establish requirements for the provision and use of products that leverage artificial intelligence, machine learning, and similar analytic and statistical modeling technologies, including credit scoring."
This appeared in FY2023's filing (for the FY2022→23 transition period) and represented the first time EU AI regulation was named as a specific risk to FICO's business.
▶Debt increased from $1.3B to $1.9BSignal
Total long-term debt rose approximately $600M year-over-year, funding continued buybacks. The debt trajectory would accelerate sharply in subsequent years.

MDA & Score Pricing

▶Score pricing remains generic; mortgage volume decline first disclosed as offsetChanged
FY2022: "primarily attributable to a higher unit price across several business-to-business offerings and an increase in unsecured credit originations volume, partially offset by a decrease in mortgage originations volume"
For the first time, declining mortgage volume appeared as a negative offset to score revenue growth, even as "higher unit price" continued to drive the segment upward.

FY2022 → FY2023: Pricing Under Scrutiny

Filed Nov 2023 | Employees: ~3,600 → ~3,500 | Revenue: $1.38B → $1.51B (+10%) | Scores Rev: 51% of total

Risk Factors

▶"Pricing strategies" first appears in FICO's own risk factor titleNew
The competition risk factor title was rewritten to name FICO's own pricing as a risk:
FY2022 and prior: "If our competitors introduce new products and pricing strategies, it could decrease our product sales and market share"
FY2023: "We are subject to significant competition in the markets in which we operate, and our products and pricing strategies, and those of our competitors, could decrease our product sales and market share."
The shift from "our competitors'" pricing to "our products and pricing strategies, and those of our competitors" acknowledged that FICO's own pricing decisions had become a material risk.
▶AI added to CFPB regulatory concern languageNew
"there has been an increased focus on laws and regulations related to our business...including by the current U.S. presidential administration, the U.S. Congress, and U.S. regulators, including the CFPB, relating to policy concerns with regard to...the use, transparency, and fairness of algorithms, artificial intelligence, and machine learning in business processes."
AI was added to the list of CFPB concerns for the first time, alongside credit data accuracy and fair lending.
▶COVID-19 risk factor deleted entirelyRemoved
The standalone COVID-19 risk factor, which had been #1 in FY2020-21 and was demoted in FY2022, was removed completely from the filing.

MDA & Score Pricing

▶Score pricing language reaches maximum opacityChanged
FY2023: "primarily attributable to a higher unit price, partially offset by a decrease in mortgage originations volume"
"Several business-to-business offerings" was dropped. The driver is now simply "a higher unit price" with no vertical attribution. Simultaneously, B2C revenue declined $18.4M as direct-to-consumer sales fell while bureau royalties continued growing.
▶Countries served dropped from ~120 to ~100Changed
The number of countries where FICO operates declined from "nearly 120" to "over 100," a reduction that was not explained in the filing.

FY2023 → FY2024: Regulators Reach Score Pricing

Filed Nov 6, 2024 | Revenue: $1.51B → $1.72B (+13%) | Operating income: $642.8M → $733.6M (+14%) | Net income: $429.4M → $512.8M | Scores: $773.8M → $919.7M (51% → 54% of revenue) | Bureau share: 41% → 45% | Buybacks: $407.3M → $833.3M against operating cash flow of $633.0M | Long-term debt: $1.81B → $2.19B

Risk Factors

▶New risk factor: closing-cost scrutiny "may affect our ability to implement price changes for FICO® Scores used in mortgage originations," citing the CFPB's May 2024 inquiryNew

Shift: new risk (+ disclosure); named +CFPB May 2024 inquiry; pricing named as the thing at risk

FY2024: "Increased regulatory focus on U.S. residential mortgage closing costs may affect our ability to implement price changes for FICO® Scores used in mortgage originations and thus limit the revenues and profitability of the FICO Score."
FY2024: "For example, in May 2024, the CFPB launched a public inquiry to obtain information on fees charged by providers of mortgages and related settlement services in the U.S. residential mortgage market, including fees for credit reports and credit scores."

History: "CFPB launched a public inquiry" appears in the FY2024 10-K only; the FY2025 10-K deletes it and keeps the heading. No 10-Q in the set mentions the CFPB.

Earlier filings described regulatory interest in credit data and algorithms in general terms. This one names mortgage score pricing, the main driver of Scores growth, as the thing a regulator could cap: the paragraph ends that "our ability in the future to increase pricing for FICO Scores used in mortgage originations may be impacted."
▶Fannie/Freddie risk factor moves from paragraph 63 to 30 and adds the bi-merge scenarioChanged

Shift: order up (33 paragraphs); condition added, a second route to lost volume (+ disclosure)

FY2024: "Other changes implemented by FHFA, Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac could also affect the demand for FICO Scores and thus could have similar adverse effects on our business, including, for example, a change permitting mortgage originators to underwrite loans using credit scores from only two of the three national consumer reporting agencies (a “bi-merge report”) rather than from all three (a “tri-merge report”)."

History: the heading "If use of the FICO® Score by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac were to cease or decline" sits at paragraph 63 of Item 1A in FY2023, 30 in FY2024 and 29 in FY2025. "Bi-merge" appears in the FY2024 10-K only; FY2025 replaces it with "fewer than three."

Until FY2023 the risk was framed only as the FICO Score losing approval. The new sentence names a way FICO could lose mortgage volume while keeping that approval: one fewer bureau pull per loan file means one fewer score sold.
▶Regulatory-focus paragraph adds "the sale and distribution of credit scores and credit reports" and a planned CFPB FCRA rulemakingChanged

Shift: scope narrowed onto FICO's own product (+ disclosure); "business of our customers" → "business of consumer reporting agencies"

FY2023: "In addition, there has been an increased focus on laws and regulations related to our business and the business of our customers, including by the current U.S. presidential administration, the U.S. Congress, and U.S. regulators, including the CFPB, relating to policy concerns with regard to the operation of consumer reporting agencies, the use and accuracy of credit and alternative data"
FY2024: "There has also been increased focus more broadly on laws and regulations in the U.S. related to our business and the business of consumer reporting agencies, including by U.S. state and federal regulators such as the CFPB, relating to policy concerns with regard to the operation of consumer reporting agencies, the sale and distribution of credit scores and credit reports"
FY2024: "For example, the CFPB has indicated that it intends to issue rules under the FCRA that would extend the FCRA to certain business practices not currently subject to that statute."

History: "the sale and distribution of credit scores and credit reports" first appears in FY2024 and is kept in FY2025; "intends to issue rules under the FCRA" appears in FY2024 only.

The list of policy concerns now starts with how scores are sold and distributed, which is the bureau channel that carries most FICO Score revenue. Read with the new pricing risk factor, the regulatory exposure is described as falling on FICO's distribution model, not only on its customers.
▶Bureau "alliance" becomes a "joint venture" selling a competing scoreChanged

Shift: degree, "alliance" → "joint venture" (+ firmer characterization)

FY2023: "For example, Experian, TransUnion and Equifax have formed an alliance that is selling a credit scoring product competitive with our products."
FY2024: "For example, Experian, TransUnion and Equifax have formed a joint venture that is selling a credit scoring product competitive with our products."

History: "formed an alliance" appears in the FY2023 10-K only; "joint venture that is selling" in the FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks.

A joint venture is a jointly owned entity rather than a loose arrangement. The bureaus, which generated 45% of FICO's revenue this year, are described in sharper terms as owners of a competing product.

MD&A & Financials

▶FHFA appears in the MD&A: Score 10 T "will be implemented" for conforming mortgages on FHFA's timelineNew

Shift: certainty, "continued to promote adoption" → "will be implemented" (+); named +Federal Housing Finance Agency

FY2023: "We continued to promote adoption of our most predictive scores, FICO® Score 10 and 10 T."
FY2024: "The adoption of our most predictive scores, FICO® Score 10 and 10 T, gained increased traction for non-conforming mortgages and will be implemented for conforming mortgages based on the timeline set forth by the Federal Housing Finance Agency for enterprise credit scoring requirements."

History: "will be implemented for conforming" appears in the FY2024 10-K only; FY2025 changes it to "was approved for conforming."

The timing is handed to FHFA. The same filing moves the Fannie/Freddie risk factor up and adds the bi-merge risk, so FHFA appears as both the route for FICO's newest score and the source of its largest mortgage risk.
▶Foreign earnings no longer all "permanently reinvested": $2.8M of withholding-tax liability booked as unremitted earnings rise to $82.6MChanged

Shift: realized, no deferred tax provided → $2.8 million provided (+ quant added); assertion narrowed from "will be permanently reinvested" to "not permanently reinvested" for certain subsidiaries

FY2023: "The Company has not provided deferred tax liabilities for foreign withholding taxes and certain state income taxes on the undistributed earnings and profits from certain non-U.S. subsidiaries that will be permanently reinvested outside the United States."
FY2024: "The Company has provided $2.8 million of deferred tax liabilities for foreign withholding taxes on the undistributed earnings and profits from certain non-U.S. subsidiaries that are not permanently reinvested outside the U.S."

History: "will be permanently reinvested" appears in the FY2023 10-K only. The FY2024 wording carries into FY2025 with the liability at $3.2 million. Unremitted earnings were $27.2 million at September 30, 2023 and $82.6 million a year later.

Booking a withholding liability means FICO now expects to bring some foreign earnings home. The amounts are small next to $833.3M of buybacks, but the change is consistent with cash being pulled to the U.S. parent in the year buybacks doubled and exceeded operating cash flow (an inference; the note gives no reason).
▶B2B Scores +$150.8M on price alone while mortgage volume fell; wording unchanged for a second yearSignal

Shift: none in the driver (word order only); quant, B2B growth $85.6M → $150.8M

FY2023: "The increase in business-to-business scores revenue was primarily attributable to a higher unit price, partially offset by a decrease in mortgage originations volume."
FY2024: "The increase in business-to-business scores revenue was primarily attributable to a higher unit price, partially offset by a decrease in volume of mortgage originations."

History: the FY2024 wording first appeared in the Q2 FY2024 10-Q and ran through the FY2024 10-K; "an increase in volume of mortgage originations" replaced it from Q1 FY2025.

With mortgage volume still a headwind, price did all of the work. B2C fell $5.0M on lower myFICO.com direct sales. Scores reached 54% of revenue and segment margin held at 88%. This is the first year in which a risk factor says a regulator could cap that pricing.
▶ACV Bookings demoted from "a more meaningful measure" to "a useful supplemental measure" as DBNRR falls 120% → 106%Changed

Shift: degree, "more meaningful" → "useful supplemental" (−)

FY2023: "However, we believe ACV Bookings is a more meaningful measure of our business as it includes estimated revenues and future billings excluded from Note 11"
FY2024: "However, we believe ACV Bookings is a useful supplemental measure of our business as it includes estimated revenues and future billings excluded from Note 9"

History: "a more meaningful measure" appears through the Q1 FY2024 10-Q; "useful supplemental measure" from the Q2 FY2024 10-Q onward, so the 10-K confirms a change first made in March 2024.

The downgrade came as the headline software metrics slowed: ARR growth fell to 8% ($721.2M) from 22%, and DBNRR fell to 106% from 120%, with non-platform DBNRR at 99%. A new sentence says usage-based estimates are "approximately 30%" of ACV Bookings.

Business Overview

▶Bureau concentration rises to 45% of revenue from 41%Signal

Shift: quant, 41% → 45% (wording unchanged)

FY2024: "During fiscal 2024, 2023 and 2022, revenues generated from our agreements with Experian, TransUnion and Equifax collectively accounted for 45%, 41% and 39% of our total revenues, respectively."

History: the sentence appears in every 10-K in the set (FY2023 41%, FY2024 45%, FY2025 51%).

A four-point rise in one year, in the same filing that calls these partners owners of a competing product.
▶India overtakes the U.S. as FICO's largest employee base: 1,367 (38%) against 1,309 (36%)Signal

Shift: quant, country ranking reversed (wording unchanged)

FY2023: "Of these, our largest representation includes 1,283 (37%) based in the United States, 1,259 (36%) based in India and 270 (8%) based in the United Kingdom."
FY2024: "Of these, our largest representation includes 1,309 (36%) based in the U.S., 1,367 (38%) based in India and 264 (7%) based in the U.K."

History: "based in India" appears in each 10-K in the set; India was second in FY2023 and is first in FY2024 and FY2025.

India added 108 people against 26 in the U.S. The FY2025 10-K adds a risk-factor sentence on India–Pakistan tensions for this workforce.
▶Footprint claim cut: "more than 100" countries becomes "more than 80"Changed

Shift: quant down, 100 → 80 countries (−)

FY2024: "FICO provides software solutions to business customers in more than 80 countries around the world."

History: "more than 100 countries" appears in the FY2023 10-K and the three FY2024 10-Qs; "more than 80 countries" from the FY2024 10-K onward, including every later 10-Q.

The customer list also falls from eight to seven of the Fortune 500 top ten. None of these cuts is explained. They fit the software strategy of concentrating on North American financial institutions, which the FY2024 MD&A calls "a significant portion of our short-term opportunity."
▶"Diversity, Inclusion and Belonging" heading renamed "Organizational Culture"; the programs stay and diversity metrics are addedChanged

Shift: de-named heading (−); quant added, workforce gender and diversity percentages (+)

FY2024: "Across our global workforce, as of September 30, 2024, the percentage of males and females was 67% and 33%, respectively."

History: the heading "Diversity, Inclusion and Belonging" appears in the FY2023 10-K only. "Affirmative Action Program" and "Commitment to Inclusion and Belonging" appear in FY2023 and FY2024 and are gone in FY2025.

FY2024 changed the labels; the content was cut in FY2025 (see next transition).

FY2024 → FY2025: Score Choice, Antitrust & Leverage

Filed Nov 7, 2025 | Employees: 3,586 → 3,811 | Revenue: $1.72B → $1.99B (+16%) | Operating income: $733.6M → $924.9M (+26%) | Net income: $512.8M → $651.9M (+27%) | Scores: $919.7M → $1.17B (54% → 59%) | Total debt: $2.2B → $3.1B | Buybacks: $833.3M → $1.4B against operating cash flow of $778.8M

Risk Factors & Legal Proceedings

▶FHFA risk rewritten around the July 2025 score-choice announcement; "two of the three" bureaus widened to "fewer than three"Changed

Shift: realized, hypothetical example → "the change announced by the FHFA Director in July 2025" (+); scope widened, "only two of the three" → "fewer than three" (+)

FY2024: "...including, for example, a change permitting mortgage originators to underwrite loans using credit scores from only two of the three national consumer reporting agencies (a “bi-merge report”) rather than from all three"
FY2025: "...including, for example, the change announced by the FHFA Director in July 2025 permitting mortgage originators to choose the credit score they submit with mortgages delivered to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac or a potential future change permitting mortgage originators to underwrite loans using credit scores from fewer than three national consumer reporting agencies."

History: "FHFA Director in July 2025" and "fewer than three" appear in the FY2025 10-K only; "bi-merge" in FY2024 only. The Q3 FY2025 10-Q, filed July 30, 2025, after the announcement, kept its "no material changes" risk-factor stub.

Score choice is now an announced change, not a hypothetical, and "fewer than three" also covers a single-file pull. The MD&A meanwhile reports that Score 10 T "was approved for conforming mortgages" by FHFA, replacing FY2024's "will be implemented."
▶New sentence: bureau pricing "may constrain" the FICO Score's ability to compete in mortgageNew

Shift: new risk, counterparty named as a threat (+ disclosure)

FY2025: "The ability of our FICO Score to compete effectively in the U.S. mortgage market may be constrained by the pricing and other business practices of the consumer reporting agencies, which could have a material adverse effect on our revenues, results of operations and stock price."

History: "may be constrained by the pricing" appears in the FY2025 10-K only; no 10-Q in the set carries it.

This is the first time FICO says in writing that the bureaus' own pricing could cost the FICO Score share once lenders have a choice of score. The wording suggests that what the bureaus charge on top of FICO's royalty is now a competitive issue, though the filing does not say how. The bureaus generated 51% of FICO's revenue this year.
▶CFPB, FTC, "the current presidential administration" and the May 2024 inquiry deleted from the mortgage-fee risk factorRemoved

Shift: de-named, CFPB / FTC / administration → "increased regulatory focus" (−); scope narrowed to mortgage closing fees

FY2024: "There has been increased focus in the U.S. by federal regulators such as the CFPB and the FTC, as well as the current presidential administration and some states, related to the transparency and fairness of certain fees charged to consumers"
FY2025: "There has been increased regulatory focus in the U.S. related to the transparency and fairness of certain fees charged to consumers in connection with the closing of a residential mortgage loan, including fees for credit reports and credit scores."

History: "CFPB launched a public inquiry" and "intends to issue rules under the FCRA" appear in the FY2024 10-K only. The heading on limits to mortgage score price changes is kept in FY2025.

The agency is gone from the text, but the warning that price increases could be limited remains, now without the "result from this inquiry" trigger. The CFPB is also dropped from the broader regulatory-focus paragraph.
▶First legal proceeding in a 10-K: a Sherman Act Section 2 claim against FICO alone survives dismissalNew

Shift: named +lawsuit; "Not applicable" → disclosed proceeding (+ disclosure)

FY2025: "The court dismissed with prejudice all claims in the lawsuit other than a Sherman Act Section 2 claim and accompanying state law claims against FICO, which were allowed to proceed through the discovery stage of the litigation. FICO intends to vigorously defend against the remaining claims in this proceeding."

History: "Sherman Act" first appears in the Q1 FY2025 10-Q (filed February 2025) and in every filing since; "vigorously defend" from the Q3 FY2025 10-Q. The FY2024 10-K's Item 3 read "Not applicable."

The consolidated putative class actions in the Northern District of Illinois name FICO and the three bureaus, but only the monopolization claim against FICO survived the November 2024 ruling. In the same filing "Antitrust and unfair competition laws" becomes a new bullet in the Item 1A regulatory list. No loss estimate is given.
▶Cloud-migration security risk restated as present: "We may be exposed" → "We are exposed"Changed

Shift: certainty, may → are (+ firmer); realized, hypothetical → actual

FY2024: "We may be exposed to additional cybersecurity threats as we migrate our software solutions and data from our legacy systems to cloud-based solutions."
FY2025: "We are exposed to additional cybersecurity threats as we continue to migrate our software solutions and data from our legacy systems to cloud-based solutions."

History: the "may be exposed" sentence stood unchanged in the FY2023 and FY2024 10-Ks; "We are exposed" appears in FY2025 only.

The migration that drives third-party hosting costs (cited for the Software margin decline this year) is now described as an exposure FICO has, not one it might have. The filing names no incident.
▶Growth claim on new markets softened: "will rest on" → "may depend on"Changed

Shift: certainty, will → may (− weaker)

FY2024: "We believe much of the future growth of our business and the success of our business strategy will rest on our ability to continue to expand into newer markets for our products and services."
FY2025: "We also believe that much of the future growth of our business and the success of our business strategy may depend on our ability to continue to expand into newer markets for our products and services."

History: "will rest on our ability" appears in the FY2023 and FY2024 10-Ks; "may depend on our ability" in FY2025 only. The same paragraph's "our revenues will decrease" became "our revenues may decrease."

The paragraph was folded into the new-products risk factor and hedged twice. Read with international revenue falling to 23% from 27%, new markets are presented as less central to growth than a year earlier (an inference from the wording).
▶International revenue falls to 23% from 27%; an India–Pakistan risk is named for the one-third of staff in IndiaNew

Shift: named +India and Pakistan (+ disclosure); quant, non-U.S. revenue 27% → 23%

Themes

Bureau Concentration Doubled

25% (FY2018) to 51% (FY2025). All three bureaus individually >10% since FY2021. Over half of all FICO revenue flows through three companies that simultaneously serve as customers, distribution partners, and competitors (via VantageScore).

Score Pricing Opacity

MDA language evolved from naming specific verticals (mortgage, auto, unsecured) to generic "higher unit price." Scores grew from 33% to 59% of revenue with approximately 88% operating margins. The pricing disclosure became less specific as the pricing itself became more aggressive.

Regulatory Arc

No CFPB risk (FY2018-22) to a new risk factor tying the CFPB's closing-cost inquiry to FICO's mortgage score price increases (FY2024), to the agency being deleted from that risk factor while the pricing warning stays (FY2025). FY2025 adds FHFA score choice, a bureau-pricing constraint, and the first legal proceeding: a surviving Sherman Act Section 2 claim against FICO. The challenge to the pricing model moved from regulators to the market and the courts.

Debt-Funded Buybacks

Total debt: $845M to $3.1B. Cumulative buybacks ~$5.5B. Stockholders' deficit: -$1.75B at FY2025 (from -$688M at FY2023). Revenue per employee: $281K to $522K. FY2025 buybacks ($1.4B) ran at nearly twice operating cash flow, and the interest-coverage covenant was removed. High-margin score cash flows service debt that funds buybacks.

Software Transition

Three segments collapsed to two (FY2021). Platform ARR rose to 35% of software ARR, but total DBNRR fell from 120% (FY2023) to 102% (FY2025), ARR growth to 4%, and Software operating income declined in FY2025 alongside a $10.9M restructuring. The opening sentence calls FICO a "software leader" while Scores supplies 59% of revenue and most of the profit.

Score Product Expansion

Score 9 to 10/10T to UltraFICO to Score XD to BNPL scores to Mortgage Simulator. International: ~20 to ~30 countries. FHFA conforming mortgage approval achieved for Score 10 T. The product lineup is expanding even as the pricing language becomes more opaque.
