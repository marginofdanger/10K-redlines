GOOG 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of 10 SEC filings (eight 10-Qs plus the FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks). The annual dashboard follows Alphabet's product story; this scanner follows the money behind it: the revenue backlog, off-balance-sheet commitments, data-center backstops, and the move from buybacks to debt to new equity, which the notes changed every quarter and the risk factors mostly did not.

Guidance & MD&A

▶ AI model teams moved out of Google Services into Google DeepMind, reported in Alphabet-level activities New

Q2 2024 segment note, with no Q1 2024 predecessor:

Q2 2024: "As announced on April 18, 2024, we further consolidated teams that focus on building AI models across Google Research and Google DeepMind to further accelerate our progress in AI. AI model development teams previously under Google Research in our Google Services segment are included as part of Google DeepMind, reported within Alphabet-level activities, prospectively beginning in the second quarter of 2024."

Model-building costs leave the segment that earns the ad revenue and sit in the unallocated line. The annual dashboard dates the "Alphabet-level activities" reporting structure to the FY2024 10-K; the quarterly filing shows when the costs actually moved. By the FY2025 10-K, Alphabet-level activities are described as "developing the frontier models that serve our businesses."

▶ Cost of revenues adds a retroactive Canadian digital services tax and a new "technical infrastructure operations" line Changed

Q1 2024 MD&A cost bullet listed TAC, content acquisition costs and depreciation, offset by lower severance and office-space charges. Q2 2024:

Q2 2024: "Cost of revenues was $35.5 billion, an increase of 11% year over year, primarily driven by increases in content acquisition costs, TAC, depreciation expense, digital services tax related to the recently enacted law in Canada, which is applied retroactively, and other technical infrastructure operations costs."

The cost-of-revenues definition also gains a fourth component and relabels the compensation line from "data centers" to "technical infrastructure":

Q2 2024: "other technical infrastructure operations costs, including bandwidth, energy, and equipment costs."

Energy enters the cost definition here for the first time in the window. The Canadian tax closes as a loop in Q1 2026, when the MD&A credits "an accrual reversal for digital services tax related to the recently repealed law in Canada."

▶ YouTube growth driver reordered: brand ahead of direct response Changed
Q1 2024: "The growth was driven by our direct response and brand advertising products, both of which benefited from increased spending by our advertisers."
Q2 2024: "The growth was driven by our brand advertising products followed by our direct response advertising products, both of which benefited from increased spending by our advertisers."

The order held through the FY2024 10-K and flipped back in Q1 2025 ("direct response advertising products followed by our brand advertising products"), where it stayed through Q2 2026. The ordering is the only qualitative read on YouTube's ad mix the filing gives.

Risk Factors

▶ Third-party cookie phase-out becomes a "proposed user choice model" Signal
Q1 2024: "Changes to our advertising policies and data privacy practices, such as our initiatives to phase out third-party cookies (which remain subject to review, affecting the timing), as well as changes to other companies' advertising and/or data privacy practices..."
Q2 2024: "Changes to our advertising policies and data privacy practices, such as our initiatives related to third-party cookies, including our recent announcement to move from phasing out all third-party cookies to a proposed user choice model (which remains subject to continuing discussions with regulators)..."

The commitment to remove cookies is withdrawn inside a quarter, and the open variable moves from "timing" to "regulators." The 10-Q caught the reversal one filing after it happened; the annual dashboard records the same language in the FY2024 10-K and its removal in the FY2025 10-K.

Themes

1. From Buyback Machine to Capital Raiser in Nine Quarters

Q1 2024 opened with $15.7B of quarterly repurchases, $11.9B of notes and a newly launched dividend. Commercial paper followed in Q3 2024, the first bond issue in May 2025, $37.3B of notes in 2025 and $31.1B more in Q1 2026, with buybacks falling to zero. Q2 2026 added a $49.6B equity raise, a mandatory convertible preferred and a $40B ATM. The buyback risk factor now says repurchases may not happen "in the near term or at all." Q2 2026 free cash flow was negative (−$5.86B), the first such quarter in the window, so the new equity funds capex rather than returns. Most steps appeared first in the debt and equity notes, not in Item 1A.

2. The Backlog and the Commitments Raced; Commitments Pulled Ahead

Revenue backlog went from $72.5B to $519.5B, almost all of it Google Cloud and including TPU hardware sales from Q1 2026. Purchase commitments went from $44.0B to $811.0B, unstarted data-center leases from $3.6B to $85.2B, and third-party backstops from zero to $51.4B. In Q2 2026 commitments exceeded backlog for the first time, and the same filing stopped sizing the TPU deals in gigawatts and dropped the sentence linking them to backstops. The risk factors added "excess capacity that we cannot easily redeploy" only in the FY2025 10-K.

3. Notes Moved, Risk Factors Lagged

All three 2025 10-Qs reported no material risk-factor changes. Over the same quarters the notes added a $32.0B acquisition, the first bond issue, a $1.4B privacy settlement, a $3.5B EC fine, a 2026 capex step-up and the first data-center backstops. The backlog definition and the commitments definition were each rewritten mid-year. Quarterly reading of the notes, not Item 1A, is where the balance-sheet shift showed up first.

4. Legal Exposure Became Cash

The annual dashboard tracks the DOJ arc from ruling to appeal. The quarterly filings show the payments: $3.0B for Shopping (Q3 2024), a $1.4B privacy accrual (Q2 2025), $3.5B for ad tech (Q3 2025), a first aggregate accrual of $15.6B (FY2025), and $2.1B PriceRunner plus $5.2B Android in Q2 2026, when accruals reached $17.4B. Private follow-on claims after regulatory findings are now a named category. The wording moved the other way: from Q3 2025 the filings stop saying courts found Google "violated" antitrust laws and say they "ruled against Google".

5. Sustainability Out, Energy In

The sustainability-linked credit facility was terminated three years early (Q1 2025), the ESG risk heading (FY2024) and "long-term sustainability goals" bullet (FY2025) were deleted, and Pillar Two warnings were softened. Energy meanwhile moved from an indeterminable footnote to the first item in the commitments list, then to take-or-pay contracts through 2054, a renewable developer acquisition (Intersect) and power-equipment guarantees. This extends the annual dashboard's "Sustainability Disappeared" theme with dates.
