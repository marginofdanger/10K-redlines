GOOG 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of 10 SEC filings (eight 10-Qs plus the FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks). The annual dashboard follows Alphabet's product story; this scanner follows the money behind it: the revenue backlog, off-balance-sheet commitments, data-center backstops, and the move from buybacks to debt to new equity, which the notes changed every quarter and the risk factors mostly did not.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001652044) | 10 Filings | Q1 2024 – Q2 2026 | Analysis: October 2026

Q1 2024 → Q2 2024

Signed Jul 23, 2024 | Revenue: $84.74B (+14%) | Operating income: $27.43B | Google Cloud: $10.35B (+29%) | Capex: $13.19B | Backlog: $72.5B → $78.8B | Employees: 180,895 → 179,582

Guidance & MD&A

▶ AI model teams moved out of Google Services into Google DeepMind, reported in Alphabet-level activities New

Q2 2024 segment note, with no Q1 2024 predecessor:

Q2 2024: "As announced on April 18, 2024, we further consolidated teams that focus on building AI models across Google Research and Google DeepMind to further accelerate our progress in AI. AI model development teams previously under Google Research in our Google Services segment are included as part of Google DeepMind, reported within Alphabet-level activities, prospectively beginning in the second quarter of 2024."

Model-building costs leave the segment that earns the ad revenue and sit in the unallocated line. The annual dashboard dates the "Alphabet-level activities" reporting structure to the FY2024 10-K; the quarterly filing shows when the costs actually moved. By the FY2025 10-K, Alphabet-level activities are described as "developing the frontier models that serve our businesses."

▶ Cost of revenues adds a retroactive Canadian digital services tax and a new "technical infrastructure operations" line Changed

Q1 2024 MD&A cost bullet listed TAC, content acquisition costs and depreciation, offset by lower severance and office-space charges. Q2 2024:

Q2 2024: "Cost of revenues was $35.5 billion, an increase of 11% year over year, primarily driven by increases in content acquisition costs, TAC, depreciation expense, digital services tax related to the recently enacted law in Canada, which is applied retroactively, and other technical infrastructure operations costs."

The cost-of-revenues definition also gains a fourth component and relabels the compensation line from "data centers" to "technical infrastructure":

Q2 2024: "other technical infrastructure operations costs, including bandwidth, energy, and equipment costs."

Energy enters the cost definition here for the first time in the window. The Canadian tax closes as a loop in Q1 2026, when the MD&A credits "an accrual reversal for digital services tax related to the recently repealed law in Canada."

▶ YouTube growth driver reordered: brand ahead of direct response Changed
Q1 2024: "The growth was driven by our direct response and brand advertising products, both of which benefited from increased spending by our advertisers."
Q2 2024: "The growth was driven by our brand advertising products followed by our direct response advertising products, both of which benefited from increased spending by our advertisers."

The order held through the FY2024 10-K and flipped back in Q1 2025 ("direct response advertising products followed by our brand advertising products"), where it stayed through Q2 2026. The ordering is the only qualitative read on YouTube's ad mix the filing gives.

Risk Factors

▶ Third-party cookie phase-out becomes a "proposed user choice model" Signal
Q1 2024: "Changes to our advertising policies and data privacy practices, such as our initiatives to phase out third-party cookies (which remain subject to review, affecting the timing), as well as changes to other companies' advertising and/or data privacy practices..."
Q2 2024: "Changes to our advertising policies and data privacy practices, such as our initiatives related to third-party cookies, including our recent announcement to move from phasing out all third-party cookies to a proposed user choice model (which remains subject to continuing discussions with regulators)..."

The commitment to remove cookies is withdrawn inside a quarter, and the open variable moves from "timing" to "regulators." The 10-Q caught the reversal one filing after it happened; the annual dashboard records the same language in the FY2024 10-K and its removal in the FY2025 10-K.

Capital & Other Bets

▶ Alphabet commits up to $5.0B to Waymo New
Q2 2024: "In July 2024, Alphabet committed to fund up to $5.0 billion for the ongoing operations of Waymo, a fully autonomous driving technology company and a consolidated VIE."

The first of three escalating Waymo funding disclosures in the window: $5.6B raised in Q3 2024 ("the majority" from Alphabet), then a $16.0B round in February 2026 ("the significant majority of which was funded by Alphabet").

Legal & Regulatory

▶ DOJ Search ruling lands; remedy framework "including structural remedies" named New
Q2 2024: "The trial ended on November 16, 2023, and we expect a decision in 2024."
Q3 2024: "...on August 5, 2024, the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia ruled that Google violated such antitrust laws. A separate proceeding is being held to determine remedies, the range of which vary widely. The DOJ has proposed a high level remedy framework, which includes alterations to our products and services and our business models and operations, including structural remedies, and/or our distribution arrangements, among other changes, some of which could have a material adverse effect on our financial statements."

The same quarter's new legal risk factor carries the remedy list into Item 1A and adds "including divestiture" to the list of possible outcomes. By the FY2024 10-K the closing phrase softens from "material adverse effect on our financial statements" to "on our business."

▶ Epic remedies ordered: "We plan to appeal" becomes "We are appealing" Changed
Q2 2024: "The presiding judge will determine non-monetary remedies in 2024, and the range of potential remedies vary widely. We plan to appeal."
Q3 2024: "The presiding judge issued a remedies decision on October 7, 2024, ordering a variety of alterations to our business models and operations and contractual agreements for Android and Google Play. We are appealing and have filed a motion to pause the implementation of some of the remedies pending the appeal."

The appeal was denied in July 2025, remedies were implemented in October 2025, a settlement to modify them was reached in March 2026, and in Q2 2026 the joint motion was withdrawn and "Google is complying with the October 2024 remedies decision."

▶ 2017 EC Shopping fine upheld and paid ($3.0B); 2019 AdSense fine annulled Changed
Q3 2024: "On September 10, 2024, the European Court of Justice rejected our appeal and upheld the €2.4 billion fine imposed in 2017. In the third quarter of 2024, we made a cash payment of $3.0 billion for the 2017 shopping fine."
Q3 2024: "On September 18, 2024, the European Union's (EU) General Court overturned the EC decision and annulled the €1.5 billion fine. The EC has until November 28, 2024 to appeal the decision."

The first EC fine to convert from bank guarantee to cash in the window. Operating cash flow for the quarter is reported "including a cash payment of $3.0 billion." The 2018 Android fine follows the same path in Q2 2026 ($5.2B paid in July 2026).

Capital & Investments

▶ Character.ai licence: $2.7B cash, booked as $2.7B of goodwill New
Q3 2024: "...we recorded $2.7 billion of goodwill and $413 million of intangible assets resulting from a transaction with character.ai... In August 2024, we entered into a license agreement with Character pursuant to which we obtained a non-exclusive license to its current large language model technology. We paid Character $2.7 billion in cash and canceled our convertible instruments. We also hired certain employees of Character."

A "non-exclusive license" plus hires is accounted for as a business combination under ASC 805. The same note introduces the convertible-note book ($921M → $2.8B) and VIE language for private-company stakes that later carries the SpaceX and "private company" gains.

▶ First commercial paper in the window: $1.0B outstanding; April 2023 buyback authorization exhausted New
Q2 2024: "As of June 30, 2024, we had no commercial paper outstanding."
Q3 2024: "As of September 30, 2024, we had $1.0 billion of short-term commercial paper outstanding."
Q3 2024: "Repurchases made pursuant to the April 2023 authorization were completed during the third quarter of 2024."

Small in dollars, but it is the first borrowing in a window that ends with $98.2B of long-term debt. The CP programme is raised from $10.0B to $25.0B in Q2 2025.

▶ Search TAC rate turns up on mobile; office-space charges of $607M Signal
Q2 2024: "The TAC rate on Google Search & other revenues was substantially consistent from the three and six months ended June 30, 2023 to the three and six months ended June 30, 2024."
Q3 2024: "The TAC rate on Google Search & other revenues increased from the three and nine months ended September 30, 2023 to the three and nine months ended September 30, 2024 primarily due to increases related to mobile searches, which carries higher TAC because more mobile searches are channeled through paid access points."

Distribution payments are the economic core of the DOJ Search case, ruled on the same quarter. The Search TAC rate kept "increased" through Q2 2025 and returned to "substantially consistent" in Q3 2025, the quarter the remedies decision restricted distribution. Separately: "During the quarter ended September 30, 2024, we incurred office space charges totaling $607 million."

Guidance & MD&A

▶ Pillar Two warning deleted from MD&A: "could increase in future years" sentence removed Removed
Q3 2024: "...we do not expect a material change to our income tax provision for the 2024 fiscal year. As additional jurisdictions enact such legislation, transitional rules lapse, and other provisions of the minimum tax legislation become effective, we expect our effective tax rate and cash tax payments could increase in future years."
FY2024: "Some countries have already implemented the legislation effective January 1, 2024, and we expect others to follow, however this did not have a material effect on our income tax provision for the 2024 fiscal year."

The forward warning survives in the risk factors and the forward-looking-statements list for now. The list bullet goes in Q2 2025; the sentence returns in the FY2025 10-K and is softened to "could be affected" in Q1 2026 (tracked below).

▶ $8.0B January 2025 gain on a private-company stake disclosed as a subsequent event New
FY2024: "In January 2025, we recognized an $8.0 billion unrealized gain on our non-marketable equity securities related to our investment in a private company. The unrealized gain reflects an increase in the fair value measurement of our investment following an observable transaction in January 2025."

The first of the mark-ups that come to dominate net income: $8.0B (Q1 2025), $10.7B of equity-security gains (Q3 2025), $36.9B (Q1 2026) and $99.0B (Q2 2026). Q2 2026 net income of $112.19B is almost three times operating income of $40.77B.

▶ Network decline now blamed on Google Ad Manager and AdMob Changed
Q3 2024: "Google Network revenues decreased $121 million from the three months ended September 30, 2023 to the three months ended September 30, 2024, primarily driven by the unfavorable effect of foreign currency exchange rates, partially offset by an increase in AdSense revenues."
FY2024: "Google Network revenues decreased $953 million from 2023 to 2024, primarily driven by a decrease in Google Ad Manager and AdMob revenues."

Currency was the lead explanation in Q3; the annual names the ad-tech products. In April 2025 the court found that Google's "publisher tools" violated antitrust law, the side of the ad-tech stack where Google Ad Manager sits. Network revenue declines in every quarter of the window.

Risk Factors (FY2023 10-K → FY2024 10-K)

▶ AI accelerator supply named as a constraint; asset useful-lives risk added New
FY2024: "For example, industry supply capacity for AI accelerators, including Graphics Processing Units, or GPUs, as well as our custom-built TPUs, is highly competitive and rapidly evolving. If we are unable to negotiate favorable contractual terms or our competitors claim the supply or capacity first, we may face supply constraints."
FY2024: "We have invested significantly and expect to continue to invest significantly in our property and equipment, including our technical infrastructure, and we expect these assets to benefit our business over their estimated useful lives. Changes in facts and circumstances... could indicate a change in the period over which we expect to benefit from the asset..."

Neither sentence appears in the FY2023 10-K or any 2024 10-Q. "Power loss" in the supply-chain risk also becomes "power and transmission availability." In FY2025 "significantly" becomes "significantly expand," and Google Cloud's fluctuation factors add "supply availability."

▶ Standalone ESG risk factor deleted; "diversity" drops out of the replacement Removed
FY2023: "Expectations relating to ESG considerations could expose us to potential liabilities, increased costs, and reputational harm."
FY2024: "Further, we are subject to evolving laws, regulations, policies, and international accords relating to matters beyond our core products and services, including environmental sustainability, climate change, human capital, and employment matters."

FY2023's list was "sustainability, climate change, human capital, and diversity." The heading is gone and the sentence is folded into the general regulation risk. "Environmental, social and governance (ESG) requirements" is also removed from the list of regulated areas. This complements the annual dashboard's DEI and sustainability signals.

Guidance & MD&A

▶ Backlog dips for the only time in the window ($93.2B → $92.4B) but the 24-month share rises from "half" to 55% Signal
FY2024: "We expect to recognize approximately half of the revenue backlog as revenues over the next 24 months with the remainder to be recognized thereafter."
Q1 2025: "We expect to recognize approximately 55% of the revenue backlog as revenues over the next 24 months with the remainder to be recognized thereafter."

Near-term backlog rose even as the total fell, which suggests shorter contracts or faster consumption. The total then climbs every quarter to $519.5B (Q2 2026). The 24-month share goes to "just over 55%" (Q3 2025) and back to "just over 50%" (FY2025) once multi-year capacity deals arrive.

▶ Tariffs and "international trade" enter MD&A and the commitments paragraph New
Q1 2025: "We are monitoring ongoing developments surrounding international trade and the macroeconomic environment. As a result of volatility in international trade and financial markets, we may experience direct and indirect effects on our business, operations, and financial results."
Q1 2025: "We may experience increases in the costs associated with our purchase commitments and other contractual obligations as a result of ongoing developments surrounding international trade."

Neither appears in the FY2024 10-K MD&A. A new forward-looking-statements bullet on "the related impact on the macroeconomic environment and our business" accompanies them. Q1 2026 adds "geopolitical tension" to the front of the same paragraph.

▶ Unstarted leases jump $7.3B → $17.3B, now "primarily related to data centers" and running to 2031 Signal
FY2024: "...we have entered into leases that have not yet commenced with short-term and long-term future lease payments of $773 million and $6.5 billion, respectively, that are not yet recorded on our Consolidated Balance Sheets. These leases will commence between 2025 and 2028..."
Q1 2025: "...we have entered into leases primarily related to data centers that have not yet commenced with future lease payments of $17.3 billion, that are not yet recorded on our Consolidated Balance Sheets. These leases will commence between 2025 and 2031..."

The figure more than doubled in one quarter, and the data-center attribution appears for the first time. It reaches $85.2B by Q2 2026 (tracked below).

Capital & Financing

▶ Wiz: $32.0B all-cash acquisition agreed New
Q1 2025: "In March 2025, we entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Wiz, a leading cloud security platform, for $32.0 billion, subject to closing adjustments, in an all-cash transaction. The acquisition of Wiz is expected to close in 2026, subject to customary closing conditions, including the receipt of regulatory approvals."

The largest cash commitment in the filing at signing, roughly two quarters of capex at the time. It closed on March 11, 2026 for $29.5B "after purchase price adjustments and excluding post combination compensation arrangements."

▶ Credit facilities renewed, and the sustainability-goal pricing link is dropped Removed
FY2024: "The interest rates for all credit facilities are determined based on a formula using certain market rates, as well as our progress toward the achievement of certain sustainability goals."
Q1 2025: "In April 2025, we entered into a new $4.0 billion revolving credit facility expiring in April 2026. Additionally, we terminated the $6.0 billion revolving credit facility expiring in April 2028 and entered into a new $6.0 billion revolving credit facility expiring in April 2030. The interest rates for all credit facilities are determined based on a formula using certain market rates."

The 2028 facility was terminated three years early and replaced on terms without a sustainability ratchet. This sits beside the annual dashboard's "Sustainability Disappeared" theme and is visible only in the debt footnote.

Legal & Risk Factors

▶ Ad-tech "mixed decision"; DMA preliminary non-compliance findings; JFTC cease-and-desist New
Q1 2025: "In April 2025, the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia issued a mixed decision in the DOJ case against Google, ruling that the DOJ failed to show that Google's advertiser tools or acquisitions of DoubleClick and AdMeld were anticompetitive, but that Google's publisher tools violated antitrust laws by excluding rivals."
Q1 2025: "In March 2025, the EC issued preliminary findings of non-compliance in both investigations, to which we plan to respond in the coming weeks. Given the preliminary stages of this matter, we cannot estimate a possible loss."

Japan's Fair Trade Commission also issued a cease-and-desist order on Android agreements, with no monetary penalty. Three new adverse findings in one quarter.

▶ Risk-factor update is empty: first of three consecutive 10-Qs with no material changes Signal

Every 2024 10-Q listed material changes (cookies, dividends, the antitrust risk factor). Q1, Q2 and Q3 2025 each carry only the incorporation paragraph:

Q1 2025: "...which could harm our business, reputation, financial condition, and operating results, and affect the trading price of our Class A and Class C stock."

Over those three quarters the filings disclosed a $32.0B acquisition, the first bond issue ($12.5B), a $1.4B privacy settlement, a $3.5B EC fine, a 2026 capex step-up and the first data-center lease backstops. None reached Item 1A until the FY2025 10-K (see Risk-Factor Update Cadence).

Search remedies concluded in May 2025, with a decision expected "in August 2025." In the ad-tech case the company is still "awaiting a final judgment" in Q2 2026, three filings after November 2025 closing arguments.

Guidance & MD&A

▶ First 2026 capex signal: "significantly increase"; the 2025 sentence weakens to "exceed full year 2024" Signal
Q2 2025: "We expect to increase, relative to 2024, our investment in our technical infrastructure, including servers, network equipment, and data centers..."
Q3 2025: "We expect full year 2025 capital expenditures to exceed full year 2024. In 2026, we expect to significantly increase, relative to 2025, our investment in our technical infrastructure..."

"Significantly" is the first intensifier on the capex sentence in the window, and Alphabet gives no dollar range in any filing. The 2025 half of the sentence says little: nine-month capex of $63.6B had already passed FY2024's $52.5B when it was written.

▶ Backlog +$49.5B in one quarter to $157.7B; "for future services" dropped from the definition Signal
Q2 2025: "Revenue backlog represents commitments in customer contracts for future services that have not yet been recognized as revenue. We expect to recognize approximately 55% of the revenue backlog..."
Q3 2025: "Revenue backlog represents commitments in customer contracts that have not yet been recognized as revenue. We expect to recognize just over 55% of the revenue backlog..."

Backlog rose 46% in the quarter, against 17% the quarter before. Dropping "for future services" leaves room for non-service contracts. Q1 2026 discloses that backlog includes agreements to supply "multiple gigawatts of TPU hardware" and Q2 2026 books the first TPU system product revenue.

Commitments & Credit Support

▶ Credit derivatives backstopping third-party data-center leases: $6.5B maximum exposure New
Q3 2025: "Additionally, we have credit derivative instruments resulting from agreements with third parties to backstop certain obligations relating to data center leases."
Q3 2025: "We account for our variable interests in certain data center leasing entities, which are in the form of credit backstop agreements as credit derivatives... As of September 30, 2025, the maximum exposure to loss is $6.5 billion."

"Certain data center leasing entities" is added to the VIE list, and these are the first guarantees of third parties' data-center obligations in the window. The same quarter's 10-Q risk-factor section reports no material changes. Exposure reaches $51.4B by Q2 2026 (tracked below).

▶ Purchase commitments redefined with "energy contracts" first: $72.5B → $93.7B; unstarted leases $23.9B → $42.6B Changed
Q2 2025: "As of June 30, 2025, we had material purchase commitments and other contractual obligations of $72.5 billion, of which $51.0 billion was short-term. These amounts primarily consist of purchase orders for certain technical infrastructure..."
Q3 2025: "We have material purchase commitments and other contractual obligations primarily related to energy contracts, licenses (including content licenses), and technical infrastructure and inventory orders."

Energy moves from a footnote ("not readily determinable") to the head of the commitments list. It becomes "energy take-or-pay contracts" in the FY2025 10-K and energy service agreements "through 2054" in Q2 2026. Unstarted leases now include "a purchase option considered reasonably certain to be exercised."

Legal & Regulatory

▶ EC ad-tech fine €3.0B ($3.5B) accrued; Search remedies impose data sharing and syndication New
Q3 2025: "The EC decision imposed a €3.0 billion (approximately $3.5 billion as of September 5, 2025) fine and directed Google to cease and desist the alleged "self-preferencing" practices. We plan to appeal the ruling. We accrued the fine in the third quarter of 2025, resulting in a charge of $3.5 billion."

Q2 2025 had warned of "structural remedies." The decision imposed behavioral and data remedies instead, and the "structural" language now applies only to the pending ad-tech case. The non-deductible fine lifted the quarterly tax rate to 20.5%, the highest in the window.

Guidance & MD&A Trends

▶ New MD&A trend: external financing and "credit support" to infrastructure counterparties New
FY2025: "We have raised capital through external financing in the form of debt and we may continue to seek debt or other forms of financing in the future to support our capital and operating needs."
FY2025: "In 2025, we raised capital through the issuance of debt and we expect to continue to assess the use of debt and other forms of financing in the future. We expect to continue to enter into finance leases, primarily for data centers. Additionally, in 2025, we provided credit support, such as through backstops and guarantees, to certain infrastructure related counterparties and may continue to provide additional credit support in the future."

The FY2024 10-K trend list had no financing bullet. "Debt or other forms of financing" foreshadows the June 2026 equity raise. Backstops reach the trend section a full quarter after they first appeared in the notes.

▶ Infrastructure operating costs "expected to significantly increase"; efficiency hedge added Changed
FY2025: "The costs associated with operating our technical infrastructure - depreciation, energy, equipment, and network capacity - are expected to significantly increase as developing and serving AI offerings require more compute power than our historical consumer and enterprise offerings."
FY2025: "Certain of our costs and expenses are relatively fixed and may not correlate to changes in revenue, and we may also not be able to continue to drive efficiencies at the rate we have in the past."

FY2024's risk factor read "We may also not be able to continue to execute our efficiency efforts successfully." The new wording concedes that the past efficiency rate is the benchmark at risk. Google Cloud's revenue fluctuation factors add "supply availability."

▶ Waymo $2.1B compensation charge; Other Bets loss $7.5B; $16.0B round mostly Alphabet-funded New
FY2025: "Other Bets operating loss of $7.5 billion for the year ended December 31, 2025 included a $2.1 billion employee compensation charge recognized in the fourth quarter for Waymo, primarily reflected in research and development expenses, based on estimated stock valuation. In February 2026, Waymo announced an investment round of $16.0 billion, the significant majority of which was funded by Alphabet."

The Other Bets loss went from $4.4B (FY2024) to $7.5B. Q3 2025's year-to-date loss was $3.9B, so most of the step-up landed in Q4 through the valuation-based charge.

Balance Sheet & Commitments

▶ Backstops $6.5B → $22.6B in a quarter; short-term legal accruals of $15.6B disclosed Signal
FY2025: "As of December 31, 2025, we provided backstops in the form of financial guarantees and credit derivatives with maximum potential amount of future payments of $5.7 billion and $16.9 billion, respectively."
FY2025: "As of December 31, 2025, we had short-term accrued legal and regulatory fines and settlements of $15.6 billion. This amount primarily included EC fines, in addition to accruals related to other legal matters and regulatory fines and settlements."

Financial guarantees on energy-equipment procurement are a second, new backstop type. The legal figure is the first aggregate accrual disclosed in the window; earlier filings said only that EC fines sat in accrued liabilities. Purchase commitments are $149.1B, against $93.7B in Q3.

Themes

1. From Buyback Machine to Capital Raiser in Nine Quarters

Q1 2024 opened with $15.7B of quarterly repurchases, $11.9B of notes and a newly launched dividend. Commercial paper followed in Q3 2024, the first bond issue in May 2025, $37.3B of notes in 2025 and $31.1B more in Q1 2026, with buybacks falling to zero. Q2 2026 added a $49.6B equity raise, a mandatory convertible preferred and a $40B ATM. The buyback risk factor now says repurchases may not happen "in the near term or at all." Most steps appeared first in the debt and equity notes, not in Item 1A.

2. The Backlog and the Commitments Raced; Commitments Pulled Ahead

Revenue backlog went from $72.5B to $519.5B, almost all of it Google Cloud and including TPU hardware sales from Q1 2026. Purchase commitments went from $44.0B to $811.0B, unstarted data-center leases from $3.6B to $85.2B, and third-party backstops from zero to $51.4B. In Q2 2026 commitments exceeded backlog for the first time. The risk factors added "excess capacity that we cannot easily redeploy" only in the FY2025 10-K.

3. Notes Moved, Risk Factors Lagged

All three 2025 10-Qs reported no material risk-factor changes. Over the same quarters the notes added a $32.0B acquisition, the first bond issue, a $1.4B privacy settlement, a $3.5B EC fine, a 2026 capex step-up and the first data-center backstops. The backlog definition and the commitments definition were each rewritten mid-year. Quarterly reading of the notes, not Item 1A, is where the balance-sheet shift showed up first.

4. Legal Exposure Became Cash

The annual dashboard tracks the DOJ arc from ruling to appeal. The quarterly filings show the payments: $3.0B for Shopping (Q3 2024), a $1.4B privacy accrual (Q2 2025), $3.5B for ad tech (Q3 2025), a first aggregate accrual of $15.6B (FY2025), and $2.1B PriceRunner plus $5.2B Android in Q2 2026, when accruals reached $17.4B. Private follow-on claims after regulatory findings are now a named category.

5. Sustainability Out, Energy In

The sustainability-linked credit facility was terminated three years early (Q1 2025), the ESG risk heading (FY2024) and "long-term sustainability goals" bullet (FY2025) were deleted, and Pillar Two warnings were softened. Energy meanwhile moved from an indeterminable footnote to the first item in the commitments list, then to take-or-pay contracts through 2054, a renewable developer acquisition (Intersect) and power-equipment guarantees. This extends the annual dashboard's "Sustainability Disappeared" theme with dates.
