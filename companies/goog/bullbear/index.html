Latest filing: 10-Q Q2 2026

What the Q2 2026 10-Q adds, for and against. Net income of $112.2B is mostly an unrealized gain on SpaceX and a private company; the cards below separate operating results from the marks and from the funding.

Bull case

P2 Google Cloud growth and marginStated
Cloud revenue +82% to $24.77B; operating income $2.83B → $8.81B, a 35.6% margin (20.7% a year earlier)
Q2 2026: "Google Cloud revenues increased $11.1 billion and $18.9 billion from the three and six months ended June 30, 2025 to the three and six months ended June 30, 2026 primarily driven by growth in Google Cloud Platform largely from infrastructure and platform services."
Q2 2026: "Google Cloud | 2,826 | 8,814 | 5,003 | 15,412"

Size: Cloud added $6.0B of the $9.5B increase in operating income (63%); it is now 18% of Services plus Cloud operating income, from 8% a year earlier.

The Cloud margin rose year over year in all eight filings in the window, and the MD&A attributes growth mainly to platform services, not only the first TPU system sales.

Next filing: Cloud margin at or above 30% as TPU hardware, a lower-margin product, ramps; a split between product and service revenue would confirm the mix.

P1 Search and advertising durabilityStated
Search & other +17% to $63.27B on paid clicks +13%; TAC rate falls to 19.8% from 20.6%
Q2 2026: "Google Search & other | 54,190 | 63,271 | 104,892 | 123,670"
Q2 2026: "The TAC rate decreased from 20.6% to 19.8% from the three and six months ended June 30, 2025 to the three and six months ended June 30, 2026, primarily due to a revenue mix shift from Google Network properties to Google Search & other properties."

Size: +$9.1B of revenue year over year; 0.8 points of TAC rate on $81.6B of advertising revenue is about $0.65B a quarter (inference).

Growth is volume-led (paid clicks +13%, cost-per-click +3%), the opposite of the query loss the AI-disruption case predicts. Q1 2025 had paid clicks +2%.

Next filing: Search growth above 12% with paid clicks still growing faster than cost-per-click.

P2 Google Cloud growth and marginStated
Backlog $519.5B, +$51.9B in the quarter; TPU system revenue began a quarter or two early
Q2 2026: "As of June 30, 2026, we had $519.5 billion of remaining performance obligations (“revenue backlog”), of which $513.9 billion related to Google Cloud."
Q2 2026: "In the second quarter of 2026, we began recognizing revenues from these agreements, with the significant majority to be recognized in 2027."

Size: Cloud backlog = 6.6x trailing-twelve-month Cloud revenue ($77.6B); "just over 50%" in 24 months implies about $130B a year of recognition (inference).

Q1 2026 had said TPU revenue would start "later in 2026"; it started in Q2. Contracted demand is far ahead of current revenue.

Next filing: backlog still rising after the TPU deliveries begin; the 24-month share holding at 50% or more.

P3 Capital intensity and commitmentsStated
Operating margin 34% vs 32% despite depreciation +43% and a $1.5B damages charge
Q2 2026: "Operating margin | 32 | 34 | 2"
Q2 2026: "For the six months ended June 30, 2025 and 2026, our depreciation on property and equipment was $9.5 billion and $13.6 billion, respectively."

Size: operating income $40.77B, +30%; about 35.3% margin excluding the $1.5B PriceRunner charge (arithmetic on disclosed figures).

So far the build-out is producing revenue faster than it produces depreciation. This is the main evidence against the overbuild case, and it is backward-looking.

Next filing: margin holding above 32% as the $122.8B of assets not yet in service starts depreciating.

P5 Balance sheet and fundingStated
$49.6B raised in equity, not debt; cash and securities $126.8B → $242.5B since December
Q2 2026: "In June 2026, we issued a combination of Class A stock and Class C stock and mandatory convertible preferred stock for aggregate net proceeds of $49.6 billion, to be used for general corporate purposes, including capital expenditures to scale AI infrastructure and global compute."
Q2 2026: "Concurrently on June 4, 2026, the company completed a $10.0 billion private placement of 14 million Class A and 14 million Class C shares to an affiliate of Berkshire Hathaway Inc."

Size: $49.6B = 50% of the $98.2B of long-term debt; cash and securities exceed debt by about $140B (about $53B excluding $87.1B of marketable equity, mostly restricted SpaceX shares).

Equity funding caps leverage while capex runs ahead of cash flow, and a $10.0B Berkshire Hathaway placement is an outside vote at about $357 a share (arithmetic: 28 million shares for $10.0B). The cost to shareholders is on the bear side.

Next filing: no further debt-funded quarter while the equity proceeds last; ATM use limited to the stated tax purpose.

Bear case

P4 Cash conversion and capital returnStated
Free cash flow −$5.86B (capex $44.92B vs operating cash flow $39.07B); no buybacks; the share count is rising
Q2 2026: "In the three and six months ended June 30, 2026, there were no repurchases of the company's Class A or Class C shares."
Q2 2026: "The proceeds of the ATM Program are primarily intended to be used to meet tax obligations associated with employee equity grants."

Size: first negative quarter in the window; H1 FCF $4.3B vs H1 net income $174.8B and H1 dividends $5.2B. Shares outstanding 12,088M → 12,230M (+1.2%) in six months; buybacks were $15.3B a quarter in Q3 2024.

Two years ago free cash flow retired about 2% of the shares a year. Now new stock is planned to cover the withholding tax that buybacks used to offset, and the risk factor says repurchases may not happen "in the near term or at all".

Next filing: FCF back above dividends, and how much of the $40.0B ATM was sold.

P3 Capital intensity and commitmentsStated
Purchase commitments $332.4B → $811.0B in one quarter, now above the $519.5B backlog; backstops $51.4B
Q2 2026: "As of June 30, 2026, we had material purchase commitments and other contractual obligations totaling $811.0 billion, of which $200.7 billion was short-term."
Q2 2026: "As of June 30, 2026, we provided backstops in the form of financial guarantees and credit derivatives with maximum potential amount of future payments of $7.6 billion and $43.8 billion, respectively."

Size: commitments = 5.5x trailing operating income ($147.6B) and 1.56x backlog (0.71x in Q1). Backstops $51.4B = 35% of trailing operating income, plus an estimated $24.1B agreed; unstarted leases $85.2B.

The commitments are "fixed or guaranteed" through 2030 for supply and to 2054 for energy, while backlog can still slip with customers. The FY2025 risk factor's "excess capacity that we cannot easily redeploy" is the downside.

Next filing: backlog growing faster than commitments; any backstop called or credit-derivative loss above the current $815M liability.

P6 Earnings qualityStated
$99.0B of equity-security gains are 71% of pre-tax income; net income $112.19B is 2.8x operating income
Q2 2026: "OI&E of $98.0 billion for the three months ended June 30, 2026 included net gains on equity securities of $99.0 billion, primarily related to unrealized gains in our equity securities portfolio from SpaceX and a private company."
Q2 2026: "Includes $80.0 billion of Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SpaceX) shares subject to short-term restrictions on the ability to sell."

Size: EPS $9.11; without the gains at the 21% statutory rate, net income would be about $34B, +20% (inference). $87.9B of private stakes were remeasured in the quarter.

The gains are unrealized, partly locked up, and taxed through a $22.8B deferred tax liability. They can reverse with the SpaceX share price; operating earnings are the number to track.

Next filing: marks on SpaceX after the lock-up and on the unnamed private company; any realized sale.

P5 Balance sheet and fundingStated
Long-term debt $98.2B, doubled since December; interest expense $261M → $1,278M a quarter
Q2 2026: "As of June 30, 2026, we had long-term debt outstanding with a total carrying value of $98.2 billion."
Q2 2026: "Furthermore, our current and any future indebtedness, including obligations arising under leases, backstops, guarantees, and potential liabilities from large commercial agreements, combined with the dilutive impact of current or future equity issuances, could harm our financial condition, depress our stock price or reduce our financial and business flexibility."

Size: face value $101.1B vs $11.9B of notes in Q3 2024; interest expense now 89% of interest income ($1,430M). Credit facilities drawn $1.3B.

The new financing risk factor groups backstops and guarantees with debt, which is the right way to read leverage here. Net interest income, once a cushion, is close to zero.

Next filing: net interest turning negative, and whether new notes follow the equity raise.

P7 Legal and regulatoryStated
PriceRunner damages of about $2.1B accrued, the first quantified follow-on award; Android fine final, $5.2B paid in July
Q2 2026: "The Court awarded the plaintiff approximately $2.1 billion (awarded in multiple currencies) in principal damages plus accrued interest and costs, which we recognized in the second quarter of 2026."
Q2 2026: "This includes private claims stemming from regulatory proceedings in which Google's liability has been fully determined and the remaining dispute concerns potential damages."

Size: $1.5B in operating expenses = 3.7% of Q2 operating income; accrued fines and settlements $17.4B (Q1 $15.6B).

Follow-on claims across Europe now have a priced template, and the filing still says it "cannot estimate a possible loss" for "other such matters".

Next filing: other Shopping follow-on judgments; the ad-tech remedies decision, where the DOJ seeks structural remedies.

P2 Google Cloud growth and marginInferred
Inventory $2.4B → $10.0B in six months, mostly TPU hardware; product revenue is not quantified
Q2 2026: "Inventory consists primarily of hardware related to TPU systems for sale to enterprise customers and devices, which primarily include the Pixel family of products."

Size: inventory build of $7.7B consumed 9% of H1 operating cash flow; Cloud backlog concentration in "a limited number of agreements" is not quantified in the filing.

TPU systems are hardware sales to a few buyers, some of them supported by Alphabet backstops. Without a product/service split, the 82% growth and the margin cannot be decomposed.

Next filing: inventory turning into revenue, and any disclosure of TPU product revenue or customer concentration.
