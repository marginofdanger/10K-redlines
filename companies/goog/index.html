FY2018 → FY2019: Pre-Cloud

Filed Feb 2020 | Employees: 98,771 → 118,899 (+20%) | Ad Rev: ~85%

Business Overview

▶Overview rewritten: "deep computer science problems" replaces product narrativesChanged
The FY2018 overview described Alphabet's spirit through product examples (Android, Google Maps). FY2019 replaced it with a research-oriented framing:
FY2018: "inspiring us to do things like rethink the mobile device ecosystem with Android and map the world with Google Maps"
FY2019: "inspiring us to do things like tackling deep computer science problems, such as our investments in artificial intelligence (AI) and quantum computing"
Product achievements replaced with research ambition.
▶TikTok named as competitor for the first timeNew
FY2018 listed digital video competitors as "Amazon, Facebook, Hulu, and Netflix." FY2019 added three new names:
"Providers of digital video services, such as Amazon, Apple, AT&T, Disney, Facebook, Hulu, Netflix and TikTok."
TikTok, Apple, AT&T, and Disney all added in a single year.
▶Sustainability expanded from 1 paragraph to full section with targetsNew
FY2018 had a single paragraph on climate under "Other Items." FY2019 elevated it to a standalone section titled "Ongoing Commitment to Sustainability" with specific achievements: 18 new energy deals totaling 1,600 megawatts, carbon neutral shipping, and the Environmental Insights Explorer expanding to 100+ cities.

FY2019 → FY2020: Cloud + COVID

Filed Feb 2021 | Employees: 118,899 → 135,301 (+14%) | Ad Rev: ~81%

Business Overview

▶Google Services and Google Cloud become separate segmentsNew
FY2019 reported Google as a single segment. FY2020 introduced the two-segment structure:
FY2019: "the largest of which is Google"
FY2020: "which we report as two segments: Google Services and Google Cloud"
Google Cloud elevated from a subsection of "Serving our users" to a standalone segment with its own revenue reporting.
▶BERT highlighted; DeepMind enters Business sectionNew
FY2020 added specific AI breakthroughs to the "Power of machine learning" section:
"a huge breakthrough in natural language understanding, called BERT, now improves results for almost every English language search query."
DeepMind appeared in the Business section for the first time: "DeepMind made a significant AI-powered breakthrough, solving a 50-year-old protein folding challenge."
▶$5.75B sustainability bonds; carbon-free 2030 target; DEI commitment addedNew
"In 2020, we issued $5.75 billion in sustainability bonds, the largest sustainability or green bond issuance by any company in history."
New 2030 target: "to run our business on carbon-free energy everywhere, at all times, by 2030." Also: "Alphabet is committed to making diversity, equity, and inclusion part of everything we do" -- first appearance of DEI language in the filing.
▶Sundar Pichai CEO role formalized; Daydream VR removed; COVID response throughoutSignal
New sentence: "We continue this work under the leadership of Sundar Pichai, who has served as CEO of Google since 2015 and as CEO of Alphabet since 2019." Daydream VR platform removed from platform listing (was in FY2018-19). COVID response woven throughout: Exposure Notification apps, Community Mobility Reports, $100M+ in grants.

FY2020 → FY2021: MUM Era

Filed Feb 2022 | Employees: 135,301 → 156,500 (+16%) | Ad Rev: ~81%

Business Overview

▶"Power of AI" replaces "Power of machine learning"Changed
The section heading changed:
FY2020: "The power of machine learning"
FY2021: "The power of AI"
The body text similarly shifted: FY2020 led with "machine learning and AI are increasingly driving many of our latest innovations." FY2021 rewrote to: "investments in AI and machine learning are increasingly driving many of our latest innovations." AI moved ahead of ML in the ordering.
▶MUM introduced; $100B R&D cumulative disclosedNew
"in May of 2021, we introduced Multitask Unified Model -- or MUM -- which has the potential to transform how Google helps with complex tasks. MUM is trained across 75 different languages"
Also new: "We have invested more than $100 billion in R&D over the last five years." First time a cumulative R&D figure was disclosed.
▶Non-ad revenue breakout: Google Play, Hardware, YouTube non-advertisingNew
FY2021 added explicit breakout of non-ad revenue streams for the first time: Google Play (apps and in-app purchases), Hardware (Fitbit wearable devices, Google Nest home products, Pixel phones), and YouTube non-advertising (YouTube Premium and YouTube TV subscriptions). Fitbit appeared for the first time following its 2021 acquisition completion.
▶Other Bets revenue: "health technology" replaces "internet and TV services"Changed
FY2020: "Revenues are primarily generated from internet and TV services, as well as licensing and R&D services."
FY2021: "Revenues from Other Bets are generated primarily from the sale of health technology and internet services."
Verily's health technology revenue overtook legacy internet/TV services as the primary Other Bets revenue source.

FY2021 → FY2022: AI Foundational

Filed Feb 2023 | Employees: 156,500 → 190,234 (+22%, peak) | Ad Rev: ~79%

Business Overview

▶"AI is a foundational and transformational technology" -- new AI manifestoNew
The "Power of AI" section was entirely rewritten into a statement of belief:
"We believe that AI is a foundational and transformational technology that will provide compelling and helpful benefits to people and society through its capacity to assist, complement, empower, and inspire people in almost every field of human endeavor."
FY2021 described specific products (MUM, DeepMind). FY2022 replaced product descriptions with a philosophical position.
▶All named competitors removed from Competition sectionRemoved
FY2021 listed 20+ named competitors across categories (Baidu, Bing, Amazon, Apple, Meta, ByteDance, Netflix, Salesforce, etc.). FY2022 removed every company name, replacing them with generic categories:
FY2021: "Social networks offered by ByteDance, Meta, Snap, and Twitter"
FY2022: "social networks, which users may rely on for product or service referrals"
Naming competitors defines relevant markets for antitrust purposes.
▶Privacy elevated to standalone section; Pixel Watch debutsChanged
Privacy language, previously embedded in the "Serving our users" section, was elevated to its own standalone "Privacy and security" section. Hardware listing updated: Pixel Watch appeared for the first time ("the new Pixel 7 and Pixel 7 Pro, and the very first Pixel Watch"), while Chromecast and Google Nest Hub were removed from the device listing.

FY2022 → FY2023: Gemini

Filed Feb 2024 | Employees: 190,234 → 182,502 (-4%) | Ad Rev: ~75%

Business Overview

▶"Since 2016, an AI-first company" + Transformer origin storyNew
The overview was rewritten to claim an AI-first identity retroactively:
"It led us to be a pioneer in the development of AI and, since 2016, an AI-first company."
A Transformer origin story was added: "Large language models (LLMs) are an exciting aspect of our work in AI based on deep learning architectures, such as the Transformer, a neural network architecture that we introduced in 2017." Also added: BERT "in 2019 that helped Search understand the intent of user search queries better than ever before."
▶Gemini launched; five new subsections added; $150B R&D cumulativeNew
"In December 2023, we launched Gemini, our most capable and general model. It was built from the ground up to be multimodal."
Five new AI-oriented subsections appeared: "Deliver the Most Advanced, Safe, and Responsible AI," "Enable Organizations and Developers to Innovate on Google Cloud," "Improve Knowledge, Learning, Creativity, and Productivity," "Build the Most Helpful Personal Computing Platforms and Devices." Cumulative R&D updated from $100B to $150B.
▶Google Cloud restructured into five capability pillarsChanged
FY2022 described Google Cloud in a single paragraph. FY2023 introduced five named pillars: AI-optimized Infrastructure, Cybersecurity, Databases and Analytics, Collaboration Tools, and AI Platform and Duet AI for Google Cloud. Each received its own bullet-point description.
▶"Developers and providers of AI products" added to competition; moonshots "now in core products"Changed
New competition category: "developers and providers of AI products and services." Previously absent. Moonshots section reframed:
"Our early investments in AI started out as moonshots but are now incorporated into our core products and central to future developments."
Also: "ten blue links" framing expanded from a one-line reference to a full multi-paragraph Search evolution narrative.

FY2023 → FY2024: Platform Shift, Capex Inflection

Filed Feb 4, 2025 | Revenue $307.4B → $350.0B (+14%) | Operating margin 27% → 32% | Capex $32.3B → $52.5B (+63%) | First dividend ($7.4B) | Employees 182,502 → 183,323 | Ad share of revenue ~77% → ~76%

Business Overview

▶AI recast from "a transformational technology" to "a profound platform shift"; all seven 2B-user products now "using Gemini"Changed
FY2023: "AI is a transformational technology that can bring meaningful and positive change to people and societies across the world, and for our business."
FY2024: "We believe AI is a profound platform shift, one that can bring meaningful and positive change to people and societies across the world, and to our business."
FY2024: "Today, all seven of our two billion-user products — Android, Chrome, Gmail, Maps, Play Store, Search, and YouTube — are using Gemini."
"Platform shift" is the language of a transition that can unseat incumbents, which is why it matters that the same filing adds AI developers to the MD&A's list of competitors for users and advertisers (see MD&A below). The overview also drops the Google Assistant sentence ("the best way to get things done seamlessly across different devices"), the product Gemini was replacing.
▶First cost-to-serve claim: AI Overviews machine costs "significantly lowered"; TPUs named for the first time; AI Overviews at 1B+ usersNew
FY2024: "since we started serving AI Overviews to our users, we have significantly lowered machine costs and latency through hardware, engineering, and technical breakthroughs. Our AI-optimized infrastructure allows us to use, and offer our customers, a range of AI accelerator options, including our own custom-built Tensor Processing Units (TPUs)."
FY2024: "AI Overviews has been released in more than one hundred countries, reaching more than one billion users."
The FY2023 10-K never mentions Tensor Processing Units. The cost sentence answers the obvious objection to putting generative answers in front of a billion Search users, but it gives no number. It was deleted again in FY2025, the year capex nearly doubled.
▶Centralized AI R&D moved into Alphabet-level activities; unallocated loss $9.2B → $10.5BChanged
FY2023: "For the year ended December 31, 2023, Alphabet-level activities include charges related to the reduction in force and our office space optimization efforts totaling $3.9 billion."
FY2024: "Supporting these businesses, we have centralized certain AI-related research and development which is reported in Alphabet-level activities."
Alphabet-level activities already existed as an unallocated line in FY2023, holding severance and office charges. FY2024 is the first year it also holds frontier AI research, so its loss grew from $9,186M to $10,541M even as severance and office charges fell. A reasonable inference is that some model-development cost no longer reaches Google Services and Google Cloud segment margins. Cloud operating income went from $1.7B to $6.1B in the same year.
▶Sustainability section trimmed: 120% water goal and SBTi validation pledge deleted; "achieve" net zero softened to "make progress toward"; AI added as an emissions uncertaintyRemoved
FY2023: "we have set goals to replenish 120% of the freshwater volume we consume, on average, across our offices and data centers by 2030"
FY2023: "We have formally committed to the Science Based Targets initiative to seek their validation of our absolute emissions reduction target."
FY2023: "In 2021, we set an ambitious goal to achieve net-zero emissions across all of our operations and value chain, by 2030. To accomplish this, we aim to reduce 50%..."
FY2024: "In 2021, we set an ambitious goal to reach net-zero emissions across all of our operations and value chain by 2030. To make progress toward this effort, we aim to reduce 50%..."
FY2024: "Our approach will continue to evolve and will require us to navigate significant uncertainty, including the uncertainty around the future environmental impact of AI, which is complex and difficult to predict."
The water goal and the third-party validation pledge are deleted outright, not reworded. The net-zero and 24/7 carbon-free energy goals are kept but hedged, and AI is named as a source of uncertainty. This is the step before FY2025, when the whole section and both 2030 goals disappear.
▶DEI commitment deleted; "diversity" goes from 8 mentions to 0 across the 10-KRemoved
FY2023: "At Alphabet, we are committed to making diversity, equity, and inclusion part of everything we do and to growing a workforce that is representative of the users we serve."
FY2024 deletes the paragraph and the link to the diversity report. The same edit runs through the rest of the filing: the Government Regulation list replaces "reporting on human capital and diversity" with "sustainability and other social matters", and employees are now meant to "perform at a high level" rather than be "happy, healthy, and productive". The word "diversity" appears 8 times in the FY2023 10-K and 0 times in FY2024. (This dashboard previously dated the removal to FY2023.)
▶Waymo named for the first time: "fully autonomous, paid ride-hailing services"New
FY2024: "In Other Bets, our fully autonomous driving technology company, Waymo, is now providing fully autonomous, paid ride-hailing services to customers in multiple cities."
The FY2023 10-K does not mention Waymo at all. FY2024 names it in the Business section and, in the notes, says it raised $5.6B as a consolidated VIE. The same moonshots paragraph drops "People thought we were crazy when we acquired YouTube and Android". In FY2025, Waymo replaces healthcare as the main source of Other Bets revenue.

Risk Factors

▶Antitrust risk goes from "lawsuits filed" to adverse rulings: DOJ Search liability (Aug 2024), Epic remedies (Oct 2024); "structural remedies" namedSignal
FY2023: "the U.S. Department of Justice, various U.S. states, and other plaintiffs have filed several antitrust lawsuits about various aspects of our business"
FY2024: "in August 2024, the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia ruled that Google violated such antitrust laws. The Court is holding a separate proceeding to determine remedies, which could include alterations to our products and services and our business models and operations, including structural remedies, and/or our distribution arrangements, among other changes."
FY2023: "The presiding judge will determine remedies in 2024 and the range of potential remedies vary widely. We plan to appeal."
FY2024: "The presiding judge issued a remedies decision in October 2024 that ordered a variety of alterations to our business models and operations and contractual agreements for Android and Google Play."
For the first time, Google's own risk factors say a remedy could be structural. The litigation paragraph is split into separate, dated paragraphs for each case, including a Texas ad tech trial "set for trial in March 2025" and a DOJ ad tech decision "expected in early 2025". The MD&A meanwhile drops the DOJ complaints from its list of examples of regulatory trends.
▶New risk that server useful lives could change, two years after extending them to six years for a $3.9B depreciation benefitNew
FY2023 (MD&A): "In January 2023, we completed an assessment of the useful lives of our servers and network equipment, resulting in a change in the estimated useful life of our servers and certain network equipment to six years. The effect of this change was a reduction in depreciation expense of $3.9 billion"
FY2024 (new risk factor): "We have invested significantly and expect to continue to invest significantly in our property and equipment, including our technical infrastructure, and we expect these assets to benefit our business over their estimated useful lives. Changes in facts and circumstances such as changes to ongoing business operations, changes in the planned use and utilization of assets, and/or technological advancements, could indicate a change in the period over which we expect to benefit from the asset"
Over a three-year span, the six-year server life went from a disclosed earnings tailwind (FY2023) to a named risk (FY2024). FY2025 then rewrites the trigger as "historical asset performance, expected technology advancements, and future network deployment plans", which reads like a review is in progress. With capex at $52.5B and rising, a shorter life would raise depreciation sharply.
▶GPU/TPU supply constraint added; margin pressure now cites "higher level of investment in technical infrastructure"; energy named as a costNew
FY2024: "industry supply capacity for AI accelerators, including Graphics Processing Units, or GPUs, as well as our custom-built TPUs, is highly competitive and rapidly evolving. If we are unable to negotiate favorable contractual terms or our competitors claim the supply or capacity first, we may face supply constraints."
FY2024: "...increasing costs ... for many aspects of our business, including higher level of investment in technical infrastructure."
FY2024: "depend on the availability and pricing of third-party equipment and other technical infrastructure operations costs, including network capacity, energy, and equipment costs."
The FY2023 margin sentence ended at "increasing costs for many aspects of our business" with no cause named. In FY2024 the cause is infrastructure, energy appears as a cost line in the AI risk factor and in the cost-of-revenues definition, and the supply sentence says competitors could "claim the supply or capacity first". These are the risks that become power, water and land constraints in FY2025.
▶Cloud: "may not attain sufficient scale and profitability" dropped; "platform switching costs" named as an industry pricing problemChanged
FY2023: "Pricing and delivery models are competitive and constantly evolving, and we may not attain sufficient scale and profitability to achieve our business objectives."
FY2024: "Pricing, including platform switching costs, are challenging across the industry, and delivery models are competitive and constantly evolving, and we may therefore not achieve our business objectives."
The profitability worry goes as Cloud operating income rises from $1.7B to $6.1B. Switching costs (the cost for a customer to move off a provider) are named for the first time. A possible reading is that Google is pointing to rivals' customer lock-in as a limit on its own pricing. FY2025 drops "switching costs" again and adds that pricing models are "subject to increasing regulatory scrutiny".
▶Third-party cookies: "phase out" becomes a "proposed user choice model"Changed
FY2023: "such as our initiatives to phase out third-party cookies"
FY2024: "such as our initiatives related to third-party cookies, including our announcement in July 2024 to move from phasing out all third-party cookies to a proposed user choice model (which remains subject to continuing discussions with regulators)"
This is a retreat from a multi-year deprecation plan, and the sentence admits the regulator still has a say. FY2025 deletes any reference to cookies from the risk factors.
▶ESG risk factor heading deleted; "diversity" and "ESG" removed from the regulatory-risk paragraph; new risk of investing in areas that "fail to gain sufficient market traction"Removed
FY2023: "Expectations relating to ESG considerations could expose us to potential liabilities, increased costs, and reputational harm."
FY2024: "Further, we are subject to evolving laws, regulations, policies, and international accords relating to matters beyond our core products and services, including environmental sustainability, climate change, human capital, and employment matters."
"Diversity" becomes "employment matters", and FY2023's warning that failing to "adhere to our public statements" could cause harm is deleted. Elsewhere, a new clause covers the risk of overbuilding:
FY2024: "or if we miscalculate those needs and desires and invest significantly in areas that fail to gain sufficient market traction"

MD&A

▶Capex $32.3B → $52.5B; purchase commitments $45.9B → $55.4B; uncommenced leases $3.3B → $6.5B; depreciation $11.9B → $15.3BSignal
FY2024: "During the years ended December 31, 2023 and 2024, we spent $32.3 billion and $52.5 billion on capital expenditures, respectively."
FY2023: "we had material purchase commitments and other contractual obligations of $45.9 billion, of which $31.6 billion was short-term."
FY2024: "we had material purchase commitments and other contractual obligations of $55.4 billion, of which $32.5 billion was short-term."
FY2024: "future short-term and long-term lease payments of $773 million and $6.5 billion, respectively, that are not yet recorded on our Consolidated Balance Sheets."
Long-term uncommenced leases doubled to $6.5B. In FY2025 that figure reaches $52.7B. A new cost-of-revenues line, "other technical infrastructure operations costs, including network capacity, energy, and equipment costs", is added to the definitions. The capex guidance sentence ("expect to increase, relative to 2024") keeps the same wording as the year before; "significantly" is added only in FY2025.
▶YouTube/Play "monetize at a lower rate" sentence replaced by AI as the monetization variable; AI developers added as competitors in MD&ASignal
FY2023: "which may affect our monetization. For example, revenues from ads on YouTube and Google Play monetize at a lower rate than our traditional search ads."
FY2024: "As interactions between users and advertisers change, and as online user behavior evolves, for example with AI, we continue to expand our product offerings to serve these changing needs, which may affect monetization of our products and services."
FY2024: "In addition, we face increasing competition for user engagement and advertisers, including from other developers and providers of AI products and services, which may affect our revenues."
The mix risk management names in MD&A is no longer YouTube versus Search but AI versus Search. The COVID explanation for slower growth ("after the outsized growth in our advertising revenues during the COVID-19 pandemic") is deleted. In FY2025 the hedge becomes explicit: AI Overviews and AI Mode "may monetize differently".
▶Search TAC rate rose on mobile "paid access points" (FY2023: "substantially consistent")Signal
FY2023: "The TAC rate on Google Search & other revenues and the TAC rate on Google Network revenues were both substantially consistent from 2022 to 2023."
FY2024: "The TAC rate on Google Search & other revenues increased from 2023 to 2024 primarily due to increases related to mobile searches, which carries higher TAC because more mobile searches are channeled through paid access points."
Paid access points (default-search distribution deals with browsers, carriers and OEMs) are what the DOJ Search case ruled on in August 2024. Total TAC rate still fell (21.4% to 20.7%) because of mix. By FY2025 the Search TAC rate is "substantially consistent" again.
▶Dividend initiated ($7.4B) and "intends to pay quarterly"; buybacks flat at $62.0B; new 1% buyback excise tax riskNew
FY2024: "During the year ended December 31, 2024 total cash dividends, which were first paid in June 2024, were $3.5 billion, $519 million, and $3.3 billion for Class A, Class B, and Class C shares, respectively."
FY2024: "The company intends to pay quarterly cash dividends in the future, subject to review and approval by the company’s Board of Directors in its sole discretion."
FY2024: "The U.S. has imposed a 1% excise tax on the fair market value of shares repurchased, which could potentially increase in the future."
Buybacks were $62.2B in 2023 and $62.0B in 2024, so the dividend added to total shareholder returns instead of replacing buybacks. FY2025 is the year that changes: buybacks fall to $45.4B as capex rises to $91.4B.
▶Subsequent event: $8.0B January 2025 unrealized gain on one private-company stake; OI&E $1.4B → $7.4BNew
FY2024: "In January 2025, we recognized an $8.0 billion unrealized gain on our non-marketable equity securities related to our investment in a private company."
A single unnamed holding, marked up on an observable transaction, produced a gain larger than all of FY2024's other income ($7.4B). Non-marketable equity carrying value went from $28.8B to $35.5B. This line foreshadows FY2025, when equity gains of $24.1B lift net income growth (+32%) well above operating income growth (+15%).

FY2024 → FY2025: Building on Borrowed Capacity

Filed Feb 4, 2026 | Revenue $350.0B → $402.8B (+15%) | Operating margin 32% flat | Capex $52.5B → $91.4B (+74%) | Long-term debt $10.9B → $46.6B | Buybacks $62.0B → $45.4B | Employees 183,323 → 190,820 | Ad share of revenue ~76% → ~73%

Business Overview

▶Sustainability section deleted: net-zero 2030 and 24/7 carbon-free 2030 goals no longer appear anywhere in the 10-KRemoved
FY2024: "In 2021, we set an ambitious goal to reach net-zero emissions across all of our operations and value chain by 2030."
FY2024: "In 2020, we set a goal to run on 24/7 CFE every hour of every day on every grid where we operate by 2030."
FY2024 (Risk Factors): "adopted reporting frameworks and principles, and announced a number of complex and ambitious goals and initiatives that may require considerable investments."
FY2025 (Risk Factors): "adopted reporting frameworks and principles that may require considerable investments. For instance, AI's energy and water demands have made efforts to reduce our emissions more complex and challenging across every level."
"Net-zero", "24/7" and "carbon-free" each appear 0 times in the FY2025 10-K (3, 2 and 1 times in FY2024). The risk factor deletes the "goals" it used to refer to and names AI as the reason. Two MD&A edits point the same way. The revolver paragraph drops the sentence tying its interest rate to "our progress toward the achievement of certain sustainability goals". Energy contracts change from "agreements to purchase renewable energy and energy attributes" to "power purchase agreements with third-party suppliers".
▶Gemini reach restated at a lower bar ("15 half-billion-user products"); AI Overviews' 1B-user figure dropped; AI Mode added without a metricChanged
FY2024: "Today, all seven of our two billion-user products — Android, Chrome, Gmail, Maps, Play Store, Search, and YouTube — are using Gemini."
FY2025: "Today, all 15 of our half-billion-user products — including seven with two billion users — use our Gemini models."
FY2024: "AI Overviews has been released in more than one hundred countries, reaching more than one billion users."
FY2025: "For example, AI Overviews makes it easier to ask Google anything and get a helpful response. AI Mode allows users to ask more nuanced questions that might have previously taken multiple searches"
The headline number grew from 7 to 15 because the threshold was halved; the count of two-billion-user products is still seven. The only user metric FY2024 gave for an AI Search product is removed. Gemini 3 ("our most intelligent AI model yet") and Ironwood, the seventh-generation TPU, are introduced. The FY2024 claim of "significantly lowered machine costs" for AI Overviews is gone.
▶AI infrastructure becomes "a key differentiator" and is listed first; GPUs named beside TPUsChanged
FY2025: "At the foundation of our full-stack approach is our AI-optimized infrastructure — a key differentiator enabling us to power our own products, such as Search and YouTube, and support the services we provide to our Google Cloud customers."
FY2025: "including specialized Graphics Processing Units (GPUs) and our own custom-built Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), such as Ironwood, our seventh-generation TPU."
The AI section loses its four subsection headings and now leads with infrastructure. TPUs now also appear in the risk factors as part of the investment being expanded ("building AI-optimized infrastructure, including our custom TPUs"), and the Cloud risk factor covers "how we deliver them to our customers". This is consistent with TPUs becoming a product offered to Cloud customers, not only an internal cost advantage.
▶Other Bets revenue now "autonomous transportation"; Waymo "entering international markets"; $2.1B Waymo comp charge and a $16.0B round mostly funded by AlphabetSignal
FY2024: "Revenues from Other Bets are generated primarily from the sale of healthcare-related services, and internet services."
FY2025: "from the sale of autonomous transportation and internet services"
FY2024: "ranging from those in the R&D phase to those that are in the beginning stages of commercialization. Our goal is for them to become thriving, successful businesses."
FY2025: "to those that are scaling commercialization, such as Waymo, which is expanding to more cities domestically, entering international markets, and further scaling operations."
FY2025 (MD&A): "Other Bets operating loss of $7.5 billion for the year ended December 31, 2025 included a $2.1 billion employee compensation charge recognized in the fourth quarter for Waymo, primarily reflected in research and development expenses, based on estimated stock valuation. In February 2026, Waymo announced an investment round of $16.0 billion, the significant majority of which was funded by Alphabet."
Other Bets revenue actually fell ($1,648M to $1,537M) while its loss rose from $4.4B to $7.5B. "Health" is also dropped from the Other Bets investment areas in Risk Factors. The $2.1B valuation-based charge and Alphabet funding most of an outside round both suggest Waymo's mark is rising faster than its revenue.

Risk Factors

▶Advertising share disclosed as "more than 70%" (was 75%); new risk that "AI is quickly reshaping the advertising industry" and that consumers "may change how they obtain information online"Signal
FY2024: "We generated more than 75% of total revenues from online advertising in 2024."
FY2025: "We generated more than 70% of total revenues from online advertising in 2025."
FY2025: "We believe AI is quickly reshaping the advertising industry, including how ads are delivered online, and we and our competitors are constantly adjusting to meet this shift and provide new and evolving advertising formats. There is no assurance that we will adapt effectively and competitively to meet this shift"
FY2025: "substantially similar to or better than ours, and the consumers may change how they obtain information online, potentially reducing the utility of our existing products and services."
The actual share was 73.2% ($294.7B of $402.8B), so the floor was lowered by more than the business moved. The first factor in the revenue-growth list is now "increasing demand for competing technologies" (FY2024: "new technologies", listed fifth). Together these are the 10-K's plainest acknowledgment so far that AI products could displace search.
▶New: infrastructure "increasingly constrained by the availability of power, water, and land"; failure would "limit our ability to train models and serve Cloud customers"New
FY2025: "Our ability to scale our technical infrastructure is increasingly constrained by the availability of power, water, and land. For example, energy supply is constrained globally due to the significant increase in demand for and limited availability of energy to power AI compute."
FY2025: "Failure to secure sufficient capacity in a timely manner would limit our ability to train models and serve Cloud customers."
The sentence uses "would", not "could". It matches an MD&A edit adding supply as a driver of Cloud revenue: FY2024 "changes in customer usage and demand" becomes FY2025 "changes in customer usage, demand, and supply availability". Cloud revenue grew 36% to $58.7B, so the filing is saying growth is capped by capacity, not demand. The pending $4.8B Intersect acquisition ("data center and energy infrastructure solutions") is the corporate response.
▶New risks from third-party data center leases, long-duration contracts, "excess capacity that we cannot easily redeploy", and backstops and guaranteesNew
FY2025: "To meet the compute capacity demands of AI training and inference, as well as traditional cloud computing services, we are entering into significant leasing arrangements with third party operators, which may increase costs and operational complexity."
FY2025: "In such nonperformance or an industry downturn, we may incur additional liabilities, have excess capacity that we cannot easily redeploy, and not receive payments from our counterparties or customers."
FY2025: "leases, backstops, guarantees, and potential liabilities from large commercial agreements, could harm our financial condition and reduce our financial and business flexibility."
Alphabet's first 10-K risk factors that describe an AI downturn scenario. The new financing risk factor ("Disruptions in our ability to access future financing or manage our indebtedness") replaces a sentence that was only about investment values. The MD&A gives the size of these obligations (below).
▶Investment risk heading "ongoing" → "increasing"; efficiency hedge sharpened to "at the rate we have in the past"; useful-life triggers rewrittenChanged
FY2024: "We may also not be able to continue to execute our efficiency efforts successfully or in a timely manner."
FY2025: "we may also not be able to continue to drive efficiencies at the rate we have in the past."
FY2025: "Changes in facts and circumstances such as changes to historical asset performance, expected technology advancements, and future network deployment plans could change the period over which we expect to benefit from the asset"
Operating margin held at 32% despite a 22% rise in operating expenses. The new wording warns that the efficiencies behind that margin may slow. Infrastructure cost now leads the margin-pressure list instead of trailing it. The useful-life triggers ("historical asset performance") read as inputs to a review, and the same wording is in the critical accounting estimates.
▶Search case: final judgment (Dec 2025) requires data sharing and syndication to competitors; both sides appealed. Ad tech: April 2025 ruling against publisher tools, DOJ seeks structural remedies. Epic paragraph deleted after settlementSignal
FY2025: "in December 2025, entered a final judgment requiring remedies, which, among other things, imposes restrictions on how we distribute our services and requires us to share certain search data with and offer syndication services to certain competitors. In January 2026, we appealed the final judgment and moved to pause implementation of certain remedies. In February 2026, the DOJ and state Attorneys General also appealed."
FY2025: "In April 2025, the presiding judge issued a mixed decision in the DOJ case against us, ruling that neither our advertiser tools nor the DoubleClick and AdMeld acquisitions were anticompetitive, but that our publisher tools unfairly excluded rivals."
FY2025: "The DOJ's remedy proposal includes structural remedies that could harm our business."
The DOJ cross-appeal means the Search remedies could get tougher, not only lighter. The FY2024 warning about "structural remedies" moves from the Search case to the ad tech case. The Epic paragraph is gone from Risk Factors because, per Note 10, "In October 2025, we reached a settlement with Epic to modify the remedies". The cost shows up in MD&A: G&A rose $7.3B on "the $3.5 billion EC fine accrued in the third quarter of 2025 and a $1.4 billion legal accrual", and accrued legal and regulatory fines reached $15.6B.
▶Third-party cookie language deleted from Risk FactorsRemoved

FY2025: "Changes to our advertising policies and data privacy practices, as well as changes to other companies' advertising or data privacy practices have in the past, and may in the future, affect the advertising services that we are able to provide."
The cookie phase-out disappears from the 10-K without any statement of how it ended. Its place in the advertising risk factor goes to the new paragraph on AI reshaping ad formats.
▶AI regulation risk moves from federal to state: "more than 1,000 AI-related bills"; California and New York frontier-model laws named; federal order "prioritizes deregulation"Changed

MD&A

▶New trend: AI Overviews and AI Mode "may monetize differently", which "could affect revenue growth rates and margin trends"Signal
FY2024: "We expect to continue to incorporate AI innovations into our products, such as AI in Search, that could affect our monetization trends."
FY2025: "As we continue to incorporate AI into our products and services, such as with AI Overviews and AI Mode in Search, and with enterprise AI solutions on our Google Cloud Platform, we may monetize differently than our historical consumer and enterprise offerings which could affect revenue growth rates and margin trends."
This is the first time management names specific AI Search products as a possible drag on both growth and margins. FY2025 also deletes "although at a slower pace than we have experienced historically" from the growth outlook. The MD&A trends section is rewritten around labeled drivers (AI offerings, revenue beyond advertising, infrastructure investment, R&D and acquisitions, TAC), plus a new bullet on debt financing.
▶Capex $52.5B → $91.4B; 2026 guide raised to "significantly increase"; infrastructure operating costs "expected to significantly increase"Signal
FY2024: "We also expect to increase, relative to 2024, our investment in our technical infrastructure, including servers, network equipment, and data centers"
FY2025: "We invested heavily in capital expenditures in 2025 and in 2026, we expect to significantly increase, relative to 2025, our investment in our technical infrastructure, including servers and network equipment, and data centers."
FY2025: "The costs associated with operating our technical infrastructure - depreciation, energy, equipment, and network capacity - are expected to significantly increase as developing and serving AI offerings require more compute power than our historical consumer and enterprise offerings."
"Significantly" appears in the capex guidance for the first time in the three filings, on top of a 74% increase. Depreciation went from $15.3B to $21.1B. R&D now lists "other technical infrastructure operations costs, including energy, equipment, and network capacity costs" as a main component, so the build-out also shows up in R&D (+$11.8B to $61.1B).
▶Off-balance-sheet build-out: uncommenced leases $7.3B → $58.5B; commitments $55.4B → $149.1B; $16.9B data-center lease backstops; $9.9B PPANew
FY2025: "As of December 31, 2025, we have entered into leases primarily related to data centers that have not yet commenced with short-term and long-term future lease payments of $5.8 billion and $52.7 billion, respectively. These leases will commence between 2026 and 2031"
FY2025: "As of December 31, 2025, the total for these commitments was $149.1 billion, of which $113.0 billion was short-term, mostly related to technical infrastructure and inventory orders."
FY2025: "we provided backstops in the form of financial guarantees and credit derivatives with maximum potential amount of future payments of $5.7 billion and $16.9 billion, respectively."
FY2025 (Note 3): "Notional amounts for credit derivatives are the backstop obligations related to certain third-party data center leases and represent the maximum potential amount of future payments that could be required in the event of certain default scenarios over remaining agreement periods of up to 15 years."
Commitments, uncommenced leases and backstops add up to roughly $230B of forward obligations on top of capex. Commitments now include "energy take-or-pay contracts". A January 2026 power purchase agreement adds $9.9B from 2027 to 2047. The backstops mean Alphabet is guaranteeing other operators' data center leases, which is the counterparty risk the new risk factor describes.
▶Debt financing becomes a stated strategy: $37.3B of notes issued, long-term debt $10.9B → $46.6B, commercial paper program $10B → $25B, buybacks cut $62.0B → $45.4BNew
FY2025: "In 2025, we raised capital through the issuance of debt and we expect to continue to assess the use of debt and other forms of financing in the future. We expect to continue to enter into finance leases, primarily for data centers. Additionally, in 2025, we provided credit support, such as through backstops and guarantees, to certain infrastructure related counterparties and may continue to provide additional credit support in the future."
FY2024: "We have a short-term debt financing program of up to $10.0 billion through the issuance of commercial paper."
FY2025: "We also have a commercial paper program of up to $25.0 billion, which is used for general corporate purposes."
Issues included US dollar notes with weighted-average maturities of about 20 to 24 years, plus €13.25B of euro notes. Future interest payments on the notes rose from $3.6B to $37.5B ($3.4B long-term plus $197M short-term, to $35.7B plus $1.8B). Buybacks fell 27% while operating cash flow rose 31% to $164.7B. Capital is being redirected to capex rather than raised to sustain returns.
▶Net income +32% vs operating income +15%: OI&E $7.4B → $29.8B on $24.1B of mostly unrealized private-equity gainsSignal
FY2025: "OI&E of $29.8 billion for the year ended December 31, 2025 included net gains on equity securities of $24.1 billion, primarily related to unrealized gains on our non-marketable equity securities."
Non-marketable equity carried under the measurement alternative went from $35.2B to $64.1B. Diluted EPS rose 34% to $10.81. Pre-tax OI&E rose $22.4B, against a $32.1B rise in net income. MD&A gives the gains their own executive-summary bullet. The constant-currency revenue section, published every prior year, is removed from the MD&A.
