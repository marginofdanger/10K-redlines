FY2018 → FY2019: Pre-Cloud

Filed Feb 2020 | Employees: 98,771 → 118,899 (+20%) | Ad Rev: ~85%

Business Overview

▶Overview rewritten: "deep computer science problems" replaces product narrativesChanged
The FY2018 overview described Alphabet's spirit through product examples (Android, Google Maps). FY2019 replaced it with a research-oriented framing:
FY2018: "inspiring us to do things like rethink the mobile device ecosystem with Android and map the world with Google Maps"
FY2019: "inspiring us to do things like tackling deep computer science problems, such as our investments in artificial intelligence (AI) and quantum computing"
Product achievements replaced with research ambition.
▶TikTok named as competitor for the first timeNew
FY2018 listed digital video competitors as "Amazon, Facebook, Hulu, and Netflix." FY2019 added three new names:
"Providers of digital video services, such as Amazon, Apple, AT&T, Disney, Facebook, Hulu, Netflix and TikTok."
TikTok, Apple, AT&T, and Disney all added in a single year.
▶Sustainability expanded from 1 paragraph to full section with targetsNew
FY2018 had a single paragraph on climate under "Other Items." FY2019 elevated it to a standalone section titled "Ongoing Commitment to Sustainability" with specific achievements: 18 new energy deals totaling 1,600 megawatts, carbon neutral shipping, and the Environmental Insights Explorer expanding to 100+ cities.

FY2019 → FY2020: Cloud + COVID

Filed Feb 2021 | Employees: 118,899 → 135,301 (+14%) | Ad Rev: ~81%

Business Overview

▶Google Services and Google Cloud become separate segmentsNew
FY2019 reported Google as a single segment. FY2020 introduced the two-segment structure:
FY2019: "the largest of which is Google"
FY2020: "which we report as two segments: Google Services and Google Cloud"
Google Cloud elevated from a subsection of "Serving our users" to a standalone segment with its own revenue reporting.
▶BERT highlighted; DeepMind enters Business sectionNew
FY2020 added specific AI breakthroughs to the "Power of machine learning" section:
"a huge breakthrough in natural language understanding, called BERT, now improves results for almost every English language search query."
DeepMind appeared in the Business section for the first time: "DeepMind made a significant AI-powered breakthrough, solving a 50-year-old protein folding challenge."
▶$5.75B sustainability bonds; carbon-free 2030 target; DEI commitment addedNew
"In 2020, we issued $5.75 billion in sustainability bonds, the largest sustainability or green bond issuance by any company in history."
New 2030 target: "to run our business on carbon-free energy everywhere, at all times, by 2030." Also: "Alphabet is committed to making diversity, equity, and inclusion part of everything we do" -- first appearance of DEI language in the filing.
▶Sundar Pichai CEO role formalized; Daydream VR removed; COVID response throughoutSignal
New sentence: "We continue this work under the leadership of Sundar Pichai, who has served as CEO of Google since 2015 and as CEO of Alphabet since 2019." Daydream VR platform removed from platform listing (was in FY2018-19). COVID response woven throughout: Exposure Notification apps, Community Mobility Reports, $100M+ in grants.

FY2020 → FY2021: MUM Era

Filed Feb 2022 | Employees: 135,301 → 156,500 (+16%) | Ad Rev: ~81%

Business Overview

▶"Power of AI" replaces "Power of machine learning"Changed
The section heading changed:
FY2020: "The power of machine learning"
FY2021: "The power of AI"
The body text similarly shifted: FY2020 led with "machine learning and AI are increasingly driving many of our latest innovations." FY2021 rewrote to: "investments in AI and machine learning are increasingly driving many of our latest innovations." AI moved ahead of ML in the ordering.
▶MUM introduced; $100B R&D cumulative disclosedNew
"in May of 2021, we introduced Multitask Unified Model -- or MUM -- which has the potential to transform how Google helps with complex tasks. MUM is trained across 75 different languages"
Also new: "We have invested more than $100 billion in R&D over the last five years." First time a cumulative R&D figure was disclosed.
▶Non-ad revenue breakout: Google Play, Hardware, YouTube non-advertisingNew
FY2021 added explicit breakout of non-ad revenue streams for the first time: Google Play (apps and in-app purchases), Hardware (Fitbit wearable devices, Google Nest home products, Pixel phones), and YouTube non-advertising (YouTube Premium and YouTube TV subscriptions). Fitbit appeared for the first time following its 2021 acquisition completion.
▶Other Bets revenue: "health technology" replaces "internet and TV services"Changed
FY2020: "Revenues are primarily generated from internet and TV services, as well as licensing and R&D services."
FY2021: "Revenues from Other Bets are generated primarily from the sale of health technology and internet services."
Verily's health technology revenue overtook legacy internet/TV services as the primary Other Bets revenue source.

FY2021 → FY2022: AI Foundational

Filed Feb 2023 | Employees: 156,500 → 190,234 (+22%, peak) | Ad Rev: ~79%

Business Overview

▶"AI is a foundational and transformational technology" -- new AI manifestoNew
The "Power of AI" section was entirely rewritten into a statement of belief:
"We believe that AI is a foundational and transformational technology that will provide compelling and helpful benefits to people and society through its capacity to assist, complement, empower, and inspire people in almost every field of human endeavor."
FY2021 described specific products (MUM, DeepMind). FY2022 replaced product descriptions with a philosophical position.
▶All named competitors removed from Competition sectionRemoved
FY2021 listed 20+ named competitors across categories (Baidu, Bing, Amazon, Apple, Meta, ByteDance, Netflix, Salesforce, etc.). FY2022 removed every company name, replacing them with generic categories:
FY2021: "Social networks offered by ByteDance, Meta, Snap, and Twitter"
FY2022: "social networks, which users may rely on for product or service referrals"
Naming competitors defines relevant markets for antitrust purposes.
▶Privacy elevated to standalone section; Pixel Watch debutsChanged
Privacy language, previously embedded in the "Serving our users" section, was elevated to its own standalone "Privacy and security" section. Hardware listing updated: Pixel Watch appeared for the first time ("the new Pixel 7 and Pixel 7 Pro, and the very first Pixel Watch"), while Chromecast and Google Nest Hub were removed from the device listing.

FY2022 → FY2023: Gemini

Filed Feb 2024 | Employees: 190,234 → 182,502 (-4%) | Ad Rev: ~75%

Business Overview

▶"Since 2016, an AI-first company" + Transformer origin storyNew
The overview was rewritten to claim an AI-first identity retroactively:
"It led us to be a pioneer in the development of AI and, since 2016, an AI-first company."
A Transformer origin story was added: "Large language models (LLMs) are an exciting aspect of our work in AI based on deep learning architectures, such as the Transformer, a neural network architecture that we introduced in 2017." Also added: BERT "in 2019 that helped Search understand the intent of user search queries better than ever before."
▶Gemini launched; five new subsections added; $150B R&D cumulativeNew
"In December 2023, we launched Gemini, our most capable and general model. It was built from the ground up to be multimodal."
Five new AI-oriented subsections appeared: "Deliver the Most Advanced, Safe, and Responsible AI," "Enable Organizations and Developers to Innovate on Google Cloud," "Improve Knowledge, Learning, Creativity, and Productivity," "Build the Most Helpful Personal Computing Platforms and Devices." Cumulative R&D updated from $100B to $150B.
▶Google Cloud restructured into five capability pillarsChanged
FY2022 described Google Cloud in a single paragraph. FY2023 introduced five named pillars: AI-optimized Infrastructure, Cybersecurity, Databases and Analytics, Collaboration Tools, and AI Platform and Duet AI for Google Cloud. Each received its own bullet-point description.
▶"Developers and providers of AI products" added to competition; moonshots "now in core products"Changed
New competition category: "developers and providers of AI products and services." Previously absent. Moonshots section reframed:
"Our early investments in AI started out as moonshots but are now incorporated into our core products and central to future developments."
Also: "ten blue links" framing expanded from a one-line reference to a full multi-paragraph Search evolution narrative.

FY2023 → FY2024: Platform Shift, Capex Inflection

Filed Feb 2025 | Revenue $307.4B → $350.0B (+14%) | Operating margin 27% → 32% | Capex $32.3B → $52.5B (+63%) | Free cash flow $69.5B → $72.8B (FCF / net income 94% → 73%) | First dividend ($7.4B) | Employees 182,502 → 183,323 | Ad share of revenue ~77% → ~76%

Business Overview

▶AI recast from "a transformational technology" to "a profound platform shift"Changed

Shift: degree: "transformational technology" → "profound platform shift" (+); hedge added: "We believe" (−)

FY2023: "AI is a transformational technology that can bring meaningful and positive change to people and societies across the world, and for our business."
FY2024: "We believe AI is a profound platform shift, one that can bring meaningful and positive change to people and societies across the world, and to our business."

History (phrase_history): "transformational technology" appears in the FY2023 10-K only; "profound platform shift" in the FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks.

"Platform shift" is the language of a transition that can unseat incumbents. The same filing adds AI developers to the MD&A's list of competitors for users and advertisers (below), so the stronger claim cuts both ways.
▶First cost-to-serve claim: AI Overviews machine costs "significantly lowered"; TPUs namedNew

Shift: named: +Tensor Processing Units (+); claim added with no figure (quant: none)

FY2024: "For example, since we started serving AI Overviews to our users, we have significantly lowered machine costs and latency through hardware, engineering, and technical breakthroughs."
FY2024: "Our AI-optimized infrastructure allows us to use, and offer our customers, a range of AI accelerator options, including our own custom-built Tensor Processing Units (TPUs)."

History (phrase_history): "Tensor Processing Units" is absent from the FY2023 10-K and every 2023-24 10-Q, and appears in FY2024, FY2025 and Q1 2026; "significantly lowered machine costs" appears in the FY2024 10-K only.

The sentence answers the obvious objection to putting generative answers in front of Search users, but gives no number. It is gone from the FY2025 10-K, the year capex nearly doubled.
▶Second wave of AI teams moved to Alphabet-level activities; the Gemini app's costs stay in Google ServicesChanged

Shift: scope: Brain team (2023) → "General AI model development teams" from Google Research (2024); named: +Gemini app team kept in segment (+ disclosure)

FY2023: "The group, called Google DeepMind, is reported within Alphabet-level activities prospectively beginning in the second quarter of 2023. Previously, the Brain team was included within Google Services."
FY2024: "General AI model development teams previously under Google Research in our Google Services segment are reported within Alphabet-level activities prospectively beginning in the second quarter of 2024."
FY2024: "The costs associated with the Gemini app team continue to be reported within our Google Services segment."

History (phrase_history): "centralized certain AI-related research" first appears in the FY2024 10-K and recurs in FY2025, Q1 2026 and Q2 2026 (not in the 2025 10-Qs); "Gemini app team" appears in the FY2024 10-K only.

Model-building cost has sat outside the segments since Q2 2023, and FY2024 moves more of it there. Alphabet-level losses rose from $9.2B to $10.5B even as severance and office charges fell by $2.4B, which suggests the AI research line grew by several billion dollars. The model cost no longer reaches Google Services or Google Cloud margins; only the consumer Gemini app does. (This dashboard previously dated the AI move to FY2024; it began in FY2023.)
▶120% water-replenishment goal deleted; net-zero "achieve" softened to "make progress toward"Removed

Shift: quant removed: "120%... by 2030" (−); certainty: "To accomplish this" → "To make progress toward this effort" (−)

FY2023: "To benefit the people and places where we operate, we have set goals to replenish 120% of the freshwater volume we consume, on average, across our offices and data centers by 2030..."
FY2023: "In 2021, we set an ambitious goal to achieve net-zero emissions across all of our operations and value chain, by 2030. To accomplish this, we aim to reduce 50%..."
FY2024: "In 2021, we set an ambitious goal to reach net-zero emissions across all of our operations and value chain by 2030. To make progress toward this effort, we aim to reduce 50%..."

History (phrase_history): "120% of the freshwater", "Science Based Targets" and "matched 100% of the electricity consumption" each appear in the FY2023 10-K only; "make progress toward this effort" in FY2024 only, before the whole section is deleted in FY2025.

The same edit deletes the Science Based Targets validation pledge and the claim of matching 100% of electricity with renewables since 2017, and adds AI as a source of "significant uncertainty". This is the step before FY2025, when both 2030 goals disappear.
▶DEI commitment deleted; "diversity" goes to zero across the 10-KRemoved

Shift: removed: commitment sentence and diversity-report link (−); named: "diversity" → "employment matters" in the regulation risk (de-named)

FY2023: "At Alphabet, we are committed to making diversity, equity, and inclusion part of everything we do and to growing a workforce that is representative of the users we serve."

History (phrase_history): "diversity" appears in the FY2023 10-K and in each 10-Q through Q3 2024, and in no filing from the FY2024 10-K on; "happy, healthy, and productive" (FY2023 only) becomes "perform at a high level" (FY2024, FY2025).

The deletion runs through the filing: the Government Regulation list replaces "reporting on human capital and diversity" with "sustainability and other social matters". The 10-Q forward-looking lists kept "our long-term sustainability and diversity goals" through Q3 2024, so this 10-K is where the word went.

Risk Factors

▶Antitrust risk goes from "lawsuits filed" to a liability ruling, with "structural remedies" named in Item 1ASignal

Shift: realized: "have filed... lawsuits" → "ruled that Google violated" (+); named: +structural remedies in Risk Factors

FY2023: "the U.S. Department of Justice, various U.S. states, and other plaintiffs have filed several antitrust lawsuits about various aspects of our business"
FY2024: "in August 2024, the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia ruled that Google violated such antitrust laws. The Court is holding a separate proceeding to determine remedies, which could include alterations to our products and services and our business models and operations, including structural remedies, and/or our distribution arrangements, among other changes."

History (phrase_history): the "ruled that Google violated" sentence first appears in the Q3 2024 10-Q and is carried into this 10-K; "structural remedies" was already in the FY2023 legal note as a generic outcome, but not in the FY2023 risk factors.

The risk is restated as having happened. The litigation paragraph is split into dated paragraphs per case, including a Texas ad tech trial "set for trial in March 2025". The Epic paragraph moves from "We plan to appeal" to an October 2024 remedies decision under appeal.
▶EU copyright regime: "may introduce new licensing regimes" becomes "have introduced"Changed

Shift: realized: certainty may → have (hypothetical → actual)

FY2023: "...including the EU Directive on Copyright in the Digital Single Market and European Economic Area transpositions, which may introduce new licensing regimes, increase liability with respect to content uploaded by users..."
FY2024: "...including the EU Directive on Copyright in the Digital Single Market and European Economic Area transpositions, which have introduced new licensing regimes, increase liability with respect to content uploaded by users..."

History (phrase_history): "which may introduce new licensing regimes" appears in the FY2023 10-K only; "which have introduced new licensing regimes" in FY2024 and FY2025.

A one-word change that turns a forecast into a fact: payments to publishers under the EU copyright rules are now a cost, not a possibility. It sits in the same bullet list where FY2025 adds "laws limiting use of data for AI training".
▶Useful-life risk moved from accounting estimates into Item 1A, two years after the six-year server life added $3.9B to earningsNew

Shift: moved up: critical-estimate boilerplate → standalone risk factor (+ prominence); quant removed: "$3.9 billion" depreciation benefit no longer restated (−)

FY2023: "The effect of this change was a reduction in depreciation expense of $3.9 billion for the year ended December 31, 2023, recognized primarily in cost of revenues and R&D expenses."
FY2024: "We have invested significantly and expect to continue to invest significantly in our property and equipment, including our technical infrastructure, and we expect these assets to benefit our business over their estimated useful lives."

History (phrase_history): "could indicate a change in the period over which we expect to benefit" stood in the FY2023 critical accounting estimates; FY2024 is the first 10-K to put it in Risk Factors. FY2025 rewrites the trigger as "historical asset performance, expected technology advancements, and future network deployment plans".

A disclosed earnings tailwind becomes a named risk once capex reaches $52.5B. A shorter server life would raise depreciation, which went from $11.9B to $15.3B in the year.
▶AI accelerator supply constraint added: competitors could "claim the supply or capacity first"New

Shift: named: +GPUs, +TPUs; new risk (condition: "If we are unable to negotiate favorable contractual terms")

FY2024: "For example, industry supply capacity for AI accelerators, including Graphics Processing Units, or GPUs, as well as our custom-built TPUs, is highly competitive and rapidly evolving. If we are unable to negotiate favorable contractual terms or our competitors claim the supply or capacity first, we may face supply constraints."

History (phrase_history): "AI accelerators" and "claim the supply or capacity first" first appear in the FY2024 10-K and are kept in FY2025; neither appears in any 2023-24 10-Q. "Higher level of investment in technical infrastructure" enters the margin risk in the same filing.

Energy also appears as a cost line in the cost-of-revenues definition and the AI risk factor. These are the risks that become "power, water, and land" constraints in FY2025.
▶Cloud: "may not attain sufficient scale and profitability" dropped; "platform switching costs" namedChanged

Shift: hedge dropped: "sufficient scale and profitability" (+); named: +platform switching costs

FY2023: "Pricing and delivery models are competitive and constantly evolving, and we may not attain sufficient scale and profitability to achieve our business objectives."
FY2024: "Pricing, including platform switching costs, are challenging across the industry, and delivery models are competitive and constantly evolving, and we may therefore not achieve our business objectives."

History (phrase_history): "sufficient scale and profitability" appears in the FY2023 10-K only; "platform switching costs" in FY2024 only (FY2025 replaces it with pricing "subject to increasing regulatory scrutiny").

The profitability worry goes as Cloud operating income rises from $1.7B to $6.1B. Naming switching costs as an industry problem is consistent with Google pointing at rivals' customer lock-in as the limit on its own pricing; that reading is an inference.
▶Third-party cookies: "phase out" becomes a "proposed user choice model"Changed

Shift: commitment weakened: "phase out" → "proposed user choice model" (−); condition added: "subject to continuing discussions with regulators" (−)

FY2023: "such as our initiatives to phase out third-party cookies"
FY2024: "such as our initiatives related to third-party cookies, including our announcement in July 2024 to move from phasing out all third-party cookies to a proposed user choice model (which remains subject to continuing discussions with regulators)"

History (phrase_history): "phase out third-party cookies" appears in FY2023 and the Q1 2024 10-Q; "proposed user choice model" in the Q2 2024 10-Q and this 10-K only. FY2025 drops cookies from the risk factors.

A multi-year deprecation plan is withdrawn, and the sentence concedes the regulator still has a say.
▶ESG risk-factor heading deleted; "diversity" becomes "employment matters"Removed

Shift: removed: standalone heading (−); de-named: "diversity" → "employment matters"

FY2023: "Expectations relating to ESG considerations could expose us to potential liabilities, increased costs, and reputational harm."
FY2024: "Further, we are subject to evolving laws, regulations, policies, and international accords relating to matters beyond our core products and services, including environmental sustainability, climate change, human capital, and employment matters."

History (phrase_history): "Expectations relating to ESG" appears in the FY2023 10-K only.

FY2023's warning that failing to "adhere to our public statements" could cause harm is deleted with the heading, the same year the water goal goes. Fewer public goals means less to fall short of.

MD&A

▶AI developers added as competitors for "user engagement and advertisers"; COVID explanation for slower growth droppedSignal

Shift: named: +"other developers and providers of AI products and services" (+ disclosure); condition dropped: COVID comparison

FY2023: "...although at a slower pace than we have experienced historically, in particular after the outsized growth in our advertising revenues during the COVID-19 pandemic. In addition, we face increasing competition for user engagement and advertisers, which may affect our revenues."
FY2024: "...although at a slower pace than we have experienced historically. In addition, we face increasing competition for user engagement and advertisers, including from other developers and providers of AI products and services, which may affect our revenues."

History (phrase_history): "increasing competition for user engagement and advertisers" appears in FY2023 and FY2024; the AI-developer clause enters the MD&A sentence in FY2024 (the Business section listed AI developers from FY2023). "although at a slower pace than we have experienced historically" is dropped in FY2025.

The trend section also replaces "revenues from ads on YouTube and Google Play monetize at a lower rate than our traditional search ads" with user behavior that "evolves, for example with AI". The mix risk management names is no longer YouTube versus Search but AI versus Search.
▶Search TAC rate rose on mobile "paid access points" (FY2023: "substantially consistent")Signal

Shift: direction: "substantially consistent" → "increased" (+ disclosure of cause)

FY2023: "The TAC rate on Google Search & other revenues and the TAC rate on Google Network revenues were both substantially consistent from 2022 to 2023."
FY2024: "The TAC rate on Google Search & other revenues increased from 2023 to 2024 primarily due to increases related to mobile searches, which carries higher TAC because more mobile searches are channeled through paid access points."

History (phrase_history): "TAC rate on Google Search & other revenues increased" appears from the Q3 2024 10-Q through Q2 2025; FY2025 returns to "substantially consistent".

Paid access points (default-search deals with browsers, carriers and OEMs) are what the DOJ ruled on in August 2024. The higher rate shows the cost of the distribution the court found unlawful was still rising.
▶Pillar Two: "we do not expect a material change" becomes "did not have a material effect"; the "could increase" warning leaves the MD&AChanged

Shift: realized: expect → did (+); removed: "could increase in future years" from MD&A (− forward warning)

FY2023: "As additional jurisdictions enact such legislation, we expect our effective tax rate and cash tax payments could increase in future years."
FY2024: "Some countries have already implemented the legislation effective January 1, 2024, and we expect others to follow, however this did not have a material effect on our income tax provision for the 2024 fiscal year."

History (phrase_history): "could increase in future years" appears in FY2023 and each 2024 10-Q; in FY2024 and Q1 2025 only in the forward-looking list, and in no filing after.

The effective tax rate rose from 13.9% to 16.4%, but the filing attributes that to a one-time 2023 IRS rule benefit, not to Pillar Two. The forward warning that remained is dropped from the 10-Q list in Q2 2025.
▶Subsequent event: $8.0B January 2025 unrealized gain on one private-company stakeNew

Shift: quant added: "$8.0 billion" (+); counterparty unnamed ("a private company")

FY2024: "In January 2025, we recognized an $8.0 billion unrealized gain on our non-marketable equity securities related to our investment in a private company."

History (phrase_history): the FY2023 10-K had no subsequent-events note; the $8.0B sentence appears in FY2024 and Q1 2025 only. FY2025's subsequent event is "approximately $32.0 billion".

A single unnamed holding, marked up on an observable transaction, produced a gain larger than all of 2024's OI&E ($7.4B). It is the first of the mark-ups that lift net income above operating income in 2025 and 2026.

FY2024 → FY2025: Building on Borrowed Capacity

Business Overview

▶Net-zero 2030 and 24/7 carbon-free 2030 goals deleted; risk factor drops "ambitious goals" and names AI's energy and water demandsRemoved

FY2024 (Risk Factors): "In response, we have implemented robust programs, adopted reporting frameworks and principles, and announced a number of complex and ambitious goals and initiatives that may require considerable investments."

The goals are not missed or revised, they stop being stated. The revolver paragraph also drops the interest-rate link to "certain sustainability goals" (removed from the Q1 2025 10-Q first), and renewable-energy purchase agreements become "power purchase agreements with third-party suppliers".
▶Gemini reach restated at a lower bar: "seven two billion-user products" becomes "15 half-billion-user products"Changed

FY2025: "Today, all 15 of our half-billion-user products — including seven with two billion users — use our Gemini models."

History (phrase_history): "all seven of our two billion-user products" appears in FY2024 only; "all 15 of our half-billion-user products" in FY2025 only. "More than one billion users" for AI Overviews (FY2024 only) is not restated.

The headline number grew because the threshold was halved; the count of two-billion-user products is still seven. The only user metric FY2024 gave for an AI Search product is dropped, and AI Mode arrives without one.
▶Other Bets revenue now led by "autonomous transportation services"; healthcare dropped from the sentenceSignal

Shift: named: +autonomous transportation, −healthcare-related services

FY2024: "Revenues from Other Bets are generated primarily from the sale of healthcare-related services, and internet services."
FY2025: "Revenues from Other Bets are generated primarily from the sale of autonomous transportation services and internet services."

Other Bets revenue fell ($1,648M to $1,537M) while the loss rose from $4.4B to $7.5B, including "a $2.1 billion employee compensation charge recognized in the fourth quarter for Waymo... based on estimated stock valuation". The charge and Alphabet funding "the significant majority" of a $16.0B Waymo round both suggest Waymo's mark is rising faster than its revenue.

Risk Factors

▶Advertising share floor lowered: "more than 75%" becomes "more than 70%" (actual 73%)Changed

Shift: quant: floor 75% → 70% (− precision)

FY2024: "We generated more than 75% of total revenues from online advertising in 2024."
FY2025: "We generated more than 70% of total revenues from online advertising in 2025."

History (phrase_history): "more than 75% of total revenues" stood in FY2023, the Q1 and Q2 2024 10-Qs and FY2024; "more than 70%" appears in FY2025 only.

The actual share was 73.2% ($294.7B of $402.8B), so the floor moved further than the business did and leaves room for further decline without another edit.
▶New: "AI is quickly reshaping the advertising industry", with "no assurance that we will adapt effectively"New
