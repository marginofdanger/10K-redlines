▶ Subsidy expiry shows up "as expected": uninsured admissions +15.6%; managed care and insurers fall to 47.5% of revenue from 49.4% Signal

The 10-K's forward-looking "expected to adversely impact" becomes a realized result one quarter later:

▶ New quarterly breakdown shows uninsured admissions already up 7.1% in Q4 2025, before the credits expired New

The 10-K gave only a full-year figure. The 10-Q's quarter-by-quarter history adds a fourth-quarter number that was not disclosed before:

FY2025 10-K: "Same facility uninsured emergency room visits declined 0.6% and same facility uninsured admissions increased 1.2% during 2025 compared to 2024."
Q1 2026: "Same facility uninsured admissions in 2025, compared to 2024, increased 7.1% in the fourth quarter, declined 2.0% in the third quarter, increased 0.4% in the second quarter and declined 0.7% in the first quarter."

The 1.2% annual figure hid a fourth-quarter jump that came before the January 1 subsidy expiry. The filing attributes only the Q1 2026 increase to the expiry and gives no cause for Q4. The jump is consistent with enrollees dropping coverage ahead of 2026 premium increases, but that is an inference.

▶ Charity care +51% to $5.513B (from $3.644B); total uncompensated care +24% to $13.6B Signal

The note to the uncompensated-care table, outside the MD&A, shows where the newly uninsured patients are being recorded:

Q1 2026: "The total uncompensated care amounts for the quarters ended March 31, 2026 and 2025 include charity care of $5.513 billion and $3.644 billion, respectively, and the related estimated costs of charity care were $507 million and $350 million, respectively."

HCA's charity policy covers non-elective patients at or below 400% of the federal poverty level. That is the income band the enhanced credits had subsidized. Charity care grew more than twice as fast as total uncompensated care. The cost-to-charges ratio also fell from 9.6% to 9.2%, which lowers the reported cost of the same gross charges.

Themes

1. The Federal Policy Arc Ran at Quarterly Cadence

Exchange-subsidy expiration entered the forward-looking factors in Q3 2024, became a standalone MD&A paragraph in Q1 2025, expanded into a full OBBBA Recent Developments section in Q2 2025, was quantified in Q3 2025 (4% sequestration, $8.0B/yr DSH), renamed FBA with first 2026 guidance in the FY2025 10-K, showed up as realized impact in Q1 2026 (uninsured admissions +15.6%, "As expected"), and accelerated in Q2 2026 to +23.3% alongside the first booked Medicaid directed-payment offset ($1.372B Florida revenues). Each stage appeared one filing at a time.

2. Capex Guidance Flat, Pipeline Up 87%

Annual capex guidance stayed within $5.0–5.5B across all ten filings, while the approved construction pipeline rose from $4.6B to a peak of $8.8B, with the two largest single-quarter increases (+$0.9B in Q1 2025, +$1.7B in Q1 2026) occurring after annual filings that left guidance unchanged. Q2 2026 recorded the series' first pipeline decline, to $8.6B, in the same filing that narrowed the guidance sentence to exclude acquisitions.

3. Hurricane Disclosure Had a Three-Filing Lifecycle

Hurricanes Helene and Milton appeared in Q3 2024 ($50M actual + $200–300M Q4 estimate), settled at a $250M full-year actual in the FY2024 10-K (the bottom of the implied $250–350M range, with Georgia dropped and the insurance-recovery sentence not carried), and were fully wound down from MD&A by Q1 2025.

4. Item 1A Carried One Material-Change Disclosure in Ten Filings

The quarterly risk-factor stub carried no material-change body in six of the eight 10-Qs. The single exception — the indebtedness section tied to the February 2025 secured-to-unsecured refinancing — was added in Q1 2025, trimmed in Q2 2025 (covenant subsection deleted), and withdrawn in Q3 2025. The Medicaid/EPTC policy changes were never run through Item 1A; they arrived in the FY2025 10-K risk factors. Q2 2026 is byte-identical to Q1 2026, and carries no Item 1A text against the April 30, 2026 issuance of 4.700% notes due 2031, 5.000% due 2033, and 5.300% due 2036 (Supplemental Indentures 54–56) — the 2025 precedent paired a refinancing with an exception body.
