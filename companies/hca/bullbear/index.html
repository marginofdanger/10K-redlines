Latest filing: 10-Q Q3 2026

What the Q3 2026 10-Q adds, for and against. It is the first quarter after the Florida catch-up, so it shows the recurring base: volume and unit costs improved, profit fell, and the funding of buybacks moved further onto commercial paper.

Bull case

P1 Volume and payer mixStated
Volume accelerates and commercial revenue grows again: equivalent admissions +2.8%, managed care and insurers +3.3%
Q3 2026: "Consolidated and same facility equivalent admissions increased 2.8% and 3.0%, respectively, in the third quarter of 2026, compared to the third quarter of 2025."
Q3 2026: "Managed care and insurers | 9,574 | 48.3 | 9,267 | 48.4"

Size: managed care and insurer revenue +$307M year over year (Q2: −$111M); 29,320 more equivalent admissions; emergency department visits +5.3%.

Consolidated equivalent admissions growth rose through 2026 (+1.1%, +2.6%, +2.8%), and commercial revenue grew in dollars despite the uninsured shift. Surgeries are still down (inpatient −1.3%, outpatient −4.2%), so the growth is again emergency-led.

Next filing: managed care and insurer revenue growth of 3% or more in Q4 with surgeries no longer falling.

P3 Margins and cost controlStated
Unit costs fall: supply cost per equivalent admission −1.4% (devices −2.5%), salaries and benefits per equivalent admission −0.2%
Q3 2026: "Supply costs per equivalent admission declined 1.4% in the third quarter of 2026, compared to the third quarter of 2025."
Q3 2026: "Salaries and benefits per equivalent admission declined 0.2% in the third quarter of 2026, compared to the third quarter of 2025."

Size: salaries 43.2% of revenue (43.6%) and supplies 14.2% (14.5%): 0.7 points, about $139M in the quarter (arithmetic on $19.849B of revenue).

Q2's test (salaries per equivalent admission flat or down again) is met, and unlike Q2 the ratios are not flattered by a catch-up in the revenue line. Same facility salaries per full-time equivalent still rose 2.7%, so the gain is productivity, not lower wages.

Next filing: per-unit labor and supply costs flat or down in Q4, and whether other operating expenses (23.0% vs 21.8%) stop absorbing the gain.

P5 Cash conversion and capital returnStated
Operating cash flow recovers to $4.461B as the SDP receivable unwinds; Q3 free cash flow $3.10B
Q3 2026: "Cash flows from operating activities increased $45 million, from $4.416 billion for the third quarter of 2025 to $4.461 billion for the third quarter of 2026."
Q3 2026: "The $1.467 billion decline in cash provided by operating activities for the first nine months of 2026 compared to the first nine months of 2025 related primarily to unfavorable changes in working capital items of $472 million"

Size: Q3 FCF $3.104B = 197% of net income attributable; receivables fell about $782M in the quarter (cash flow statement, six vs nine months); DSO 53 (Q2 55). Q3 buybacks plus dividends were 101% of FCF.

The six-month working-capital drag of $1.099B, which Q2 tied to SDP receivables, shrank to $472M, and the receivable clause is gone. The +$45M is also net of about $611M more tax paid than in Q3 2025, when the Tennessee deferral was in force (arithmetic on the six- and nine-month figures).

Next filing: full-year FCF of at least 2025's $7.69B and DSO back to about 51.

P7 Growth investmentStated
Capex guide firmed: "approximately" dropped from the $5.0–5.5B range; nine-month capex +7.3%
Q3 2026: "Planned capital expenditures are expected to be between $5.0 billion and $5.5 billion in 2026, excluding acquisitions."

Size: nine months $3.707B vs $3.455B; Q4 needs $1.29–1.79B against $1.489B in Q4 2025, so 2026 is on course to beat 2025's $4.944B.

In 2024 and 2025 the guide was trimmed mid-year and spending landed below it. This year the wording firmed in Q3 instead, the first guide without "approximate" or "approximately" since at least the FY2023 10-K.

Next filing: FY2026 capex of $5.0B or more in the 10-K, and the construction pipeline (flat at $8.6B) rising again.

No bullish evidence on the balance sheet or on SDP this quarter. The $1.010B revolver draw was repaid, but with commercial paper; the filing claims "modest increases" in SDP net benefit without a figure.

Bear case

P4 Earnings quality and reservesStated
First clean quarter after the catch-up: net income attributable −3.9%, pre-tax income −2.0%; EPS +4.5% only on 8.0% fewer shares
Q3 2026: "Net income attributable to HCA Healthcare, Inc. totaled $1.579 billion, or $7.27 per diluted share, for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $1.643 billion, or $6.96 per diluted share, for the quarter ended September 30, 2025."
Q3 2026: "The third quarter results for 2026 reflected improvement in certain areas of our business"

Size: net income attributable −$64M; nine-month pre-tax income $7.151B vs $7.160B, and about $6.73B (−6%) without the $423M of pre-2026 Florida profit booked in Q2 (arithmetic on the Q2 10-Q's $980M and $557M).

Q2's test was a quarter without prior-period SDP items. Q3 is that quarter (gains $13M, equity-award tax benefits about $3M) and the recurring base is below last year. It is the first year-over-year decline in quarterly net income attributable in the 10-Qs from Q2 2024 on. The MD&A's new summary sentence describes only what improved.

Next filing: Q4 pre-tax income against Q4 2025; a second quarterly decline in net income attributable would confirm that the recurring base is shrinking.

Q3 2026: "Adjusted segment EBITDA | 1,480 | 1,288 | 1,555 | 3,922 | 4,554 | 4,338"

Q3 2026: "Other operating expenses, as a percentage of revenues, were 23.0% in the third quarter of 2026 and 21.8% in the third quarter of 2025."

Atlantic, which holds Florida, grew revenue 4.5% but earned less, consistent with SDP program costs running ahead of SDP revenue (inference; the quarterly discussion gives no cause). American has not grown EBITDA in any 2026 quarter and is down 5.2% for nine months.

Next filing: Atlantic and American EBITDA growth in Q4 and other operating expenses back near 22% of revenue would refute; a fourth margin decline confirms.

Q3 2026: "Same facility uninsured admissions in 2026, compared to 2025, increased 27.4% in the third quarter, increased 23.4% in the second quarter and increased 15.5% in the first quarter."

Next filing: uninsured admissions growth below 20% would refute; above 25% against the tougher Q4 base confirms.

Next filing: the size of a new authorization (each $10B program has lasted about five quarters) and whether 2027 repurchases stay within FCF.
