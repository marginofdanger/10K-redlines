Latest filing: 10-Q Q2 2026

What the Q2 2026 10-Q adds, for and against. The quarter's headline (+8.7% revenue, +11.6% EPS) rests on a one-time Florida catch-up; the cards below separate what is recurring from what is not.

Bull case

P2 Medicaid supplemental / SDPStated
Florida directed payment approved: $1.372B revenue and $829M expense booked, covering October 2024 to June 2026
Q2 2026: "The second quarter of 2026 includes incremental revenues of $1.372 billion and other operating expenses of $829 million related to the Florida directed payment program for the time period October 1, 2024 through June 30, 2026"
Q2 2026: "Certain states in which we operate have submitted applications to CMS and received approval to increase payments up to the average commercial rate before the step down begins in 2028."

Size: $1.372B = 6.8% of Q2 revenue ($20.230B); net of expense about $543M = 22% of Q2 pre-tax income ($2.494B). The recurring 2026 portion is $392M of revenue.

The FY2025 10-K guided SDP revenue to decline in 2026 before approvals; one approval more than reversed that, and grandfathered rates now run at the commercial-rate ceiling until 2028.

Next filing: whether Florida revenue continues at the run-rate (about $196M a quarter of 2026-period revenue) and whether other state approvals follow.

P1 Volume and payer mixStated
Volume reaccelerates: admissions +2.4% and equivalent admissions +2.6% (Q1: +0.7% and +1.1%)
Q2 2026: "During the quarter ended June 30, 2026, consolidated admissions increased 2.4% and same facility admissions increased 2.5% compared to the quarter ended June 30, 2025."

Size: about 2.4% more admissions year over year; emergency department visits +3.5%.

Demand for hospital care held up after a weak, respiratory-light Q1. The growth came through the emergency department rather than scheduled surgery, so it is volume of lower quality (see bear side).

Next filing: admissions growth at or above 2% with surgeries stabilizing.

P3 Margins and cost controlStated
Labor cost per unit falls: salaries and benefits per equivalent admission −0.7%
Q2 2026: "Salaries and benefits, as a percentage of revenues, were 41.0% in the second quarter of 2026 and 43.7% in the second quarter of 2025."

Size: salaries and benefits $8.3B in the quarter; a 0.7% per-unit decline against 2.5% wage growth per full-time equivalent. The 2.7-point ratio drop is mostly the Florida revenue in the denominator.

The per-admission measure is not flattered by the Florida catch-up, and is consistent with the "financial resiliency initiatives" the 10-K introduced (inference; the 10-Q does not credit them).

Next filing: salaries per equivalent admission flat or down again in Q3.

P6 Balance sheet and liquidityStated
2026 maturities refinanced at lower coupons: $3.0B issued at 4.70–5.30%, $2.5B of 5.25–5.375% notes redeemed
Q2 2026: "During April 2026, we issued $3.000 billion aggregate principal amount of senior notes comprised of (i) $1.000 billion aggregate principal amount of 4.700% senior notes due 2031"
Q2 2026: "During May 2026, we repaid all $1.500 billion aggregate principal amount of 5.250% senior notes due 2026 and all $1.000 billion aggregate principal amount of 5.375% senior notes due 2026."

Size: average effective debt rate 4.9% vs 5.1%; 0.2 points on about $48B of average debt is roughly $95M a year of interest (inference from stated rates).

Market access remains open at investment-grade terms, and the near-term maturity wall for 2026 is cleared.

Next filing: average rate holding near 4.9% as commercial paper is termed out.

P5 Cash conversion and capital returnStated
Share count −7.9% year over year lifts EPS +11.6% on net income +2.8%
Q2 2026: "Shares used for diluted earnings per share were 222.828 million shares for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 and 241.911 million shares for the quarter ended June 30, 2025."

Size: 19.1M fewer diluted shares; $7.210B of authorization left after $3.635B used in H1.

Per-share compounding continues. Whether it is a bull point depends on funding, which this quarter was debt (see the bear card on cash).

Next filing: buyback pace relative to free cash flow once SDP receivables are collected.

Bear case

P1 Volume and payer mixStated
Payer mix deteriorates: uninsured admissions +23.4% same facility; managed care and insurer revenue down 1.2%
Q2 2026: "The second quarter increase in uninsured admissions includes the impact of attrition related to Exchange volumes from the first quarter that occurred during the second quarter."
Q2 2026: "Managed care and insurers | 9,013 | 44.6 | 9,124 | 49.1"

Size: commercial revenue $111M lower year over year; uninsured 8% of admissions (7%); total uncompensated care $15.076B, +30%.

The EPTC effect accelerated rather than leveled off, and the filing says Q1 enrollees lapsed in Q2, so the run-rate may still be rising. Inpatient surgeries −2.3% and outpatient −4.4% point the same way.

Next filing: uninsured admissions growth below 20% and managed care revenue back to growth would refute; another acceleration confirms.

P3 Margins and cost controlStated
Outside Florida, segment EBITDA falls: American Group $1.578B → $1.369B (−13%), National $1.271B → $1.184B (−7%)
Q2 2026: "Adjusted segment EBITDA | 1,184 | 1,827 | 1,369 | 2,442 | 3,266 | 2,783"
Q2 2026 (prior year): "Adjusted segment EBITDA | 1,271 | 1,352 | 1,578 | 2,403 | 2,814 | 2,996"

Size: $296M less EBITDA from two groups that produce 58% of revenue; the Atlantic Group (Florida) added $475M.

The MD&A discusses consolidated results only. Without the segment note, a reader would not see that the two non-Florida groups lost operating leverage on flat revenue.

Next filing: American and National EBITDA versus Q3 2025; a third quarter of decline in National would confirm a trend.

P4 Earnings quality and reservesStated
$980M of revenue and $557M of expense in the quarter belong to periods before 2026
Q2 2026: "Of those amounts, approximately $980 million of incremental revenues and $557 million of other operating expenses related to periods prior to 2026."

Size: about $423M pre-tax = 17% of Q2 pre-tax income; without it pre-tax income is about $2.07B, −14% year over year (arithmetic on disclosed figures).

Reported Q2 growth is a catch-up. The 10-Q gives no adjusted figure, and the "$400 million of incremental net benefit" it cites does not reconcile to the $543M net of the Florida amounts.

Next filing: Q3 results with no prior-period SDP items, to show the underlying trend.

P5 Cash conversion and capital returnStated
Operating cash flow −$1.875B year over year; H1 buybacks $3.635B against about $2.0B of free cash flow
Q2 2026: "Cash flows from operating activities declined $1.875 billion, from $4.210 billion for the second quarter of 2025 to $2.335 billion for the second quarter of 2026."
Q2 2026: "During the first six months of 2026, net cash flows used in financing activities included a net increase of $3.065 billion in our indebtedness"

Size: H1 operating cash flow $4.349B less capex $2.350B = $2.0B FCF; buybacks were 1.8x that. Days revenue in receivables 55 vs 51.

Part of the gap is timing (Florida receivables, a $594M deferred Tennessee tax payment), so this can reverse. But buybacks did not slow while cash fell.

Next filing: collection of SDP receivables (DSO back near 51) and buybacks within free cash flow.

P6 Balance sheet and liquidityStated
Commercial paper at the $4.000B program limit, revolver drawn $1.010B, availability $4.336B → $3.086B
Q2 2026: "the aggregate amount outstanding under the commercial paper program ($3.890 billion and $4.000 billion as of June 30, 2026 and July 24, 2026, respectively))"
Q2 2026: "Senior unsecured credit facility (effective interest rate of 4.8 %) | 1,010 | —"

Size: total debt $49.718B, up $3.23B since December; variable-rate debt $5.200B; a one-point rate rise now costs $52M pre-tax (was $40M).

Short-term funding is now at its stated ceiling, and the revolver is in use for the first time since Q1 2025, after the 10-K removed the covenant on restricted payments.

Next filing: commercial paper below the limit and the revolver repaid, or another term issue.

P2 Medicaid supplemental / SDPStated
Provider-tax final rule (February 2026) and proposed rule (July 2026) restrict how states fund their SDP share
Q2 2026: "As a result of these changes, some taxes on managed care organizations and providers permitted prior to the enactment of the FBA must be reduced, or are no longer permissible, subject to transition periods."

Size: not quantified in the filing; it bears on SDP and supplemental revenue of about $6.2B in 2025.

The Florida and Texas programs rely on local provider taxes; a squeeze on that funding can shrink SDP payments before the 2028 FBA step-down begins (inference).

Next filing: any state program reduced or restructured, or HCA quantifying the provider-tax effect.
