HCA HEALTHCARE (HCA): INVESTMENT THESIS PILLARS
Written October 2026 from the 10-K dashboard themes and tracked signals, the 10-Q scanner,
and the FY2025 10-K and Q2 2026 10-Q. Stances move only when two or more filings point the same way.

P1 Volume and payer mix
  Bull assumes: admissions and outpatient volume keep growing 2-3% a year in Florida/Texas growth
    markets, with commercial (managed care and insurers) holding near half of revenue.
  Bear fears: the end of enhanced premium tax credits (EPTCs) at end-2025 and FBA Medicaid
    eligibility rules push patients into the uninsured column; surgeries shift out of hospitals.
  Stance: Weakening (FY2025 10-K: admissions growth halved to +2.7% and the filing expects EPTC
    expiry to raise the uninsured rate; Q2 2026: uninsured admissions +23.4%, managed care and
    insurer revenue down 1.2% in dollars, surgeries down).
  Tests: uninsured admissions growth, managed care revenue in the payer table, surgical volumes,
    Florida/Texas share of admissions.

P2 Medicaid supplemental and state directed payments (SDP)
  Bull assumes: SDP and supplemental programs (about $6.2B of 2025 revenue) stay large through
    2027 under grandfathering, with approvals arriving as catch-ups.
  Bear fears: FBA caps (100% / 110% of Medicare from 2028, 10 points a year), provider-tax limits
    that fund the state share, and timing risk on approvals.
  Stance: Intact (FY2025 bear: caps legislated, 2026 decline guided; Q2 2026 bull: Florida approved,
    $1.372B booked, approvals "up to the average commercial rate"; Q2 2026 bear: provider-tax final rule).
  Tests: SDP revenue disclosed, new approvals, provider-tax rule outcomes, step-down start in 2028.

P3 Margins and cost control
  Bull assumes: labor productivity and supply chain keep salaries and benefits and supplies falling
    as a share of revenue; "financial resiliency initiatives" offset part of the policy hit.
  Bear fears: wage inflation, tariffs, professional fees and rising provider-tax/SDP expense absorb
    pricing; segments outside Florida lose operating leverage.
  Stance: Intact (FY2025: salaries 43.5% vs 44.1%; Q2 2026: salaries 41.0% but other operating
    expenses +4.5 points and American/National segment EBITDA down).
  Tests: segment adjusted EBITDA, salaries per equivalent admission, other operating expense ratio.

P4 Earnings quality and reserves
  Bull assumes: reported EPS reflects recurring operations; reserves are conservative.
  Bear fears: catch-up payments for prior periods, tax items and reserve releases flatter EPS;
    professional liability reserves develop adversely.
  Stance: Weakening (FY2025: third straight year of adverse prior-year malpractice development, $126M;
    Q2 2026: $980M of revenue and $557M of expense relate to periods before 2026).
  Tests: prior-period components in each quarter, reserve development table in the 10-K.

P5 Cash conversion and capital return
  Bull assumes: operating cash flow exceeds net income, and buybacks/dividends are funded from free
    cash flow, shrinking the share count 5-8% a year.
  Bear fears: buybacks outrun free cash flow and are funded with debt while receivables build.
  Stance: Intact (FY2025 bull: FCF $7.69B = 113% of net income; but $10.07B of buybacks exceeded it;
    Q2 2026 bear: operating cash flow down $1.875B, H1 buybacks $3.635B vs H1 FCF about $2.0B).
  Tests: operating cash flow vs net income, DSO, buybacks vs FCF.

P6 Balance sheet and liquidity
  Bull assumes: investment-grade unsecured structure, ample revolver capacity, debt grows only with
    EBITDA.
  Bear fears: debt-funded buybacks, short-term commercial paper reliance, shrinking headroom.
  Stance: Weakening (FY2025: debt +$3.46B to $46.49B, restricted-payment covenant gone, revolver
    availability $7.986B -> $5.779B, working capital negative; Q2 2026: debt $49.718B, CP at the
    $4.000B limit, revolver drawn $1.010B, availability $3.086B).
  Tests: total debt, CP outstanding vs $4.0B limit, revolver availability, ratings language.

P7 Growth investment
  Bull assumes: a rising construction pipeline (beds, outpatient, freestanding EDs) sustains volume
    growth; capex stays near $5B, funded internally.
  Bear fears: capex underspends guidance or is cut to protect buybacks; acquisitions creep outside
    the guided number.
  Stance: Intact (FY2025 bull: pipeline $4.7B -> $7.1B; Q2 2026: pipeline $8.8B -> $8.6B, first
    decline, and guide now "excluding acquisitions").
  Tests: construction pipeline, capex vs guidance, acquisition spend.
