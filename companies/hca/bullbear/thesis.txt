HCA HEALTHCARE (HCA): INVESTMENT THESIS PILLARS
Written October 2026 from the 10-K dashboard themes and tracked signals, the 10-Q scanner, and the
eight filings from the Q3 2024 10-Q to the Q2 2026 10-Q. Stances move only when two or more filings
point the same way; "Last moved" names the filing that completed the run.

P1 Volume and payer mix
  Bull assumes: admissions and outpatient volume keep growing 2-3% a year in Florida/Texas growth
    markets, with commercial (managed care and insurers) holding near half of revenue.
  Bear fears: the end of enhanced premium tax credits (EPTCs) at end-2025 and FBA Medicaid
    eligibility rules push patients into the uninsured column; surgeries shift out of hospitals.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q1 2026 10-Q.
  History: Q3 2024 and FY2024 strong (admissions +4.5% and +5.0%, uninsured falling). 2025 slowed to
    +2-3% (Q2 2025 +2.1%), with the OBBBA (Q2 2025) and the FY2025 10-K both expecting coverage
    losses. Q1 2026 made it concrete: uninsured admissions +15.6%, managed care revenue +0.5%,
    admissions +0.7%. Q2 2026 confirmed it (uninsured +23.4%, managed care -1.2%, surgeries down)
    despite admissions recovering to +2.4%.
  Tests: uninsured admissions growth, managed care revenue in the payer table, surgical volumes,
    Florida/Texas share of admissions.

P2 Medicaid supplemental and state directed payments (SDP)
  Bull assumes: SDP and supplemental programs (about $6.2B of 2025 revenue) stay large through
    2027 under grandfathering, with approvals arriving as catch-ups.
  Bear fears: FBA/OBBBA caps (100% / 110% of Medicare from 2028, 10 points a year), provider-tax
    limits that fund the state share, DSH cuts, Medicare sequestration, and timing risk on approvals.
  Stance: Intact (never moved in the window).
  History: bull and bear evidence arrived together in almost every filing. Bull: FY2024 program
    revenue $4.9B (+26%); Q3 2025 new approvals (Medicaid revenue +58%); FY2025 $6.2B; Q1 2026
    preliminary grandfathering approvals; Q2 2026 Florida $1.372B. Bear: Q1 2025 "reduced or
    eliminated" wording; Q2 2025 OBBBA enacted; Q3 2025 DSH cuts and up-to-4% sequestration; FY2025
    caps legislated and 2026 decline guided; Q2 2026 provider-tax final and proposed rules. The
    near term improved, the 2028+ end-state worsened; net Intact.
  Tests: SDP revenue disclosed, new approvals, provider-tax rule outcomes, step-down start in 2028.

P3 Margins and cost control
  Bull assumes: labor productivity and supply chain keep salaries and benefits and supplies falling
    as a share of revenue; "resiliency" initiatives offset part of the policy hit.
  Bear fears: wage inflation, devices, professional fees and rising provider-tax/SDP expense absorb
    pricing; segments outside Florida lose operating leverage.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q2 2026 10-Q.
  History: Q3 2024 to FY2025 the labor ratio fell in every filing (46.6% -> 43.5% range), but other
    operating expenses rose with provider fees (FY2024 +1.2 points) and unit costs accelerated in 2025
    (Q2 2025 salaries per equivalent admission +3.5%, devices +10.2%), so the stance held at Intact.
    Q1 2026 (pre-tax margin 12.0% vs 12.7%, pre-tax income -1.7%) and Q2 2026 (12.3% vs 13.0% even with
    the Florida catch-up; American and National segment EBITDA down) point the same way.
  Tests: segment adjusted EBITDA, pre-tax margin, salaries per equivalent admission, other operating
    expense ratio.

P4 Earnings quality and reserves
  Bull assumes: reported EPS reflects recurring operations; reserves are conservative.
  Bear fears: catch-up payments for prior periods, tax items and reserve releases flatter EPS;
    professional liability reserves develop adversely.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q1 2026 10-Q.
  History: FY2024 was clean in the other direction (a $276M tax charge and $250M of hurricane costs
    depressed EPS; Q1 2025 growth came despite lapping a $201M gain). Then: Q3 2025 growth leaned on
    unquantified newly approved supplemental revenue; FY2025 third straight year of adverse
    malpractice development ($126M); Q1 2026 EPS +10.9% on net income +0.6% with $103M of equity-award
    tax benefits; Q2 2026 $980M of revenue for periods before 2026.
  Tests: prior-period components in each quarter, reserve development table in the 10-K, tax benefits.

P5 Cash conversion and capital return
  Bull assumes: operating cash flow exceeds net income, and buybacks/dividends are funded from free
    cash flow, shrinking the share count 5-8% a year.
  Bear fears: buybacks outrun free cash flow and are funded with debt while receivables build.
  Stance: Intact (never moved in the window).
  History: annual conversion improved (FCF/net income 98% in 2024, 113% in 2025) and diluted shares
    fell 5.3% then 8.5%. But shareholder returns exceeded FCF in every period covered (FY2024 119%,
    FY2025 140%, H1 2026 about 2x), Q2-Q3 2025 cash was flattered by $1.259B of deferred taxes, and
    Q2 2026 operating cash fell $1.875B on SDP receivables. The funding gap is scored under P6.
  Tests: operating cash flow vs net income, DSO, buybacks vs FCF.

P6 Balance sheet and liquidity
  Bull assumes: investment-grade unsecured structure, ample revolver capacity, debt grows only with
    EBITDA.
  Bear fears: debt-funded buybacks, short-term commercial paper reliance, shrinking headroom.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q1 2026 10-Q.
  History: debt $42.97B (Q3 2024) -> $43.03B (FY2024) -> $44.58B (Q1 2025, unsecured refinancing; the
    restricted-payments covenant dropped) -> flat through Q3 2025 while a $4.0B commercial paper
    program was added and working capital turned negative -> $46.49B (FY2025) -> $48.02B (Q1 2026,
    CP $3.65B, availability $4.34B) -> $49.72B (Q2 2026, CP at the $4.0B limit, revolver drawn).
  Tests: total debt, CP outstanding vs $4.0B limit, revolver availability, ratings language.

P7 Growth investment
  Bull assumes: a rising construction pipeline (beds, outpatient, freestanding EDs) sustains volume
    growth; capex stays near $5B, funded internally.
  Bear fears: capex underspends guidance or is cut to protect buybacks; acquisitions creep outside
    the guided number.
  Stance: Strengthening, moved at the FY2025 10-K.
  History: pipeline $4.7B (Q3 2024, FY2024) -> $5.6B -> $5.9B -> $6.0B (2025 10-Qs) -> $7.1B (FY2025)
    -> $8.8B (Q1 2026) -> $8.6B (Q2 2026). Q2 2025 trimmed the 2025 capex guide to about $5.0B and
    capex undershot guidance in both 2024 and 2025, so the stance waited until Q3 2025 and FY2025
    (pipeline up again, 2026 ceiling raised to $5.5B). Q2 2026's first pipeline dip is one filing.
  Tests: construction pipeline, capex vs guidance, acquisition spend.
