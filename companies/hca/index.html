FY2018 → FY2019: Pre-COVID Growth

Filed Feb 2019 | Employees: ~262K → ~275K | Revenue: $46.7B → $51.3B (+10%) | Hospitals: 179 → 184

Business

▶Mission Health acquisition; hospitals +5, entered new stateNew
HCA completed the acquisition of Mission Health System in North Carolina for approximately $1.68 billion, adding 5 hospitals and marking entry into a new state. This was the largest single acquisition in the period and represented the peak of the acquisition-led growth strategy before the pivot to organic capex.
▶Strategy language unchanged; five-pillar growth agenda stableSignal
The core strategy description and five-pillar growth agenda remained essentially unchanged from the prior year. This stability is itself a signal -- the pre-COVID business model was operating as designed, with +10% revenue growth representing the strongest organic growth year in the analysis period.

Risk Factors

▶Minimal changes; pre-COVID baselineSignal
Risk factors saw minimal year-over-year changes, establishing the pre-COVID baseline against which all subsequent filings would diverge dramatically. The stability of this filing makes it the cleanest control period in the corpus.

FY2019 → FY2020: COVID & Modernization

Filed Feb 2021 | Employees: ~275K → 275K | Revenue: $51.3B → $51.5B (flat) | Hospitals: 184 → 185

Business

▶COVID-19 subsection added; CARES Act $6.2B received then returnedNew
An entirely new COVID-19 subsection was added to the Business section, detailing the pandemic's operational impact. HCA disclosed receiving approximately $6.2 billion in CARES Act provider relief funds, which were subsequently returned. Revenue was essentially flat at $51.5B despite massive operational disruption.
▶Human Capital Resources section added (SEC mandate): Culture, DEI, RecruitmentNew
Responding to SEC modernization requirements, HCA added a new Human Capital Resources section covering corporate culture, diversity/equity/inclusion initiatives, and recruitment strategies. This section would undergo dramatic evolution over subsequent years, culminating in complete DEI removal by FY2024.
▶Strategy language added: "provider system of choice" + enterprise capabilitiesChanged
New strategic framing introduced:
"provider system of choice" + "unique enterprise capabilities and best in class economies of scale"
This language would persist until the FY2022 filing, when it was replaced with "health care system of choice" -- a subtle but significant shift from provider identity to system identity.
▶Freestanding endoscopy centers (21) first reported as separate categoryNew
Endoscopy centers were broken out as their own facility category for the first time, with 21 centers disclosed. This category would grow to 31 centers by FY2025, making it the fastest-growing facility type in HCA's portfolio -- a signal of the shift toward procedure-specific ambulatory settings.

Risk Factors

▶Summary Risk Factors section added (SEC modernization)New
A new Summary Risk Factors section was added at the top of Item 1A, consistent with SEC disclosure modernization efforts. This condensed overview format would be retained through all subsequent filings.
▶COVID risk category with vaccine, PPE, unemployment, stay-at-home impactsNew
COVID-19 became the dominant risk category, with extensive new language covering vaccine distribution challenges, PPE supply chain disruption, unemployment-driven shifts in payer mix, and the impact of state stay-at-home orders on elective procedure volumes.
▶Capex cut from $4.16B to $2.84B: "reduced certain planned projects"Changed
Capital expenditures were cut by 32%, from $4.16B to $2.84B, with the filing noting HCA
"reduced certain planned projects"
in response to COVID uncertainty. This was the low point in the capex cycle -- spending would steadily increase to $4.94B by FY2025.

FY2020 → FY2021: Recovery & Capital Return

Filed Feb 2022 | Employees: 275K → 284K | Revenue: $51.5B → $58.8B (+14%) | Hospitals: 185 → 182

Business

▶Climate/ESG language first appears: scope 1/2 GHG baselining, four-pillar environmental strategyNew
For the first time, HCA introduced substantive climate and ESG language including scope 1 and scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions baselining and a four-pillar environmental strategy. This represented peak ESG ambition -- all of this language would be systematically stripped by FY2025.
▶Human capital risks become standalone category; vaccine mandates, nursing shortage, labor unionsNew
Human capital risks were elevated to a standalone risk factor category for the first time, reflecting the acute labor crisis. Specific disclosures included vaccine mandates, the nursing shortage, and labor union organizing activity -- all new risk dimensions that had not appeared in prior filings.
▶COVID recovery: "patient volumes improved as effects of pandemic moderated"Changed
COVID language shifted from crisis mode to recovery framing:
"patient volumes improved as effects of pandemic moderated"
Revenue surged +14% to $58.8B, the strongest growth rate in the period, driven by pent-up demand for deferred procedures.

Risk Factors

▶"Risks related to human capital" as new standalone risk categoryNew
The elevation of human capital to a standalone risk category -- separate from general operational risks -- signaled that labor had become an existential concern. This category would persist through all subsequent filings, becoming permanently embedded even as the acute crisis moderated.
▶LIBOR replaced with "borrowing costs" (more specific)Changed
References to LIBOR transition risk were replaced with more specific "borrowing costs" language, reflecting the completion of the LIBOR-to-SOFR transition.

Financial

▶Share repurchases begin: $8.2B in FY2021New
HCA initiated its massive capital return program with $8.2 billion in share repurchases in FY2021 alone. This would grow to $10.1B by FY2025, totaling $35.5B in buybacks over five years on a ~$75B revenue company -- one of the most aggressive capital return programs in healthcare.
▶Debt grew $31.0B to $34.6BSignal
Total debt increased from $31.0B to $34.6B, the beginning of a steady leverage expansion that would reach $46.5B by FY2025 (+42% from FY2021). Notably, the "highly leveraged" language was still present at this point -- it would not be dropped until the FY2023 filing.

FY2021 → FY2022: Strategy Rewrite

Filed Feb 2023 | Employees: 284K → 294K | Revenue: $58.8B → $60.2B (+2%) | Hospitals: 182 → 182

Business

▶Strategy major rewrite: "provider system of choice" replaced with "health care system of choice"Changed
The core strategic identity underwent a significant rewrite:
FY2021: "provider system of choice" + "unique enterprise capabilities and best in class economies of scale"
FY2022: "health care system of choice" + "comprehensive networks locally" + "enterprise expertise and economies of scale"
The shift from "provider" to "health care system" signals a broadening of scope beyond acute care hospitals to an integrated delivery network. "Comprehensive networks locally" emphasizes geographic density as a moat.
▶Growth agenda expanded: added "retain," "digital capabilities," "innovative care solutions," "workforce development"Changed
The growth agenda was meaningfully updated: "retain" was added alongside "recruit" (reflecting the labor crisis), and three new priorities appeared: "digital capabilities," "innovative care solutions," and "workforce development." These additions foreshadowed the AI and Galen College disclosures that would follow.
▶Galen College nursing expansion disclosed for nursing shortageNew
HCA disclosed its expansion of Galen College of Nursing as a strategic response to the nursing shortage. Rather than relying solely on labor markets, HCA was vertically integrating its workforce pipeline -- a differentiated approach among large hospital systems.
▶ESG risk factor added: "achieve our ESG goals"New
A new risk factor was added related to ESG:
"achieve our ESG goals"
This represented the peak of ESG commitment in HCA's filings. The ESG risk factor would be removed from the summary by FY2024 and ESG language would be stripped to bare compliance by FY2025.

Risk Factors

▶Technology/cybersecurity becomes standalone risk categoryChanged
Technology and cybersecurity risks were elevated from embedded discussion to a standalone risk category, reflecting increasing digital dependence and the growing threat landscape in healthcare IT.
▶COVID language softened: "is significantly affecting" became "has affected, and may continue to affect"Changed
COVID risk language shifted from present tense to past tense with a prospective hedge:
FY2021: "is significantly affecting"
FY2022: "has affected, and may continue to affect"
The transition from active crisis to historical reference had begun.
▶Vaccine mandate language droppedRemoved
Vaccine mandate risk language was removed entirely, reflecting the end of federal vaccine requirements and the normalization of workforce COVID policies.

Financial

▶Managed care dropped 51.6% to 48.3%; managed Medicare surged 13.7% to 15.2%Signal
The payer mix continued its structural shift: managed care (commercial) declined from 51.6% to 48.3% while managed Medicare surged from 13.7% to 15.2%. This ~$6B+ revenue shift carries different reimbursement dynamics and margin profiles, and managed Medicare continued rising every single year through FY2025.

FY2022 → FY2023: Behavioral & AI Emerge

Filed Feb 2024 | Employees: 294K → 310K | Revenue: $60.2B → $65.0B (+8%) | Hospitals: 182 → 186

Business

▶"Psychiatric" renamed to "behavioral" hospitals (5 to 6)Changed
All references to "psychiatric hospitals" were changed to "behavioral hospitals," and the count increased from 5 to 6. This reflects industry-wide destigmatization and an expanded service scope beyond acute psychiatry to include addiction, eating disorders, and other behavioral health conditions.
▶AI first mentioned: "our physicians use generative AI to assist with the taking of medical notes"New
AI appeared in HCA's filing for the first time:
"our physicians use generative AI to assist with the taking of medical notes"
Notably, this was framed as deployment, not aspiration -- HCA was already using AI in clinical settings. This first mention would escalate to AI becoming a strategic pillar within just three filings.
▶"Highly leveraged" dropped from MD&A; now "significant debt service requirements"Changed
The characterization of HCA's leverage softened:
FY2018-22: "We are a highly leveraged company with significant debt service requirements"
FY2023-25: "significant debt service requirements"
The "highly leveraged" self-description was dropped even as total debt continued growing -- from $38.1B at drop to $46.5B by FY2025 (+22%).

Risk Factors

▶AI regulatory risk added: "development and use of artificial intelligence and other predictive algorithms"New
Alongside the business disclosure of AI deployment, a new risk factor was added covering:
"development and use of artificial intelligence and other predictive algorithms"
This dual disclosure -- AI as both opportunity and risk -- is a pattern seen across industries as companies navigated the early regulatory landscape for AI in healthcare.
▶Value-based purchasing risk: "The industry trend toward value-based purchasing may negatively impact our revenues"New
A new risk factor explicitly addressed value-based care:
"The industry trend toward value-based purchasing may negatively impact our revenues"
This acknowledges the structural shift from fee-for-service to outcomes-based reimbursement as a threat to HCA's volume-driven model.
▶COVID category renamed "Risks related to public health crises"Changed
The dedicated COVID risk category was renamed to the generic "Risks related to public health crises," absorbing COVID-specific language into a broader framework. This is the penultimate step before complete COVID removal -- which would come by FY2025.

FY2023 → FY2024: Policy Risk Arrives; DEI and Climate Goals Exit

Filed Feb 2025 | Revenue: $65.0B → $70.6B (+8.7%) | Diluted EPS: $18.97 → $22.00 | Operating cash flow: $9.43B → $10.51B | Hospitals: 186 → 190 | Employees: 310K → 316K

Business

▶Premium tax credit expiry: "may be adversely impacted" becomes "significantly increase the uninsured rate"Changed

Shift: scope widened, Exchange enrollment → the uninsured rate (+ disclosure); degree added “significantly”; certainty may → would, under “we believe”

The Exchange-subsidy sentence went from a conditional enrollment risk to a stated belief about the uninsured rate, and the same wording was added to the risk factors and the forward-looking factor list:
FY2023: "If these subsidies are not extended beyond 2025, Exchange enrollment may be adversely impacted."
FY2024: "However, further extension is uncertain, and we believe their expiration would adversely impact Exchange enrollment and significantly increase the uninsured rate."

History: “Exchange enrollment may be adversely impacted” appears in FY2023 only; “significantly increase the uninsured rate” first appears in FY2024 (Business once, Risk Factors twice) and survives once, in Risk Factors, in FY2025.

This is the first time HCA tied the subsidy cliff to the uninsured rate rather than to Exchange enrollment alone. The credits did expire at the end of 2025. In the first two quarters of 2026, uninsured admissions rose 15.6% and then 23.3% (see the 10-Q scanner).
▶Reform threat changes direction: "Medicare for All" deleted; Medicare Advantage shift, ACA repeal and Medicaid trigger laws addedChanged

Shift: direction reversed, coverage expansion → contraction; named −single-payer, +Medicare Advantage shift, +ACA repeal, +Medicaid trigger laws

The reform risk HCA describes moved from an expansion of government coverage to a contraction of it:
FY2023: "Some members of Congress have proposed measures that would expand government-sponsored coverage, including proposals to expand coverage of federally-funded insurance programs as an alternative to private insurance or establish a single-payer system"
FY2024: "For example, some members of Congress have proposed changes intended to accelerate the shift from traditional Medicare to Medicare Advantage, repealing the Affordable Care Act or eliminating some of its consumer protections."
FY2024: "Further, some states have trigger laws that would end their Medicaid expansion if federal funding is reduced."

History: “establish a single-payer system” ran in the FY2023 10-K and every 10-Q from Q2 2023 to Q3 2024; “accelerate the shift from traditional Medicare to Medicare Advantage” and “trigger laws” first appear in FY2024 and stand in FY2025.

The Medicaid funding-formula and trigger-law language is new in FY2024. Work requirements moved from rescinded waivers to something future CMS administrators "may" allow. Those are the mechanisms the 2025 Federal Budget Act used about five months later.
▶New "Developments in Health Care Public Policy" section: 2024 election, Loper Bright and an agency-cutting executive orderNew

Shift: named +2024 federal election, +Loper Bright, +presidential advisory commission on agencies (+ disclosure)

A new Business subsection puts the election and the end of Chevron deference side by side as sources of regulatory uncertainty:
FY2024: "The outcome of the 2024 federal election may increase regulatory uncertainty and the potential for significant policy changes."
FY2024: "Recent presidential executive orders have been issued that impact or may impact the health care industry, including an order establishing a presidential advisory commission focused on restructuring and streamlining government agencies and reducing or eliminating regulations and federal government programs and other expenditures."

History: “2024 federal election” and “presidential advisory commission” appear in FY2024 only; “Loper Bright” in FY2024 and FY2025.

The executive-order sentence, written weeks before filing, is HCA's first reference to an administration effort to cut federal agencies and programs. The FY2025 10-K followed it with HHS restructuring and "holds on or cancellations of congressionally authorized spending."
▶Medicaid supplemental and SDP revenue $3.9B → $4.9B (+26%); May 2024 CMS rule caps SDPs at the average commercial rateChanged

Shift: quant, one year → two, $3.9B → $4.9B (+); named +May 2024 CMS rule cap at “the average commercial rate”

The figure moved from one year to two and was added to the MD&A operations summary:
FY2023: "Revenues from these programs totaled approximately $3.9 billion in 2023."
FY2024: "Our revenues from Medicaid state directed and supplemental payment programs totaled approximately $4.9 billion and $3.9 billion in 2024 and 2023, respectively."
FY2024: "The rule also requires provider payment levels for SDPs for certain services, including inpatient and outpatient hospital services, to not exceed the average commercial rate."

History: “Revenues from these programs totaled approximately” appears in the FY2023–FY2025 Business sections; “not exceed the average commercial rate” in FY2024 only (FY2025 replaced it with Medicare-based caps).

The $1.0B increase is about 18% of the year's $5.6B revenue growth. The review language also changed from "currently being reviewed by certain state agencies" to "regularly reviewed by certain government agencies." The FY2025 10-K later restated both years upward, to $5.5B and $4.4B.
▶Generative AI: "as currently employed" for notes becomes "adopting ... among other tasks"; state AI laws in CA, UT and CO namedChanged

Shift: hedge dropped “As currently employed”; scope widened “among other tasks”; named +California AB 3030, Utah AIPA, Colorado AI Act

The risk-factor header now names AI, and the use case widened:
FY2023: "As currently employed, our physicians use generative AI to assist with the taking of medical notes regarding our patients."
FY2024: "For example, our physicians are adopting the use of generative AI to assist with the taking of patient medical notes, among other tasks."
FY2024: "There is also a risk that our confidential information becomes part of a model that is accessible by other third-party AI applications or users"

History: “As currently employed, our physicians use generative AI” appears in FY2023 only; “adopting the use of generative AI” and “becomes part of a model” in FY2024 and FY2025.

New in the Business section: California AB 3030, Utah's AIPA and the Colorado AI Act. The data-leakage sentence is new, and it shows the AI work now involves third-party models.
▶DEI apparatus deleted: Chief Diversity Officer, DEI councils, resource groups, workforce demographics, board-diversity search ruleRemoved

Shift: removed, workforce quant (78% / 45%) dropped (−); named −Chief Diversity Officer, −DEI councils; “inclusion” → “compassion and respect”

The "Diversity, Equity and Inclusion" subsection is gone, and so is every named program in it:
FY2023: "Our domestic workforce is comprised of approximately 78% women and 45% people of color."
FY2023: "We are committed to fostering a culture of inclusion that embraces and supports our patients, colleagues, partners, physicians and communities."
FY2024: "We strive to foster a culture of compassion and respect, across our system, to provide high-quality care for our patients, unlock opportunities for our colleagues and improve the health of our communities."

History: “78% women and 45% people of color” and “fostering a culture of inclusion” appear in FY2023 only; “diversity”, “diverse” and “inclusion” occur 20 times in the FY2023 10-K and 0 in FY2024.

The risk factor "a highly qualified and diverse workforce" lost "and diverse." The words "diversity," "diverse" and "inclusion" appear 20 times in FY2023 and zero times in FY2024.
▶Paris-aligned 2030 emissions plan, scope 3 work, TCFD report and ESG risk factor deleted; "climate change" becomes "changing global weather patterns"Removed

Shift: removed, dated target (2030) dropped (−); named −Paris Agreement, −climate change

The emissions commitment was removed in this filing, not in FY2025:
FY2023: "We are pursuing a plan to reduce our scope 1 and scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions by 2030 in line with the Paris Agreement"
FY2023: "Our business and operations are subject to risks related to climate change."
FY2024: "Our business and operations are subject to risks related to changing global weather patterns."

History: “in line with the Paris Agreement” and “related to climate change” appear in FY2023 only; “changing global weather patterns” first appears in FY2024 and is in every filing since, 10-Qs included.

The scope 1 and 2 baselining, the first scope 3 measurement and the 2022 TCFD report paragraph were all removed, and so was the standalone ESG-objectives risk factor. Only a four-pillar environmental strategy ("Preparing for weather impacts," formerly "Enhancing our climate resilience") was left for FY2025 to remove.
▶Executive officer list cut from 19 to 7; Michael Marks replaces William Rutherford as CFOChanged

Shift: scope narrowed, 19 → 7 executive officers named; named +Marks as CFO

FY2024: "Marks was appointed Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer, effective May 1, 2024."

History: the FY2023 list (as of February 1, 2024) has 19 names, the FY2024 list (February 1, 2025) 7; “Marks was appointed Executive Vice President” appears in FY2024 and FY2025.

The FY2023 list named 19 executive officers, including the three group presidents, the CIO, the controller and the heads of government relations, development, internal audit and payer contracting. The FY2024 list names 7: the CEO, CFO, COO, chief clinical officer, chief legal and administrative officer, CHRO and chief nurse executive. The filing does not explain the narrower definition.

Risk Factors

▶SDP risk sharpened: "We may also be impacted by SDP arrangements" becomes "could result in such payments being reduced or eliminated"Changed

Shift: degree, "may also be impacted" → "reduced or eliminated" (− outlook, + candor); conditions made explicit ("if funding ... is diverted", "if we do not satisfy")

FY2023: "We may also be impacted by SDP arrangements, which allow states to direct certain Medicaid managed plan expenditures, particularly as funding may be diverted from other payment programs, and we may not satisfy applicable criteria when payments are directed to a specific subset of providers."
FY2024: "Structural and other changes to these programs could result in such payments being reduced or eliminated, for example if funding for SDP arrangements is diverted from other payment programs, and if we do not satisfy applicable criteria when payments are directed to a specific subset of providers."

History: "could result in such payments being reduced or eliminated" appears in FY2024 only; the FY2025 10-K rewrote the paragraph around the FBA's Medicare-based cap. The MD&A's "reduced or eliminated" had been dropped from the Q2 and Q3 2024 10-Qs and returns in this filing.

The risk factor now names elimination as an outcome in the same filing that first sizes these programs at $4.9B, about 7% of revenue. It is the highest-ranked change in the year's sentence signals.
▶July 2023 breach affecting ~11 million patients deleted from risk factors and forward-looking statementsRemoved

Shift: quant removed, “11 million patients” (−); named −July 2023 incident

The only named cyber incident in the filing was removed after one year:
FY2023: "Approximately 11 million patients were affected by the security incident."
FY2023: "Although to date no cyberattack or other information or security breach, including those experienced by us in 2023, has resulted in material losses"

History: “Approximately 11 million patients” appears in the Q2 2023 10-Q and FY2023 only; “including the data security incident disclosed in July 2023” in every filing from Q2 2023 to Q3 2024.

The forward-looking factor "including the data security incident disclosed in July 2023" became "involving us or our vendors and other third parties." The rewritten cyber risk factor is longer and more generic, and it adds nation-state actors, ransomware groups and AI-enabled attacks.
▶Tariffs, the debt ceiling and holds on appropriated spending enter the risk factors for the first timeNew

Shift: named +tariffs, +debt ceiling, +holds on appropriated spending (+ disclosure); certainty “may impact”

None of these terms appears in FY2023:
FY2024: "These may include changes to trade policy and new or increased tariffs, which may impact our supply chain operations."
FY2024: "Further, any shutdown of the federal government, failure to enact annual appropriations, hold on congressionally authorized spending or interruptions in the distribution of governmental funds could adversely affect our financial results."

History: “new or increased tariffs” and “hold on congressionally authorized spending” first appear in FY2024 and stand in FY2025; “debt ceiling” occurs once in FY2024 and not in FY2023.

The tariff clause is a single sentence here. In FY2025 it grew into a paragraph saying tariffs "have raised" supply costs. A related physician-recruitment line is also new: "If states enact legal restrictions on the provision of medical care, such restrictions may impact providers' recruitment and retention efforts in certain states."

MD&A

▶Uninsured ER visits +13.8% (from +4.0%); uninsured admissions turn positive; uncompensated-care cost +$646MSignal

Shift: direction reversed, uninsured admissions −0.4% → +1.3%; realized, Medicaid enrollment “expected to continue to decline” → “declined in 2024” (+)

FY2023: "Consolidated and same facility uninsured admissions each declined 0.4%, and consolidated and same facility uninsured emergency room visits increased 4.0% and 4.4%, respectively, for 2023, compared to 2022."
FY2024: "Consolidated and same facility uninsured admissions increased 1.3% and 1.0%, respectively, and consolidated and same facility uninsured emergency room visits increased 13.8% and 13.5%, respectively, for 2024, compared to 2023."

History: “increased 13.8% and 13.5%” appears in FY2024 only; “expected to continue to decline through mid-year 2024” in FY2023 only, replaced by “declined in 2024 in comparison to 2023” (FY2024 only).

The estimated cost of uncompensated care rose from $3.720B to $4.366B (+17%), the largest increase of the three years shown. This came while Medicaid enrollment was falling after redeterminations. The filing says enrollment "declined in 2024 in comparison to 2023." The uninsured trend was already worsening before the subsidy cliff.
▶Managed Medicare revenue passes traditional Medicare ($11.99B vs $10.78B); two-midnight rule lifts admissions while outpatient surgeries fall 1.9%Signal

Shift: quant, payer-mix crossover; named +two-midnight rule as an admissions driver (Business section)

In the payer table, managed Medicare is 17.0% of revenue and traditional Medicare 15.3%. A year earlier it was 16.2% against 16.3%. Two new sentences explain part of the 5.0% admissions growth:
FY2024: "The implementation of the two-midnight rule had a modest impact on our 2024 admissions growth."
FY2024: "Outpatient surgical volumes declined 1.9% on a consolidated basis and declined 1.6% on a same facility basis during 2024, compared to 2023."

History: “two-midnight rule” appears in the FY2023–FY2025 Business sections; the “modest impact on our 2024 admissions growth” sentence in FY2024 only; “declined 1.9% on a consolidated basis” in FY2024 only.

Part of the inpatient growth came from a coverage rule for Medicare Advantage plans, not from new demand. Outpatient surgery, which had grown 2.1% in 2023, declined.
▶Capex guide trimmed to $5.0–5.2B from $5.1–5.3B as buyback authorizations step up $3B → $6B → $10BChanged

Shift: guidance range lowered $0.1B at both ends (−); buyback authorization $6B → $10B (+)

FY2023: "Planned capital expenditures are expected to approximate between $5.1 billion and $5.3 billion in 2024."
FY2024: "Planned capital expenditures are expected to approximate between $5.0 billion and $5.2 billion in 2025."
FY2024: "During January 2023, January 2024 and January 2025, our Board of Directors authorized $3 billion, $6 billion and $10 billion, respectively, for share repurchases"

History: “$5.1 billion and $5.3 billion” appears in FY2023 and the Q1–Q2 2024 10-Qs (Q3 2024 cut it to “$5 billion”); “$5.0 billion and $5.2 billion” in FY2024 and Q1 2025; “January 2023, January 2024 and January 2025” in FY2024 only.

Actual 2024 capex was $4.88B, below the guided floor. 2024 buybacks were $6.04B on 17.8M shares. The construction backlog rose from $4.1B to $4.7B. The guide moved down while the buyback authorization rose two-thirds.

FY2024 → FY2025: SDP Caps Named, Covenants Loosened, Malpractice Reserves Running Adverse

Filed Feb 2026 | Revenue: $70.60B → $75.60B (+7.1%) | Diluted EPS: $22.00 → $28.33 | Operating cash flow: $10.51B → $12.64B | Free cash flow: $5.64B → $7.69B | Buybacks: $10.07B (diluted shares −8.5%) | Total debt: $43.03B → $46.49B

Business

▶SDP rate ceiling moves from average commercial rates to 100% of Medicare (110% in non-expansion states); Texas named as affectedNew

Shift: named +Texas; quant added (+): a payment ceiling stated as a percentage of Medicare for the first time

FY2025: "Under the revised regulations, rates will generally be capped at 100% of the total published Medicare payment rate in Medicaid expansion states, including states with waiver-based expansions, and at 110% of the total published Medicare payment rate in non-expansion states."
FY2025 (Risk Factors): "tying caps to Medicare payment rates instead of average commercial rates, a change that we anticipate will impact payment rates in many states in which we operate, including Texas."

History: "110% of the total published Medicare" appears in FY2025 only; FY2023 and FY2024 described SDP limits only through the May 2024 managed-care rule.

Texas is the only state named. Its facilities produced $20.96B of 2025 revenue, 28% of the total. Grandfathered programs step down 10 points a year from 2028, so the cap sets when this revenue begins to shrink, not whether.

Themes

From Scale to Network to AI

Moat narrative evolved from cost advantage → network coordination → AI/digital in 7 years. Each evolution visible in the exact strategy language of the Business section, tracking management's evolving understanding of durable competitive advantage.

Organic Over Acquisitive

Acquisitions fell from $1.68B (Mission Health, FY2019) to $0.40B (FY2025) while organic capex rose steadily. The $7.1B construction backlog (+51% YoY) signals HCA has entered a buildout era, favoring de novo development over M&A.

The MA Substitution

Managed Medicare: 11.8% → 17.8% of revenue, and larger than traditional Medicare since FY2024 ($13.44B vs $11.27B in FY2025). The risk factors now name proposals to "accelerate the shift from traditional Medicare to Medicare Advantage," and FY2025 adds payer "downcoding policies" and the WISeR prior-authorization model.

The DEI/ESG Reversal

Peak to trough in two filings. The FY2024 10-K deleted the DEI subsection (Chief Diversity Officer, councils, workforce demographics) and the Paris-aligned 2030 emissions plan. FY2025 removed the remaining environmental strategy, and "climate" no longer appears. The filings give no reason. The timing coincides with the 2024 election and the policy sections added in the same filings.

AI: Zero to Strategic Pillar in 3 Filings

From no mention (FY2018-22) to 6th growth agenda item with full MDA subsection (FY2025). The new EHR platform is the execution vehicle. First mention was deployment ("physicians use generative AI"), not aspiration -- HCA skipped the aspiration phase entirely.

Capital Return Machine

$35.1B of buybacks in FY2021–FY2025, $10.1B of it in FY2025. Capex came in below the guided range in both 2024 ($4.88B vs $5.1–5.3B) and 2025 ($4.94B vs $5.0–5.2B) while the backlog reached $7.1B. The FY2025 move to unsecured debt removed the covenant on restricted payments, and debt rose to $46.5B.

Medicaid Supplemental Payments: The Swing Factor

Disclosed SDP and supplemental revenue: $3.9B (FY2023 as first reported) → $4.9B (FY2024) → $6.2B (FY2025), with 2023 and 2024 restated to $4.4B and $5.5B in the FY2025 10-K. The FBA caps these programs at Medicare-based rates, with a step-down from 2028, and HCA names Texas. HCA guided 2026 SDP revenue lower, then booked $1.372B from Florida's approval in Q2 2026. On both the upside and the downside, these payments now move results more than volume does.
