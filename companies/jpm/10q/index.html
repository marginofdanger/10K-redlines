Themes

1. Capital Return Ran Ahead of the Ratio, Then Stopped

Standardized CET1 fell in seven consecutive filings, from 15.7% (FY2024) to 14.0% (Q3 2026), while repurchases ran at $6–8B a quarter and $31.6B in 2025. The requirement cut to 11.5% made that look affordable. Q3 2026 changed the picture: RWA up $196B in nine months, a $7.9B AOCI loss as rates rose, and a 5.5% GSIB surcharge disclosed for 2028. Buybacks fell to $1.1B with nothing bought in August or September, and the MD&A gave no reason. Whether that is a pause or a new pace is the first thing the FY2026 10-K has to answer.

2. Regulatory Relief Is Real but Front-Loaded

Each 2025–2026 rule change helped near-term capital: the SCB fell to the 2.5% floor and is frozen through 2027, the SLR requirement fell from 5.0% to 4.3%, the Apple Card RWA charge shrank from $110B to $30B, and the Basel rewrite now costs about 6% of required CET1 rather than 25%. The offsets land together on January 1, 2028: a 5.5% GSIB surcharge under current rules and an SCB set by a single 2027 test on re-proposed models. Q3 2026 dropped the sentence saying the stress-test changes would not materially move the SCB.

3. Guidance Moves Up on Both Lines

NII guides were raised in each year and landed where the last guide pointed. Expense guides rose with them and, in 2026, faster ($105B to $108.6B against $103B to $106.2B). Card charge-off guides were cut in 2025 and 2026 and actual rates (2.91% in Q3 2026) undercut them. The risk to earnings in the text is not credit or NII but cost growth, which the firm has guided at or above NII in every full-year guide since the FY2024 10-K.

4. Credit Language Names the Loss, Not the Borrower

The credit story moved from card seasoning (2024) to the macro overlay (Q1 2025) to “apparent borrower fraud in certain secured lending facilities” (Q3 2025), firmed to “borrower fraud” in the 10-K and then dropped from the 2026 10-Qs. 2026 wholesale builds are attributed to commitment growth and “certain exposures”, with criticized exposure rising in Consumer & Retail and TMT. None of these is sized individually.

5. Legal Risk Changed Character

The litigation note lost its federal enforcement matters (SEC, CFPB Zelle, trading venues) and its legacy cases (1MDB, the shareholder suits), and the loss range fell to $1.1B. What replaced them is political and consumer-facing: Fair Access to Banking (an Executive Order, a suit by the President against the bank and its CEO, an OCC report on nine banks and a CFPB statement about “customer harm”), cash sweep class actions, and a new interchange class for the post-2019 period. Russian judgments went from possible to enforceable. The range has moved only $0.2B through all of it.
