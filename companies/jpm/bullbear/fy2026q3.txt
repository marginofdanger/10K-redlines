JPMORGAN CHASE (JPM) Q3 2026 10-Q (filed November 3, 2026; quarter ended September 30, 2026)
Fundamentals answers. Marker = versus the Q2 2026 10-Q (growth rates are year on year against Q3
2025). Figures from the filing's financial highlights, MD&A and SEC XBRL.

1. Growth and pricing [BETTER]
   Net revenue $50.2B, +8%. NII $27.0B, +13% ("higher Markets net interest income, higher deposit
   balances, higher balances in Card Services, and higher wholesale loan balances, partially offset
   by the impact of lower rates"); ex-Markets $24.7B, +6% (Q2 +4%). Markets revenue $10.3B, +15%;
   investment banking fees $2.96B, +13%; asset management fees +15%; AUM $5.2T. Average loans +10%,
   average deposits +8%. Executive Overview.

2. Margins and unit economics [WORSE]
   Noninterest expense $27.3B, +12%, against revenue +8%: compensation (revenue-related pay, wage
   inflation, more front-office staff), brokerage and distribution fees, technology and marketing.
   Pre-provision profit $22.9B, +3%. ROTCE 20% (20% a year earlier). Corporate expense up partly
   on the international consumer initiatives. Consolidated Results of Operations.

3. Earnings quality [BETTER]
   No one-off gains. Net income $15.3B (+6%) absorbed $658M of net investment securities losses
   ($498M after tax) from repositioning (sales of U.S. Treasuries) and a net loss on certain
   equity investments in Corporate. Effective tax rate 24.0%. Provision $2.7B (-19%). Corporate
   results.

4. Cash conversion [WORSE]
   Not a useful measure for a bank. Capital generation: Q3 net income applicable to common about
   $15.0B against dividends ~$4.4B and net treasury-stock purchases ~$1.0B (36% paid out; nine
   months 52%). But ~$7.9B of unrealized AFS losses went through AOCI, and TBVPS fell in the
   quarter ($113.35 -> $112.64). Capital rollforward.

5. Capital allocation [WORSE]
   Repurchases $1.057B, all in July; none in August or September; $48.9B of the new $50B program
   unused. Dividend raised to $1.65 (declared September 15). Strategic Investment Group equity
   $3.7B -> $5.7B, no longer described as within the Security and Resiliency Initiative. No reason
   given for the buyback slowdown. Capital Risk Management; Part II, Item 2.

6. Balance sheet and contingent exposure [WORSE]
   CET1 14.0% (Q2 14.2%) against 11.5%; cushion 2.5 points. Standardized RWA $2.18T (+$196B in
   nine months). SLR 5.5% against 4.3%. Total assets $5.09T (+15% since December). HQLA ~$947B;
   liquidity sources ~$1.5T. Cash-flow-hedge losses expected to reach income over 12 months
   $(2.6)B (Q2 $(1.7)B). GSIB surcharge 5.5% from January 1, 2028 under current rules.

7. Concentration [HELD]
   Uninsured deposits $1,780.9B, 64% of $2.78T deposits (our arithmetic). Apple Card exposure ~$107B
   at closing. Criticized exposure up in Consumer & Retail and TMT, "including held-for-sale
   commitments". Wholesale charge-offs down $390M, in SPEs and Real Estate.

8. Accounting judgments [WORSE]
   Adverse-scenario GDP gap widened from 2.1% to 2.3% below the central case; weighted peak
   unemployment 5.6%. Wholesale build ($902M for nine months) now "largely driven by the impact of
   net increases in the loan and lending-related commitment portfolios"; Q2's offsets (accounts
   receivable, Markets loss assumptions) gone. Apple Card still outside the sensitivity analysis.

9. Legal, regulatory and governance [WORSE]
   Fair Access to Banking: OCC report on nine banks including JPM; CFPB "intent to address any
   customer harm"; Trump suit transferred to federal court in New York, motion to dismiss filed.
   New interchange class action for merchants accepting cards after January 2019. Range unchanged at
   $0 to ~$1.3B. SCB final rules: 2.5% through December 31, 2027 (bullish), 2028 SCB from one test.
   Note 24; Capital Risk Management.

10. Guidance and commitments [BETTER]
   2026 NII guide ~$105.5B -> ~$106.2B (ex-Markets ~$97.2B); adjusted expense ~$107.5B -> ~$108.6B;
   card NCO ~3.2% held, with Q3 at 2.91%. Q4 guide: NII ~$28B, adjusted expense ~$27.5B. Outlook.
