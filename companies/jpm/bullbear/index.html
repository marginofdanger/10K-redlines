Latest filing: 10-Q Q3 2026

What this filing adds, for and against. The case for the franchise got stronger; the case for capital return got weaker.

Bull case

P1 Net interest income and depositsStated
NII +13% to $27.0B with ex-Markets NII accelerating to +6%; 2026 NII guide raised again to ~$106.2B
Q3 2026: "... of $27.0 billion, up 13%, driven by higher Markets net interest income, higher deposit balances, higher balances in Card Services, and higher wholesale loan balances, partially offset by the impact of lower rates. NII excluding Markets was $24.7 billion, up 6%."
Q3 2026: "Management expects net interest income to be approximately $106.2 billion and net interest income excluding Markets to be approximately $97.2 billion, market dependent."

Size: ex-Markets NII +$1.3B year on year in the quarter; the 2026 NII guide is $3.2B (3%) above the FY2025 10-K’s ~$103B.

Core NII growth rose from +4% in Q2 to +6% in Q3 while rates fell, on deposit and loan volume (average loans +10%, deposits +8%). The NII guide has been raised in two straight filings and actual NII landed on the last guide in 2024 and 2025.

Next filing: FY2026 NII at or above ~$106.2B (Q4 ~$28B) and a 2027 ex-Markets guide above 2026’s ~$97.2B.

P2 Fee franchise: CIB, Markets, AWMStated
Investment banking fees +14% in CIB with a #1 ranking; Markets +15%; asset management fees +15%
Q3 2026: "Investment banking fees up 14% YoY, down 8% QoQ; #1 ranking for global investment banking fees with 9.2% wallet share year-to-date"
Q3 2026: "Markets revenue up 15%, with Fixed Income Markets down 4% and Equity Markets up 49%"

Size: Markets revenue $10.3B (21% of net revenue); firmwide IB fees $2.96B (+13%); asset management fees $5.9B (+15%). Nine-month IB fees +24%.

Fee lines grew faster than the balance sheet in both scored quarters (Markets +35% in Q2). The Q3 mix leans on Equity Markets; Fixed Income fell 4%.

Next filing: Full-year IB fees and Markets revenue up on 2025, and AWM net inflows holding with market levels.

P4 Credit quality and reservesStated
Card net charge-off rate 2.91%, the lowest in the scanner’s ten filings, against a 3.2% full-year guide
Q3 2026: "Average loans up 2% YoY and 1% QoQ; Card Services net charge-off rate of 2.91%"
Q3 2026: "Management expects the net charge-off rate in Card Services to be approximately 3.2%."

Size: 29 basis points under the guide; firmwide net charge-offs $2.2B, down $389M year on year, “driven by wholesale”.

Consumer credit, the larger loss pool, is running below both guides in the window (3.4% then 3.2%), and consumer reserves were released on home prices this year.

Next filing: FY2026 card rate at or under ~3.2% and a 2027 card guide no higher.

P5 Capital strength and returnStated
SCB final rules keep JPM’s buffer at the 2.5% floor through December 31, 2027, a quarter longer than before
Q3 2026: "The SCB requirement for firms, including JPMorganChase, will remain at current levels through December 31, 2027, with an updated SCB calculation effective January 1, 2028 based solely on the results of the 2027 Federal Reserve stress test."

Size: the Standardized CET1 requirement stays at 11.5% through 2027 (it was 12.3% with a 3.3% SCB in 2025).

Requirement certainty for five more quarters, at the lowest SCB the rules allow.

Next filing: the 2027 stress-test scenarios and the re-proposed noninterest-income models.

Bear case

P5 Capital strength and returnStated
Buybacks fall from $6.7B to $1.1B, all in July; nothing repurchased in August or September
Q3 2026: "Aggregate purchase price of common stock repurchases (a) | 1,057 | 8,315 | 16,088 | 23,378"
Q3 2026: "September | — | — | — | 48,943 | (c)"

Size: $1.1B against $6.7B (Q2) and $8.3B (Q3 2025); 2% of the $50B program used. Total Q3 payout (dividends plus buybacks) about 36% of net income applicable to common, against 59% in the first half.

The filing gives no reason; the repurchase policy text is unchanged. The stop coincides with CET1 at 14.0%, the same 2.5-point cushion as the Q3 2025 low, and a disclosed 2028 GSIB step-up that would cut the cushion to 1.5 points at today’s ratio (our arithmetic).

Next filing: Q4 repurchases in the FY2026 10-K; any stated CET1 target or buyback pace; whether the 2028 surcharge changes.

P5 Capital strength and returnStated
About $7.9B of unrealized securities losses hit CET1 in the quarter "driven by the impact of higher interest rates"; TBVPS falls
Q3 2026: "higher net unrealized losses in AOCI, driven by the impact of higher interest rates on the AFS securities portfolio and cash flow hedges in Treasury and CIO."
Q3 2026: "Unrealized gains/(losses) on investment securities | (9,974)"

Size: nine-month AFS loss in CET1 $(2.1)B at June → $(10.0)B at September, so about $7.9B in Q3, roughly 36 basis points of CET1 and about half of Q3 net income. TBVPS $113.35 → $112.64. Cash-flow-hedge losses due through income over 12 months $(2.6)B.

Rates now move JPM’s capital as much as earnings do. The AOCI loss alone was seven times the quarter’s buybacks.

Next filing: AOCI direction in Q4 and the FY2026 CET1 ratio; whether securities repositioning continues.

P3 Expense disciplineStated
Expense +12% against revenue +8%; pre-provision profit +3%; 2026 expense guide raised to ~$108.6B
Q3 2026: "Noninterest expense was $27.3 billion, up 12%, predominantly driven by higher compensation expense as a result of higher revenue-related compensation, wage inflation, and growth in the number of front office employees"
Q3 2026: "Management expects adjusted expense to be approximately $108.6 billion, market dependent."

Size: expense +$3.0B vs revenue +$3.7B year on year; the 2026 expense guide is up $3.6B since the FY2025 10-K against +$3.2B for NII.

Negative operating leverage in a quarter without one-offs. Part of the growth is investment (front-office hires, technology, marketing, international consumer); the filing does not separate it.

Next filing: the 2027 expense guide versus the 2027 NII guide, and Q4 adjusted expense against ~$27.5B.

P7 Legal, regulatory and politicalStated
OCC publishes a fair-banking review of nine banks including JPM; the CFPB says it will "address any customer harm"
Q3 2026: "In October 2026, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency ... issued a report entitled ... following its review of the activities of nine banks, including JPMorganChase"
Q3 2026: "The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau also announced its intent to address any customer harm caused by the practices identified in the OCC’s report."

Size: not quantified in the filing; the aggregate range of reasonably possible losses stayed at $0 to ~$1.3B.

The matter moved from information requests to a published supervisory review and a stated remediation intent. A new interchange class action for the post-2019 period was filed the same month.

Next filing: any CFPB action or remediation amount, the ruling on the motion to dismiss the Trump suit, and the loss range.

P4 Credit quality and reservesInferred
Wholesale build doubles to $902M for nine months; criticized exposure rises in Consumer & Retail and TMT; downside scenario harsher
Q3 2026: "The increase in criticized exposure was predominantly driven by certain exposures in Consumer & Retail and Technology, Media & Telecommunications, primarily resulting from downgrades and new lending-related commitments, including held-for-sale commitments."
Q3 2026: "a weighted average U.S. real GDP level that is 2.3% lower than the central case at the end of the fourth quarter of 2027."

Size: Q3 allowance build $539M, “primarily in wholesale”; allowance $32.0B; GDP gap 2.1% → 2.3%.

The text ties the build mainly to commitment growth, but downgrades in two sectors and a harsher downside point to some deterioration behind fast loan growth (inference). Not yet visible in charge-offs.

Next filing: wholesale nonaccrual and criticized totals, wholesale charge-offs, and whether the adverse weight is raised.
