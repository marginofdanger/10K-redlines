JPMORGAN CHASE (JPM): INVESTMENT THESIS PILLARS
Written November 2026 from the 10-K redline dashboard (FY2018-FY2025) and the 10-Q scanner (Q2 2024
to Q3 2026). The Bull / Bear page scores two filings: the Q2 2026 10-Q (filed August 6, 2026) and the
Q3 2026 10-Q (filed November 3, 2026). Every pillar starts at Intact; the scanner's record since
Q2 2024 is the background for each stance, and a stance moves only when the two scored filings point
the same way. Figures are from the filings' financial highlights and SEC XBRL unless quoted.

P1 Net interest income and the deposit franchise
  Bull assumes: deposit and loan growth carry NII higher even as rates fall, with ex-Markets NII
    growing on the core balance sheet, not only on trading balances.
  Bear fears: rate cuts compress deposit margins, NII growth leans on Markets NII that has offsets
    in trading revenue, and a deposit base that is mostly uninsured wholesale money is less sticky.
  Stance: Strengthening, moved at the Q3 2026 10-Q.
  History: 2024 NII $92.6B; 2025 $95.4B, each landing on the last guide. 2026 guide $103B (FY2025
    10-K) -> $105.5B (Q2) -> $106.2B (Q3); ex-Markets $95B -> $96.5B -> $97.2B. Quarterly NII +10%
    (Q2 2026) and +13% (Q3 2026); ex-Markets +4% and +6%. Average deposits +7% and +8%; average
    loans +10% in both. Corporate NII down on "the impact of lower rates" in both quarters.
  Tests: ex-Markets NII growth, the NII guide, average deposits, deposit margin language, uninsured
    deposit share.

P2 Fee franchise: CIB, Markets and AWM
  Bull assumes: investment banking, Markets and asset management take share and grow faster than
    the balance sheet, so fee revenue compounds with low capital intensity.
  Bear fears: Markets revenue is cyclical and consumes RWA; IB fees mean-revert; AWM flows follow
    market levels.
  Stance: Strengthening, moved at the Q3 2026 10-Q.
  History: Markets revenue +35% (Q2 2026) and +15% (Q3 2026); investment banking fees +13% in Q3
    with a #1 wallet ranking at 9.2% year to date; asset management fees +15%; AUM $5.2T (+13%).
    Nine-month investment banking fees +24%. Capital allocated to CIB raised $8.5B in Q2 2026 "in
    connection with growth in the business", and Standardized RWA rose $196B in nine months.
  Tests: Markets and IB fees year on year, AWM net inflows, CIB capital and RWA consumption.

P3 Expense discipline and operating leverage
  Bull assumes: technology and marketing investment pays back in revenue, keeping the overhead
    ratio stable and pre-provision profit growing with revenue.
  Bear fears: expense growth outruns revenue: guides above NII, revenue-related pay, technology,
    branches and international consumer launches that sit in Corporate.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q3 2026 10-Q.
  History: adjusted expense guide at or above the NII guide since the FY2024 10-K. 2026 expense
    guide $105B -> $107.5B (Q2) -> $108.6B (Q3), +$3.6B against +$3.2B for NII. Expense +15% in Q2
    2026 (revenue +15% excluding the Visa and equity gains) and +12% in Q3 against revenue +8%;
    Q3 pre-provision profit +3%. Chase Europe launched in Germany in May 2026.
  Tests: expense growth vs revenue growth, the expense guide, Corporate expense, pre-provision profit.

P4 Credit quality and reserves
  Bull assumes: card losses stay below guide, wholesale losses stay idiosyncratic, and the
    allowance (with a standing adverse overlay) is ample.
  Bear fears: wholesale builds and criticized exposure rise with fast loan growth; borrower fraud
    and private-credit style exposures surprise; Apple Card adds ~$107B of exposure at closing.
  Stance: Intact (never moved in the window).
  History: card net charge-off rate 3.34% (Q2 2026) and 2.91% (Q3 2026), guide cut to 3.2% in Q2;
    consumer reserves released on home prices. Wholesale build $473M (H1) -> $902M (nine months);
    criticized exposure up in Consumer & Retail and TMT (Q3); weighted GDP gap 2.1% -> 2.3%.
    "Borrower fraud" (Q3 2025, FY2025) is not repeated in 2026. Allowance $31.5B -> $32.0B.
  Tests: card NCO rate vs guide, wholesale build drivers, criticized and nonaccrual exposure,
    adverse-scenario wording, Apple Card allowance.

P5 Capital strength and capital return
  Bull assumes: a CET1 ratio well above requirement, a 2.5% SCB and regulatory relief fund steady
    buybacks of $6-8B a quarter plus a rising dividend.
  Bear fears: balance sheet growth, AOCI losses and the 2028 GSIB step-up (4.5% -> 5.5% under
    current rules) absorb the cushion and buybacks shrink.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q3 2026 10-Q.
  History: CET1 15.7% (FY2024) fell in seven straight filings to 14.0% (Q3 2026); cushion 2.5
    points. Q2 2026: CET1 14.2%, buybacks $6.7B, second $50B authorization, dividend to $1.65.
    Q3 2026: buybacks $1.1B (none in August or September), ~$7.9B AOCI loss on higher rates. Relief
    on the other side: SCB frozen at 2.5% through 2027, SLR requirement 4.3%, Basel re-proposal
    sized at +6% (+4% net) versus +25% for the 2023 proposal.
  Tests: CET1 vs requirement, quarterly buybacks, AOCI, RWA growth, 2028 GSIB and SCB outcomes.

P6 Earnings quality
  Bull assumes: reported earnings are recurring; one-off gains are disclosed and small relative to
    a ~$15B quarterly run-rate.
  Bear fears: Visa exchanges, equity remeasurements, tax benefits and securities repositioning
    move reported profit by billions; Corporate equity marks grow.
  Stance: Intact (never moved in the window).
  History: Q2 2026 included $5.6B of pre-tax one-offs ($4.6B Visa, $1.0B equity investments) on
    $27.5B pre-tax income. Q3 2026 ran the other way: $658M securities losses and a net loss on
    certain equity investments. Strategic Investment Group equity $3.7B -> $5.7B, mostly at the
    measurement alternative. Effective tax rate 23.1% and 24.0%.
  Tests: named one-offs in each quarter, Corporate NIR, equity investment carrying values, tax rate.

P7 Legal, regulatory and political exposure
  Bull assumes: legacy and federal enforcement matters keep closing, the loss range stays near
    $1-1.5B, and the regulatory climate stays favorable.
  Bear fears: politically driven matters (Fair Access to Banking) and consumer class actions grow
    into remediation or damages; Russian judgments are enforced abroad.
  Stance: Intact (never moved in the window).
  History: range $1.7B (2024) -> $1.1B (2025 low) -> $1.3B (2026). Q2 2026: Shareholder Litigation
    dismissed with prejudice and removed; cash sweep plaintiffs move to certify a class. Q3 2026:
    OCC report on nine banks including JPM; CFPB to "address any customer harm"; Trump suit moved
    to federal court in New York; new post-2019 interchange class action; SCB rules finalized.
  Tests: loss range, Fair Access developments, cash sweep class status, interchange, Russia.
