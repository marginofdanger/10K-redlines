JPMorgan Chase 10-K Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of eight JPMorgan Chase 10-Ks: how the wording on capital, credit, guidance and legal risk moved ahead of the numbers.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0000019617) | 8 annual reports on Form 10-K, fiscal years 2018-2025 | Analysis: November 2026
FY2018 → FY2019

CECL Sized, Climate Named

Record year: managed revenue $118.7B, net income $36.4B, ROTCE 19%. CECL day-one cost $4.3B vs $4-6B estimate. First climate risk factor; new Capital category. CET1 range floor raised to 11.5%. NII guide cut to ~$57B, market dependent.

FY2019 → FY2020

COVID, CECL and the Reserve Build

COVID-19 leads the risk factors. $12.2B reserve build plus $4.3B CECL: allowance $14.3B to $30.8B. Net income down 20% to $29.1B. Buybacks suspended March 2020. Deposits up 37% to $2.14T. CET1 target 12%. Metals DPA, ~$920M. Charge-off guidance dropped.

FY2020 → FY2021

Reserves Released, Deposits Parked

Net income $29.1B to $48.3B on a $12.1B allowance release; ROTCE 14% to 23%. Deposits +15% to $2.46T, cash at banks $714B, uninsured deposits $1,489.6B first disclosed. NII guide rebased to ex-Markets >$53B; expense guide $77B; CET1 12-13% range.

FY2021 → FY2022

Rates Rise, Deposits Leave

Managed NII $52.7B to $67.1B (+27%) but net income -22% to $37.7B on a $6.4B provision. Deposits fell 5% to $2.34T on attrition. +100bp sensitivity flipped to -$2.0B. SCB 4.0%, CET1 target 13.0-13.5%, buybacks paused July 2022.

FY2022 → FY2023

First Republic and the Basel Proposal

First Republic bought from the FDIC in May 2023: $2.8B bargain purchase gain, $50B FDIC note, 80% loss-sharing. Basel III proposal sized at ~30% more RWA; 13.5% CET1 target dropped. $2.9B FDIC special assessment. NII $89.3B vs $73B guide; CET1 15.0%.

FY2023 → FY2024

One Wholesale Bank, Basel Fades

CIB and Commercial Banking merged into one segment, three in total. Basel impact estimate and capital-build sentence deleted; proposal "would have" revised rules. Repurchases $9.9B to $18.8B; CET1 15.7%. NII $92.6B, up 4%, on deposit margin compression. DEI wording cut.

FY2024 → FY2025

Capital Relief, Apple Card, Political Suits

Net income $57.0B (-2%), ROTCE 20%; provision $14.2B incl. $2.2B Apple Card reserve; SCB 3.3% to 2.5% floor; Advanced ratios binding (CET1 14.1%); buyback authorization $50B; 2026 NII guide ~$103B, expense ~$105B; AI risk factor added, ESG/DEI paragraphs cut; Trump fair-banking lawsuit

Risk Factors

▶CECL restated as done: allowance "will need to... increase" → "increased as a result of the implementation of CECL, which has a negative impact" on capitalSignal

Shift: realized: hypothetical → actual (signals score 8.5); certainty: "may have" → "has" a negative impact on capital (+); tense: "will be required to implement" → "has implemented" (+)

FY2018: "JPMorgan Chase expects that under CECL, it will need to, among other things, increase the allowance for credit losses related to its loans and other lending-related commitments, which may have a negative impact on its capital levels."
FY2019: "The allowance for credit losses related to JPMorgan Chase’s loans and other lending-related commitments increased as a result of the implementation of CECL, which has a negative impact on JPMorgan Chase’s capital levels."

History (phrase_history): "will need to, among other things, increase the allowance" is in FY2018 only; "increased as a result of the implementation of CECL" and the new bullet "unexpected increases in the allowance for credit losses" are in FY2019 only.

The 10-K is dated after the January 1, 2020 adoption, so the risk is written as an event. The new "ongoing impact" list now warns of unexpected allowance increases, a few weeks before COVID-19 turned that into the 2020 reserve build.
▶Climate change risk factor added: "could have a material adverse impact on JPMorgan Chase’s business operations, clients and customers"New

Shift: named: +climate change (+); physical risks listed: droughts, flooding, wildfires, rising sea levels, rising heat index; transition risk to clients' business models (+)

FY2019: "Climate change could have a material adverse impact on JPMorgan Chase’s business operations, clients and customers."
FY2019: "In addition, these physical changes may prompt changes in regulations or consumer preferences which in turn could have negative consequences for the business models of JPMorgan Chase’s clients."

History (phrase_history): "climate change" does not appear anywhere in the FY2018 10-K; it is in every 10-K from FY2019 to FY2025. The wording "could be disrupted by climate change" is FY2019 only.

The bank's first climate disclosure covers both physical damage and the risk that rules or tastes change under its clients. The same 10-K's Recent events records a February 25, 2020 commitment to facilitate $200 billion toward the UN development goals, $50 billion of it green.
▶New "Capital" risk category, placed between Liquidity and Operational: capital "is critical to support... and distribute capital to shareholders"New

Shift: new category: Capital (+); "continued uncertainty remains" over new capital proposals (−)

FY2019: "Maintaining the appropriate level and composition of capital is critical to support JPMorgan Chase’s business activities, meet regulatory requirements and distribute capital to shareholders."
FY2019: "...prudential regulators have recently issued new regulatory capital proposals, and continued uncertainty remains as to the manner in which these requirements ultimately will apply to JPMorgan Chase."

History (phrase_history): the "is critical" heading is in FY2019 only. FY2020 rewrites it as "Maintaining the required level and composition of capital may impact", which stands through FY2024.

Capital moves out of the regulatory section into its own category, with the capital plan, stress tests and buyback limits gathered under it. A year later the stress capital buffer and the 2020 buyback halt made those limits bind (next transition).
▶LIBOR end firmed: "substantial risk that LIBOR will be discontinued or modified by 2021" → "highly likely that LIBOR will be discontinued on or about the end of 2021"Changed

Shift: certainty: substantial risk → highly likely (+); scope: "discontinued or modified" → "discontinued" (+); new: transition litigation from clients (+)

FY2018: "This announcement indicates that the continuation of LIBOR on the current basis cannot be guaranteed after 2021, and there is a substantial risk that LIBOR will be discontinued or modified by 2021."
FY2019: "These statements indicate that it is highly likely that LIBOR will be discontinued on or about the end of 2021."
FY2019: "JPMorgan Chase could also become involved in litigation and other types of disputes with clients, customers, counterparties and investors as a consequence of the transition from LIBOR and other benchmark rates to replacement rates..."

History (phrase_history): "substantial risk that LIBOR will be discontinued" is FY2018 only; "highly likely that LIBOR will be discontinued" is FY2019 only.

"Modified" drops out: the bank now plans for LIBOR to end, not to be reformed. A five-item list of claims clients could bring over the switch (unfair treatment, poor disclosure, conflicts) is added beside it.

MD&A

▶Medium-term CET1 range floor raised: "between 11% and 12%" → "between 11.5% and 12%"Changed

Shift: guidance figures: 11%-12% → 11.5%-12% (+, floor up 50 bps, ceiling unchanged)

FY2018: "The Firm believes that it will operate with a Basel III CET1 capital ratio between 11% and 12% over the medium term."
FY2019: "The Firm believes that it will operate with a Basel III CET1 capital ratio between 11.5% and 12% over the medium term."

History (phrase_history): "between 11% and 12% over the medium term" is FY2018 only; "between 11.5% and 12%" is FY2019 only. FY2020 drops the range for a single "target... of 12%" (next transition).

Only the floor moved, so the room to run capital down shrank by half. CET1 ended 2019 at 12.4%, above the top of the range, even as buybacks rose from $20.0B to $24.0B and the authorization from $20.7B to $29.4B.
▶Goodwill headroom cut: reporting-unit fair values exceed carrying value by "approximately 20% or higher" → "approximately 15% or higher"Changed

Shift: quant: minimum cushion 20% → 15% (−)

FY2018: "The fair values of these reporting units exceeded their carrying values by approximately 20% or higher and did not indicate a significant risk of goodwill impairment based on current projections and valuations."
FY2019: "The fair values of these reporting units exceeded their carrying values by approximately 15% or higher and did not indicate a significant risk of goodwill impairment based on current projections and valuations."

History (phrase_history): "approximately 20% or higher" is FY2018 only; "approximately 15% or higher" is FY2019 only; FY2020 says "at least 15%".

The weakest reporting unit lost about a quarter of its headroom in a record-earnings year. The filing does not name the unit, and no impairment was taken.
▶Wholesale credit "was stable" → "remained favorable" while criticized exposure rose from $12.1B to $14.3B on "select client downgrades"Signal

Shift: degree: "credit quality... was stable" → "credit performance... remained favorable" (+); quant: criticized $12.1B → $14.3B, down-move → up-move (−)

FY2018: "The credit quality of the wholesale portfolio was stable for the year ended December 31, 2018, characterized by low levels of criticized exposure, nonaccrual loans and charge-offs."
FY2019: "The credit performance of the wholesale portfolio remained favorable for the year ended December 31, 2019, characterized by continued low levels of criticized exposure, nonaccrual loans and charge-offs."
FY2019: "The total criticized component of the portfolio, excluding loans held-for-sale and loans at fair value, was $14.3 billion at December 31, 2019, compared with $12.1 billion at December 31, 2018. The increase was driven by select client downgrades."

History (phrase_history): "remained favorable for the year ended" is FY2019 only. FY2020 replaces it with "broad-based credit deterioration" and criticized exposure of $41.6B (next transition).

Criticized exposure rose 18% after falling the year before, and the wholesale provision also rose on client downgrades. The adjective got warmer as the number got worse.
▶Forward-looking-statement disaster item adds "health emergencies, the spread of infectious diseases, pandemics" and "climate change"Changed

Shift: named: +health emergencies, +infectious diseases, +pandemics, +climate change (+)

FY2018: "Occurrence of natural or man-made disasters or calamities or conflicts and the Firm’s ability to deal effectively with disruptions caused by the foregoing"
FY2019: "Occurrence of natural or man-made disasters or calamities, including health emergencies, the spread of infectious diseases, pandemics or outbreaks of hostilities, or the effects of climate change, and the Firm’s ability to deal effectively with disruptions caused by the foregoing"

History (phrase_history): "the spread of infectious diseases, pandemics" is in the FY2019 and FY2020 10-Ks only. "Pandemics" already appeared in the FY2018 risk factors. Neither "COVID" nor "coronavirus" appears anywhere in the FY2019 10-K.

The 10-K is dated February 25, 2020. Infectious disease enters the cautionary list weeks before the WHO pandemic declaration, but the outbreak itself is not named, and the 2020 outlook in the same filing carries no pandemic caveat.

Risk Factors

▶COVID-19 becomes the first risk category: the pandemic "has caused and is causing significant harm" and has already raised the allowanceNew

Shift: new first category: COVID-19 Pandemic, ahead of Regulatory (+); realized: "has caused and is causing"; "have had a negative impact" (+)

FY2020: "The COVID-19 pandemic has caused and is causing significant harm to the global economy and could further negatively affect certain of JPMorgan Chase’s businesses."
FY2020: "The adverse economic conditions caused by the pandemic have had a negative impact on certain of JPMorgan Chase’s businesses and results of operations, including..."

History (phrase_history): "COVID-19" appears nowhere in the FY2018 or FY2019 10-Ks; it is in the FY2020 risk factors 17 times, FY2021 13, FY2022 2, and gone from the risk factors from FY2023. "Has caused and is causing significant harm" is FY2020 only.

▶Allowance models admitted to be off their data: variables "well outside the range of historical data used to train the models"; the bank "has made... significant adjustments"Signal

Themes

Capital: from a stated target to a cushion nobody names

The CET1 cushion over the Standardized requirement doubled from 1.2-1.9 points (FY2019-FY2022) to 3.1-3.6 points (FY2023-FY2025). The 13.5% target was declared "no longer meaningful" in FY2023, when the Basel III proposal was sized at about 30% more RWA. By FY2025 the SCB had fallen to the 2.5% floor, the Apple Card deal made the Advanced approach binding for the first time, and buyback authority went to $50B. The firm now holds capital for rules that have not been written, not against a number it publishes.

Basel III endgame: five years of holding language, one year of numbers, then the past tense

  • FY2018-FY2022: the same "considering how to appropriately apply" sentence
  • FY2023: quantified, about 30% more RWA and about 25% more CET1
  • FY2024: the estimate deleted; the proposal "would have significantly revised" the rules
  • FY2025: dropped from the risk factors; a re-proposal expected

Guidance credibility runs one way since 2021

The NII guide in the 10-K was missed three years running (FY2018-FY2020 guides) and beaten four years running (FY2021-FY2024 guides). Card charge-offs came in under every guide from FY2022 to FY2024, and FY2025 lowered the card guide for the first time (about 3.6% to about 3.4%). The FY2025 10-K also lifts the adjusted expense guide by $10B to about $105B, the largest step-up in the eight 10-Ks (it rose $1B-$9B a year before), against an NII guide of about $103B.

Credit risk changes its name: office, then fraud

Office went from an unnamed line to the named driver of wholesale charge-offs in FY2023-FY2024 as its investment-grade share fell from 74% to 47%. In FY2025 it disappears from the charge-off explanations, replaced by commercial and industrial loans and "borrower fraud" in secured lending, and a new risk-factor paragraph says collateral fraud losses have already happened. Private credit is named for the first time. The allowance is back to about $31B, its FY2020 pandemic level, with more weight on adverse scenarios.

Risks restated as having happened

As with other large filers, the most telling edits move a risk from "could" to "has": operational failures including "the exfiltration of customer personal data" (FY2023), Russian courts that "have disregarded" forum clauses and later made judgments "now enforceable" (FY2024-FY2025), and collateral fraud (FY2025). Each moved a hypothetical into the record a year or more after the event.

Politics moves from the reputation paragraph into the litigation note

FY2024 dropped DEI from the human-capital text and named it as a subject of criticism. FY2025 deleted the activist, ESG and DEI paragraphs, recast reputation risk as "decisions to engage or not engage with a client," and added a Fair Access to Banking matter: an August 2025 Executive Order, government requests and a January 2026 civil lawsuit by President Trump against the bank and its CEO. The reasonably possible loss range fell from $1.4B to $1.2B in the same note.
