FY2018 → FY2019

Chase Bank USA merged into JPMorgan Chase Bank; Capital and climate risk factors added; CET1 range raised

Chase Bank USA, N.A. merged into JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. in May 2019, and references to it were removed throughout the filing. Risk Factors added a Capital category and a climate change risk factor and removed the tax reform factor and the Competition category; CECL language moved from planned to adopted. The 2020 outlook set NII at approximately $57 billion and adjusted expense at approximately $67 billion, and the medium-term CET1 range moved from 11%–12% to 11.5%–12%.

FY2019 → FY2020

COVID-19 risk category added; CECL adopted; SCB replaces CCAR objection; Human capital section added

The FY2020 10-K adds COVID-19 as the first risk category, a new forward-looking-statement item and COVID-19 sub-sections across MD&A, and replaces the incurred-loss allowance language with CECL expected-loss language. Net income fell to $29.1 billion on $12.2 billion of net allowance additions, average deposits rose 25%, and the capital section reports an SCB of 3.3%, a 12% CET1 target and a share repurchase suspension. Item 1 adds a Human capital section and Merchant Services moved into a new Wholesale Payments unit in CIB.

FY2020 → FY2021

COVID language moved to past/conditional tense; NII guidance ex-CIB Markets; DEI sub-section added

The FY2021 10-K removes the Brexit and accounting-standards risk factors, the COVID-19 regulatory-developments section and the quarterly outlook items, and restates COVID-19 risk language in conditional or past tense. Full-year 2022 guidance is given as NII excluding CIB Markets in excess of $53 billion and adjusted expense of approximately $77 billion, and the 12% CET1 target is replaced by a 12.0%–13.0% operating range. New disclosures include a Diversity, equity and inclusion sub-section, estimated uninsured deposits, and an expected 4.5% GSIB surcharge from January 1, 2024.

FY2021 → FY2022

COVID-19 risk category removed; War in Ukraine and Climate Risk Management sections added; CET1 target raised

The FY2022 10-K removes the COVID-19 Pandemic risk category and Business Developments sub-section, adds War in Ukraine disclosures in MD&A, country risk and operational risk, and adds a Climate Risk Management section. The CET1 target changes from a 12.0%–13.0% range to 13.0% for 1Q23 rising to 13.5% for 1Q24, with the SCB at 4.0% and share repurchases disclosed as suspended in July 2022 and resumed in 1Q23. NII guidance changes to approximately $73 billion total and approximately $74 billion excluding Markets, and the deposit discussion adds attrition and migration-into-investments language.

FY2022 → FY2023

First Republic acquisition added; CET1 target withdrawn after U.S. Basel III proposal; Ukraine and LIBOR sections removed

The FY2023 10-K adds the May 2023 First Republic acquisition across Business, a new risk factor, MD&A drivers, credit tables and the allowance. The capital section adds the July 2023 U.S. Basel III proposal with an estimated ~30% RWA increase and replaces the 13.5% CET1 target with language that the target is no longer meaningful. The War in Ukraine Business Developments sub-section and the LIBOR transition risk factor are removed, and new legal matters (Trading Venues, Zelle, Russian Litigation, SEC Inquiries) are added.

FY2023 → FY2024

CIB and CB combined; CET1-target and Basel III impact sentences removed; "diverse" dropped from human-capital text

The FY2024 10-K reports three reportable segments after the second-quarter 2024 combination of the Corporate & Investment Bank and Commercial Banking, and uses the "JPMorganChase" name style throughout. NII explanations replace "higher rates" and "deposit margin expansion" with balance sheet actions and "deposit margin compression"; the Basel III impact estimate and the CET1-target sentence are removed, and the SCB rises from 2.9% to 3.3%. "Diverse" is removed from human-capital, risk-factor and forward-looking-statement text, the First Republic risk factor is replaced by a data-quality risk factor, and the Russian litigation disclosure moves from possible outcomes to judgments entered and executed.

FY2024 → FY2025

Apple Card commitment; Advanced ratios binding; risk factors rewritten with AI risk added

Net income fell 2% to $57.0 billion and the provision rose to $14.2 billion, including $2.2 billion for the Apple Card forward purchase commitment entered into December 30, 2025. Advanced risk-based ratios became binding, the SCB fell to 2.5% and is held through September 30, 2027, and the buyback authorization rose to $50 billion. Item 1A was redrafted with a merged Legal and Regulatory category, a new AI risk factor, and the removal of ESG, activist and consumer-fee rule references.
