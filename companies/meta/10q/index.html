META 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of 11 SEC filings (ten 10-Qs plus the FY2025 10-K). The value is speed: catching language changes at quarterly cadence that annual 10-K comparisons would miss by 3–9 months.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001326801) | 11 Filings | Q1 2023 – Q1 2026 | Analysis: March 2026, extended June 2026

Q1 2023 → Q2 2023

Headcount: 77K → 71K | Capex: $30-33B → $27-30B
▶ "Discipline and habits" first appears in Investment Philosophy New

Q1 2023 MDA opens with a cost-focused framing. Q2 2023 introduces a new sentence anchoring the efficiency program as cultural:

"We expect to continue to build on the discipline and habits that we developed in 2022 when we initiated several efforts to increase our efficiency, while still remaining focused on investing in significant opportunities."

Q1 2023 had no equivalent phrase — it led with "We expect our total expenses in 2023 to be driven by headcount-related expenses."

▶ "Board Chair" replaces "Chairman" Changed

Q1 2023 risk factor TOC:

"...our founder, Chairman, and CEO."

Q2 2023 risk factor TOC:

"...our founder, Board Chair, and CEO."

This title reverted to "Chairman, and Chief Executive Officer (CEO)" from Q1 2024 onward.

▶ RL loss guidance adds "continue to" Changed

Q1 2023 MDA:

"...we expect our RL operating losses to increase in 2023 and beyond."

Q2 2023 MDA:

"...we expect our RL operating losses to continue to increase in 2023 and beyond."

The addition of "continue to" signals losses were already growing, not just projected to grow.

▶ AI paragraph expands from single paragraph to multi-paragraph Signal

Q1 2023 risk factor on AI was one paragraph covering initiatives, competition, and regulation. Q2 2023 splits AI into a standalone risk factor with its own header and adds the sentence:

"We are making significant investments in artificial intelligence (AI) initiatives, including generative AI, to, among other things, recommend relevant unconnected content across our products, enhance our advertising tools, develop new products, and develop new features for existing products."

Q1 2023 had used "develop new product features using generative AI" as a single item. Q2 2023 split this into "develop new products" + "develop new features for existing products" — a broader scope claim.

▶ Capex guidance cut from $30-33B to $27-30B Signal

Q1 2023 MDA:

"We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $30 billion to $33 billion in 2023."

Q2 2023 MDA:

"We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $27 billion to $30 billion in 2023."

A $3B cut at both ends of the range — the efficiency era was pulling back infrastructure spend.

▶ RL loss timeline shifts from "2023 and beyond" to "increase meaningfully in 2024" Changed

Q3 2025 → FY2025 10-K

Annual Filing (Jan 29, 2026) | FY2025 revenue $200.97B (+22%) | Capex: $70-72B (2025) → $115-135B (2026) | Headcount: 78,450 → 78,865 | Long-term debt: $29B → $59B

Guidance & MD&A

▶ 2026 capex guided $115-135B; attribution flips to "AI efforts and core business" Signal

Q3 2025 MDA:

"We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $70 billion to $72 billion in 2025 to support our core business and AI efforts, and expect significant capital expenditures growth in 2026."

FY2025 10-K MDA:

"We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $115 billion to $135 billion in 2026 to support our AI efforts and core business."

The midpoint (~$125B) is roughly 1.7x FY2025 actual capex of $72.22B. The attribution word order also flips: AI is listed ahead of the core business for the first time since "generative AI" entered the capex sentence in the FY2024 10-K.

▶ RL loss guidance pivots to "remain similar to 2025" — first non-increase guidance Changed

Q3 2025 MDA:

"...we continue to expect our full-year RL operating losses to increase in 2025."

FY2025 10-K MDA:

"In 2025, our RL segment reduced our overall operating profit by approximately $19.19 billion, and we expect our 2026 RL operating losses to remain similar to 2025."

Every filing since Q1 2023 had guided RL losses upward ("increase," "continue to increase," "increase meaningfully"). This is the first flat guidance in the dataset, issued after a $19.19B FY2025 RL operating loss.

▶ RL reframing: "metaverse and wearables" → "RL efforts" / "VR, Horizon, and wearables"; "mixed reality" dropped Changed

Q3 2025 MDA:

"We are also making significant investments in our metaverse and wearables efforts, including developing virtual, augmented, and mixed reality devices, software for social platforms..."

FY2025 10-K MDA:

"We are also making significant investments in our RL efforts, including developing virtual and augmented reality devices, software for social platforms, neural interfaces, and other foundational technologies."

The parallel risk-factor sentence changes from "as part of our metaverse and wearables efforts" to "as part of our VR, Horizon, and wearables efforts." "Mixed reality" is dropped from the segment description throughout, after being added in Q1 2024.

Balance Sheet & Financing

▶ Long-term debt doubles: $29.0B → $59.0B Signal

Q3 2025 (as of September 30, 2025):

"...senior unsecured notes for an aggregate principal amount of $29.0 billion, which mature from 2027 through 2064."

FY2025 10-K (as of December 31, 2025):

"...we had outstanding long-term debt in the form of senior unsecured notes for an aggregate principal amount of $59.0 billion, which mature from 2027 through 2064."

The $30B fixed-rate note issuance occurred in November 2025, between the two filings. Combined short-term and long-term future interest obligations roughly doubled, to $2.98B + $56.74B.

▶ Contractual commitments $81.19B → $131.05B in one quarter Signal

Q3 2025:

"We also have $81.19 billion of non-cancelable contractual commitments as of September 30, 2025. These commitments are mostly related to third-party cloud capacity arrangements..."

FY2025 10-K:

"We also have $131.05 billion of contractual commitments as of December 31, 2025, mostly related to third-party cloud capacity arrangements and our continued investments in servers and network infrastructure, data centers, and consumer hardware products in Reality Labs with $30.63 billion due in 2026."

A $49.9B increase in one quarter. The pipeline of leases signed but not yet commenced also grew from ~$58.14B to ~$103.77B, with lease terms of "greater than one year to 30 years."

Business & Strategy

FY2025 10-K Part I overview:

"We are innovating in artificial intelligence (AI) technologies to build transformative experiences and capabilities across our Family of Apps and new platforms, and to advance our vision to deliver personal superintelligence for everyone."

The business section also adds a definition sentence: "advance our vision to build superintelligence, which we define as AI that surpasses human intelligence." "Superintelligence" first appeared in the Q2 2025 risk factors; this is its promotion to the company's stated vision.

"We also compete with companies that develop and deliver consumer hardware and augmented, mixed, and virtual reality products and services."

FY2025 10-K:

Related changes: the key-personnel risk changes "senior engineering talent" to "senior engineering and AI research talent," and a new computing-needs sentence states "Our increased efforts toward building frontier AI models have driven a significant further expansion in our computing needs."

▶ Violating accounts estimate rises <3% → <5% of DAP on methodology change Changed

FY2024 10-K (the metric is annual-only; not carried in 10-Qs):

"In the fourth quarter of 2024, we estimated that less than 3% of our worldwide DAP consisted solely of violating accounts."

FY2025 10-K:

"...in the fourth quarter of 2025, we estimated that less than 5% of our worldwide DAP consisted solely of violating accounts. We believe the increase compared to our prior estimation was a result of the methodology update described above."

The filing attributes the higher estimate to updated data signals incorporated into the estimation methodology in Q4 2025, not to a stated change in underlying behavior.

Risk Factors

▶ Model-weights and AI-agent cybersecurity language first appears New

FY2025 10-K risk factor:

"...our efforts to develop and deploy AI models, internal and third-party AI tools, and other AI applications expose us to increased and novel risks and vulnerabilities, including prompt injection, errors, and other issues related to AI agents, as well as compromise of valuable intellectual property including source code, model weights, and other assets."

"Model weights" and "prompt injection" have zero mentions in any prior filing in the dataset. Model weights are framed as compromisable intellectual property alongside source code.

▶ Youth litigation bellwether trials scheduled for 2026 New

FY2025 10-K risk factor:

"For example, several bellwether trials in our youth-related litigation matters are scheduled for 2026 and beyond."

Legal proceedings expand the claim list to "age verification, AI and AI chatbots, deceptive advertising, illicit or illegal activity with respect to drugs, fraud, and firearms, and privacy-related matters," and the mass-arbitration count grows from "tens of thousands" of individual claimants (Q3 2025) to "over one hundred thousand" (10-K).

Legal Proceedings

▶ FTC antitrust: judgment in Meta's favor; FTC appeals Changed

"...the court is expected to issue a decision in the fourth quarter of 2025 or later."

FY2025 10-K:

"On November 18, 2025, the court granted judgment in our favor. On January 20, 2026, the FTC filed a notice of appeal of that ruling."

This resolves (at the trial level) the FTC monopolization case that had been tracked in legal proceedings since the start of the dataset; the appeal keeps the matter open.

▶ Spanish media judgment: €542M damages awarded against Meta New

FY2025 10-K:

Companion suits are disclosed: a separate Spanish claim by UTECA, and a French claim filed by "67 media companies" (Amaury et al.) with "Trial... expected to take place in 2027."

The cost-sharing dispute escalated across two quarters. Q2 2025 disclosed the Tax Court opinion in the 2010 tax-year case, which valued the transferred IP at $7.79B — $1.48B above Meta's reported position. Q3 2025 then disclosed a new deficiency notice covering three more years:

Themes

1. Capex as Leading Indicator

Capex guidance changed every single quarter from Q1 2023 through Q3 2025. The range went from $30-33B to $70-72B with a 2026 growth signal. Annual comparisons would miss the intra-year cuts (Q1-Q2 2023), the sequential floor raises (Q2-Q3 2024), and the forward signals for future years that appeared mid-cycle.

2. "Discipline and Habits" Had a 7-Quarter Lifecycle

First appeared Q2 2023 as an efficiency mantra. Refined to "operating efficiency" framing by Q1 2024. Removed entirely Q1 2025 when the investment posture shifted to AI infrastructure. The quarterly cadence pinpoints the exact quarter this phrase was born and the exact quarter it died — invisible in annual 10-K comparisons.

3. AI From Risk to Identity

AI risk factor went from one paragraph embedded in product risk (Q1 2023) to a multi-page section with "superintelligence" in the investment sentence, "products liability" in the risk enumeration, and FTC/Congress investigations named (Q3 2025). The word "superintelligence" first appeared in Q2 2025 — a signal not detectable from annual filings.

4. Regulatory Names Accumulate Quarterly

DMA/DSA named in TOC (Q3 2023), UK DMCC added (Q2 2024), EU AI Act added (Q3 2024), UK OSA added (Q3 2025). Each addition represents a new enforcement surface detected 3–9 months before the annual filing would capture it. By Q3 2025, the risk factor TOC named 6 specific laws where Q1 2023 named zero.
