META 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of 12 SEC filings (eleven 10-Qs plus the FY2025 10-K). The value is speed: catching language changes at quarterly cadence that annual 10-K comparisons would miss by 3–9 months.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001326801) | 12 Filings | Q1 2023 – Q2 2026 | Analysis: March 2026, extended June 2026, July 2026; Q2 2024–Q2 2026 re-analyzed sentence by sentence October 2026

Q1 2023 → Q2 2023

Headcount: 77K → 71K | Capex: $30-33B → $27-30B
▶ "Discipline and habits" first appears in Investment Philosophy New

Q1 2023 MDA opens with a cost-focused framing. Q2 2023 introduces a new sentence anchoring the efficiency program as cultural:

"We expect to continue to build on the discipline and habits that we developed in 2022 when we initiated several efforts to increase our efficiency, while still remaining focused on investing in significant opportunities."

Q1 2023 had no equivalent phrase — it led with "We expect our total expenses in 2023 to be driven by headcount-related expenses."

▶ "Board Chair" replaces "Chairman" Changed

Q1 2023 risk factor TOC:

"...our founder, Chairman, and CEO."

Q2 2023 risk factor TOC:

"...our founder, Board Chair, and CEO."

This title reverted to "Chairman, and Chief Executive Officer (CEO)" from Q1 2024 onward.

▶ RL loss guidance adds "continue to" Changed

Q1 2023 MDA:

"...we expect our RL operating losses to increase in 2023 and beyond."

Q2 2023 MDA:

"...we expect our RL operating losses to continue to increase in 2023 and beyond."

The addition of "continue to" signals losses were already growing, not just projected to grow.

▶ AI paragraph expands from single paragraph to multi-paragraph Signal

Q1 2023 risk factor on AI was one paragraph covering initiatives, competition, and regulation. Q2 2023 splits AI into a standalone risk factor with its own header and adds the sentence:

"We are making significant investments in artificial intelligence (AI) initiatives, including generative AI, to, among other things, recommend relevant unconnected content across our products, enhance our advertising tools, develop new products, and develop new features for existing products."

Q1 2023 had used "develop new product features using generative AI" as a single item. Q2 2023 split this into "develop new products" + "develop new features for existing products" — a broader scope claim.

▶ Capex guidance cut from $30-33B to $27-30B Signal

Q1 2023 MDA:

"We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $30 billion to $33 billion in 2023."

Q2 2023 MDA:

"We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $27 billion to $30 billion in 2023."

A $3B cut at both ends of the range — the efficiency era was pulling back infrastructure spend.

Guidance & MD&A

▶ Ad enforcement admitted to cost revenue, "which may be material"; ad-demand risk now names "our enforcement efforts" New

Shift: ADDED (MD&A); realized: enforcement "adversely affect[s] our revenue" (+ disclosure); degree added to the risk factor: "and could be material"

Q3 2024: "Our ongoing efforts to enhance enforcement against ads and marketers which violate our advertising policies adversely affect our revenue, and we expect that the continued enhancement of such efforts will have an impact on our revenue in the future, which may be material."
Q2 2024: "The occurrence of any of these or other factors in the future could result in a reduction in demand for our ads, which may reduce the prices we receive for our ads, or cause marketers to stop advertising with us altogether, either of which would negatively affect our revenue and financial results."
Q3 2024: "The occurrence of any of these or other factors in the future, including our enforcement efforts, could result in a reduction in marketer spend or demand for our ads, which may reduce the prices we receive for our ads, could cause marketers to stop advertising with us altogether, or could otherwise negatively affect our revenue and financial results, and could be material."

History (phrase_history): the MD&A sentence and "including our enforcement efforts" both first appear in Q3 2024 and are in every filing since (8 of 8 through Q2 2026); the old risk-factor sentence had stood unchanged for 5 filings (since Q2 2023).

Meta concedes that part of its revenue comes from ads that break its own policies and that cleaning them up may cost a material amount. It arrives in the same filing as the CFPB's warning on financial-product ads (below), and it never quantifies the amount.

▶ Capex floor raised $37B → $38B; 2025 growth hedge "currently expect" dropped for "continue to expect significant capital expenditures growth" Signal

Shift: guidance figures: floor $37B → $38B, ceiling held at $40B (+); hedge dropped: "currently" (+); the purpose clause "to support our AI research and product development efforts" removed (−)

Q2 2024: "We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $37 billion to $40 billion in 2024."
Q2 2024: "We currently expect our capital expenditures to grow significantly in 2025 as we invest to support our AI research and product development efforts."
Q3 2024: "We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $38 billion to $40 billion in 2024 and continue to expect significant capital expenditures growth in 2025."

History (phrase_history): "currently expect our capital expenditures to grow significantly" in Q2 2024 only; "significant capital expenditures growth in 2025" and the $38-40B range in Q3 2024 only. The FY2024 10-K then guided 2025 to $60-65B.

The 2025 signal moves into the guidance sentence itself and loses its hedge. Nine-month capex including finance lease principal was $24.39B, so the $38B floor implies at least about $13.6B in Q4 (computed), the largest quarter of the year.

▶ RL revenue driver de-named: "Meta Quest sales" → "consumer hardware products sold" Changed

Shift: name dropped: Meta Quest (de-named, −)

Q2 2024: "The increases in RL revenue were driven by a net increase in the volume of Meta Quest sales."

History (phrase_history): "consumer hardware products sold" appears in Q3 2024 and the FY2024 10-K only; "Meta Quest sales" returns in Q1 2025 and is in every filing since.

RL revenue grew 29%. With Ray-Ban Meta named beside Quest in the segment description, the generic wording is consistent with glasses, not headsets, driving the quarter, but the filing does not say so.

Risk Factors

▶ Policy failures restated as happening: "We may also be unsuccessful" → "From time to time we are unsuccessful" Signal

Shift: certainty: may → are (+); realized: hypothetical → actual (signals rank 1, score 8.8); hedge added: "from time to time"

Q2 2024: "We may also be unsuccessful in our efforts to enforce our policies or otherwise prevent or remediate any such incidents."
Q3 2024: "From time to time we are unsuccessful in our efforts to enforce our policies or otherwise prevent or remediate any such incidents."

History (phrase_history): the "may" sentence stood in every filing from Q2 2023 to Q2 2024 (unchanged for 4 periods by the signals count); the realized wording first appears here and is in every filing since.

The "incidents" are the list just before it: election interference, child exploitation, illicit drugs, scams and other illegal activity. Meta now states that its enforcement fails on these, in the same filing that admits ad enforcement costs revenue. Both later underpin the youth and scam litigation.

▶ Youth laws named (Arkansas, Utah, Texas, California, Florida) with a compliance warning: "we may not be able to comply with these statutes by their respective effective dates" New

Shift: ADDED; named: +Arkansas, Utah, Texas, California, Florida; condition: "Should enforcement... not be enjoined"

Q3 2024: "For example, several U.S. states, including Arkansas, Utah, Texas, California, and Florida, among others, have passed laws restricting our ability to offer services to minors without parental consent or otherwise limiting the services that we can provide to minors."
Q3 2024: "Should enforcement of one or more of these statutes not be enjoined, we may not be able to comply with these statutes by their respective effective dates."

History (phrase_history): "Arkansas, Utah, Texas, California, and Florida" runs Q3 2024 to Q1 2025; "may not be able to comply with these statutes" appears in Q3 2024 only. "youth, safety, consumer protection, advertising" enters the summary bullet in the same filing and stays through Q2 2026.

A company rarely says it may be unable to comply with a law on time. The warning lasted one quarter; by Q2 2025 the youth paragraph had moved to "We are also subject to new legislation... that restrict" our services to minors.

▶ EU AI Act named in the risk-factor summary; "youth, safety... advertising" added to the law categories New

Shift: named: +Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act); scope widened: +youth, +safety, +advertising

Q2 2024: "...complex and evolving U.S. and foreign privacy, data use, data combination, data protection, content, competition, consumer protection, and other laws and regulations, including the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Digital Markets Act (DMA), Digital Services Act (DSA), and the UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumer Act (DMCC);"
Q3 2024: "...complex and evolving U.S. and foreign privacy, data use, data combination, data protection, content, competition, youth, safety, consumer protection, advertising, and other laws and regulations, including the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Digital Markets Act (DMA), Digital Services Act (DSA), Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act)..."

History (phrase_history): "Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act)" first appears in Q3 2024 and is in every filing since; the EU AI Act was already discussed in the risk-factor body from Q1 2024.

The summary bullet is the list Meta puts in front of every reader. Youth and advertising enter it in the quarter the youth-law and ad-enforcement disclosures appear, and the AI Act joins as the first AI-specific law named there.

▶ AI deployment made dependent on "computing power, and related energy requirements," not just hardware and network capacity Changed

Shift: named: +computing power, +related energy requirements, +"technical" infrastructure; scope widened

Q2 2024: "Further, our ability to continue to develop and effectively deploy AI technologies is dependent on access to specific third-party equipment and other physical infrastructure, such as processing hardware and network capacity, as to which we cannot control the availability or pricing, especially in a highly competitive environment."
Q3 2024: "Further, our ability to continue to develop and effectively deploy AI technologies is dependent on access to specific third-party equipment and other technical and physical infrastructure, such as processing hardware, network capacity, computing power, and related energy requirements, as to which we cannot control the availability or pricing, especially in a highly competitive environment."

History (phrase_history): the new AI wording first appears in Q3 2024 and is in every filing since. "related energy requirements" was already in the general infrastructure risk from Q1 2024; Q3 2024 extends it to the AI-dependency sentence (the earlier version of this page misquoted this sentence and dated energy to Q3).

Power joins chips and networks as an input Meta says it cannot control on price or availability. By Q2 2026 the same risk factor adds local opposition to data-center construction.

Risk Factors

Shift: ADDED; quant added: EUR €200 million; certainty: "we expect we will need" (+); date given: Q3 2025

Themes

1. Capex as Leading Indicator

Capex guidance changed in every quarter of the window. The range went from $30-33B (Q1 2023) to $70-72B (Q3 2025), then to $115-135B in the FY2025 10-K, $125-145B in Q1 2026, and $130-145B in Q2 2026. Annual comparisons would miss the intra-year cuts (Q1-Q2 2023), the sequential floor raises (Q2-Q3 2024, Q1-Q2 2026), and the forward-year signals that appeared mid-cycle. Actual quarterly capex including finance leases reached $31.08B in Q2 2026, against $6.76B in Q3 2023, roughly equal to that quarter's ~$31.86B of operating cash flow (computed), so the build is now funded at the margin by debt.

2. "Discipline and Habits" Had a 7-Quarter Lifecycle

First appeared Q2 2023 as an efficiency mantra. Refined to "operating efficiency" framing by Q1 2024. Removed entirely Q1 2025 when the investment posture shifted to AI infrastructure. The quarterly cadence pinpoints the exact quarter this phrase was born and the exact quarter it died — invisible in annual 10-K comparisons. The successor sentence added "we also continue to evaluate our workforce" in Q1 2026 and carried over verbatim into Q2 2026, the quarter that disclosed a ~8,000-person reduction and $1.18B of severance.

3. AI From Risk to Identity to Balance Sheet

The AI risk factor went from one paragraph embedded in product risk (Q1 2023) to a multi-page section with "superintelligence" (Q2 2025), "products liability" and named FTC/Congress investigations (Q3 2025), "frontier AI models" and "model weights" (FY2025 10-K), agentic-AI attack framing (Q1 2026), and "model extraction or theft" plus regulation of "the release of frontier AI models" (Q2 2026). Over the same period AI moved into the financing disclosures: third-party cloud capacity became the largest named commitment category (Q3 2025), and Q2 2026 added the first risk-factor sentence tying indebtedness and lease obligations to funding these investments.

4. Regulatory Names Accumulate Quarterly

DMA/DSA named in the risk-factor summary (Q3 2023), UK DMCC added (Q2 2024), EU AI Act added (Q3 2024), UK OSA added (Q3 2025). By Q3 2025 the summary bullet named 6 specific laws where Q1 2023 named zero, and it has held at 6 through Q2 2026. Accumulation continued in the body instead: the DOJ Bulk Data Rule, "various U.S. state AI laws," and Nebraska were added in Q2 2026, and the EU AI Act moved into a risk-factor heading. Each addition is a new enforcement surface detected 3–9 months before the annual filing would capture it. The verbs moved too: enforcement failures became "From time to time we are unsuccessful" (Q3 2024), and scam and youth rules went from "may also subject us" and "may restrict" to "we are also, and expect to continue to be, subject" and "that restrict" (Q2 2025).
