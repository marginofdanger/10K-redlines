Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of 12 SEC filings (eleven 10-Qs plus the FY2025 10-K). The value is speed: catching language changes at quarterly cadence that annual 10-K comparisons would miss by 3–9 months.

▶ "Discipline and habits" first appears in Investment Philosophy New

Q1 2023 MDA opens with a cost-focused framing. Q2 2023 introduces a new sentence anchoring the efficiency program as cultural:

"We expect to continue to build on the discipline and habits that we developed in 2022 when we initiated several efforts to increase our efficiency, while still remaining focused on investing in significant opportunities."

Q1 2023 had no equivalent phrase — it led with "We expect our total expenses in 2023 to be driven by headcount-related expenses."

"...our founder, Chairman, and CEO."

"...our founder, Board Chair, and CEO."

This title reverted to "Chairman, and Chief Executive Officer (CEO)" from Q1 2024 onward.

▶ RL loss guidance adds "continue to" Changed

Q1 2023 MDA:

"...we expect our RL operating losses to increase in 2023 and beyond."

"...we expect our RL operating losses to continue to increase in 2023 and beyond."

The addition of "continue to" signals losses were already growing, not just projected to grow.

▶ AI paragraph expands from single paragraph to multi-paragraph Signal

Q1 2023 risk factor on AI was one paragraph covering initiatives, competition, and regulation. Q2 2023 splits AI into a standalone risk factor with its own header and adds the sentence:

Q1 2023 had used "develop new product features using generative AI" as a single item. Q2 2023 split this into "develop new products" + "develop new features for existing products" — a broader scope claim.

Guidance & MD&A

▶ 2026 capex guided $115-135B; attribution flips to "AI efforts and core business" Signal

Q3 2025 MDA:

"We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $70 billion to $72 billion in 2025 to support our core business and AI efforts, and expect significant capital expenditures growth in 2026."

FY2025 10-K MDA:

"We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $115 billion to $135 billion in 2026 to support our AI efforts and core business."

The midpoint (~$125B) is roughly 1.7x FY2025 actual capex of $72.22B. The attribution word order also flips: AI is listed ahead of the core business for the first time since "generative AI" entered the capex sentence in the FY2024 10-K.

▶ RL loss guidance pivots to "remain similar to 2025" — first non-increase guidance Changed

Q3 2025 MDA:

"...we continue to expect our full-year RL operating losses to increase in 2025."

FY2025 10-K MDA:

"In 2025, our RL segment reduced our overall operating profit by approximately $19.19 billion, and we expect our 2026 RL operating losses to remain similar to 2025."

Every filing since Q1 2023 had guided RL losses upward ("increase," "continue to increase," "increase meaningfully"). This is the first flat guidance in the dataset, issued after a $19.19B FY2025 RL operating loss.

▶ Users dipped, ad load rose: first disclosed sequential DAP decline (Iran, Russia) while impressions grew 19%, now credited partly to "frequency of ads shown" Signal
"For example, in the first quarter of 2026, we experienced a slight decline on a quarter-over-quarter basis in the total number of Family daily active people that was driven by internet disruptions in Iran, as well as a restriction on access to WhatsApp in Russia."

Themes

1. Capex as Leading Indicator

Capex guidance changed in every quarter of the window. The range went from $30-33B (Q1 2023) to $70-72B (Q3 2025), then to $115-135B in the FY2025 10-K, $125-145B in Q1 2026, and $130-145B in Q2 2026. Annual comparisons would miss the intra-year cuts (Q1-Q2 2023), the sequential floor raises (Q2-Q3 2024, Q1-Q2 2026), and the forward-year signals that appeared mid-cycle. Actual quarterly capex including finance leases reached $31.08B in Q2 2026, against $6.76B in Q3 2023, roughly equal to that quarter's ~$31.86B of operating cash flow (computed), so the build is now funded at the margin by debt.

2. "Discipline and Habits" Had a 7-Quarter Lifecycle

First appeared Q2 2023 as an efficiency mantra. Refined to "operating efficiency" framing by Q1 2024. Removed entirely Q1 2025 when the investment posture shifted to AI infrastructure. The quarterly cadence pinpoints the exact quarter this phrase was born and the exact quarter it died — invisible in annual 10-K comparisons. The successor sentence added "we also continue to evaluate our workforce" in Q1 2026 and carried over verbatim into Q2 2026, the quarter that disclosed a ~8,000-person reduction and $1.18B of severance.

3. AI From Risk to Identity to Balance Sheet

The AI risk factor went from one paragraph embedded in product risk (Q1 2023) to a multi-page section with "superintelligence" (Q2 2025), "products liability" and named FTC/Congress investigations (Q3 2025), "frontier AI models" and "model weights" (FY2025 10-K), agentic-AI attack framing (Q1 2026), and "model extraction or theft" plus regulation of "the release of frontier AI models" (Q2 2026). Over the same period AI moved into the financing disclosures: third-party cloud capacity became the largest named commitment category (Q3 2025), and Q2 2026 added the first risk-factor sentence tying indebtedness and lease obligations to funding these investments.

4. Regulatory Names Accumulate Quarterly

DMA/DSA named in the risk-factor summary (Q3 2023), UK DMCC added (Q2 2024), EU AI Act added (Q3 2024), UK OSA added (Q3 2025). By Q3 2025 the summary bullet named 6 specific laws where Q1 2023 named zero, and it has held at 6 through Q2 2026. Accumulation continued in the body instead: the DOJ Bulk Data Rule, "various U.S. state AI laws," and Nebraska were added in Q2 2026, and the EU AI Act moved into a risk-factor heading. Each addition is a new enforcement surface detected 3–9 months before the annual filing would capture it.
