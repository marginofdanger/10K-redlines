Meta Platforms 10-K Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of SEC filings reveals strategic pivots, expense signals, and forward guidance tells invisible to surface-level reading.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001326801) | Fiscal Years 2020-2025 | Analysis: March 2026; FY2023→FY2025 transitions re-analyzed October 2026
FY2020 → FY2021

The Metaverse Pivot

Facebook → Meta. Two-segment reporting. "Metaverse" embedded in mission. RL losses disclosed at $10.2B. Apple ATT realized. TikTok named.

FY2021 → FY2022

The Reckoning

First-ever revenue decline. "Growth" → "scale." First debt ($10B). $4.6B restructuring. "Discovery engine" and "generative AI" first appear.

FY2022 → FY2023

The Turnaround

Year of Efficiency: headcount -22%, margin +10pp. AI = #1 priority. First dividend. China advertisers disclosed. "Discipline and habits."

FY2023 → FY2024

AI Infrastructure Era

Mission rewritten. "Metaverse" renamed "next computing platform" (RL split relabeled, not moved). AI joins RL as a named margin drag. CapEx $60-65B guided. Ad price −9% → +10%. Layoffs recast as "from time to time." FTC trial set.

FY2024 → FY2025

The Superintelligence Bet

Superintelligence as stated goal; AI strategy gets the "or at all" failure clause. CapEx $72B actual, $115-135B guided; FCF falls. RL losses guided flat. Buybacks demoted to "any return of capital." FTC won; "hundreds of billions" exposure. Debt doubled to $59B.

FY2020 → FY2021: The Metaverse Pivot

Filed Feb 2022 | Headcount: 58,604 → 71,970 (+23%) | Revenue: $86.0B → $117.9B (+37%) | Op Margin: 38% → 40%

Business Overview

▶Mission statement reframed around metaverseNew
FY2020's mission: "to give people the power to build community and bring the world closer together." FY2021 appended:
"All of our products, including our apps, share the vision of helping to bring the metaverse to life."
The entire company reframed around a new strategic concept in a single sentence.
▶Two-segment reporting (FoA / Reality Labs) introducedNew
Products reorganized under "Family of Apps" and "Reality Labs" with separate financials. First time investors could see FoA operating income ($56.9B, 49% margin) versus RL operating loss ($10.2B). Previously a single segment.
▶European data transfer: "may be unable" escalated to "will likely be unable"Changed
FY2020: "we may be unable to operate material portions of our business in Europe"
FY2021: "we will likely be unable to offer a number of our most significant products and services, including Facebook and Instagram, in Europe"
▶IGTV removed, Reels elevated; Digital Markets Act disclosed; remote work codifiedSignal
Instagram dropped "IGTV" from its product listing. DMA disclosed as "proposed" regulation for the first time. Remote work policy formalized: "We offer most of our full-time employees the option to work remotely on a regular basis."

Risk Factors

▶New standalone risk: "We may not be successful in our metaverse strategy"New
Acknowledged the metaverse bet may fail and "may also divert resources and management attention from other areas of our business." RL losses quantified at "$10 billion" in risk factors for the first time.
▶Apple ATT: prospective → past tense ("has negatively impacted")Changed
FY2020: Apple "plans to make certain changes... that will reduce"
FY2021: Apple "made certain changes... which has negatively impacted, and we expect will continue to negatively impact"
Specific attribution added: "our advertising revenue in the second half of 2021 was negatively impacted."
▶TikTok explicitly named; first-ever global DAU decline; Haugen whistleblowerNew
TikTok named as having "reduced some users' engagement" (previously only "ByteDance"). Q4 2021: first-ever global quarter-over-quarter DAU decline. Frances Haugen spawned new "user well-being" language, government investigations, and securities class actions.
▶Reels monetization gap; climate risk; "opt in or out" replaces "opt out"New
"We plan to continue to promote Reels, which we do not currently monetize at the same rate as our feed or Stories products." Climate change risk added. "Opt out" updated to "opt in or out" reflecting Apple's ATT opt-in framework.

Legal Proceedings

▶FTC antitrust survived dismissal; whistleblower litigation; IDPC stay liftedSignal
FTC amended complaint sustained January 2022 -- existential divestiture risk for Instagram/WhatsApp remains. Illinois biometrics ($650M) and 2018 cyberattack settlements resolved. New stockholder derivative action. Whistleblower-related securities class actions filed. IDPC data transfer inquiry stay lifted.

Key Forward Guidance

▶Unprecedented bearish margin warning; CapEx guided $29-34B; headcount "foreseeable future"Signal
"In 2022, we also expect that our year-over-year total expense growth rates may significantly exceed our year-over-year revenue growth rates, which would adversely affect our operating margin and profitability."
This sentence appeared only once in the entire 5-year corpus and proved prophetic. CapEx guided at $29-34B (vs $19B actual). Headcount guidance still: "We expect headcount growth to continue for the foreseeable future."

FY2021 → FY2022: The Reckoning

Filed Feb 2023 | Headcount: 71,970 → 86,482 (+20%, but 11K layoff announced) | Revenue: $117.9B → $116.6B (-1%) | Op Margin: 40% → 25%

Business Overview

▶"Discovery engine" and "generative AI" appear for the first timeNew
FY2022 Technology section: "we are making significant investments in AI and machine learning, including to recommend relevant unconnected content across our products through our AI-powered discovery engine... and to develop new product features using generative AI." This is the earliest filing signal of the AI pivot.
▶All named competitors removed from Business sectionRemoved
FY2021 named 8 competitors (Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, ByteDance/TikTok, Microsoft, Snap, Tencent, Twitter). FY2022 deleted the entire list. Coincides with FTC antitrust discovery -- naming competitors defines relevant markets.
▶"Expect headcount growth to continue" → 11,000-employee layoff disclosureChanged
FY2021: "We expect headcount growth to continue for the foreseeable future." FY2022 replaced this with detailed layoff disclosure -- "one of the most difficult changes in Meta's history" -- including severance terms (16 weeks + 2 per year of service). "Growth" replaced by "scale" throughout the filing.
▶FoA/RL cost split disclosed; Portal killed; DEI goals removed; "Feed" replaces "News Feed"Signal
First-ever FoA/RL cost split (82%/18%) with admission: "our ability to support our metaverse efforts is dependent on generating sufficient profits from other areas of our business." Portal removed from product listings. Specific diversity targets deleted. "News Feed" became simply "Feed"; Reels moved to 2nd position.

Risk Factors

▶First-ever debt ($10B) as risk; "financial results will fluctuate" elevated to standaloneNew
Meta went from zero debt to $10B in fixed-rate notes. New risk: "the incurrence of indebtedness." "Financial results will fluctuate" elevated to its own risk factor -- the era of predictable growth officially declared over.
▶Cost-cutting acknowledged as risk; Apple accused; DMA/DSA enacted; Section 230 SCOTUSChanged
"Cost reduction measures... may adversely affect these or other business initiatives." Apple described as "expanding their own advertising business" while restricting others. DMA/DSA shifted from "proposed" to enacted. Section 230 Supreme Court review disclosed. Russia/Ukraine war added.
▶Risk ordering: Competition now #1 (was COVID); COVID dropped to bottomSignal
The first operational risk factor in FY2021 was COVID. FY2022 moved Competition to #1 and pushed COVID to near the bottom. The top-3 operational risks became: (1) Competition, (2) Metaverse strategy [new], (3) Financial fluctuations.

Legal Proceedings

▶$725M Cambridge Analytica; social media addiction lawsuits; EU Marketplace tyingNew
$725M Cambridge Analytica settlement (largest consumer privacy settlement at the time). Social media addiction lawsuits (new category): alleging "addiction" in teenagers. FTC challenge to Within Unlimited acquisition. European Commission Marketplace tying complaint. Texas biometrics lawsuit. Ad audience inflation class certified. Q4 2021 earnings securities class action filed.

Key Forward Guidance

▶CapEx guidance $30-33B ("anticipate" replaces "expect"); no total expense range; restructuring $4.6BSignal
Modal verb shifted from "expect" to "anticipate" (weaker commitment). Total expense dollar range eliminated for the first time -- replaced by qualitative warning: "investments will continue to drive expense growth in 2023, which will adversely affect our operating margin." Restructuring section (new): $4.61B in charges across facilities ($2.30B), severance ($975M), data center assets ($1.34B).

FY2022 → FY2023: The Turnaround

Filed Feb 2024 | Headcount: 86,482 → 67,317 (-22%) | Revenue: $116.6B → $134.9B (+16%) | Op Margin: 25% → 35%

Business Overview

▶AI elevated to strategic centerpiece; six investment priorities named for first timeNew
New sentence:
"Across our work, we are innovating in artificial intelligence (AI) technologies to build new experiences."
Six explicit priorities: AI, metaverse, discovery engine, monetization, regulatory readiness, developer efficiency. FY2022 had no structured framework. Investment Philosophy rewritten from aspirational to: "We expect to continue to build on the discipline and habits that we developed in 2022."
▶Threads, WhatsApp Channels, "Subscription for no ads" in EUNew
Three major disclosures: Threads (Twitter competitor), WhatsApp Channels (broadcast messaging), and "subscription for no ads" -- a fundamental change to the European business model where Meta changed its legal basis for behavioral advertising from "legitimate interests" to "consent."
▶Metaverse stripped from RL segment definition; Ray-Ban glasses get Meta AIChanged
FY2022: "RL reflects our efforts to develop the metaverse and generates revenue..."
FY2023: "RL generates revenue from sales of consumer hardware products..."
Ray-Ban upgraded from "Stories" (no AI) to featuring "Meta AI, our advanced conversational assistant."
▶Headcount -22%; "Equity" dropped from DEI heading; COVID language removed; Shops dropped from InstagramSignal
Headcount declined from 86,482 to 67,317. "Diversity, Equity and Inclusion" renamed "Diverse and Inclusive Workplace." Specific ethnicity breakdowns replaced with broad "underrepresented people" percentages. COVID language almost entirely removed. Instagram dropped "Shops" and "Video" from its feature list. Horizon Workrooms dropped.

Risk Factors

▶Entirely new AI risk factor (7 paragraphs) -- most significant addition in the filingNew
Covers: harmful content, misinformation, bias, IP infringement, open-source risks (Llama), hardware dependency ("processing hardware and network capacity, as to which we cannot control the availability or pricing"), and regulatory uncertainty. Did not exist in any form in FY2022.
▶Zuckerberg key-person risk: "combat sports, extreme sports, recreational aviation"New
"Mr. Zuckerberg and certain other members of management participate in various high-risk activities, such as combat sports, extreme sports, and recreational aviation, which carry the risk of serious injury and death."
Unusual and specific enough to suggest legal/insurance counsel required it.
▶COVID standalone risk eliminated; infrastructure risk expanded for AI compute; EUR 1.2B IDPC fineChanged
COVID absorbed into general "catastrophic events" risk. Infrastructure risk adds "availability of components, power, or network capacity." "Platform Integrity" added to risk category name. "Costs are continuing to grow" softened to "We incur significant expenses." EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework adopted but CJEU invalidation warning added. EUR 1.2B IDPC fine disclosed.

Legal Proceedings

▶Section restructured with new categories; FTC administrative proceeding; AI copyright suitsNew
Legal Proceedings reorganized into labeled categories: Privacy, Competition, Securities, Youth-Related (new), Other. Opening preamble: "the number and potential significance of the litigation and investigations involving the company have increased." FTC proceeding seeking to prohibit minors' data for commercial purposes. AI copyright litigation (Kadrey v. Meta, Chabon v. Meta). Multi-state AG youth lawsuits expanded. $725M Cambridge Analytica settlement paid.

Key Forward Guidance

▶RL losses "increase meaningfully in 2024"; first dividend; FB metrics discontinued; China advertisers disclosedSignal
RL: "we expect our RL operating losses to increase meaningfully in 2024" -- the intensifier was deliberately strong. Dividend: First-ever cash dividend ($0.50/quarter). Metrics: "we will no longer report DAUs, MAUs, ARPU, and MAP" -- major transparency reduction. Revenue: Growth "driven mainly by marketer spending in online commerce, which benefited from marketers based in China" -- Temu/Shein implied. CapEx guided $30-37B ($7B range = widest ever, peak uncertainty).

FY2023 → FY2024: The Metaverse Gets Renamed

Filed Jan 2025 | Revenue: $134.90B → $164.50B (+22%) | Op Income: $46.75B → $69.38B (margin 35% → 42%) | CapEx incl. finance leases: $28.10B → $39.23B; 2025 guided $60-65B | Headcount: 67,317 → 74,067 (+10%)

Business Overview

▶Mission rewritten: "give people the power to build community" → "build the future of human connection and the technology"Changed
FY2023: "Our mission is to give people the power to build community and bring the world closer together."
FY2024: "Our mission is to build the future of human connection and the technology that makes it possible."
The mission had been unchanged in every filing from FY2020 to FY2023 in this dataset. The subject flips from what users are empowered to do to what Meta builds, and "the technology that makes it possible" makes hardware and AI part of the mission itself. The same sentence was rewritten in the MD&A.
▶"Metaverse" renamed "next computing platform"; RL budget split relabeled, not moved (AR 50% → "wearables" 50%; VR 40% + social 10% → "metaverse" 50%)Changed
FY2023: "...toward immersive experiences like augmented and virtual reality to help build the metaverse, which we believe is the next evolution in social technology."
FY2024: "...toward immersive experiences like augmented, mixed, and virtual reality to help build the next computing platform."
The opening sentence "All of our products, including our apps, share the vision of helping to bring the metaverse to life" was deleted, and the risk factor heading changed from "metaverse strategy" to "Reality Labs strategy." The forward spending split changed its labels but not its proportions:
FY2023: "...approximately 50% of our Reality Labs operating expenses on our AR initiatives, approximately 40% on our VR (including MR) initiatives, and approximately 10% on social platforms and other initiatives."
FY2024: "...approximately 50% of our Reality Labs operating expenses on our wearables initiatives, and the remaining 50% on our metaverse initiatives."
AR became "wearables" and VR plus social platforms were merged into "metaverse," so 2025 spending stayed where it was for 2024. The word "metaverse" was narrowed from the company's whole vision to the VR half of one segment. The real reallocation came a year later (FY2025: 70/30).
▶Investment priorities: "regulatory readiness" and "developer efficiency" dropped; "infrastructure capacity" and Threads added; Llama open-source stance statedChanged
FY2023: "In 2024, we intend to focus on six key investment areas: AI, the metaverse, our discovery engine, monetization of our products and services, regulatory readiness, and enhancing developer efficiency to build, iterate, and optimize products quickly."
FY2024: "In 2025, we intend to focus on several key investment areas: generative AI, our discovery engine, the metaverse and wearables, Threads, monetization of our products and services, platform integrity and community support, and infrastructure capacity."
Infrastructure capacity is a named priority for the first time, in the same filing that guided 2025 capex to $60-65B. The count changes from "six" to "several." A new paragraph sets out the open-source position that FY2025 would later qualify: "We also believe in the power of open source innovation and have released our Llama models." Meta AI is named as an assistant "available across our apps, on Ray-Ban Meta AI glasses and on the web."
▶Layoffs recast from a dated event ("Beginning in November 2022") to standing practice ("From time to time"); severance terms and diversity data deletedChanged
FY2023: "Beginning in November 2022, we took a number of steps to reduce our expense base."
FY2024: "From time to time, we implement organizational changes to pursue greater efficiency and realign our business and strategic priorities."
The specific severance terms ("severance of 16 weeks of base pay plus two additional weeks for every year of service") became "severance payments." In Risk Factors, two hedges were also deleted: "some members of our management do not have significant experience managing a large global business operation" and the caveat that restructuring efforts might not "achieve our intended results." Taken together, the text no longer presents restructuring as a one-off. The Q2 2026 10-Q went on to disclose a reduction of about 8,000 people. In the same Human Capital section, the "Diverse and Inclusive Workplace" heading and all workforce diversity statistics were removed, and a new sentence says: "In early 2025, we announced changes to our diversity programs in light of the shifting legal and policy landscape." "Political views" was added to the list of valued forms of diversity.

Risk Factors

▶AI joins Reality Labs as a named drag on margins; training-data access and energy added as AI dependenciesChanged
FY2023: "We incur significant expenses in operating our business, and some of our investments, particularly our investments in Reality Labs, have the effect of reducing our operating margin and profitability."
FY2024: "We incur significant expenses in operating our business, and some of our investments, particularly our investments in our artificial intelligence initiatives as well as Reality Labs, have the effect of reducing our operating margin and profitability."
The AI investment paragraph gains a failure clause, "If our investments are not successful longer-term, our business and financial performance could be harmed," and two new dependencies: "Our AI initiatives also depend on our access to data to effectively train our models" and access to "computing power, and related energy requirements." Until FY2024, Reality Labs was the only investment the filing named as hurting margins. AI is now named alongside it, a year before the capex guide doubled again.
▶Enforcement goes two ways: tighter on ads (revenue hit "may be material"), looser on content (January 2025 change flagged as a risk)New
A new MD&A paragraph:
FY2024: "Our ongoing efforts to enhance enforcement against ads and marketers which violate our advertising policies adversely affect our revenue, and we expect that the continued enhancement of such efforts will have an impact on our revenue in the future, which may be material."
The ad-demand risk factor now names enforcement and ends with a materiality clause it did not have before: "including our enforcement efforts, could result in a reduction in marketer spend or demand for our ads... and could be material." Content moderation is moving the opposite way:
FY2024: "We may also be subject to increased risk as a result of changes to our content policies and enforcement efforts which we began to implement in January 2025 to further free expression on our platform and mitigate over-enforcement of certain of our content policies."
This is the first time the filing says ad-policy enforcement costs revenue in amounts that may be material. Read together, the two changes suggest enforcement effort is being moved from content toward advertisers. By FY2025 the content-policy sentence had lost its January 2025 date (it now says "from time to time").
▶China: U.S. government action added to the China-ad-revenue risk; "marketers based in China" dropped from the revenue explanationChanged
FY2023: "...it is possible that the Chinese government could take action that reduces or eliminates our China-based advertising revenue, whether as a result of the trade dispute with the United States..."
FY2024: "...it is possible that the Chinese, United States, or other government could take action that reduces or eliminates our China-based advertising revenue, whether as a result of the trade dispute with the United States, including any tariffs implemented by the United States or China..."
The FY2023 MD&A credited growth to "marketer spending in online commerce, which benefited from marketers based in China." FY2024 says only that "the online commerce vertical was the largest contributor to the increase in advertising revenue." Tariffs appear in the ad-demand risk bullet for the first time ("the implementation of tariffs by the United States, China, or other governments"). Meta named the risk to its China-sourced revenue more specifically in the same filing where it stopped crediting that revenue for growth.
▶EU fix conceded to weaken ads: "less personalized ads... expected to be less relevant and effective"; DMA non-compliance finding disclosedNew
FY2024: "In response to these developments, in November 2024, we began offering users in the European Union, European Economic Area, and Switzerland who elect to continue using our services free-of-charge, supported by ads, an option to see less personalized ads, which are expected to be less relevant and effective than our premium ad offerings."
The DMA proceeding is now dated: formal proceedings opened in March 2024, and preliminary findings issued in July 2024 "reflecting its preliminary view that our model does not comply." This is the first time the filing says the regulatory remedy itself lowers ad quality in Europe. By FY2025, "expected to be" had been deleted, so the ads are simply "less relevant and effective." Youth regulation gets named examples for the first time: "Arkansas, Utah, Texas, California, and Florida," plus Australia's under-16 ban.
▶Facebook-only user metrics retired; duplicate (~10% of MAUs) and false (~4%) account estimates deletedRemoved
FY2023: "In the fourth quarter of 2023, we estimated that duplicate accounts may have represented approximately 10% of our worldwide MAUs."
FY2023: "In the fourth quarter of 2023, we estimated that false accounts may have represented approximately 4% of our worldwide MAUs."
Both sentences, and the whole duplicate/false-account methodology paragraph, are gone along with DAUs, MAUs, ARPU and MAP. The only quality metric left is violating accounts, now measured against DAP: "In the fourth quarter of 2024, we estimated that less than 3% of our worldwide DAP consisted solely of violating accounts." ARPP was redefined as FoA revenue divided by DAP and restated for prior periods. The FY2023 filing had announced the metric change, but readers lost the duplicate-account estimate, the largest quantified caveat on Meta's user counts.

Legal Proceedings

▶Section 230 defense eroding: youth cases "survived motions to dismiss... through the use of products liability theories"; mass arbitration beginsNew
FY2024: "...we are facing numerous cases in the United States in which plaintiffs are attempting to avoid or limit the application of Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act to their claims and certain of those matters have survived motions to dismiss, including through the use of products liability theories."
FY2024: "Beginning in November 2024, counsel for thousands of individual claimants began sending mass arbitration demands relating to "social media addiction" and related harms allegedly caused by Instagram."
Youth plaintiffs now include Brazil, Canada and tribal nations. The European Commission opened DSA proceedings on minors (May 2024) and on elections and deceptive ads (April 2024). This is the legal foundation for the FY2025 "high tens of billions" claims and the 2026 verdicts: the main legal shield has been successfully worked around in court.
▶FTC antitrust trial set for April 14, 2025; EU Marketplace fine EUR 798M; CFPB warns a suit could come "in the near-term"Signal
FY2024: "Trial is set to begin on April 14, 2025."
FY2024: "...imposed a fine of approximately EUR €798 million."
FY2024: "The result of the NORA process is uncertain at this time, but if the Director authorizes an action against us, the CFPB could file a lawsuit in the near-term and seek financial penalties and equitable relief."
Summary judgment was split in November 2024, which sent the Instagram/WhatsApp divestiture case to trial. The 2022 Statement of Objections became a fine. The CFPB paragraph is new; it was deleted without explanation in FY2025. The derivative suit was set for trial on April 2, 2025, with the insider-trading claim surviving only against Mark Zuckerberg.

Key Forward Guidance

▶2025 capex guided $60-65B, ~1.6x the $39.23B spent in 2024, with the first stated purpose; "infrastructure and headcount" moves into MD&ASignal
FY2023: "We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $30 billion to $37 billion in 2024."
FY2024: "We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $60 billion to $65 billion in 2025 to support our core business and generative AI efforts."
This is the first capex guide with a purpose clause, and core business is listed first (FY2025 reversed the order). The MD&A adds a sentence that had previously appeared only in Risk Factors: "In particular, we expect our AI initiatives will require increased investment in infrastructure and headcount." The liquidity sentence now lists "investments in infrastructure and AI initiatives" ahead of "share repurchases and dividend payments" among the uses of cash.
▶RL loss guide drops "meaningfully": loss grew $1.61B (+10%) after +$2.40B (+18%)Changed
FY2023: "...we expect our RL operating losses to increase meaningfully in 2024."
FY2024: "...we continue to expect our RL operating losses to increase in 2025."
The RL loss was $17.73B against $16.12B. Removing the intensifier matched the slowdown in loss growth, and it was the step before FY2025's "remain similar."
▶Ad price turns from −9% to +10% as impression growth slows from 28% to 11%; younger users named as the competitive gapSignal
FY2023: "In 2023, the average price per ad decreased by 9%, as compared with a decrease of 16% in 2022."
FY2024: "The increase in average price per ad in 2024 was driven by an increase in advertising demand, which we believe is mainly due to ongoing improvements to our ad performance from our ad targeting and measurement tools."
The FY2023 sentence that held the price decline against "the improvements to our ad targeting" was deleted, because price itself now carries the story. A new competitive admission: "However, we continue to face competition from other products and services within certain demographics, in particular younger users." Growth came from pricing, not volume, in the same year Meta conceded it was losing younger users to competitors.
▶Accounting tailwinds: server life extended to 5.5 years (~$2.9B less 2025 depreciation); $1.55B legal accrual reversal; contingency wording made two-sidedSignal
FY2024: "In January 2025, we completed an assessment of the useful lives of certain servers and network assets, which resulted in an increase in their estimated useful life to 5.5 years, effective beginning fiscal year 2025."
FY2024: "The decrease was mostly driven by a favorable impact of $1.55 billion in legal-related costs."
The loss-contingency estimate wording changed in the same filing:
FY2023: "...if one or more of these matters were resolved against us for amounts in excess of management's estimates of losses, our results of operations and financial condition... could be materially adversely affected."
FY2024: "...if one or more of these matters were resolved against us for amounts that materially differ from management's estimates of losses, it could have a favorable or unfavorable impact on our results of operations and financial condition..."
The useful-life change is a judgment call that reduces 2025 depreciation as capex nearly doubles. The legal reversal helped G&A fall 15%. The contingency sentence now allows for reversals as well as losses. All three improve reported margins without operating change; FY2025 later dropped the $2.9B quantification.

FY2024 → FY2025: The Superintelligence Bet

Filed Jan 2026 | Revenue: $164.50B → $200.97B (+22%) | Op Income: $69.38B → $83.28B (margin 42% → 41%) | Net Income: $62.36B → $60.46B ($15.93B OBBBA tax charge) | CapEx incl. finance leases: $39.23B → $72.22B; 2026 guided $115-135B | Free cash flow: $52.10B → $43.59B | Headcount: 74,067 → 78,865 (+6%)

Business Overview

▶Superintelligence defined and adopted as the AI goal, with no timeline: "inherently difficult to predict when"New
FY2025: "We are also working to develop the next generation of AI models and advance our vision to build superintelligence, which we define as AI that surpasses human intelligence."
FY2025: "Although it is inherently difficult to predict when superintelligence may be achieved, we are investing now because we believe this has the potential to begin a new era of individual empowerment, where people can direct superintelligence towards what they value in their own lives."
FY2024 did not use the word; FY2025 uses it nine times. It reaches the company overview ("to advance our vision to deliver personal superintelligence for everyone"), the AI investment sentence and the AI risk factors. The justification copies the one used for the metaverse ("We are investing now because we believe...") but has no monetization clause. The RL version promises to "unlock monetization opportunities"; the superintelligence version promises "individual empowerment."
▶Compute demand driver changes: users and engagement are now "historically"; frontier AI models drive "a significant further expansion"Changed
FY2024: "As our user base grows, as engagement with products like video, VR, and MR increases, and as we deepen our investment in new technologies, our computing needs continue to expand."
FY2025: "Historically, the increases in our computing needs have been driven by growth in our user base as well as engagement with products like video and VR. Our increased efforts toward building frontier AI models have driven a significant further expansion in our computing needs and we expect our computing needs to continue to expand as a result."
The infrastructure build is no longer tied to user growth. That matters for reading capex. Spending sized to serve users can be checked against DAP and engagement, but spending sized to train frontier models has no such external check. The competition section adds "the development of frontier AI models" as a field Meta competes in.
▶Open-source commitment qualified: "We have not released everything... combination of open and closed models"Changed
FY2024: "We also believe in the power of open source innovation and have released our Llama models, consisting of state-of-the-art foundation models that are available for researchers and developers to use and build upon."
FY2025: "We have not released everything we have developed historically and expect to continue training a combination of open and closed models going forward."
The FY2025 paragraph opens with "We have a history of open-sourcing in AI," which puts open source in the past tense. The Llama paragraph was one year old. Closed models are now the stated plan, which fits the shift to frontier-model compute described above.
▶RL spending actually reallocated this time: wearables 50% → 70%; "metaverse" 50% → "VR and Horizon" 30%; EMG wristband shipsChanged
FY2024: "For example, in 2025, we expect to spend approximately 50% of our Reality Labs operating expenses on our wearables initiatives, and the remaining 50% on our metaverse initiatives."
FY2025: "In 2026, we expect to spend approximately 70% of our Reality Labs operating expenses on our wearables initiatives, and the remaining 30% on our VR and Horizon initiatives."
FY2024 only relabeled the split (see the FY2024 transition). FY2025 moves 20 points of the RL budget. The surviving VR bucket is no longer called "metaverse." The statement that wearables are "primarily directed toward longer-term research" was deleted, and the neural interface listed in FY2024 as exploratory research now ships: "It comes with the Meta Neural Band, which is a wrist-worn wearable device that uses electromyography to let people control their AI glasses using neuromuscular signals." "Mixed reality" was dropped throughout.
▶Priorities: "the metaverse" and Threads drop out; Reels and youth added; Threads and WhatsApp named as ad surfacesChanged
FY2024: "In 2025, we intend to focus on several key investment areas: generative AI, our discovery engine, the metaverse and wearables, Threads, monetization of our products and services, platform integrity and community support, and infrastructure capacity."
FY2025: "In 2026, we intend to focus on several key investment areas: AI, Reels and our discovery engine, wearables, monetization of our products and services, youth, platform integrity and community support, and infrastructure capacity."
FY2025: "Marketers purchase ads that can appear in multiple places including on Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, Threads, and WhatsApp, as well as third-party applications and websites."

The Superintelligence Bet

FY2025 defines superintelligence as "AI that surpasses human intelligence," names "personal superintelligence for everyone" as the vision, and says compute demand is now driven by frontier-model training rather than user growth. The capital behind it is large and partly off-balance-sheet: $115-135B of 2026 capex guidance, $131.05B of contractual commitments, $103.77B of unstarted leases, and debt doubled to $59B. The same filing hedges the bet in the language it once used for the metaverse ("our AI strategy and investments may not be successful in the foreseeable future, or at all"), gives no timeline ("inherently difficult to predict when"), and moves from open source to "a combination of open and closed models."
