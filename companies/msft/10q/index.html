MSFT 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of 11 SEC filings: eight 10-Qs plus the FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026 10-Ks (Microsoft's fiscal year ends June 30). The annual dashboard follows the argument year to year; this scanner shows the quarter each change arrived in: AI as the stated cause of a six-point cloud-margin decline and then its removal, leases and supplier financing that grew ahead of capex, a backlog that lengthened, OpenAI's path from an unquantified line to 10.8% of revenue, and the Q1 FY2027 two-segment reset.

Risk Factors

▶ Cloud-margin warning drops its benchmark: "reduce the operating margins we have previously achieved" → "reduce the operating margins" Changed

Shift: benchmark dropped: "we have previously achieved" (−); degree: costs → "significant costs" (+); heading adds "and AI"

Q3 FY2024: "Besides software development costs, we are incurring costs to build and maintain infrastructure to support cloud computing and AI services. These costs will reduce the operating margins we have previously achieved."
FY2024 10-K: "We are incurring significant costs to build and maintain infrastructure to support cloud computing and AI services. These costs will reduce the operating margins."

History (phrase_history): "operating margins we have previously achieved" appears in the FY2023 10-K and the Q3 FY2024 10-Q and in no later filing; "These costs will reduce the operating margins" then runs from the FY2024 10-K to Q3 FY2025 before the FY2025 10-K turns it into "reducing operating margins".

The March 10-Q still measured cloud margins against the pre-cloud past. The 10-K drops the comparison, calls the costs "significant" and retitles the risk "Our focus on cloud-based and AI services presents execution and competitive risks." Microsoft Cloud gross margin was 72% in the March quarter and 71% for the year. The quarterly record shows the change was made in the 10-K, not in a 10-Q.

▶ New-product margins softened: "will not be as high as the margins we have experienced historically" → "may not achieve operating margins as high" Changed

Shift: certainty: will not → may not (−); condition dropped: "even if they are profitable"

Q3 FY2024: "New products and services may not be profitable, and even if they are profitable, operating margins for some new products and businesses will not be as high as the margins we have experienced historically."
FY2024 10-K: "New products and services may not be profitable or may not achieve operating margins as high as we have experienced historically."

History (phrase_history): the "will not be as high" sentence appears in the FY2023 10-K and Q3 FY2024; the "may not achieve operating margins as high" version runs unchanged in all ten filings from the FY2024 10-K to Q1 FY2027.

The old sentence said as a fact that some new businesses would earn lower margins; the new one makes it a possibility. The same rewrite turned weaker PC demand from "our revenue will be adversely affected" into "our results of operations may be adversely affected". Both soften in the filing that drops "margins we have previously achieved", while the cloud margin was falling a point.

▶ "Ubiquitous computing and ambient intelligence" strategy and the IoT risk deleted; the section is rebuilt around AI services Removed

Shift: deletion: strategic-vision sentence and IoT sub-risk (−); de-named: Azure IoT Edge

Q3 FY2024: "Our strategic vision is to compete and grow by building best-in-class platforms and productivity services that utilize ubiquitous computing and ambient intelligence to drive insights and productivity gains."
Q3 FY2024: "Our success in driving ubiquitous computing and ambient intelligence will depend on the level of adoption of our offerings such as Azure, Azure AI, and Azure IoT Edge."
FY2024 10-K: "Whether we succeed in cloud-based and AI services depends on our execution in several areas, including:"

History (phrase_history): "ubiquitous computing and ambient intelligence" appears in the FY2023 10-K and Q3 FY2024 only; "The development of the IoT presents security, privacy, and execution risks" is also gone from the FY2024 10-K on.

The cloud risk factor had been written around the "intelligent cloud and intelligent edge" strategy. The 10-K deletes the vision statement, the IoT paragraph and the product list, and frames success as execution in "cloud-based and AI services". The rewrite also removes named products from the R&D sentence ("including the Windows operating system, Microsoft 365, Bing, SQL Server...").

Risk Factors & Contingencies

▶ Unpatched old equipment admitted as a way in: patch failures "have and may continue to result in unauthorized access" New

Shift: realized: added in the present perfect (+); named: "legacy network equipment"

Q1 FY2025: "Challenges or failures in applying current security patches to all hardware and devices connected to our systems, including legacy network equipment, have and may continue to result in unauthorized access to our IT systems and data in the future."

History (phrase_history): "legacy network equipment" appears in Q1 FY2025 only; from Q2 FY2025 the sentence reads "including end-of-life and end-of-support equipment" and runs through Q1 FY2027, where "have and may continue" becomes "have resulted and may continue".

The same section already described the Midnight Blizzard breach, which began with "a legacy test account". The new sentence goes further: gaps in patching Microsoft's own older equipment "have" let attackers into its systems. It is an admission about Microsoft's infrastructure, not its customers'.

Themes

1. AI Moved From the Margin Explanation to a Mix Effect

The Microsoft Cloud gross margin fell from 72% (Q3 FY2024) to 66% (Q3 FY2026) and held there. AI infrastructure was first an item inside an improving margin (FY2024), then the stated cause of each decline from Q1 FY2025, joined by AI usage in Q1 FY2026. Q1 FY2027 removes AI from both margin sentences: the decline is now "sales mix shift to Azure", Azure is said to be improving its own margin, and Azure has been redefined to include "GPU infrastructure-as-a-service". The number did not recover; the explanation changed. The same filing restates AI regulation and datacenter permitting as costs already being incurred.

2. The Build-Out Showed Up in Commitments Before Capex

Unstarted leases fell four filings in a row to $92.7B (FY2025), then rose to $364.6B in five, with a new condition clause from the FY2026 10-K. Component receivables went from $8.2B to $35.7B and $12.6B of investments became restricted for a supplier (Q3 FY2026 on). RPO jumped $233B in Q2 FY2026 while the share due within a year fell to 25%, and the same filing made RPO a critical estimate subject to "potential renegotiation of commitments". Long-term debt fell from $42.66B to $31.09B: the financing is in leases, supplier arrangements and customer contracts, not bonds. This extends the 10-K dashboard's "buildout moved off the balance sheet" theme quarter by quarter.

3. OpenAI: From "including OpenAI" to 10.8% of Revenue

Q1 FY2025 named OpenAI inside Other, net without a figure. The FY2025 10-K described exclusivity, a right of first refusal and reciprocal revenue sharing; within three quarters the first refusal was gone (Q1 FY2026), the $250B Azure commitment had been stated once and dropped (Q1 to Q2 FY2026), and revenue sharing ran one way (Q3 FY2026). Losses were quantified only from Q1 FY2026, alongside non-GAAP measures that exclude them; a $10.0B dilution gain followed. By Q1 FY2027 OpenAI was $10.0B of quarterly revenue and $7.2B of receivables, and Microsoft's stake had been diluted from about 27% to 24%.

4. The 10-Qs Said It First

Several changes the annual dashboard dates to the FY2026 10-K first appeared in a 10-Q: the AI emissions concession (Q2 FY2026), the RPO duration and critical-estimate language (Q2 FY2026), HLBV accounting (Q2 FY2026), the OpenAI non-GAAP adjustment (Q1 FY2026), the one-way revenue share and the supplier-restricted investments (Q3 FY2026), and the headcount decline (Q3 FY2026). Microsoft restates its full risk factors every quarter, and the could-to-has moves (tracked signal #8) came mostly in 10-Qs. The 10-K history lines have been corrected to match.

5. Each Q1 Resets the Yardsticks

Q1 FY2025 merged Devices into Windows OEM and began AI points; the FY2025 10-K dropped the AI points and the lease split; the FY2026 10-K dropped the consumer subscriber count; Q1 FY2027 cut three segments to two and redefined Azure, Microsoft 365 commercial cloud and Microsoft Cloud without old-basis growth rates. Some disclosure was added (RPO duration, OpenAI revenue, an Azure dollar change), but the series that tested the AI story most directly (AI points, Azure on a constant definition, segment margins for the cloud businesses) were the ones that changed.
