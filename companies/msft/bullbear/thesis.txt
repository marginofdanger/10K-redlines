MICROSOFT (MSFT): INVESTMENT THESIS PILLARS
Written November 2026 from the 10-K redline dashboard (FY2019-FY2026) and the 10-Q scanner (Q3 FY2024
to Q1 FY2027). The Bull / Bear page scores two filings: the FY2026 10-K (filed July 29, 2026) and the
Q1 FY2027 10-Q (filed October 28, 2026). Microsoft's fiscal year ends June 30. Every pillar starts at
Intact; the scanner's quarterly record since Q3 FY2024 is the background for each stance, and a stance
moves only when the two scored filings point the same way. Figures are SEC XBRL unless quoted.

P1 Azure and AI demand
  Bull assumes: Azure and the Microsoft Cloud keep growing 30%+ on AI and cloud demand, and the
    commercial backlog converts to revenue on schedule.
  Bear fears: growth leans on a few AI labs (OpenAI first), the backlog is long-dated and partly
    renegotiable, and redefined metrics hide a slowdown.
  Stance: Strengthening, moved at the Q1 FY2027 10-Q.
  History: Azure growth 31% (Q3 FY2024) -> 34% (FY2025) -> 41% (FY2026) -> 48% (Q1 FY2027, new
    definition). Microsoft Cloud +23% (FY2025), +27% (FY2026), +30% (Q1 FY2027, new definition).
    Commercial RPO $235B (March 2024) -> $678B (June 2026) -> $677B (September 2026); 12-month share
    45% -> 25% (Q2 FY2026) -> 30%; weighted-average duration 2.5 -> 2.3 years. AI points were given
    for three quarters of FY2025 and dropped; Q1 FY2027 redefines Azure with no old-basis growth.
  Tests: Azure growth on the new basis, commercial RPO and its 12-month share, duration, any OpenAI
    share of RPO.

P2 Cloud margins and AI unit economics
  Bull assumes: AI infrastructure costs are absorbed by scale and efficiency, so the Microsoft Cloud
    gross margin stabilizes and operating margin rises.
  Bear fears: AI capacity (GPU infrastructure) is structurally lower-margin, so mix keeps pulling the
    margin down whatever the company calls the cause.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the FY2026 10-K.
  History: Microsoft Cloud gross margin 72% (Q3 FY2024) -> 69% (FY2025) -> 66% (Q3 FY2026, FY2026 and
    Q1 FY2027). Stated cause: "scaling our AI infrastructure" (from Q1 FY2025), plus "growing AI
    product usage" (from Q1 FY2026), replaced in Q1 FY2027 by "sales mix shift to Azure". Company
    gross margin 68.8% (FY2025) -> 67.9% (FY2026); operating margin 45.6% -> 46.8% -> 49.8% (Q1 FY2027).
  Tests: Microsoft Cloud gross margin, Agents and Infra cost of revenue vs revenue, operating margin,
    headcount and opex growth.

P3 Capital intensity and cash conversion
  Bull assumes: operating cash flow keeps pace with capex, so free cash flow still covers the dividend
    and buybacks while the build-out earns its return.
  Bear fears: capex grows faster than cash flow, free cash flow stalls and FCF / net income keeps
    falling, and the stated capex plan stays open-ended.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q1 FY2027 10-Q.
  History: capex $44.48B (FY2024), $64.55B (FY2025), $115.95B (FY2026), $39.37B in Q1 FY2027 alone.
    FCF $74.07B -> $71.61B -> $66.99B; FCF / net income 84% -> 70% -> 50% (FY2026) and 51% (Q1
    FY2027). Forward-capex sentence lost "increase" in the FY2025 10-K and gives no figure.
  Tests: FCF and FCF / net income, capex vs operating cash flow, dividends and buybacks vs FCF.

P4 OpenAI relationship
  Bull assumes: OpenAI is a large paying customer and a valuable equity stake, and Microsoft keeps the
    IP rights and a revenue share.
  Bear fears: Microsoft depends on one counterparty for a growing share of revenue and receivables,
    while exclusivity has gone and the stake is diluted.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q1 FY2027 10-Q.
  History: FY2025 10-K: API "exclusive to Azure", "right of first refusal", "reciprocal revenue-sharing".
    Q1 FY2026: first refusal ends; $250B incremental Azure commitment (stated once). Q3 FY2026: revenue
    sharing described as one-way to Microsoft. FY2026: $24.1B revenue (7.3%), $6.0B receivable, ~25%
    stake. Q1 FY2027: $10.0B revenue in the quarter (10.8%), $7.2B receivable (11.6%), ~24% stake.
  Tests: OpenAI revenue and receivable share, stake, gains and losses, any change to the commercial
    terms or to RPO concentration.

P5 Off-balance-sheet commitments and supply
  Bull assumes: leases and supplier arrangements lock in scarce capacity ahead of competitors on terms
    Microsoft can fund.
  Bear fears: commitments outrun demand: unstarted leases, supplier financing and restricted
    investments become fixed costs and stranded capacity if AI demand slows.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q1 FY2027 10-Q.
  History: leases not yet commenced $117.0B (FY2024) -> $92.7B (FY2025) -> $329.1B (FY2026, "subject
    to certain contractual conditions") -> $364.6B (Q1 FY2027, commencing to FY2034). Server-component
    receivables $8.2B (FY2025) -> $27.8B (FY2026) -> $35.7B; supplier-restricted investments $11.5B
    (Q3 FY2026) -> $12.6B. Long-term debt $42.7B (FY2024) -> $31.1B.
  Tests: leases not yet commenced and their conditions, component receivables, restricted investments,
    finance lease liabilities, the FY2026 risk factor's "impairment of assets" language.

P6 Productivity franchise (Microsoft 365 and Copilot)
  Bull assumes: Copilot and premium suites (E5, E7) lift revenue per user on a large, still-growing
    seat base.
  Bear fears: seat growth slows and Copilot pricing does not offset it; AI usage raises the cost to
    serve.
  Stance: Intact (has not moved in the window).
  History: Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud +17% in FY2026 and +17% in Q1 FY2027 (new definition, with
    GitHub); seats +6% (FY2026) -> +5% (Q1 FY2027). The consumer subscriber count was dropped as a
    metric in FY2026.
  Tests: Microsoft 365 commercial cloud growth, seat growth, Copilot and E7 language, consumer cloud.

P7 Consumer and devices
  Bull assumes: Windows, XBOX and search are cash generators that hold their ground.
  Bear fears: PC demand and gaming decline and impairments follow.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q1 FY2027 10-Q.
  History: FY2026: XBOX revenue -7% with "impairment and other related expenses in our XBOX business";
    Windows OEM and Devices "decreased slightly" with inventory "that remained elevated". Q1 FY2027:
    Windows OEM and devices -22% on "inventory drawdowns"; XBOX -2%; search and advertising ex TAC +12%.
  Tests: Devices and Consumer revenue and margin, Windows OEM, XBOX, impairment language.

P8 Regulation, legal and tax
  Bull assumes: legal accruals stay small relative to earnings and the IRS dispute is contested
    successfully.
  Bear fears: tariffs, AI rules and datacenter permitting become recurring costs, and the $28.9B IRS
    claim is upheld.
  Stance: Weakening, moved at the Q1 FY2027 10-Q.
  History: reasonably possible losses beyond accruals $600M (FY2024) -> $800M -> $600M -> $300M ->
    $400M (Q2 FY2026 to Q1 FY2027). IRS NOPAs seek "$28.9 billion plus penalties and interest"
    (unchanged). FY2026: tariffs and export controls "has and may continue to increase operational
    costs". Q1 FY2027: AI regulation "is increasing costs"; datacenter rules "are restricting and
    delaying infrastructure development". None of these costs is quantified.
  Tests: reasonably possible losses, IRS status, quantification of regulatory costs, LinkedIn GDPR
    appeal.
