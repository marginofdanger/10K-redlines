Microsoft 10-K Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual comparison of Microsoft's annual reports, FY2019–FY2026 (fiscal years end June 30). Old and new filing text shown side by side.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0000789019) | Fiscal Years 2019-2026 | Analysis: October 2026
FY2019 → FY2020

COVID-19 sections added; government-customer risk factor added; server useful life extended

The FY2020 10-K adds COVID-19 text to Business, Risk Factors and MD&A, and four new Business sub-sections including the carbon-negative-by-2030 commitment. Item 1A adds one risk factor (government customers) and records the July 2020 Privacy Shield invalidation. MD&A adds a Metrics subsection and a change in accounting estimate extending server and network equipment useful lives to four years.

FY2020 → FY2021

Human Capital section added; Item 1A grouped into categories; Solorigate and Exchange attacks named; ZeniMax and Nuance added

FY2021 adds a Human Capital Resources section, groups risk factors under seven category headings, and names the Solorigate/Nobelium and Exchange Server attacks. ZeniMax (completed March 2021, $8.1 billion) and Nuance (agreement April 2021, $19.7 billion) appear in Business, Risk Factors and MD&A. MD&A cites the server useful-life change as a driver of margins and operating income, and the capital-expenditure outlook sentence is unchanged.

FY2021 → FY2022

Commercial cloud renamed Microsoft Cloud; Nuance closed, Activision announced; useful lives 4 → 6 years

The FY2022 10-K removes the COVID-19 subsections from Business and MD&A, renames commercial cloud as Microsoft Cloud and Azure as Azure and other cloud services, and records the completed $18.8 billion Nuance acquisition alongside the announced $68.7 billion Activision Blizzard deal. Risk Factors add Ukraine-related cybersecurity and sanctions language, a datacenter energy sentence, a unionization paragraph, and proposed EU AI legislation. MD&A discloses a July 2022 extension of server and network useful lives from four to six years, estimated to add $3.7 billion to FY2023 operating income.

FY2022 → FY2023

AI and OpenAI language added across sections; Q2 charge and workforce reduction; IDPC matter

The FY2023 filing adds generative AI, copilot and OpenAI language to Business and Risk Factors, names AI competitors, and adds GPUs, land and energy to the datacenter dependency sentence in three sections. MD&A discloses a $1.2 billion Q2 charge with approximately 10,000 job reductions, and the capex sentence adds AI infrastructure. The Contingencies note adds the Irish Data Protection Commission LinkedIn matter and the legal accrual rises from $364 million to $617 million.

FY2023 → FY2024

AI moved into opening sentence; Activision closed; nation-state breach and $28.9B IRS NOPAs disclosed

The FY2024 10-K places AI in the company's opening sentence, names Anthropic and OpenAI as AI competitors, and adds Azure Maia and Azure Cobalt custom silicon. Risk factors add the late-2023 nation-state email intrusion, the FTC's continuing challenge to the completed Activision Blizzard acquisition, the IRS NOPAs seeking $28.9 billion, and a new sustainability risk factor. MD&A attributes revenue growth to all three segments and the capex sentence adds "and training" to AI infrastructure.

FY2024 → FY2025

Segment realignment; OpenAI moved from Item 1 to MD&A; legal risk factors consolidated

Segments were realigned in August 2024, moving the commercial components of Microsoft 365 into Productivity and Business Processes and replacing the metric set. Item 1 dropped the ambition narratives, the OpenAI paragraph, named competitors and diversity language, while MD&A added an OpenAI partnership paragraph and attributed the Microsoft Cloud gross margin decline to 69% to scaling AI infrastructure. Five legal and regulatory risk factors were merged into one with new AI, trade/tariff and cybersecurity bullets, and the capex sentence changed from "expect … to increase" to "will continue to invest".

FY2025 → FY2026

Cloud/AI risk factor split; infrastructure risk factor added; OpenAI exclusivity text removed; non-GAAP added

Risk Factors grew 18%, with the cloud/AI risk factor split into two and new language on capital expenditures, financing, impairment, power and community opposition to datacenters. MD&A removed the OpenAI API exclusivity and right-of-first-refusal sentences, added October 2025 and April 2026 partnership extensions, and introduced non-GAAP measures excluding OpenAI gains and losses. Revenue rose 18% with XBOX and More Personal Computing revenue declining, and the reasonably possible legal loss range fell from $600 million to $400 million.
