Microsoft 10-K Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of eight Microsoft 10-Ks: how the wording on cloud margins, datacenter commitments, OpenAI and risk moved ahead of the numbers.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0000789019) | 8 annual reports on Form 10-K, fiscal years 2019-2026 (years end June 30) | Analysis: November 2026
FY2019 → FY2020

The Pandemic Enters the 10-K

Revenue +14% to $143.01B, op margin 34% to 37%; commercial cloud +36% to $51.7B. COVID risk factor reports Q3-Q4 hits to supply chain, licensing, ads. Server life 3 to 4 years, +$2.7B FY2021 op income. Privacy Shield invalidated.

FY2020 → FY2021

Hacked, Then Buying

Revenue +18% to $168.09B, op margin 37% to 42% with $2.7B from server-life change; commercial RPO $141B. Solorigate and Exchange zero-days named. ZeniMax $8.1B closed, Nuance $19.7B pending. Construction commitments $5.0B to $9.5B.

FY2021 → FY2022

Microsoft Cloud, Metaverse, Activision

Commercial cloud renamed Microsoft Cloud ($91.2B, +32%). Revenue $198.27B (+18%); headcount 181,000 to 221,000. Nuance closed ($18.8B), Activision announced ($68.7B). Server lives 4 to 6 years (+$3.7B FY2023 op income). Metaverse, unions, datacenter energy enter.

FY2022 → FY2023

OpenAI, Copilots and Cost Cuts

OpenAI, generative AI and copilots enter; metaverse gone. Revenue $211.91B (+7%); Azure growth 45% to 29%. $1.2B Q2 charge, about 10,000 jobs cut, salaries frozen. Capex guide adds AI infrastructure; GPUs named. Activision close date dropped; GDPR 4% cap deleted.

FY2023 → FY2024

The AI Buildout Begins

Activision Blizzard closed at $75.4B; AI added to the opening sentence; nation-state email breach and IRS NOPAs seeking $28.9B disclosed; capex $28.11B to $44.48B; datacenter leases not yet commenced $42.1B to $117.0B.

FY2024 → FY2025

OpenAI Exclusivity Loosens

OpenAI exclusivity narrowed to the API plus a right of first refusal; $13B OpenAI commitment disclosed; Microsoft Cloud gross margin 71% to 69% on AI infrastructure; capex $64.55B (+45%); leases not yet commenced fell to $92.7B; tariffs and AI Diffusion Rule added.

FY2025 → FY2026

OpenAI Exclusivity Ends, Leases Triple

Revenue $331.8B (+18%); Microsoft Cloud $214.4B, gross margin 69% to 66%; commercial RPO $678B (+84%); cash capex $116B; leases not yet commenced $92.7B to $329.1B; OpenAI $24.1B revenue, $5.0B gain; headcount 228,000 to 223,000.

▶Supplier concentration disclosed: "The majority of our hardware products contain components for which there is only one qualified supplier"New

Shift: added (+, more disclosure); concentration stated as "the majority" and "only one"

FY2019: "Our devices are primarily manufactured by third-party contract manufacturers. We generally have the ability to use other manufacturers if a current vendor becomes unavailable or unable to meet our requirements."
FY2020: "The majority of our hardware products contain components for which there is only one qualified supplier. Extended disruptions at these suppliers could lead to a similar disruption in our ability to manufacture devices."

History (phrase_history): "only one qualified supplier" appears in the FY2020 10-K only; absent from FY2019. FY2021 rewrote it to "very few qualified suppliers" for "some" products (see the FY2021 transition).

The new sentence sits right after the unchanged claim that Microsoft can "generally" switch manufacturers, and partly contradicts it. It arrives in the year the COVID risk factor reports supply-chain disruption in the third and fourth quarters.

▶Pandemic risk factor added with impacts already felt: supply chain, "a slowdown in transactional licensing, and lower demand for our advertising services"Signal

▶Government customers get their own risk factor: audits, debarment, "terminate without cause"; the shutdown sentence is deletedNew

Shift: new risk factor (+); scope: "U.S. government contracts" → "government contracts"; removed: federal shutdown and debt-ceiling risk (−)

History (phrase_history): "Our business with government customers" first appears in FY2020 and is in every 10-K since (7 of 8); "An extended federal government shutdown" is in FY2019 only.

The risk moves from a spending-cycle problem (shutdowns) to a contract-terms problem: audits, debarment, rescission and termination without cause. It widens from U.S. government to all governments. The filing names no contract.
▶Privacy Shield restated as struck down: "currently subject to challenges... may lead" → "invalidated... has led" to uncertaintySignal

Shift: realized: hypothetical → actual (signals rank 1, score 10.0); hedge dropped: "currently" (+)

History (phrase_history): "invalidated a framework called Privacy Shield" appears in the FY2020, FY2021 and FY2022 10-Ks; the "currently subject to challenges" wording is FY2019 only.

The ruling came in the month the 10-K was filed. The uncertainty now reaches the "other legal mechanisms" that EU-to-US transfers still rely on, which matters for a cloud business serving EU customers.
▶Cloud capacity named as a failure mode: "inadequate storage and compute capacity" added to the outage riskChanged

Shift: named: +storage and compute capacity, +datacenter builds in "geographies with higher safety risks" (+)

Capacity shortfall joins connectivity and data loss as a named cause of service failure. It matches the COVID risk factor's bullet on "adequate capacity of cloud services" during the usage surge.

History (phrase_history): first appears in FY2020 and is in every 10-K since (7 of 8).

The sentence is added to the competing-platforms risk. Distribution through rival platforms becomes a stated constraint. The filing names no store or product.

Themes

The buildout moved off the balance sheet before it moved into capex

Each step of the AI build showed up first as a commitment in the notes, then as spending a year later:

  • FY2021: construction commitments $5.0B → $9.5B under a capex sentence that did not change a word
  • FY2024: leases not yet commenced $42.1B → $117.0B; capex $28.11B → $44.48B
  • FY2025: unstarted leases fell to $92.7B as sites came online, and capex rose to $64.55B
  • FY2026: unstarted leases $329.1B, running to FY2033, with a new hedge ("subject to certain contractual conditions"); capex $115.95B, 35% of revenue

Long-term debt fell from $66.7B to $31.1B over the same years. The financing is in leases, not bonds.

Margin wording turned before the margin did

The cloud gross-margin story has three acts. FY2021-FY2023 gains were partly accounting: server lives went from three to four to six years, adding $2.7B and then $3.7B of operating income, and FY2023 admitted the margin "decreased slightly" without the change. From FY2024 the stated driver became AI infrastructure. The margin went from 72% (FY2023) to 66% (FY2026), and FY2026 added "growing AI product usage" as a cause. The risk factor that once protected "margins we have previously achieved" dropped that phrase in FY2024.

OpenAI: from partner to related party, exclusivity unwound in two steps

  • FY2023: first named; Azure is its "exclusive cloud provider"
  • FY2025: narrowed to API exclusivity plus a right of first refusal; $13B funding commitment; losses run through Other, net
  • FY2026: exclusivity and first refusal deleted; OpenAI is a related party (about 25% interest, HLBV accounting) that bought $24.1B of Microsoft services; a $6.5B pretax gain is stripped out by new non-GAAP measures

The same filing that books OpenAI as a large customer drops its name from the AI risk factor and warns that partners who are big Azure customers may cut consumption.

Backlog grew faster than it converts

Commercial RPO went from $107B (FY2020) to $678B (FY2026). The share due within 12 months fell from about half (FY2019-FY2021) to 45% (FY2022), 40% (FY2025) and 30% (FY2026), and FY2026 gave a weighted-average duration of 2.3 years (first disclosed as 2.5 years in the Q2 FY2026 10-Q). The headline backlog is growing, but more of it is long-dated, and RPO became a critical accounting estimate (Q2 FY2026 10-Q, then the FY2026 10-K).

Hypothetical risks restated as having happened

Microsoft's risk factors moved from "could" to "has" again and again: COVID damage (FY2020), Solorigate and Exchange zero-days (FY2021), attackers who "have gained unauthorized access" (FY2024), software-quality failures (FY2025), and in FY2026 tariffs, export controls, supply shortages and AI-driven emissions. Wording that firms up in this way is the most reliable tell on the page.

The company says less about its people and its rivals

Headcount rose from 144,000 (FY2019) to 228,000 (FY2024) and fell for the first time to 223,000 in FY2026, after about 10,000 cuts in FY2023 and a salary freeze. FY2025 cut the Human Capital section to two paragraphs and removed every named competitor from Item 1 (17 companies in FY2024, including Anthropic and OpenAI as AI rivals). FY2026 dropped the Microsoft 365 Consumer subscriber count. The disclosure is getting thinner just as the investment gets bigger.
