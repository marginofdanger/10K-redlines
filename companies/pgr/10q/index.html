PGR 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of ten SEC filings — eight 10-Qs from Q1 2024 (quarter ended March 31, 2024) through Q2 2026 (June 30, 2026), with the FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks (MD&A incorporated from the Exhibit 13 annual report) interleaved at the year boundaries. Nine transitions covering MD&A, Item 1A, Item 2 repurchases and the litigation note. The arc: a growth push funded by record advertising (2024), a profit windfall that tripped Florida’s excess-profit statute (2025), and by mid-2026 growth language demoted behind profitability as competition, retention and pricing all turn.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0000080661) | The Progressive Corporation (NYSE: PGR) | 10 filings, 9 transitions | Q1 2024 – Q2 2026 | Analysis: October 2026

Q1 2024 → Q2 2024

Filed Aug 5, 2024 | NPW $17.9B (+22%) | Combined ratio 91.9 (Q1: 86.1) | Policies in force 32.3M (+9%) | Advertising +147%

Growth & Pricing (MD&A)

▶ Personal auto new applications swing from −9% to +34%; the overview stops crediting retention and lists the growth levers instead Signal

Q1 2024:

"Although personal auto new business applications were down from the prior year, reflecting the rate and non-rate actions we took throughout 2023 to manage profitability, policy in force growth benefited from improved retention in both our Personal Lines and Property businesses."

Q2 2024:

"During the quarter we increased our advertising spend, continued to unwind non-rate restrictions we put in place throughout 2023 to manage profitability, and worked closely with our independent agents to get back into their quote flows. We also focused on delivering competitive rates to consumers and had eight personal auto states with small rate decreases during the second quarter."

The first quarter in the series to name rate decreases. The non-rate restrictions, the agency quote flows and media spend are the three levers this scanner tracks through 2026; the focus sentence changes from "maximizing profitable growth" to "driving growth, delivering competitive rates to consumers, and providing an exceptional customer experience to our policyholders."

▶ Advertising up 147% in the quarter; first-half spend of $1.6B already equals all of 2023 Changed

Q1 2024 had reported a 7% decrease in advertising. Q2 2024:

"For the first six months of 2024, our total companywide advertising costs were $1.6 billion, which is equal to the amount of advertising spend for the full year of 2023."

The expense ratio flips from 2.3 points better to 2.5 points worse year over year. The spend condition is still "a cost below the maximum amount we are willing to spend to acquire a new customer"; Q3 2024 rewrites it (see next transition).

▶ Retention warning hardens: trailing 3-month PLE "no longer showing improvement" becomes "down 5%"; trailing 12-month gain halves, +20% → +9% Changed

Q1 2024:

"Rate increases could have an adverse impact on our retention, as potentially indicated by our total personal auto trailing 3-month policy life expectancy no longer showing improvement, compared to the same period last year."

Q2 2024:

"However, our total personal auto trailing 3-month policy life expectancy was down 5% at the end of the second quarter 2024, due to rate and non-rate actions put in place throughout 2023, compared to the 40% increase experienced in the same period last year"

The hedge ("could", "potentially") becomes a measured decline. The Direct channel's trailing 12-month gain fell from +13% to +2%; it turns negative next quarter. See Retention.

Property & Catastrophe

▶ Property segment posts a 166.3 combined ratio with 75.6 catastrophe points New
"Our Personal and Commercial Lines businesses both generated an 88.6 combined ratio for the quarter, while the Property business generated an underwriting loss with a combined ratio of 166.3 that included catastrophe losses of 75.6 points."

Q1 2024's Property combined ratio was 93.4. Q3 2024 reverses most of this: 29.7 points of favorable development on current-year storms, "just over 75%" related to May 2024 events.

▶ Reinsurance cost complaint deleted; replaced by "readily available and competitively priced"; aggregate program $3–40M from attaching Changed

Q1 2024:

"While the cost of our reinsurance, in the markets in which we participate, increased for the coverages placed in the beginning of 2024, compared to the prior years, and the availability of reinsurance is subject to many forces outside of our control, we did not, and do not expect to in the near term, experience a significant lack of availability of any of the types of reinsurance that we typically purchase."

Q2 2024:

"...the types of reinsurance that we elected to purchase during the first half of 2024 were readily available and competitively priced. On a year-over-year basis, we did not incur a material change in the aggregate costs of our reinsurance programs."

New disclosure of the June 2024 per-occurrence renewal ($200M retention, $2.0B of coverage, $2.3B including the Florida Hurricane Catastrophe Fund) and of how close the aggregate cover was to attaching:

"As of the end of the second quarter 2024, we estimate that, based on the particular contract, we were about $3 million to $40 million below the annual retention thresholds under the first coverage layer of our 2024 catastrophe aggregate excess of loss program."

Litigation (Note 11)

▶ Litigation note gains "except as to any settlements and accruals"; "if any" dropped from the accrual sentence; certified total-loss classes 5 → 7 states Changed

Q1 2024:

"Based on information known at March 31, 2024, we determined that losses from these lawsuits are reasonably possible but neither probable nor reasonably estimable."

Q2 2024:

"Based on information known at June 30, 2024, and except as to any settlements and accruals as described below, we determined that losses from these lawsuits are reasonably possible but neither probable nor reasonably estimable."

The non-policy lawsuit sentence changes from "the accruals that we have established, if any, were not material" to "the accruals that we have established were not material" — consistent with accruals now existing, though still immaterial. Alabama and South Carolina join Colorado, Georgia, Indiana, New York and Pennsylvania as certified total-loss valuation classes; the overtime-classification and Washington medical-bill classes drop off the list. See Total-Loss Class Actions.

Q2 2024 → Q3 2024

Filed Nov 4, 2024 | NPW $19.5B (+25%) | Combined ratio 89.0 | Policies in force 33.9M (+14%) | Advertising +394% | Hurricane Helene $563M

Growth & Pricing (MD&A)

▶ The 96 combined ratio goal moves ahead of "high-quality customer service" — a quarter before the FY2024 10-K made the same reordering Signal

Q2 2024:

"We will continue to manage growth and profitability in accordance with our long-standing goal of growing as fast as we can, as long as we can provide high-quality customer service, at or below a companywide 96 combined ratio on a calendar-year basis. Given the solid underwriting profitability we generated during the first half of 2024, we took measures that we believe will position us to accelerate profitable growth during the year"

Q3 2024:

"We will continue to manage growth and profitability in accordance with our long-standing goal of growing as fast as we can, at or below a companywide 96 combined ratio on a calendar-year basis, as long as we can provide high-quality customer service. Given the solid underwriting profitability we generated during the first nine months of 2024, we plan to continue to take measures that we believe will position us to achieve profitable growth during the remainder of the year, including increasing our media spend."

The annual dashboard flags this reordering as the FY2024 10-K's key signal; the 10-Q had it first. "Accelerate" becomes "achieve", and lifting non-rate actions drops out of the list of growth measures. This is the last 10-Q in the series to carry the "as fast as we can" sentence at all (see 96 CR Growth Sentence).

▶ Rate outlook firms from "lesser magnitude" to "stay stable in the fourth quarter" Changed

Q2 2024:

"We currently anticipate that aggregate vehicle rate changes throughout 2024 will be of lesser magnitude than those taken in each of the prior two years"

Q3 2024:

"Aggregate vehicle rate changes year to date have been modest, relative to the prior two years, and we expect rates to stay stable in the fourth quarter 2024."

Nine-month personal auto rate increases total 3%, against 19% in 2023. The relative comparison becomes a near-term flat-rate expectation; the FY2024 10-K extends it to all of 2025.

▶ Agent compensation program named as a growth lever; advertising condition loosened as spend rises nearly 400% New
"...work closely with our independent agents to leverage our agent compensation program to reward writing profitable business as we remained focused on driving growth."

Nine-month advertising of $2.8 billion "exceeded the amount of advertising spend for any previous full annual period." The spend condition changes. Q2 2024:

"We will continue this focus as long as we remain on track to achieve our target profitability and generate sales at a cost below the maximum amount we are willing to spend to acquire a new customer."

Q3 2024:

"We will continue to advertise to maximize growth as long as the advertising spend is efficient and we remain on track to achieve our target profitability."

The explicit per-customer cost ceiling gives way to "efficient". A version of the ceiling ("at or below our target acquisition cost") survives in the underwriting-expense section.

▶ Direct-channel retention turns negative: "lengthening" becomes "decrease", now blamed on competition and shopping Signal

Q2 2024:

"Our trailing 12-month policy life expectancy in the Direct auto business experienced a lengthening of retention at the end of the second quarter 2024, on a year-over-year basis."

Q3 2024:

"Our trailing 12-month policy life expectancy in the Direct auto business experienced a decrease in retention at the end of the third quarter 2024, on a year-over-year basis. We believe the drivers of the change were due to the competitiveness in the marketplace and increased shopping."

Total personal auto trailing 12-month PLE falls to flat (from +9%), Direct −6%. None of the seven later readings (10-Qs and 10-Ks) shows trailing 12-month retention up again.

Property & Catastrophe

▶ Hurricanes Helene ($563M, nothing ceded) and Milton (~$140M property net; ~$600M combined vehicle) first appear New
"In the third quarter 2024, we incurred $563 million of catastrophe losses, or 3.1 loss ratio points, on a companywide basis, as a result of Hurricane Helene"
"No amounts were ceded related to Hurricane Helene, as our incurred Property losses and allocated loss adjustment expenses (ALAE) did not exceed our retention threshold under either the Florida Hurricane Catastrophe Fund or our occurrence excess of loss reinsurance program."
"As of October 31, 2024, we estimated that our vehicle businesses will incur combined losses of approximately $600 million from Hurricanes Helene and Milton, which includes the $401 million incurred in the third quarter 2024."

About half of Helene's losses were in auto; boat accounted for about 60% of special lines losses. The FY2024 10-K does not reconcile the October estimates and says only that the two storms accounted for "nearly one third" of 2024 catastrophe losses.

▶ Florida non-renewal program (up to 115,000 policies) stalled by hurricane moratoriums; Property retention flips from lengthening to shortening New
"We believe the moratoriums put in place in response to Hurricanes Helene and Milton, which temporarily limit an insurer's ability to non-renew policies, will slow this progress; however, we intend to resume the planned non-renewals when the moratoriums expire."

The program had been "expected to continue over the following 12 months"; the FY2024 10-K reports the moratoriums expired in December 2024, and Q2 2025 reports it "substantially completed." Property PLE goes from lengthened (Q2: "primarily driven by our competitiveness in the marketplace") to shortened, attributed to renters mix, rate increases, "and the non-renewals for certain Florida policies." Volatile-state homeowners policies in force fall 9%, from −6%.

Litigation (Note 11)

▶ Data-security class action certified; Ohio becomes the eighth certified total-loss valuation state New

New entry in the certified-class list:

"...we failed, based on a vendor data security incident, to properly secure and safeguard personally identifiable information; and that certain of our compensation practices are improper."

Total-loss certified states: Alabama, Colorado, Georgia, Indiana, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania, South Carolina. The data-security class is gone from the Q1 2025 list.

Q3 2024 → FY2024 10-K

Filed Mar 3, 2025 | FY2024 NPW $74.4B (+21%) | Underwriting margin 11.2% | Advertising $4.0B (+150%) | Dividends declared $4.90/share

Structure, Pricing & Outlook (Annual Report MD&A)

▶ Three segments become two: Property folded into Personal Lines Changed

Q3 2024 still reported Personal Lines, Commercial Lines and Property margins (10.4%, 11.3%, 21.5%). FY2024 10-K:

"For the year ended December 31, 2024, we are reporting two operating segments – Personal Lines and Commercial Lines."

Personal property becomes "about 5%" of Personal Lines premiums. The structural change the annual dashboard records; for this scanner it means property results from Q1 2025 onward are a sub-line ("personal property") inside Personal Lines.

▶ Stable-rate expectation extended from Q4 2024 to all of 2025; commercial auto guided to "relatively modest" increases Signal

Q3 2024 expected rates "to stay stable in the fourth quarter 2024." FY2024 10-K:

"Aggregate personal auto rate changes during 2024 were modest, relative to the prior two years, and we expect rates to stay relatively stable in 2025. In our personal property business, we increased rates about 19% countrywide, in the aggregate during 2024, and we expect continued rate increases in 2025."
"...we currently believe that, in most states, we are adequately priced in our personal auto product and in our core commercial auto product we expect to have relatively modest rate increases in 2025."

Eight weeks later, Q1 2025 raises the commercial auto and property guidance to "near double-digit" (next transition).

▶ Tariffs enter the loss-cost factor list for the first time New
"...which may include new and used car prices, miles driven, driving patterns, loss severity, weather events, building materials, construction costs, inflation, tariffs, and other factors, on a state-by-state basis."

Tariffs are also added to the severity-monitoring list. No 2024 10-Q mentions tariffs; Q1 2025 gives them a standalone paragraph. See Tariff Language.

▶ Full-year retention negative: trailing 12-month PLE −4%, Direct −7% Changed
"Our trailing 12-month total personal auto policy life expectancy was down 4% year over year, with agency personal auto up 2% and direct down 7%. On a trailing 3-month basis, our personal auto policy life expectancy was down 4% year over year, which we believe is due to increased shopping and the competitiveness in the marketplace."

Against the FY2023 10-K's +24%. The cause given shifts from Progressive's own 2023 rate actions (Q3 2024) to market shopping.

Property & Capital

▶ Property retreat formalized: exit from non-owner-occupied homes begins; Florida non-renewals resume after the moratoriums Changed
"We prioritized insuring lower-risk properties (e.g., new construction, existing homes with newer roofs), accepting new business for homeowners/condo products only when bundled with a Progressive personal auto policy, where permitted, and began exiting the non-owner occupied home market."
"This effort slowed while the moratoriums were in place in response to Hurricanes Helene and Milton, which temporarily limited an insurer’s ability to non-renew policies, but resumed once the moratoriums expired in December 2024."

Volatile-state homeowners policies in force fall 13% for 2024; the growth-oriented states are up 15% (Q3 2024: about 19%).

▶ $4.50 annual-variable dividend declared ($4.90 total for 2024); 2024 buybacks just 0.7M shares Signal
"Common Share Dividends - declared aggregate dividends of $4.90 per common share, or $2.9 billion."

Capital went out through the variable dividend, not repurchases: 0.7 million shares for $134 million, mostly withholding on equity awards. The pattern holds through FY2025 ($13.90 declared, 0.7 million shares) and breaks in Q1 2026 (see Buybacks, Dividends & Debt).

FY2024 10-K → Q1 2025

Filed May 5, 2025 | NPW $22.2B (+17%) | Underwriting margin 14.0% | Policies in force 36.3M (+18%) | Advertising $1.3B (+86%)

Growth & Pricing (MD&A)

▶ The "growing as fast as we can" 96 combined ratio sentence disappears from the 10-Q MD&A — and does not return Removed

Q3 2024 (last appearance):

"We will continue to manage growth and profitability in accordance with our long-standing goal of growing as fast as we can, at or below a companywide 96 combined ratio on a calendar-year basis, as long as we can provide high-quality customer service."

Q1 2025 has no equivalent, and neither does any later 10-Q; "as fast as we can" returns zero hits in all five 2025–2026 quarterly filings. The 10-K Business section keeps the goal (annual dashboard, signal #1). From here the quarterly MD&A states the goal only as the "4% companywide calendar-year underwriting profit goal" in the opening sentence. The one quarterly sentence that tied growth to a speed is gone; the profit threshold stays.

▶ Standalone tariff paragraph: tariffs "will likely result in higher loss costs" and "higher than currently anticipated rate increases throughout 2025 and 2026" New
"Starting in the first quarter 2025, the U.S. government announced additional tariffs on goods imported into the U.S. from numerous countries, which have, in response, resulted in additional tariffs against the U.S."
"While our focus has been on trying to maintain stable rates for customers, tariffs and other retaliatory actions will likely result in higher loss costs, which could result in a reduction in profitability and higher than currently anticipated rate increases throughout 2025 and 2026."

"Will likely" is the strongest wording the tariff paragraph reaches in the series; it softens in Q2 2025 and again in the FY2025 10-K, and is deleted in Q2 2026.

▶ Rate guidance escalates in one quarter: commercial auto "relatively modest" → "near double-digit" Signal

FY2024 10-K: "in our core commercial auto product we expect to have relatively modest rate increases in 2025". Q1 2025:

"We currently believe we are adequately priced in our personal auto products in most states and will continue to monitor the impact from tariffs and potential changes in the regulatory environment. We expect both the personal property and core commercial auto products to have near double-digit rate increases through the remainder of 2025."

A quantified range appears where the 10-K gave none. It survives a single quarter (removed in Q2 2025). Commercial auto ultimately took about 9% for 2025 per the FY2025 10-K, consistent with the guidance.

▶ Federal debt-ceiling default risk added to the liquidity discussion New
"While U.S. Treasury securities are viewed as having lower risk than many other investment opportunities, the U.S. Treasury announced it had reached its authorized borrowing limit and defaults under government obligations, including payments related to U.S. Treasury securities, could occur as soon as this summer. Although not likely probable, it is possible that the federal government could fail to raise the federal debt ceiling to avoid default."

With 56% of the portfolio in short-term investments and Treasuries. Removed in Q2 2025.

▶ Core commercial auto retention "sequential month-over-month improvement" while still down 6% Changed
"While still below the prior year, the policy life expectancy has experienced sequential month-over-month improvement since the end of the third quarter 2024, which we believe is due to the moderation of our rate increases, compared to competitor rate increases, improving our competitiveness in the marketplace."

Personal auto went the other way: trailing 12-month PLE −5%, trailing 3-month −6%. By Q2 2025 commercial auto PLE is +5% and personal auto stays negative — the two segments' retention lines cross.

Property

▶ Homeowners/condo new applications down "just over 55%"; Robinsons (bundlers) decline because of the property pullback Signal
"The new application growth in our homeowners/condo products was down just over 55% on a year-over-year basis for the first quarter 2025, with decreases in both less volatile weather-related states and in the more volatile"

The FY2024 10-K had homeowners/condo new applications up 3%. Declines now hit the less volatile "growth-oriented" states too, and the bundled-household strategy pays for it: Robinsons had "a significant decline in new applications during the first quarter 2025." Q1 2025 also "expanded our cost sharing through mandatory wind and hail deductibles and roof depreciation schedules."

Litigation (Note 11)

▶ Certified total-loss classes peak at nine states; accruals now acknowledged for specific suits; "illusory" UIM class added Changed
"Based on information available to us, we determined that losses from these lawsuits are reasonably possible but neither probable nor reasonably estimable, other than for suits for which accruals have been established and are not material, as of March 31, 2025."

Arkansas is added (nine certified states: AL, AR, CO, GA, IN, NY, OH, PA, SC); the claim is now described as "by applying a negotiation adjustment". New certified class: "we sell illusory underinsured motorist coverage in New Mexico." The data-security and Texas PIP classes drop off.

Q1 2025 → Q2 2025

Filed Aug 4, 2025 | NPW $20.1B (+12%) | Underwriting margin 13.8% | Policies in force 37.3M (+15%) | Advertising $1.2B (+35%) | First personal auto rate cut

Growth, Pricing & Regulation (MD&A)

▶ Florida excess-profit exposure first disclosed: "it is possible" profit will exceed the statutory limit New
"In response to these trends, we have lowered Florida personal auto rates twice in the last year. Despite these actions, it is possible that our profit for personal auto in Florida for the 2023 to 2025 period will exceed the statutory profit limit that a Florida statute imposes on the profit that any insurance group can earn on personal auto insurance over any three-calendar-year period"
"We expect to be able to reasonably estimate any potential exposure by the end of the fourth quarter 2025, and to pay amounts owed, if any, during late 2025 or 2026."

No earlier filing in the series mentions the statute. The wording moves "possible" → "probable" ($950M booked, Q3 2025) → "exceeded" ($1.2B, FY2025 10-K) → paid (Q1 2026). See Florida Excess-Profit Credit.

▶ Personal auto rates cut in aggregate for the first time in the series; Florida named among the markets with decreases Signal

Q1 2025: "we increased personal auto rates less than 1%". Q2 2025:

"During the second quarter 2025, on a countrywide basis, we decreased personal auto rates less than 1% and increased our personal property rates about 4%, in the aggregate."

The agency written-premium explanation gains "rate decreases in certain markets, including Florida". Personal auto average written premium per policy turns negative (−1%). Q3 2025 is back to a sub-1% increase; FY2025, Q1 2026 and Q2 2026 are all decreases.

▶ "Near double-digit" rate guidance withdrawn after one quarter; tariff sentence gains "the possible need for" Removed

Q1 2025: "We expect both the personal property and core commercial auto products to have near double-digit rate increases through the remainder of 2025." Q2 2025:

"While we expect to continue increasing rates in our personal property and core commercial auto products through the remainder of 2025, we will continue to monitor the impact from tariffs and other potential changes in the regulatory environment as we evaluate the possible need for additional rate increases."

The quantity is dropped. In the tariff sentence, "could result in a reduction in profitability and higher than currently anticipated rate increases" becomes "...and the possible need for higher than currently anticipated rate increases" — an added hedge, while "will likely result in higher loss costs" stays.

▶ "Competition" first appears in the retention explanation New

Q1 2025 blamed "increased shopping and the competitiveness in the marketplace". Q2 2025:

"On a trailing 3-month basis, our personal auto policy life expectancy was down 7% for the second quarter 2025, compared to the same period last year, which we believe is due to a shift in the mix of business, increased shopping, and increased competition in the marketplace."

The word "competition" appears 0 times in the five MD&As from Q1 2024 to Q1 2025, then 1, 1, 2, 4 and 7 times. See Retention.

▶ Debt-ceiling paragraph removed; One Big Beautiful Bill Act tax note added ("do not currently expect" a material effect) Changed

The Q1 2025 Treasury-default paragraph is deleted in full. New:

"We are in the process of evaluating the provisions applicable to Progressive and, while this assessment is not yet complete, we do not currently expect the Act will have a material effect on our financial condition or results of operations."

Q3 2025 firms this to "These provisions will not have a material adverse effect on our financial condition or results of operations."

Property & Reinsurance

▶ 2025 per-occurrence program: Florida-specific $75M retention appears; Florida non-renewals "substantially completed" Changed
"This reinsurance program has a retention threshold for losses and allocated loss adjustment expenses (ALAE) from a single catastrophic event of $200 million for a storm outside of Florida and $75 million for a storm in Florida."

The 2024 program had a single $200 million retention. Florida coverage drops from an estimated $2.3 billion to $2.2 billion. Separately: "This effort was substantially completed by the end of the second quarter 2025." Q1 2025 had said "We currently expect this effort to be substantially completed". The sentence on the replacement-carrier agreement for non-renewed Florida policyholders is deleted.

Litigation (Note 11)

"...we improperly value total loss claims by applying a negotiation adjustment in Alabama, Arkansas, Colorado, Georgia, Indiana, North Carolina, Ohio, Pennsylvania, and South Carolina"

Still nine total-loss states; the certified-class list shrinks from five matters to three. New Mexico UIM reappears in Q1 2026 and is gone again in Q2 2026.

Q2 2025 → Q3 2025

Growth & Profitability (MD&A)

▶ Florida: "possible" becomes "probable"; $950M policyholder credit expense booked against 2.7 million policyholders Signal

Q2 2025: "it is possible that our profit for personal auto in Florida for the 2023 to 2025 period will exceed the statutory profit limit". Q3 2025:

"Despite actions to lower rates in the last year, it is probable that our personal auto profit in Florida for the 2023 to 2025 period will exceed the statutory profit limit that a Florida statute imposes on the profit that any insurance group can earn on personal auto insurance over any three-calendar-year period."
"As a result, in September 2025, we recorded a $950 million policyholder credit expense, which represents our current estimate of the profit we will earn on the three-calendar-year period ending December 31, 2025, in excess"
"As of September 30, 2025, we had approximately 2.7 million personal auto policyholders active in Florida."

The credit is booked as an underwriting expense: 4.6 points companywide, 5.5 points off the personal vehicle margin. Excluding it, Personal Lines would have earned 15.8%. Q2 2025 had promised an estimate "by the end of the fourth quarter 2025"; it came one quarter early.

▶ Growth now credited to renewals of last year's new business; personal auto new applications fall 2%, the first decline since Q1 2024 Signal

Q2 2025 credited growth to "increased advertising spend, competitively priced products, and our agency incentive compensation programs." Q3 2025:

"This growth was primarily driven by our personal auto products and reflects renewal application growth, driven by new applications gained over the last year renewing during the quarter."
"New and renewal personal auto applications decreased 2% and increased 20%, respectively, for the third quarter 2025, compared to the same period in the prior year."

The opening sentence's growth adjective falls from "substantial" (Q1–Q2 2025) back to "strong". Advertising rose only 10%, after +35% and +86%.

▶ "Customer preservation team" disclosed: existing customers re-quoting into new policies depresses the retention metric New
"Due, in part, to the efforts of our customer preservation team, during the period we saw an increase in existing customers who went through our new customer quote process to either modify existing coverage or to find a lower rate."

Such customers' replacement policies "negatively impacted policy life expectancy but retained the customer." This tells the reader that part of the PLE decline is measurement, not lost customers. It also says existing customers are shopping for lower rates inside Progressive. Trailing 12-month PLE −6% (from −5%), trailing 3-month −7%. The attribution order also flips: shopping and competition now come before mix.

▶ 10-Q adds the 3.5-to-1 premiums-to-surplus approval and the variable-dividend intent, a quarter before a $13.50 variable dividend New
"...two states have permitted us to target a premiums-to-surplus ratio for our vehicle businesses to a maximum ratio of 3.5 to 1 based on our strong financial condition."
"We have previously announced our intention to pay a dividend on our common shares on a quarterly basis and to consider paying a variable dividend on at least an annual basis."

This paragraph had appeared only in the annual report. Statutory surplus is $33.7B at September 30, 2025; the December 2025 variable dividend ($13.50, $7.9B) drops it to $28.4B at year end. The paragraph is gone again from Q1 2026.

Property

Q2 2025 planned to continue the restrictions and "determine readiness for new business growth at the state level". Q3 2025:

"During the third quarter 2025, we began to take actions in several markets"
"Some of these actions include expanding agency relationships, lifting certain agency restrictions put in place in 2024, and reopening new business in our direct channel."

Growth-oriented-state homeowners policies in force: +14% (Q1 2025) → +7% → +2%. Volatile states −15%. Homeowners new applications −35%. Personal property margin 36.5%.

The filing does not say whether the three were settled, decertified or dismissed; the accrual language ("other than for suits for which accruals have been established and are not material") is unchanged. The list falls again to three in Q1 2026.

Q3 2025 → FY2025 10-K

Filed Mar 2, 2026 | FY2025 NPW $83.2B (+12%) | Underwriting margin 12.6% | Florida credits $1.2B | Dividends declared $13.90/share ($8.1B)

Florida, Pricing & Tariffs (Annual Report MD&A)

▶ Annual opener leads with the profit goal; growth becomes "excellent results" Signal

FY2024 10-K:

"Progressive reported strong year-over-year growth in both premiums written and policies in force, an underwriting profit better than our 4% companywide calendar-year underwriting profit goal in 2024, and a 50% increase in recurring investment income, compared to 2023."

FY2025 10-K:

Themes

1. Growth Was Demoted One Sentence at a Time

The quarterly MD&A reordered the 96 combined ratio sentence in Q3 2024, a quarter ahead of the 10-K. It dropped the "growing as fast as we can" sentence from Q1 2025 on. The FY2025 10-K led with profit, and by Q2 2026 growth had left the opening sentence, returning as "steady ... despite increased competition." This mirrors the annual dashboard's "Profitability Over Growth" theme. The quarterly record shows each step and shows that it tracked slowing NPW growth (+25% at the Q3 2024 peak, +5% by Q2 2026), not only a stated philosophy.

2. Price Cuts, Falling Retention and a Widening Adequacy Claim Arrived Together

Personal auto rates have been flat to down since Q2 2025, and personal auto retention has been negative in every reading since the FY2024 10-K. "Competition" went from absent to seven mentions per MD&A, and the stated cause moved from Progressive's own mix to the market. In Q2 2026 management called all three major products adequately priced "through the remainder of the year." In the same quarter it deleted its tariff warning, cut commercial auto rates in targeted segments, and reported severity +4% with a 1.6-point worse accident-year loss ratio. That combination is consistent with pricing for share in a softer market; the filings do not say so.

3. The Florida Windfall Was Disclosed at Quarterly Speed

The excess-profit statute moved from first mention to a $950M accrual, a $1.2B charge and a cash payment in four consecutive filings. The measurement basis was quietly redefined from calendar years to accident years. The Florida rate cuts came with the first aggregate countrywide personal auto rate decrease in the series (Q2 2025). Florida is now absent from the 2026 rate discussion, and nothing in the 10-Qs addresses whether the next three-year window is exposed.

4. Property Is Smaller, More Profitable and Not Yet Growing Again

The 2024–2025 retreat (non-renewals, bundling requirements, a non-owner-occupied exit, mandatory deductibles) turned a 166.3 Q2 2024 combined ratio into 20%-plus margins. The "reopening" announced from Q3 2025 has not reversed policy counts: growth-oriented markets went from +20% to −2% and volatile markets reached −17%. Meanwhile the per-occurrence retention rose to $300M. The bundling strategy the annual dashboard highlights paid for this through weaker Robinson (bundled household) applications through 2025.

5. Capital Return Switched From Special Dividends to Buybacks Funded Alongside New Debt

2024–2025 returned capital through annual-variable dividends ($4.50, then $13.50) with token buybacks. In 2026, after the $8.0B payout, Progressive issued $1.5B of notes and bought back 5.4M shares in six months, more than in the prior two years combined, at average prices near $200 against $275.78 in Q2 2025. The buyback wording moved step by step: "or opportunistically" (FY2025 10-K), then "opportunistically" first (Q2 2026). Item 1A carried the same no-material-changes stub in all eight quarters, through the Florida charge, tariffs and the competition language.
