Scoreboard

Two years in one view. Profitability stayed far above Progressive's 4% underwriting profit goal throughout; what changed is the direction. Growth, retention and the accident-year loss ratio have turned, while reserves and capital kept getting stronger.

Latest filing: 10-Q Q2 2026

What the Q2 2026 10-Q adds, for and against. Net income rose $136M, but underwriting profit fell $78M: the accident-year loss ratio worsened for a second quarter and larger reserve releases covered part of it.

Bull case

P3 Reserve adequacyStated
Prior-year reserves release $551M in the quarter and $1,002M in H1; $670M comes from accident year 2025
Q2 2026: "We experienced favorable reserve development of $1,002 million and $607 million during the first six months of 2026 and 2025, respectively"
Q2 2026: "The favorable prior-year reserve development included approximately $670 million attributable to accident year 2025, $220 million to accident year 2024, and the remainder to accident years 2023 and prior."

Size: $1,002M = 18% of H1 underwriting profit ($5,582M) and 2.4 points of the H1 combined ratio.

Accident year 2025 was reserved on a lower severity assumption (7.2% vs 8.8% a year earlier), and it is releasing, citing "lower than anticipated bodily injury severity". So far the reserving looks conservative, not stretched.

Next filing: development still favorable in Q3; the FY2026 10-K's assumed severity for accident year 2026.

P6 Capital and shareholder returnsStated
Buybacks of 5.4M shares ($1.1B) in H1, at lower prices, and a fresh 25M-share authorization
Q2 2026: "During the first six months of 2026, we repurchased 5.4 million common shares, at a total cost of $1.1 billion, both in the open market and to satisfy tax withholding obligations"
Q2 2026: "In May 2026, the Board of Directors approved an authorization for the company to repurchase up to 25 million of its common shares."

Size: $1.1B = 18% of H1 net income ($6.13B) and 14% of H1 operating cash flow ($8.0B); diluted shares 584.2M vs 587.8M (−0.6%). Q2 average price $199.85 vs $275.78 in Q2 2025.

In 2024 and 2025 buybacks were about 0.7M shares a year, mostly withholding on equity awards. The policy sentence now puts "opportunistically" first, and cash flow covers the purchases.

Next filing: continued repurchases with debt-to-total capital near 20% and no new debt.

P1 Policy growthStated
Policies in force pass 40 million: +2.8M (+7%) year over year, direct auto +10%
Q2 2026: "We also surpassed the 40 million policies in force milestone by adding 0.5 million more policies during the quarter and ending the second quarter with 2.8 million more policies in force than at June 30, 2025."

Size: 40.086M vs 37.315M policies; net premiums earned +6% to $21.6B.

Renewals of the 2024–2025 new-business wave (+11% renewal applications) keep the book growing, and direct conversion rose 9%, so the brand still wins the shoppers it sees.

Next filing: policies in force growth holding at 5% or more.

P2 Pricing and loss trendsStated
Commercial Lines margin 14.7% (13.2%) and personal property 22.0% (16.4%)
Q2 2026: "Commercial Lines reported an underwriting profit margin of 14.7% for the second quarter 2026, compared to 13.2% in the same period last year."
Q2 2026: "The strong underwriting profit margin in our personal property products was primarily driven by a low level of incurred catastrophe losses, lower loss frequency during the period, and increased rates."

Size: Commercial Lines $397M (+$33M) and property $171M (+$43M) of underwriting profit, together 21% of the companywide $2,730M.

The two lines that took heavy rate in 2024–2025 now earn more than personal auto, which gives room to reopen property for bundles.

Next filing: property margin through hurricane season (Q3), and commercial auto margin after targeted rate cuts.

P7 Investment portfolioStated
Net income +$136M on higher investment income while underwriting profit fell
Q2 2026: "For the second quarter 2026, we experienced a $136 million year-over-year increase in net income, compared to the second quarter 2025, primarily reflecting an increase in total net investment income."

Size: net income $3.31B (+4%) with underwriting profit $2,730M (−$78M); portfolio $97.2B at a 4.2% book yield, duration 3.5 years.

A second earnings engine is growing with the float, cushioning a normalizing underwriting margin.

Next filing: book yield at or above 4.2% as about $11.2B of non-Treasury principal reinvests over 12 months.

Bear case

P2 Pricing and loss trendsStated
Accident-year loss ratio 70.0 vs 68.4, the second quarter of deterioration; bodily injury severity +7%
Q2 2026: "Accident year – Loss & loss adjustment expense ratio 2 | 70.0 | 68.4 | 1.6 | 69.1 | 67.8 | 1.3"
Q2 2026: "The year-over-year increase in total severity was predominantly driven by bodily injury coverage, due to higher medical costs, more large losses, and a higher rate of plaintiff-attorney represented claims"

Size: 1.6 points on $21.6B of earned premium = about $345M a quarter; catastrophes were nearly flat (3.6 vs 3.5 points). Q1 2026 was +0.9 points.

Loss costs per claim rose 4% while personal auto written premium per policy fell 2%: the core product's margin is compressing from the inside. The filing still calls all three products "adequately priced".

Next filing: Q3 accident-year loss ratio vs 66.6 a year earlier; any return to personal auto rate increases.

P3 Reserve adequacyInferred
Reserve releases supply 2.6 points of margin (1.6 a year ago); without them the margin is about 10.1% vs 12.2%
Q2 2026: "(Increase) decrease to calendar-year combined ratio | 2.6 | pts. | 1.6 | pts. | 2.4 | pts. | 1.5 | pts."
Q2 2026: "Our total loss and LAE ratio increased 0.6 points and 0.4 points, for the three and six months ended June 30, 2026, respectively, compared to the same periods last year, primarily due to higher severity, partially offset by greater favorable prior accident years reserve development."

Size: $551M = 20% of Q2 underwriting profit; margin excluding development 10.1% vs 12.2% (arithmetic on disclosed figures).

The headline margin decline (1.1 points) understates the current-year decline (2.1 points before development). Releases cannot grow forever; if they shrink, the trend shows in full.

Next filing: development as a share of underwriting profit; a fall below 1 point with the accident-year ratio still near 70 would confirm.

P1 Policy growthStated
Premiums +5%, personal vehicle new applications +1%, direct quotes −7%
Q2 2026: "Companywide net premiums written were $21.1 billion, an increase of $1.0 billion, or 5%, compared to the second quarter last year, while net premiums earned increased 6%."
Q2 2026: "Personal vehicles experienced an increase in new business applications of 1% and an increase in renewal business applications of 11% during the second quarter 2026"

Size: premium growth 25% (Q3 2024) → 12% (2025) → 5%; policies in force growth 18% (2024) → 7%. Advertising +16% bought 1% more new applications.

Growth now comes from renewing last year's customers, and renewals themselves stay with Progressive for less time (see P4).

Next filing: new applications growth back above low single digits, or premium growth below 5%.

P4 Retention and competitive positionStated
Personal auto policy life expectancy −8% (12-month) and −9% (3-month); rates cut again
Q2 2026: "For the second quarter 2026, trailing 12-month total personal auto policy life expectancy decreased 8% year over year, while trailing 3-month policy life expectancy decreased 9%, compared to the same period last year."
Q2 2026: "On a countrywide basis, during the second quarter 2026, we decreased personal auto rates by less than 1% and increased personal property rates about 1%, in the aggregate."

Size: direct trailing 3-month −10%; personal auto written premium per policy −2%. The 3-month measure, which the filing calls a possible indicator of the 12-month trend, is the weaker of the two.

The cause list now leads with "increased shopping and competition"; mix, the cause Progressive controls, comes last. Shorter policy lives raise the acquisition cost of each policy.

Next filing: trailing 3-month life expectancy better than −9%.

P2 Pricing and loss trendsStated
Commercial auto buys growth with targeted rate cuts while its injury severity rises
Q2 2026: "Increases in both net premiums written and policies in force were primarily driven by volume growth due to rate decreases in targeted state and BMT combinations, and increased advertising and agent incentive spend."
Q2 2026: "partially offset by higher than anticipated injury severity and litigation defense costs in our core commercial auto products"

Size: core commercial auto trailing 12-month severity +5%; companywide advertising $1.4B (+16%, 0.5 points of expense ratio). Commercial auto took about 9% of rate in 2025.

Two quarters after a year of rate increases, the line is cutting price in places while its own reserves show adverse injury trends. The 14.7% margin is helped by TNC reserve releases.

Next filing: core commercial auto severity and its development line in the reserve note.
