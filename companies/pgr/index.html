FY2018 → FY2019: Destination Era Expands

Filed Feb 2019 | Headcount: 37,346 → 41,571 (+11%) | NPW: $32.6B → $37.6B (+15%)

Business Overview

▶BOP insurance launched in 8 states -- CL expanded beyond auto into GL and propertyNew
Progressive launched its BusinessOwners Policy (BOP) in 8 states, expanding Commercial Lines beyond auto into general liability and property coverage for small businesses. This marked a significant diversification of the CL product suite and laid the groundwork for broader small-business offerings.
▶TNC business detailed for first time: 7% of CL NPW; Uber in 13 states, Lyft in 3New
Transportation Network Company (TNC) insurance was detailed for the first time, comprising 7% of Commercial Lines NPW. Uber coverage was available in 13 states and Lyft in 3 states. This disclosure revealed the emerging importance of rideshare insurance as a distinct CL sub-segment.
▶HQX buy button: 4→14 states; portfolio quoting: 3→33 states; Platinum agents: ~3,300→~3,700Changed
HomeQuote Explorer (HQX) buy button expanded from 4 to 14 states, while portfolio quoting grew dramatically from 3 to 33 states. Platinum agent count increased from ~3,300 to ~3,700. These metrics reflected the rapid scaling of Progressive's Destination Era bundling strategy.
▶Property reporting moved from CFO to PL President -- deeper integration signalChanged
Property reporting was moved from the CFO to the Personal Lines President, signaling a shift in how Progressive viewed its property business -- less as a financial line item and more as an integrated component of the personal lines strategy. This organizational change foreshadowed the eventual absorption of property into PL.

Risk Factors

▶Rating factor restrictions: new paragraph on legislative/regulatory pressure to limit pricing variablesNew
A new paragraph was added addressing legislative and regulatory pressure to restrict the use of certain rating factors in insurance pricing. This was an early signal of the growing scrutiny around data-driven pricing practices that would intensify over subsequent years with AI and algorithmic pricing concerns.
▶Vehicle manufacturing companies (Tesla) added as potential competitorsNew
Vehicle manufacturers, specifically Tesla, were added as potential competitors for the first time. This reflected early recognition that OEMs with direct customer relationships and vehicle data access could enter the insurance market -- a threat that would only grow as connected vehicle technology advanced.
▶Tariff risk and TNC customer risk profile added to pricing uncertaintiesNew
New risk language was added around tariff-related cost pressures on auto parts and the unique risk profile of TNC customers. These additions reflected Progressive's growing awareness of macroeconomic and gig-economy factors that could affect loss costs and pricing adequacy.

FY2019 → FY2020: COVID & Modernization

Filed Mar 2021 | Headcount: 41,571 → 43,326 (+4%) | NPW: $37.6B → $40.6B (+8%)

Business Overview

▶COVID-19 risk factor section added (entirely new Section VII) with comprehensive pandemic impactsNew
An entirely new Section VII was added to risk factors dedicated to COVID-19, covering comprehensive pandemic impacts including operational disruptions, claims frequency changes, investment volatility, and workforce safety. This was the most significant single risk factor addition in the filing's history.
▶Human Capital section replaces "Employees" line -- diversity, retention 91%, 9 ERGs, GainshareNew
The simple "Employees" line item was replaced with a full Human Capital section, driven by new SEC disclosure requirements. The section detailed employee retention at 91%, 9 Employee Resource Groups, and the Gainshare profit-sharing program. This marked a significant expansion in workforce-related disclosure.
▶ARX 100% ownership completed (April 2020); ARX/NCI discussion removed from filingChanged
Progressive completed its acquisition of 100% ownership of ARX (American Strategic Insurance) in April 2020. The ARX/non-controlling interest discussion was subsequently removed from the filing, reflecting the simplified corporate structure post-acquisition.
▶BOP expanded from 8 to 17 states; Snapshot ProView launched for CL without ELDsNew
The BusinessOwners Policy expanded from 8 to 17 states, more than doubling its geographic reach. Additionally, Snapshot ProView was launched for Commercial Lines, enabling usage-based insurance for commercial vehicles without requiring Electronic Logging Devices (ELDs) -- a significant friction reduction.
▶Product model development language generalized from "next generation" event to ongoing processChanged
The language describing product model development shifted from characterizing updates as discrete "next generation" events to describing an ongoing, continuous process. This subtle change reflected the maturation of Progressive's product development approach from episodic upgrades to continuous improvement.

Risk Factors

▶ESG/social responsibility language appears for first time -- brand risk, investment risk, stakeholder languageNew
ESG and social responsibility language appeared in the 10-K for the first time, covering brand risk, investment risk, and broader stakeholder considerations. This marked the beginning of Progressive's ESG disclosure journey that would evolve significantly over subsequent years.
▶Climate language elevated to risk factor heading: "changing climate conditions may be exacerbating these impacts"Changed
Climate language was elevated from incidental mention to a risk factor heading, with the specific language:
"changing climate conditions may be exacerbating these impacts"
This set the stage for the further escalation in FY2021 when "may" would change to "have been."
▶LIBOR/SOFR transition risk factor addedNew
A new risk factor was added addressing the LIBOR-to-SOFR transition, reflecting the industry-wide shift from the London Interbank Offered Rate to the Secured Overnight Financing Rate. This affected Progressive's investment portfolio and any floating-rate financial instruments.
▶Work-from-home, ransom payments, cyber insurance (self-insurance sentence removed) risks updatedChanged
Cyber risk language was updated to address work-from-home vulnerabilities and ransom payment risks. Notably, a sentence about self-insurance for cyber risk was removed, suggesting Progressive may have obtained external cyber coverage. These changes reflected the rapidly evolving cyber threat landscape during the pandemic.

Financial/Forward Indicators

▶Reinsurance fundamentally restructured: ASL ended; aggregate excess of loss introduced; retention $60M→$80M; Florida coverage $1.8B→$1.95BChanged
Progressive's reinsurance program was fundamentally restructured. The Aggregate Stop Loss (ASL) program was ended, replaced by an aggregate excess of loss structure. Per-event retention increased from $60M to $80M, and Florida catastrophe coverage expanded from $1.8B to $1.95B. This shift reflected growing confidence in the balance sheet and a more sophisticated approach to catastrophe risk management.
▶HQX: 14→23 states; portfolio quoting: 33→46 states + DC; agencies: 35,000+→40,000+Changed
HomeQuote Explorer expanded from 14 to 23 states, while portfolio quoting grew from 33 to 46 states plus DC. Total agency count increased from 35,000+ to 40,000+. These metrics demonstrated continued rapid scaling of the Destination Era distribution infrastructure.

FY2020 → FY2021: Post-COVID Acceleration

Filed Mar 2022 | Headcount: 43,326 → ~49,000 (+13%) | NPW: $40.6B → $46.4B (+14%)

Business Overview

▶Protective Insurance acquired for ~$338M (workers' comp, fleet commercial auto)New
Progressive acquired Protective Insurance Corporation, adding workers' compensation and fleet commercial auto to its product portfolio. This marked a significant expansion beyond core personal and commercial auto insurance, diversifying Progressive's underwriting base for the first time in years. Deal valued at approximately $338 million.
▶HQX buy button expanded from 23 states to "almost all states"Changed
HomeQuote Explorer (HQX), Progressive's property insurance quoting platform, expanded from availability in 23 states to "almost all states." This signals the acceleration of Progressive's property bundling strategy -- making home insurance available alongside auto at nearly every point of sale.
▶Snapshot driving data: 35B → 43B miles; commercial UBI expandedChanged
Snapshot usage-based insurance data grew from 35 billion to 43 billion miles collected. Commercial UBI expanded to "almost all states." Progressive continued to lead the industry in telematics data collection, reinforcing its pricing advantage in both personal and commercial lines.
▶COVID responses section removed from Human CapitalRemoved
The dedicated COVID-19 workplace response section that appeared in the FY2020 filing was removed entirely from the Human Capital discussion. This signals Progressive's view that COVID-related operational disruptions had been fully absorbed into normal business operations.

Risk Factors

▶Climate language: "may be adversely affected" → "have been and will likely continue to be"Changed
FY2020: "may be adversely affected"
FY2021: "have been and will likely continue to be adversely affected"
A significant escalation from prospective to retrospective language. Progressive acknowledged climate impacts as realized, not merely theoretical. This is the strongest climate language upgrade in the entire 7-year corpus.
▶ESG investment risk factor addedNew
A new risk factor addressing ESG-related investment considerations was added for the first time. This reflected growing regulatory and investor pressure around environmental, social, and governance factors in Progressive's substantial investment portfolio ($51.5B).
▶ML/AI risk first mentioned: "challenges in using machine learning and artificial intelligence"New
First appearance of AI/ML risk language in Progressive's 10-K filings. The language was broad and prospective, identifying "challenges in using machine learning and artificial intelligence" as a risk factor. This planted the seed for what would become an increasingly detailed AI risk disclosure over subsequent years.
▶Workplace policy risk (remote/hybrid) addedNew
New risk language addressing the challenges of remote and hybrid work policies, reflecting post-COVID workforce management concerns including talent retention, productivity, and workplace culture in a distributed environment.

Key Forward Indicators

▶Platinum agents: 3,976 → ~4,500; Investment portfolio: $47.5B → $51.5BSignal
Platinum agent count grew ~13%, tracking headcount growth. Investment portfolio expanded to $51.5B as premium volume growth flowed into investable assets. Both metrics signal Progressive's accelerating scale in the post-COVID recovery period.

FY2021 → FY2022: The Inflationary Shock

Filed Feb 2023 | Headcount: ~49,000 → ~55,100 (+12%) | NPW: $46.4B → $51.1B (+10%)

Business Overview

▶Vision statement and four strategic pillars formalized for first timeNew
Progressive formalized its vision statement and four strategic pillars for the first time in its 10-K filing. This represented a shift from implicit strategy to explicit strategic framework communicated to investors. The pillars provided a structured lens for evaluating management's priorities across subsequent filings.
▶TNC (Uber/Lyft) at 10% of Commercial Lines NPW (was 6%)Changed
Transportation Network Company insurance grew from 6% to 10% of Commercial Lines NPW. This near-doubling of TNC share reflects both the post-COVID rideshare recovery and Progressive's success in capturing share in this specialized segment.
▶Property claims staff first disclosed (~880); plan to increaseNew
For the first time, Progressive disclosed its property claims staffing level at approximately 880 employees, along with plans to increase this number. This disclosure signaled management's commitment to building property claims capabilities as the segment grew in strategic importance.
▶Diversity metrics first disclosed: 59% women, 39% POCNew
Progressive disclosed detailed workforce diversity metrics for the first time: 59% women and 39% people of color. This represented the high-water mark of D&I disclosure granularity, which would later be scaled back significantly.

Risk Factors

▶96 combined ratio goal first formally disclosed in risk factorsNew
The 96 combined ratio target was formally disclosed in risk factors for the first time with the language:
"growing as fast as we can as long as we can provide high-quality customer service at or below a companywide 96 combined ratio"
This codified what had been an understood but informal target, creating an explicit accountability benchmark in the filing.
▶AI/ML risk expanded: added "and effective manner"Changed
The AI/ML risk factor language was incrementally expanded, adding "and effective manner" to the risk description. A small but notable tightening -- signaling that effectiveness, not just implementation, was now recognized as a risk dimension.
▶"Big data" regulatory scrutiny language addedNew
New language addressing regulatory scrutiny of "big data" usage in insurance pricing. This foreshadowed the more specific AI/algorithm regulatory language that would appear in subsequent filings as state regulators began examining insurer use of data and algorithms.

Financial Signals

▶Investment total return swung from +$1.2B to -$4.3B (rate-driven fixed income losses)Signal
Progressive's investment portfolio total return swung dramatically from +$1.2 billion to -$4.3 billion, driven by unrealized losses in the fixed income portfolio as interest rates rose sharply. This was the most severe investment result in the 7-year period and highlighted the interest rate sensitivity of Progressive's large investment portfolio.
▶ESG investment language strengthened to affirmative risk beliefChanged
ESG investment language was strengthened from passive acknowledgment to an affirmative statement of risk belief. Progressive moved from noting ESG as a consideration to actively incorporating ESG factors into investment risk assessment. This language would persist even as the "ESG" label was later changed to "Sustainability."

FY2022 → FY2023: Rate-Driven Recovery

Filed Feb 2024 | Headcount: ~55,100 → ~61,400 (+11%) | NPW: $51.1B → $61.6B (+21%)

Business Overview

▶Commercial Lines deliberately pulled back (18% → 16% of NPW)Signal
Commercial Lines' share of total NPW declined from 18% to 16%, reflecting a deliberate strategic pullback. Progressive chose to prioritize underwriting profitability over top-line growth in commercial, redirecting capacity to personal lines where rate adequacy was improving more rapidly.
▶Snapshot mileage dropped from "over 48 billion" to generic "billions of"Changed
FY2022: "over 48 billion miles"
FY2023: "billions of miles"
The shift from specific to generic language suggests Snapshot/UBI data collection had matured to the point where specific mileage figures were no longer a competitive differentiator worth highlighting. The program had become table stakes.
▶Patents added for chatbots, commercial classification, document classificationNew
Progressive disclosed new patents related to chatbots, commercial classification, and document classification. These patent filings signal investment in AI-powered customer service and underwriting automation -- the operational infrastructure for scaling without proportional headcount growth.
▶Property claims staff: 880 → ~1,100Changed
Property claims staff grew approximately 25% from ~880 to ~1,100, reflecting Progressive's continued investment in building property claims capabilities. This growth outpaced overall headcount growth (+11%), signaling property as a priority investment area.

Risk Factors

▶Standalone GenAI risk factor added -- biased datasets, discriminatory outcomes, NAIC model bulletin, Colorado SB 169New
A standalone generative AI risk factor was added for the first time, covering: biased datasets, discriminatory outcomes, the NAIC AI model bulletin, and Colorado SB 169. This was a significant escalation from the generic ML/AI language in prior years, reflecting the rapid emergence of GenAI as both an operational tool and a regulatory concern in the insurance industry.
▶Rating factor restrictions strengthened: "algorithms, AI, and complex models" under scrutinyChanged
Regulatory language around rating factors was strengthened to explicitly name "algorithms, AI, and complex models" as subject to increased scrutiny. This reflected the growing state-level regulatory focus on algorithmic pricing transparency in insurance.

Financial Signals

▶NPW surge +21% from rate increases flowing throughSignal
Net premiums written surged 21% to $61.6 billion, driven primarily by rate increases flowing through the book. This was the culmination of Progressive's pricing response to 2022 severity trends, demonstrating the company's ability to achieve rate adequacy faster than competitors.
▶Investment total return recovered: -$4.3B → +$3.8BSignal
Investment portfolio total return recovered sharply from -$4.3 billion to +$3.8 billion, a swing of over $8 billion. The recovery reflected stabilizing interest rates and the benefit of reinvesting at higher yields, creating a tailwind that would persist into subsequent years.

FY2023 → FY2024: Profit Target Met, Growth Switched Back On

Filed Mar 2025 | Revenue: $62.1B → $75.4B | Net income: $3.90B → $8.48B | Diluted EPS: $6.58 → $14.40 | NPW: $61.6B → $74.4B (+21%) | Employees: ~61,400 → ~66,300

MD&A: Growth vs. Profitability

▶MD&A deletes both "profitability takes precedence over growth" sentences; focus "shifted ... to driving growth"Removed
FY2023: "While growth is important, we strongly believe that achieving target profitability is our most important objective and will take precedence over growth."
FY2024: "we continued to lift the temporary non-rate actions implemented in prior years in our personal auto and core commercial auto businesses, as our focus shifted from achieving our target profit margin to driving growth"
The FY2023 MD&A said profit came first twice (the second time as "Our focus on achieving our target underwriting profitability takes precedence over growth"). Neither sentence survives in the FY2024 MD&A. The precedence line lives on only in the risk factors, where it was already present in FY2023. In the management discussion, the 2023 profit-first posture is now described as finished. The underwriting margin of 11.2% against a 4% target gave them room to say so.
▶Advertising up 150% to a record $4.0B, with a new floor: "at or above this level"New
FY2023: "Our total advertising spend was 21% lower in 2023, compared to the prior year."
FY2024: "Throughout the year we increased our media spend to maximize growth and will continue to spend at or above this level as long as we remain on track to achieve our target profitability and can acquire customers at or below our target acquisition cost."
Spend went from $1.6B to $4.0B, and the expense ratio rose 2.4 points, mainly because of it. The filing then commits to keeping that dollar level as a floor, subject to the profit target and acquisition-cost conditions. Watch next year whether the floor survives.
▶Personal auto declared "adequately priced"; 2024 auto rate +3% vs. +19% in 2023, while property rates rose 19%New
FY2024: "we currently believe that, in most states, we are adequately priced in our personal auto product and in our core commercial auto product we expect to have relatively modest rate increases in 2025."
FY2024: "Aggregate personal auto rate changes during 2024 were modest, relative to the prior two years, and we expect rates to stay relatively stable in 2025. In our personal property business, we increased rates about 19% countrywide, in the aggregate during 2024, and we expect continued rate increases in 2025."
This is the first time in the period that the filing says the auto rate cycle is over: auto rates were up 3% in 2024 against 19% in 2023. Property is the opposite case, with a 19% increase and more promised. The FY2025 filing tests both statements: auto rates were cut and Florida profits had to be refunded.
▶Auto retention reverses (12-month PLE +24% → -4%; direct -7%), and the 3-month measure is newly called a leading indicatorSignal
FY2023: "Our trailing 12-month total personal auto policy life expectancy was up 24% year over year, with Agency up 29% and Direct up 19%."
FY2024: "Although the latter can reflect more volatility and is more sensitive to seasonality, this measure is more responsive to current experience and may be an indicator for the future trend of our 12-month measure. Our trailing 12-month total personal auto policy life expectancy was down 4% year over year, with agency personal auto up 2% and direct down 7%."
The trailing 3-month measure was also down 4%. The added sentence tells readers to treat it as a forecast of the 12-month number, which implies further decline. Direct conversion fell 9% as advertising brought in "a greater number of casual shoppers obtaining quotes". The growth push is buying policies with shorter expected lives. In FY2025, both measures fell further (-7% and -10%).
▶Homeowners new business restricted to auto bundles in "about half of the country"; non-owner-occupied homes exitedNew
FY2023: "In regions where our appetite to write new business is limited, we continued to prioritize Progressive auto bundles, as well as lower risk properties, such as new construction or homes with newer roofs."
FY2024: "putting underwriting restrictions in place in about half of the country, by the end of 2024, to only accept new personal property business (e.g., home or condo) when the property policy is bundled with a Progressive personal auto policy, where permitted; (iii) began restricting new business and non-renewing policies that provide coverage for non-owner occupied properties"
"Prioritize" became "only accept", the restriction now has a stated geographic scope, and a whole market segment is being exited. Mandatory wind and hail deductibles and roof depreciation schedules were also expanded. Property policies in force fell 13% in volatile-weather states. In FY2025, these restrictions are blamed for stalling growth in the Robinsons (home-and-auto bundle) segment.
▶Portfolio credit floor lowered from A+ to A, effective January 1, 2025; duration extended 3.0 → 3.3 yearsChanged
FY2023: "Credit Risk This exposure is managed by maintaining an A+ minimum weighted average portfolio credit quality rating"
FY2024: "Effective January 1, 2025, in light of the downgrade of the U.S. government obligations, to manage our credit risk exposure, we moved our internal ratings guideline for our portfolio down from an A+ to an A minimum weighted average portfolio credit rating."
The stated reason is the Treasury downgrade: U.S. government obligations are 57% of the portfolio. Even so, a lower minimum quality guideline gives the portfolio room to add credit risk. Duration was also lengthened "to take advantage of higher yields."

Business Overview

▶Platinum agents fell from nearly 7,500 to nearly 7,100, the first decline in the bundling-agent programSignal
FY2023: "we had nearly 7,500 Platinum agents"
FY2024: "we had nearly 7,100 Platinum agents"
The decline started a year earlier than this dashboard previously showed. The prior version recorded FY2023 as ~5,800 and FY2024 as a +22% "surge". The drop coincides with the homeowners restrictions above, since Platinum agents are the channel for bundled auto and home. The share of 12-month agency auto policies, which Progressive writes "to promote bundled personal auto and property growth," also fell from 14% to 12%.
▶Property segment folded into Personal Lines in Q4 2024; homeowners rank slips to 11th and the ranking outlook becomes "unable to determine"Changed
FY2024: "beginning in the fourth quarter 2024, personal residential property insurance for homeowners and renters. The Personal Lines segment accounted for 85% of our total net premiums written"
FY2023: "We were the tenth largest homeowners carrier in the U.S., based on 2022 premiums written, and we estimate that we retained this ranking for 2023."
FY2024: "We were the eleventh largest homeowners carrier in the U.S., based on 2023 premiums written, and we are currently unable to determine if we will hold that ranking for 2024."
Property is no longer a reportable segment. Its results, a 98.3 combined ratio in 2024, now sit inside Personal Lines as "personal property", about 5% of the segment. The ranking hedge fits the shrinking footprint: the following year the company ranks 12th.
▶Personal umbrella reinsurance dropped for 2025; reinsurance now "readily available and competitively priced"Changed
FY2024: "we chose not to reinsure our personal umbrella business in 2025."
FY2023: "the cost of reinsurance in the markets in which we participate increased for the coverages placed during 2023 and the beginning of 2024"
FY2024: "were readily available and competitively priced. On a year-over-year basis, we did not incur a material change in the aggregate costs of our reinsurance programs."
Progressive now retains a line it used to cede, at a time when it describes the reinsurance market as easy, not tight. Florida first-event cover fell from $2.4B to $2.1B as a $200M cat bond matured. The aggregate program, which was triggered in 2023 by $17.9M, ceded nothing in 2024.
▶Regulators let personal-vehicle insurers exceed the standard 3:1 premiums-to-surplus ratioNew
FY2024: "however, certain states have permitted us to target a premiums-to-surplus ratio for our personal vehicle insurance companies in excess of 3 to 1 based on our strong financial condition."
This is new relief. The FY2023 text stated only the generally accepted 3:1 limit. A higher ratio means less surplus is needed per premium dollar at the insurance subsidiaries. This fits the record dividends that followed: $2.9B declared in 2024 and $8.1B in 2025. The FY2025 filing quantifies the relief: two states, a 3.5:1 maximum, and 91% of premiums.

Risk Factors

▶New TNC customer-concentration risk: loss of "geographic coverage" from rideshare customers, with TNC at 15% of Commercial LinesNew
FY2024: "Additionally, our Commercial Lines business may be adversely affected by a loss of or reduction in geographic coverage from one or more customers, including transportation network company customers."
TNC rose to 15% of Commercial Lines premiums (13% in 2023), written for just two counterparties: Uber in 16 states and Lyft in 4. This new sentence anticipated what happened next. In FY2025, Lyft disappears from the Business section and Commercial Lines premiums fall 3% "primarily due to certain TNC policies that were not renewed."
▶AI regulation moves from "likely" to present tense; "nearly half" of states adopted the NAIC bulletin; Colorado SB 169 detail deletedChanged
FY2023: "Additionally, it is likely that we will be subject to new AI-focused regulations"
FY2024: "Additionally, we are subject to new AI-focused regulations and regulatory expectations"
FY2024: "Nearly half of all departments of insurance have adopted the NAIC AI model bulletin. Other states have adopted or are considering alternatives, including comprehensive AI legislation"
What was a forecast is now stated as fact. In FY2023 the risk named one adopter, "Alaska recently adopted the NAIC model bulletin". FY2024 says nearly half of state insurance departments have adopted it. The Colorado SB 169 governance-regulation sentence was removed. A clause saying observers' views of its GenAI use could "negatively impact our stock price" was also deleted.
▶Reserve-risk list adds jury verdicts and punitive damagesNew
FY2024: "the outcome of lawsuits against us or our competitors, including unanticipatedly high jury verdicts or punitive damage awards"
This is a new bullet among the factors that could make loss reserves inadequate. It names social inflation directly, in the same year Progressive's assumed accident-year severity for personal auto liability reserves rose from 7.4% to 8.8%. In FY2025, the MD&A attributes higher bodily-injury severity to "a higher rate of plaintiff-attorney represented claims."
▶Workforce risk flips direction: from too many remote workers to too fewChanged
FY2023: "including any impact arising from an increase in remote and hybrid workers relative to historic levels"
FY2024: "including any impact arising from a decrease in virtual and hybrid workers relative to recent market trends"
The risk used to be that remote work would erode culture. Now it is that fewer virtual and hybrid workers, meaning more in-office work, could hurt productivity. The workplace-policy risk was also rewritten to name "virtual, hybrid, and in-person work protocols". Both changes are consistent with a return-to-office move, though the filing does not describe the policy.

Human Capital

▶Demographic data, the "DEI" label and the 10%→20% senior-leader diversity goal all removed a year before its deadlineRemoved
FY2023: "As of December 31, 2023, we had about 61,400 employees of whom 60% were women and 41% were people of color"
FY2023: "we introduced an ambitious aspiration to double the representation of people of color in senior leadership from 10% to 20% by the end of 2025."
FY2024: "As of December 31, 2024, we had about 66,300 employees. We disclose our consolidated EEO-1 data on our website."
The whole "Demographic Data" block was cut: workforce, hires, promotions and senior-leader percentages. So were the DEI acronym, the DEI website link, the Board-diversity paragraph and the executives' "DEI leadership job objectives". The goal was deleted at 18%, with its end-2025 deadline still a year away. The retreat happened in FY2024, not FY2025 as this dashboard previously recorded.

FY2024 → FY2025: Profit Cap, TNC Loss and a Stalled Bundle

Filed Mar 2026 | Revenue: $75.4B → $87.7B | Net income: $8.48B → $11.31B | Diluted EPS: $14.40 → $19.23 | NPW: $74.4B → $83.2B (+12%) | Employees: ~66,300 → ~70,000

MD&A: Pricing, Growth & Retention

▶Florida statutory profit cap triggered: $1.2B policyholder credit, 1.7 points of Personal Lines margin; risk factors add "limit the profit an insurer may earn"New
FY2025: "Despite actions to lower rates, beginning in the fourth quarter 2024 and through 2025, our personal auto profit in Florida for the 2023 to 2025 period exceeded the statutory profit limit that a Florida statute imposes on the profit that any insurance group can earn on personal auto insurance over any three-accident-year period."
FY2024: "the ability to discontinue or exit unprofitable businesses or jurisdictions, impose marketing restrictions"
FY2025: "the ability to discontinue or exit unprofitable businesses or jurisdictions, limit the profit an insurer may earn, impose marketing restrictions"
Florida tort reform helped profits so much that a profit ceiling took effect. That made it a $1.2B expense booked as policyholder credits. Without it, the Personal Lines margin would have been 14.2%, not 12.5%. The regulatory-limits list in Business now also includes "excess profitability". Profit caps now appear as a named risk, not only as a one-off charge.
▶Auto rates cut (<1%) in 2025, but tariffs could force "rate increases throughout 2026"Changed
FY2024: "we expect rates to stay relatively stable in 2025"
FY2025: "While our focus has been on trying to maintain stable rates for customers, effective tariffs and other retaliatory actions may result in higher loss costs, which could result in a reduction in profitability and the possible need for rate increases throughout 2026."
Personal auto rates fell slightly ("we decreased personal auto rates less than 1%") while total auto severity rose 6% after 1% in 2024. Bodily injury severity rose 10%. The "adequately priced" claim is kept but now comes with a tariff warning. That hedge was not in FY2024's "relatively stable" outlook.
▶Advertising rises to $5.1B, but the "at or above this level" floor is droppedChanged

FY2025: "We invested heavily in advertising during 2025 to capture consumer shopping, and will continue to advertise to maximize growth, as long as we remain on track to achieve our profitability goal and can acquire customers at or below our target acquisition cost."
Last year's spending floor did not carry into FY2025, even though 2025 spend set another record (+$1.1B). The commitment is now only to keep advertising. That leaves room to cut spending as premium growth slows (+12% vs. +21%) and new auto applications decelerate (+9% vs. +44%).

Last year the filing named the 3-month measure as the leading indicator. It is now worse than the 12-month figure, so the decline is not yet bottoming. The new paragraph argues that some of the decline is an artifact of re-quoting, not lost customers, but it gives no size for that effect. "Changes in billing plans" are also cited.

The property de-risking reported in FY2024 succeeded on margin: personal property's underwriting margin was 24.9%. This confirms in reported results the litigation risk added to the reserve-risk list in FY2024. Frequency kept falling, with total personal auto down 3% in 2025 and the BI line down 1%, and that masked the severity rise.

Themes

Profitability Over Growth

Profit-first was the FY2022-2023 posture: rate over volume and a Commercial Lines pullback. FY2024 reversed it in the MD&A ("shifted from achieving our target profit margin to driving growth"; ads +150%). FY2025 shows the bill for over-earning: a $1.2B Florida profit-cap credit and an ad-spend floor quietly dropped. The risk factors still say profitability "takes precedence over growth".

Direct Channel Winning

50% → 57% in PL vehicle; 23% → 28% in property. The shift accelerated in the most recent year (+2pp). Platinum agents fell two years running (nearly 7,500 → 6,000), and FY2025 adds a risk factor on agent-channel consolidation. Retention is the cost: personal auto PLE fell 4% then 7% as advertising brought in shoppers.

AI as Competitive Threat

From no mention (FY2020) to "agentic AI" and competitors able to "replace legacy systems" (FY2025) in 7 years. AI regulation went from "likely" (FY2023) to present tense (FY2024). Progressive now also names its own "legacy systems" as a barrier to innovation.

Property as Bundling Enabler

Separate segment → folded into PL (Q4 2024). This theme is contradicted in FY2025: the de-risking restrictions (homeowners only with auto, in about half the country) cut homeowners new apps 45% and left Robinsons flat. Restrictions began lifting in late 2025. Next-gen models now cover ~90% of premiums, and embedded renters offers a bundle without a home policy.

NPW 2.5x in 7 Years ($32.6B → $83.2B)

$32.6B → $83.2B. Rate + share gains in a hardening market. Investment portfolio grew from $33.6B to $97.4B. Statutory surplus more than doubled ($11.7B → $28.4B). One of the fastest scale-ups in P&C history.

ESG → Sustainability

Label changed but investment approach unchanged. Climate risk language ("have been and will likely continue to be") locked in since FY2021, though FY2024 softened "may be becoming more severe" to "may become". D&I demographics appeared in FY2022-2023 and were removed in FY2024 with the senior-leader diversity goal; FY2025 renamed the section.
