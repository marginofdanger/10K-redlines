Latest filing: 20-F FY2025

What the FY2025 20-F adds, for and against. The operating record is the strongest in the window on every margin line; the bear evidence is about what comes next: a capex step-up that outpaces cash flow, more revenue from fewer customers, and U.S. trade measures aimed directly at chips.

Bull case

P1 AI / HPC demand and growthStated
Revenue +31.6% to NT$3,809.1B; HPC +48% to NT$2,192.9B, now 58% of revenue (51%)
FY2025: "The increase in our net revenue from 2024 to 2025 mainly came from High Performance Computing of NT$716,040 million, or a 48% year-over-year increase, and from Smartphone of NT$105,686 million, or a 11% year-over-year increase."
FY2025: "High Performance Computing | 934,769 | 43% | 1,476,891 | 51% | 2,192,931 | 58%"

Size: HPC added NT$716.0B = 78% of the NT$914.7B revenue increase. In US$ at weighted-average rates revenue rose about 36% (US$90.1B to US$122.4B; arithmetic on stated rates).

Second year in a row of 30%+ growth led by HPC. Unlike 2024, it came with volume as well as price: wafer shipments +16.4%, revenue per wafer +13.1%.

Next filing: HPC share above 58% and wafer shipments rising toward the “exceeded 17 million” capacity.

P3 MarginsStated
Gross margin 59.9% (56.1%) and operating margin 50.8% (45.7%), despite a stronger NT dollar
FY2025: "In 2025, our gross margin increased to 59.9% of net revenue from 56.1% in 2024, mainly attributable to higher capacity utilization and cost improvement, partially offset by unfavorable foreign exchange rate."

Size: operating income NT$1,936.1B (+46.4%); 3.8 points of gross margin on NT$3,809.1B revenue is about NT$145B of gross profit. The NT$ appreciated about 3% (NT$32.13 → NT$31.11), roughly 1 point of operating margin at the stated 0.3-point-per-1% sensitivity (inference).

Margins rose for a second year while Arizona and Kumamoto were ramping and N2 entered production; electricity and N3 costs named in FY2024 are no longer listed as offsets.

Next filing: gross margin held near 60% through the N2 ramp and a full year of overseas fabs.

P2 Technology lead and pricingStated
Advanced nodes 74% of wafer revenue (69%); N2 in volume production on schedule
FY2025: "Advanced technologies (7-nanometer and below) accounted for 74% of total wafer revenue, up from 69% in 2024."
FY2025: "Our 2-nanometer technology entered volume production in 2025."

Size: 3nm 24% (18%) and 5nm 36% (34%) of wafer revenue; wafer revenue is about 86% of net revenue. Revenue per 12-inch-equivalent wafer NT$253.6K vs NT$224.2K (+13.1%; arithmetic).

The FY2024 20-F said N2 volume production was “expected in 2025”; it arrived, and the next node (16-angstrom) is guided to risk production in 2026.

Next filing: 2nm shown as a line in the wafer-revenue table, and A16 risk production in 2026 as guided.

P5 Balance sheet and liquidityStated
Cash and current securities NT$3,068.6B (+NT$646.6B) against NT$1,033.0B of long-term debt
FY2025: "Our cash, cash equivalents and current marketable securities increased to NT$3,068,595 million (US$97,820 million) as of December 31, 2025, compared to NT$2,422,019 million as of December 31, 2024."
FY2025: "In 2025, we generated NT$2,274,976 million (US$72,521 million) net cash from operating activities"

Size: net cash about NT$2.04T (NT$1.40T) = 38% of equity of NT$5,396.2B; operating cash flow +NT$448.8B (+24.6%). Short-term loans nil; bonds carry fixed rates of 0.41% to 4.63%.

Cash and cash equivalents still rose NT$640.2B after capex and dividends, so the 2026 step-up in capex can be met from the balance sheet if cash flow stalls.

Next filing: net cash held above NT$2T with 2026 capex at US$52–56B.

P4 Capital intensity and cash returnStated
Quarterly dividend raised twice in 2025, NT$4.50 → NT$5.00 → NT$6.00; NT$22 declared for the year (NT$17)
FY2025: "Cash dividends to shareholders | $ 129,663.1 | $ 129,662.9 | $ 155,595.1 | $ 155,595.1 Cash dividends per share (NT$) | $ 5.00 | $ 5.00 | $ 6.00 | $ 6.00"

Size: NT$570.5B declared for 2025 = 34% of net income; cash dividends paid NT$466.8B = 47% of FCF of NT$1,002.6B.

The dividend grew 29% and was still covered about twice by free cash flow, with no debt-funded return of capital.

Next filing: dividends paid still under 60% of FCF as capex rises.

P7 Geopolitics, trade and export controlsStated
U.S.–Taiwan deal caps reciprocal tariffs at 15%, with preferential Section 232 treatment for chipmakers investing in the U.S.
FY2025: "Further, in January 2026, the U.S. announced it had reached a trade and investment agreement with Taiwan which would reduce U.S. reciprocal tariff rates on Taiwanese goods to no more than 15%."
FY2025: "pursuant to which we may need to obtain an export license prior to shipping products using 16- nanometer or below process to specified destinations unless specific conditions are met"

Size: not quantified in the filing; it bears on North America revenue of NT$2,875.3B (75%). The 16nm licence rule now reaches “specified destinations” where the FY2024 20-F said “any global destination”.

TSMC’s U.S. investment is the condition for the preferential treatment, so Arizona doubles as a tariff hedge (inference). The filing calls the details “still unclear”.

Next filing: confirmed Section 232 exemption or offset for TSMC, and no tariff named as a cost.

Bear case

P4 Capital intensity and cash returnStated
2026 capex guided to US$52–56B (2025: US$40.9B); purchase obligations NT$1,534.6B (+34%)
FY2025: "Our capital expenditures in 2026 are expected to be between US$52 billion and US$56 billion, which, depending on market conditions, may be adjusted later."
FY2025: "Capital Purchase or Other Purchase Obligations(4) | 1,534,575 | 1,220,393 | 314,181 | 1 | —"

Size: +27% to +37% in US$; at NT$31.11 about NT$1.62–1.74T = 71–77% of 2025 operating cash flow (capex was 56% in 2025). FCF / net income already fell to 59% from 75%. Purchase obligations = 67% of 2025 operating cash flow, NT$1,220.4B due within a year.

Spending is committed ahead of demand: a fall in AI orders would leave depreciation and commitments in place. FCF grew 15% in 2025 while net income grew 47%.

Next filing: 2026 capex inside the guide with FCF / net income at or above 59%.

P4 Capital intensity and cash returnStated
Customer prepayments for reserved capacity fell from NT$291.1B to NT$189.9B
FY2025: "Noncurrent portion (classified under other noncurrent liabilities) | 92,499.2 | 43,298.9 $ 291,101.8 | $ 189,858.2"

Size: −NT$101.2B (−35%) = 4.4% of 2025 operating cash flow; accrued expenses and other current liabilities were a NT$103.2B working-capital outflow (2024: +NT$74.7B).

Temporary receipts are customers’ payments to retain capacity. They are being offset against receivables faster than new ones arrive, so customers are funding a smaller share of the build-out while capex rises (inference; the filing gives no reason).

Next filing: temporary receipts rebuilt, or a further decline alongside higher capex.

P6 Customer and market concentrationStated
Ten largest customers 78% of revenue (76%); second largest 17% (12%); North America 75% (70%)
FY2025: "our ten largest customers in 2023, 2024 and 2025 accounted for approximately, 70%, 76% and 78% of our net revenue in the respective year."
FY2025: "Our second largest customer in 2023, 2024 and 2025 accounted for 11%, 12%, and 17% of our net revenue in the respective year."
FY2025: "North America | 1,470,215 | 68% | 2,031,326 | 70% | 2,875,270 | 75%"

Size: top two customers NT$1,372.2B = 36% of revenue (34%). The customer that was 12% in 2024 is now the largest: NT$352.3B → NT$727.0B (+106%; note 38).

The fastest-growing customer is now the biggest, and growth comes from one end market and one region. The bull reading (the largest customer’s share fell to 19%) is a change of leader, not a broader base.

Next filing: ten-largest share at or below 78% and no single customer above 20%.

P7 Geopolitics, trade and export controlsStated
Section 232: 25% U.S. tariff on certain advanced computing chips; Section 301 investigations name Taiwan
FY2025: "As a result, the U.S. issues a proclamation imposing a 25% ad valorem tariff on the import of certain advanced computing chips and derivative products meeting specified technical performance thresholds, unless such products are imported for designated use in the U.S."
FY2025: "in March 2026, the U.S. Trade Representative, using authority in Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, initiated two investigations relating to alleged manufacturing overcapacity and failures to take action on forced labor, targeting various economies, including Taiwan, which could result in trade actions such as the imposition of additional tariffs."

Size: not quantified in the filing; the tariff targets the AI chips behind HPC revenue of NT$2,192.9B (58%). The Nanjing VEU also expired in December 2025 (now an annual licence; China noncurrent assets NT$65.0B, 1.7% of the total).

In the FY2024 20-F semiconductors were excluded from the tariffs; this filing describes a chip-specific tariff in force and new investigations, with the offsets “still unclear”.

Next filing: whether any tariff cost or demand effect is named, and the outcome of the Section 301 probes.

P8 Overseas expansion executionInferred
Losses attributed to minority holders nearly tripled, NT$0.86B → NT$2.48B, as the overseas joint-venture fabs ramp
FY2025: "Non-controlling interests | ( 712.3 ) | ( 856.3 ) | ( 2,479.1 ) | ( 79.0 )"
FY2025: "adverse changes relating to government grants or other government incentives, including non-receipt, delay and potential clawbacks of government grants"

Size: NT$2.48B is 0.1% of net income. The main minority-owned subsidiaries are JASM (TSMC 72.6%) and ESMC (70.0%), so their combined losses are likely several times that figure (inference; the filing gives no subsidiary results). Grants received NT$76.3B = 6% of capex.

Small today, but it is the only line in the statements that tracks the economics of the overseas fabs, and it is moving the wrong way as Kumamoto 2 and Dresden ramp.

Next filing: the minority-interest loss narrowing, or any disclosure of overseas fab margins.

Earlier filings

20-F FY2024 (filed April 17, 2025)

Bull case

P1 AI / HPC demand and growthStated
Revenue +33.9% to NT$2,894.3B after a 4.5% decline; HPC +58% to 51% of revenue (43%)
FY2024: "The increase in our net revenue from 2023 to 2024 mainly came from High Performance Computing of NT$542,122 million, or a 58% year-over-year increase, and from Smartphone of NT$190,216 million, or a 23% year-over-year increase."

Size: HPC added NT$542.1B = 74% of the NT$732.6B increase; net income NT$1,158.4B (+36.0%).

The recovery from 2023 was led by AI data-center demand rather than a smartphone cycle, and it was mostly price/mix: revenue per wafer +24.5% on shipments +7.6%.

Next filing: HPC growth continuing above company growth (FY2025: +48%, 58% of revenue).

P3 MarginsStated
Gross margin 56.1% (54.4%) and operating margin 45.7% (42.6%) despite the N3 ramp and power costs
FY2024: "In 2024, our gross margin increased to 56.1% of net revenue from 54.4% in 2023, mainly attributable to higher capacity utilization and productivity gains, and partially offset by 3-nanometer ramp-up and higher electricity cost."

Size: operating income NT$1,322.1B (+43.5%); the NT$ weakened (NT$31.19 → NT$32.13), a tailwind of about 1 point of operating margin at the stated 0.4-point-per-1% sensitivity (inference).

Margins expanded while absorbing two named cost headwinds, which says pricing power was intact; part of the gain was currency.

Next filing: margin held without the FX tailwind (FY2025: 59.9% despite NT$ appreciation).

P2 Technology lead and pricingStated
7nm and below 69% of wafer revenue (58%); 3nm at 18% in its first full year
FY2024: "In 2024, the 3-nanometer, 5-nanometer and 7-nanometer revenues represented 18%, 34% and 17% of total wafer revenue, respectively. Advanced technologies (7-nanometer and below) accounted for 69% of total wafer revenue, up from 58% in 2023."

Size: an 11-point mix shift in one year; revenue per wafer NT$224.2K vs NT$180.1K (arithmetic).

The fastest mix shift in the three years shown, and the main reason revenue grew four times faster than shipments.

Next filing: N2 in volume production in 2025 as guided (FY2025: met; advanced share 74%).

P4 Capital intensity and cash returnStated
Capex flat at NT$956.0B while operating cash flow rose 47%: FCF NT$870.2B, 75% of net income (34%)
FY2024: "Our capital expenditures in 2022, 2023 and 2024 were NT$1,082,672 million, NT$949,817 million and NT$956,007 million (US$29,755 million, translated from a weighted average exchange rate of NT$32.13 to US$1.00), respectively."
FY2024: "In 2024, we generated NT$1,826,177 million (US$55,693 million) net cash from operating activities"

Size: FCF NT$870.2B vs NT$292.2B (XBRL); dividends paid NT$363.1B = 42% of FCF; capex inside the US$28–32B guide.

The year capex paused let cash conversion reset higher; dividends were raised from NT$3.50 to NT$4.50 a quarter, all from free cash flow.

Next filing: FCF conversion held as capex rises to US$38–42B (FY2025: 59%, lower).

Bear case

P6 Customer and market concentrationStated
Ten largest customers 76% of revenue, up from 70%; second largest 12%
FY2024: "our ten largest customers in 2022, 2023 and 2024 accounted for approximately, 68%, 70% and 76% of our net revenue in the respective year."
FY2024: "These shifting business models could lead to significant variations in our sales if the growth of their products and services, particularly in the AI sector, is volatile or not sustainable."

Next filing: ten-largest share stable (FY2025: 78%, higher again).

P7 Geopolitics, trade and export controlsStated
TSMC told U.S. and Taiwan authorities a customer’s chip may have reached a restricted entity; new licence rule for 16nm and below
FY2024: "In October 2024, we notified relevant U.S. and Taiwan authorities that one type of our customer’s chip manufactured by us might have been diverted to a restricted entity or incorporated into a restricted entity’s product"

Size: not quantified in the filing; no penalty is estimated. The licence rule covers nodes that produced the large majority of wafer revenue.

First disclosure of a possible export-control breach; the filing warns of “government investigations and penalties”. The April 2025 tariffs were added in the same filing, with semiconductors excluded for now.

Next filing: resolution of the inquiry (FY2025: still cooperating, no outcome) and the licence rule’s reach (FY2025: narrowed to specified destinations).

P3 MarginsStated
Taiwan electricity +25% in April 2024 and +14% in October 2024; tax rate 17.7% (13.1%)
FY2024: "For example, effective from April 1, 2024, we became subject to a higher electricity tariff rate in Taiwan, which increased by 25% as compared to the tariff rate applicable to us in 2023. In October 2024, the electricity tariff rate further increased by 14%."
FY2024: "In addition, the increase in surtax imposed on unappropriated earnings was a result of higher undistributed earnings in 2024."

Size: electricity cost not quantified in the filing. Income tax NT$248.3B (+93.6%) against pre-tax income +43.6%; 4.6 points of tax rate = about NT$65B of net income.

Two structural costs of a Taiwan base: power that is rising in price, and a tax on retained earnings that grows with the cash pile.

Next filing: electricity no longer named as a margin offset (FY2025: not named) and tax rate stable (FY2025: 17.0%).

P8 Overseas expansion executionStated
Overseas start-up costs push G&A and marketing up 35.6%; CHIPS grants repayable on noncompliance and the parent guarantees the loans
FY2024: "General and administrative and marketing expenses in 2024 increased by NT$25,425 million, or 35.6%, compared to 2023, mainly reflecting higher start-up expenses of overseas fabs preparation and employee profit sharing expenses due to higher net income."
FY2024: "Noncompliance with the terms and conditions of the grants that we may receive could result in a delay or forfeiture of all or a portion of any future amounts to be received, as well as obligate us to repay all or a portion of amounts already received pursuant to the grants."

Next filing: start-up costs fading as fabs ramp (FY2025: G&A −1.7%, but minority-interest losses tripled).
