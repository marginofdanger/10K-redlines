TSM 20-F Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company's 20-F filings reveals the arc from pure foundry to geopolitical linchpin -- export controls, geographic expansion, AI demand, and market redefinition tracked through seven years of SEC disclosure.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001046179) | 20-F Filings, FY2018-2025 | Analysis: May 2026
FY2018 → FY2019

Export Controls Emerge

End-market categories rewritten. Export controls added to customer risk. #2 customer crosses 10% threshold. GlobalFoundries litigation.

FY2019 → FY2020

Huawei & Arizona

Huawei shipments halted September 2020. Arizona fab first disclosed. Droughts added to disaster risks. China blocking statute.

FY2020 → FY2021

Global Expansion

"Military" added to geopolitical heading. Japan (JASM) disclosed. Global subsidy race language. "Large capacity" becomes "scale and capacity."

FY2021 → FY2022

CHIPS Act & Controls

CHIPS Act named. October 2022 export controls. Global operations complexity risk factor added. Nuclear-free language dropped. Nanjing demoted behind Arizona.

FY2022 → FY2023

AI Surge

AI first appears in risk factors. "Highly competitive" becomes "fierce." HPC listed before Smartphone. AI in cybersecurity risk. Supply chain diversification as competitive factor.

FY2023 → FY2024

AI Dominance & Antitrust

"World's largest dedicated foundry" removed. Antitrust self-targeting. Trump tariffs. January 2025 16nm rules. "Foundry 2.0" replaces prior market share metric. CHIPS Act $6.6B+$5B secured.

FY2024 → FY2025

Tariffs, 2nm & U.S. Build-Out

Section 232 25% chip tariff; IEEPA reciprocal tariffs reversed by Feb 2026 SCOTUS ruling; January 2026 U.S.-Taiwan trade agreement. 2nm volume production at Fab 20/22. "Foundry 2.0" metric and named-customer roster removed. Capex US$40.9B; FY2026 guide US$52-56B.

FY2018 → FY2019: Export Controls Emerge

Filed April 2020 | Revenue: NT$1,031.5B → NT$1,069.9B (+4%)

Business & Risk Factors

▶End-market categories rewritten from product types to application typesChanged
FY2018: "derived from customers who use our services in communication products, computing products, consumer electronics products and industrial/standard products"
FY2019: "who use our products in smartphones, high performance computing, IoT, automotive electronics, and digital consumer electronics"
▶Export control added to customer concentration riskNew
"regulatory restrictions such as export control directed at our major customers could impact our ability to supply products to those customers, reduce those customers demand for our products and services and impact their business operations"
▶#2 customer crosses 10% revenue threshold at 14%Signal
Second-largest customer share rose to 14% of net revenue, crossing the named-disclosure threshold for the first time.
▶GlobalFoundries patent infringement litigation disclosedNew
"assertions and litigations initiated by semiconductor companies seeking to disrupt our business such as the patent infringement lawsuits in August 2019 filed by GlobalFoundries (GF)"

FY2019 → FY2020: Huawei & Arizona

Filed April 2021 | Revenue: NT$1,069.9B → NT$1,339.3B (+25%)

Export Controls & Geopolitics

▶Huawei shipments halted -- first named customer cutoff in TSMC historyNew
"In May 2020 and again in August 2020, the U.S. tightened its export control measures against Huawei Technology Co. Ltd. and its affiliates (collectively, Huawei), including an expanded license requirement for providing Huawei with items subject to the U.S. export control jurisdiction. To comply with relevant laws and regulations, we have discontinued shipment of products to Huawei since September 15, 2020."
▶China blocking statute disclosed as counter-measureNew
"in January 2021, China adopted a blocking statute that, among other matters, entitles Chinese entities incurring damages from a multinationals compliance with foreign laws to seek civil remedies"

Capacity & Operations

▶Arizona fab first disclosed in capital expenditure plansNew
"expanding buildings/facilities for Fab 18 and a 300mm wafer fab in Arizona, United States of America"
▶"Droughts" added to disaster risk headingChanged
"materially and adversely affected by disruptive events, such as earthquakes and droughts"
Taiwan experienced a severe drought in 2020-2021 that forced water rationing at fabs.

FY2020 → FY2021: Global Expansion

Filed April 2022 | Revenue: NT$1,339.3B → NT$1,587.4B (+19%)

Geopolitical & Risk Language

▶"Military" added to geopolitical risk headingChanged
FY2020: "risks associated with political, economic, financial or other conditions or developments"
FY2021: "risks associated with political, economic, financial, military or other conditions or developments in various jurisdictions"
▶Global subsidy race language introducedNew
"The governments of Europe, the United States, South Korea, and Japan also have incentive programs to incentivize developments of their domestic semiconductor industries."
▶"Distributor" dropped from inventory adjustment languageChanged
FY2020: "the timing of customer or distributor inventory adjustments"
FY2021 removed "or distributor" -- reflecting the shift toward direct fabless-to-foundry relationships.

Capacity & Competitive

▶Japan (JASM) first disclosed as subsidiaryNew
"Japan Advanced Semiconductor Manufacturing (JASM)"
Construction commenced 2022, commercial production expected 2024. TSMC owned approximately 81.0% of JASM equity as of February 28, 2022.
▶"Large capacity" upgraded to "scale and capacity"Changed
FY2020: "We believe that our large capacity, particularly for advanced technologies, is a major competitive advantage."
FY2021: "We believe that our scale and capacity, particularly for advanced technologies, is a major competitive advantage."
▶Construction risk added to expansion plansNew
With Arizona and JASM under construction simultaneously, new risk language addressed fab buildout delays, cost overruns, and hiring challenges for overseas operations.
▶HPC enters customer concentration rationaleChanged
Customer concentration explanation updated to reference the "structural shift to mobile and high performance computing" as a driver of top-customer revenue share.

FY2021 → FY2022: CHIPS Act & Controls

Filed April 2023 | Revenue: NT$1,587.4B → NT$2,263.9B (+43%)

Export Controls & Regulation

▶CHIPS Act named by full title for the first timeNew
"the Creating Helpful Incentives to Produce Semiconductors and Science Act of 2022 (the 'U.S. CHIPS Act'), which provides financial incentives to incentivize the development of U.S. semiconductor industry"
▶October 2022 export controls disclosedNew
"In October 2022, the U.S. adopted additional export controls over specified countries (including China) under the U.S. Export Administration Regulations on certain advanced computing integrated circuits, computer commodities that contain such ICs, and certain semiconductor manufacturing items"
▶Global operations complexity as standalone risk factorNew
"If we are unable to successfully manage the complexity of our global operations and deal with the challenges and risks related to our overseas expansion, our business, financial condition and results of operations could be adversely affected."
▶"Forced labor prohibition" added to compliance languageNew
Forced labor prohibition language appeared in the compliance and regulatory sections for the first time, reflecting both U.S. and EU supply chain due diligence requirements.

Capacity & Positioning

▶Nuclear-free language dropped from environmental sectionRemoved
FY2021 included "nuclear-free" in energy policy references. FY2022 removed this language entirely, coinciding with Taiwan's evolving energy policy debate as power demands for advanced fabs increased.
▶Nanjing demoted in capacity location ordering -- Arizona listed firstChanged
FY2021: "including in Taiwan, in Nanjing, China, in Arizona, U.S., and in Kumamoto, Japan"
FY2022: "including in Taiwan, in Arizona, U.S., in Nanjing, China and in Kumamoto, Japan"
▶AWS added to named customer listNew
"including, but not limited to, Advanced Micro Devices, Inc., Amazon Web Services, Inc., Broadcom Limited, Intel Corporation, MediaTek Inc., NVIDIA Corporation"
AWS appeared for the first time in FY2022, reflecting system companies designing their own silicon.

FY2022 → FY2023: AI Surge

Filed April 2024 | Revenue: NT$2,263.9B → NT$2,161.7B (-5%)

AI & Competition

▶AI appears in risk factors for the first timeNew
"the global surge in the development of artificial intelligence ('AI') has had a significant impact on customer demand for advanced semiconductor chips and the market dynamics in our industry"
▶"Highly competitive" upgraded to "fierce"Changed
FY2022: "The markets for our foundry services are highly competitive."
FY2023: "The competition in the semiconductor foundry industry is fierce."
▶Supply chain diversification added as competitive factorNew
FY2023 added "supply chain diversification and resilience" to the list of dimensions on which TSMC competes, alongside technology, capacity, quality, and customer satisfaction.
▶HPC listed before Smartphone in end-market orderingChanged
Prior filings listed "smartphones, high performance computing" in that order. FY2023 reversed to HPC first, reflecting that HPC overtook Smartphone as the largest revenue platform.

AI Risk & Cybersecurity

▶AI in cybersecurity risk -- attackers using AINew
"as AI continues to evolve, cyber-attackers could also use AI to develop malicious codes and sophisticated phishing attempts"
▶AI in internal operations acknowledged as competitive necessityNew
"We also believe that the effective use of AI in our internal operations is important to our long-term success. As the AI technologies are rapidly evolving, if we are unable to deploy new AI technologies in our internal operations as effectively as our competitors, it may hurt our competitive position."

FY2024 → FY2025: Tariffs, 2nm & U.S. Build-Out

Filed April 16, 2026 | Revenue: NT$2,894.3B → NT$3,809.1B (+32%) | US$121.4B

Tariffs & Trade Policy

▶Section 232 25% chip tariff imposed December 2025New
"in April 2025, the U.S. government initiated a Section 232 investigation related to imports of Semiconductor Items, which concluded in December 2025. As a result, the U.S. issues a proclamation imposing a 25% ad valorem tariff on the import of certain advanced computing chips and derivative products meeting specified technical performance thresholds, unless such products are imported for designated use in the U.S. The proclamation also signaled the potential for broader tariffs on Semiconductor Items following further trade negotiations, accompanied by a tariff offset program intended to incentivize the U.S. domestic manufacturing."
▶IEEPA reciprocal tariffs reversed by February 2026 Supreme Court rulingNew
FY2024 disclosed the April 2, 2025 announcement as a forward risk. FY2025 describes the full arc: announcement, IEEPA legal basis, exclusion of "Semiconductor Items," delayed collection, and reversal.
"in April 2025, the U.S. President announced a 10% baseline tariff on imports into the U.S. from all countries, as well as varying reciprocal tariffs on most of the U.S.'s trading partners under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act ('IEEPA'). Collection of the reciprocal tariffs was delayed until August 2025, and while they excluded semiconductors, semiconductor manufacturing equipment and their respective derivative products (collectively 'Semiconductor Items'), these tariffs nonetheless affected our cost of operations in the U.S. These tariffs were declared unlawful by a February 2026 U.S. Supreme Court ruling."
▶U.S.-Taiwan trade and investment agreement (January 2026)New
"in January 2026, the U.S. announced it had reached a trade and investment agreement with Taiwan which would reduce U.S. reciprocal tariff rates on Taiwanese goods to no more than 15%. The agreement also grants preferential Section 232 treatment for Taiwanese semiconductor producers investing in U.S. manufacturing capacity."
▶Section 301 investigations targeting Taiwan initiated March 2026New
"in March 2026, the U.S. Trade Representative, using authority in Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, initiated two investigations relating to alleged manufacturing overcapacity and failures to take action on forced labor, targeting various economies, including Taiwan, which could result in trade actions such as the imposition of additional tariffs."
▶January 2025 16nm rule scope narrowed: "any global destination" to "specified destinations"Changed
FY2024: "we may need to obtain an export license prior to shipping products using 16-nanometer or below process to any global destination unless specific conditions are met."
FY2025: "we may need to obtain an export license prior to shipping products using 16-nanometer or below process to specified destinations unless specific conditions are met."
▶Nanjing VEU expired December 2025; replaced by annual export licenseNew
"Although the VEU authorization expired in December 2025, the U.S. Department of Commerce has granted TSMC Nanjing Company Limited ('TSMC Nanjing') an annual export license that allows U.S. export-controlled items to be supplied to TSMC Nanjing, which ensures uninterrupted fab operations and product deliveries. However, there is no assurance that this annual export license will not be terminated, or we can timely get the license renewed in the future."
▶"President Donald Trump" attribution removed; references depersonalizedChanged
FY2024 attributed the April 2 tariff announcement to "U.S. President Donald Trump." FY2025 references the same actions as "the U.S. President," "the U.S. government," and "the U.S. Trade Representative."

FY2025: "We count among our customers many of the world's leading semiconductor companies, ranging from fabless semiconductor companies, system companies to integrated device manufacturers."

▶Structural-shift ordering: "mobile and HPC" becomes "HPC and smartphone"Changed
FY2024: "the structural shift to mobile and HPC devices and applications and software..."
FY2025: "the structural shift to HPC and smartphone applications and software..."
▶European Commission antitrust history removedRemoved
The recounting of the September 2017 European Commission investigation (closed May 2020) is dropped; the antitrust subsection is now generic.

▶Antitrust risk factor folded into broader compliance headingChanged
The standalone antitrust risk-factor heading from FY2024 is consolidated into the broader compliance risk factor in FY2025.
FY2025 heading: "Our failure to comply with applicable laws and regulations material to our operations, such as export control, antitrust, environmental and climate related laws and regulations..."

Themes

1. Trade Policy & Export Control Escalation Ladder

Export controls went from absent (FY2018) to generic risk (FY2019) to named customer cutoff (FY2020 Huawei) to comprehensive regime (FY2022 October rules) to global 16nm licensing requirements (FY2024). FY2025 added a Section 232 25% chip tariff (December 2025) with a tariff offset program for U.S. domestic manufacturing, IEEPA reciprocal tariffs that were reversed by the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling, two new Section 301 investigations targeting Taiwan (March 2026), and a January 2026 U.S.-Taiwan trade and investment agreement granting preferential Section 232 treatment for Taiwanese semiconductor producers investing in U.S. capacity. The Nanjing VEU expired December 2025, replaced with an annual export license. The 16nm rule scope was narrowed from "any global destination" to "specified destinations." The "Donald Trump" attribution from FY2024 was removed in favor of "the U.S. President" / "the U.S. government."

2. Geographic Diversification Under Geopolitical Pressure

Arizona appeared in FY2020, Japan (JASM) in FY2021, Germany (ESMC) in FY2022. By FY2024, TSMC had secured $6.6B + $5B from U.S. CHIPS Act and EUR5B from the European Chips Act. Nanjing was demoted from second in location ordering (FY2021) to dropped entirely (FY2024). FY2025 disclosed Arizona Fab 1 in high-volume production, Fab 2 ongoing, Fab 3 construction commenced 2025, and an additional parcel of land acquired 2026. North America revenue share rose to 75% (from 70% in FY2024); China dropped to 9% (from 11%). North America headcount nearly doubled in two years (2,668 to 5,290).

3. AI as Revenue and Risk Driver

AI was absent from all filings through FY2022. In FY2023, it appeared in risk factors, cybersecurity warnings, and internal operations language simultaneously. By FY2024, HPC was rebranded "HPC (including AI applications)" at 51% of revenue, and TSMC warned that AI-sector growth may be "volatile or not sustainable." FY2025 inserted "AI" into the megatrends list ("5G, AI and HPC"), reframed the technological-leader risk around "AI smartphones, AI PCs, and AI accelerators" (replacing "smartphones"), and HPC reached 58% of revenue while Smartphone fell to 29%. The "volatile or not sustainable" warning was retained verbatim.

4. Market Definition as Strategic Tool

TSMC's share metric changed four times: 56% of dedicated foundries (FY2018), 28% of semiconductor ex-memory (FY2023), 34% of "Foundry 2.0" (FY2024), and no metric cited (FY2025). FY2025 also dropped the named-customer roster (AMD, AWS, Broadcom, Intel, MediaTek, NVIDIA, NXP, Qualcomm, Renesas, Sony) from the Business Overview, and folded the standalone antitrust risk factor into a broader compliance heading. The "increasing criticism on the concentration of the semiconductor industry and sometimes directly on us" language is retained.

5. Pricing Power Inflection

From FY2018 through FY2023, every filing described "the historical and current trend of declining average selling prices." FY2024 dropped "and current," acknowledging that ASP decline is no longer the present-tense reality. The competition heading simultaneously added "equal access to governmental financial incentives" -- TSMC now frames competition as subsidy-driven rather than price-driven. "Highly competitive" became "fierce" in FY2023, but the underlying competitive dynamic shifted from price to capacity access.

6. Customer Concentration and Platform Shift

Top-10 customer concentration rose from 68% (FY2018) to 78% (FY2025). The #2 customer crossed 10% at 14% in FY2019, dropped below 10% after FY2020, then returned above 10% in FY2023 (11%), 12% (FY2024), and 17% (FY2025). The #1 customer share moved 25% → 22% → 19% over the same period -- the gap between #1 and #2 narrowed from 14 to 2 percentage points. HPC overtook Smartphone as the largest platform in FY2022 (41% vs 40%) and reached 58% in FY2025; Smartphone fell to 29%.

7. Capex Step-Change and 2nm Production

Capex stepped from US$29.8B (FY2024) to US$40.9B (FY2025), with FY2026 guidance of US$52-56B. 2-nanometer entered volume production in 2025 at Fab 20 (Hsinchu) and Fab 22 (Kaohsiung); 16-angstrom risk production targeted for 2026. Advanced node mix (7nm and below) reached 74% of wafer revenue. The FY2026 capex bullets dropped Fab 23 (Kumamoto) from the specialty/packaging expansion list, leaving only Fab 24 (Dresden) for that category. Gross margin reached 59.9% and operating margin 50.8% -- both new highs in the filing history.
