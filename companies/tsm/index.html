TSM 20-F Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company's 20-F filings reveals the arc from pure foundry to geopolitical linchpin -- export controls, geographic expansion, AI demand, and market redefinition tracked through seven years of SEC disclosure.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001046179) | 20-F Filings, FY2018-2025 | Analysis: May 2026; FY2023-FY2025 transitions redone from sentence-level signals with phrase-history checks October 2026

Export Controls Emerge

End-market categories rewritten. Export controls added to customer risk. #2 customer crosses 10% threshold. GlobalFoundries litigation.

▶HPC listed before Smartphone in end-market orderingChanged
Prior filings listed "smartphones, high performance computing" in that order. FY2023 reversed to HPC first, reflecting that HPC overtook Smartphone as the largest revenue platform.

FY2023 → FY2024: "Largest" Dropped, Controls Go Global, Nanjing's VEU Granted

Filed April 17, 2025 | Revenue: NT$2,161.7B → NT$2,894.3B (+33.9%) | Gross margin 54.4% → 56.1% | Operating margin 42.6% → 45.7% | Net income attributable NT$851.7B → NT$1,158.4B | Capex US$30.4B → US$29.8B | Free cash flow NT$292.2B → NT$870.2B

Self-Description & Market Position

▶Both "largest" claims deleted: "world's largest dedicated foundry" and "possessed the largest capacity" become "built a strong position in manufacturing capacity"Removed

Claim removed (business, REMOVED in signals file) (-); superlative replaced by unranked "strong position" (-)

FY2023: "We believe we are currently the world's largest dedicated foundry in the semiconductor industry."
FY2023: "Since our establishment, we have possessed the largest capacity among the world's dedicated foundries."
FY2024: "Since our establishment, we have built a strong position in manufacturing capacity as a dedicated foundry."

History: "world's largest dedicated foundry" and "largest capacity among" appear in FY2023 only of the three downloaded 20-Fs (FY2023-FY2025); this dashboard's self-description table records the first claim from FY2018. Neither returns in FY2025.

The filing gives up both size superlatives in the same year it adds that it faces "increasing criticism on the concentration of the semiconductor industry and sometimes directly on us" (see below). TSMC's position did not shrink, so the deletions are consistent with deliberately softening dominance language rather than a change in fact.

▶Share metric switched from "semiconductor excluding memory" (28%, down from 30%) to "Foundry 2.0" (34%, up from 28%)Changed

Quant: old figures (28%, 30%) and their explanation removed (-); new metric and figure (34%) added under a self-chosen definition

FY2023: "We produced 28 percent of the world semiconductor excluding memory output value in 2023, as compared to 30 percent in the previous year, mainly due to an inventory correction within the semiconductor industry."
FY2024: "We produced 34% of the "Foundry 2.0" industry, which we define as all logic wafer manufacturing, packaging, testing, mask-making and others, an increase from 28% in 2023."

History: "Foundry 2.0" appears in FY2024 only; FY2025 drops it with no replacement.

FY2023 had to report a falling share and explain it. The new definition adds packaging, testing and masks and produces a rising series; 2023 is 28% under both definitions, so the change in denominator, not the restatement, is what turns a decline into growth. The old metric is not carried forward.

▶New warning that AI customers' growth may be "volatile or not sustainable", in the year the end-market list is renamed "HPC (including AI applications)"New

Risk added (customer risk factor) (+ disclosure); named: AI sector

FY2024: "These shifting business models could lead to significant variations in our sales if the growth of their products and services, particularly in the AI sector, is volatile or not sustainable."
FY2023: "A vast majority of our revenue is derived from customers who use our products in high performance computing ("HPC"), smartphones, IoT, automotive, and digital consumer electronics."
FY2024: "A vast majority of our revenue is derived from customers who use our products in HPC (including AI applications), smartphones, IoT, automotive, and digital consumer electronics."

History: "volatile or not sustainable" appears in FY2024 and FY2025, not FY2023. In the business section, "one of the key growth drivers" (FY2023) became "the key growth driver" (FY2024 and FY2025).

The same filing makes HPC the single growth engine (51% of revenue, +58%) and, in the risk factors, says for the first time in the set that AI demand may not last. The FY2025 filing keeps the sentence word for word.

▶Antitrust risk now names TSMC itself as a target of "increasing criticism" over concentrationNew

Risk sharpened: "For example" (a past, closed inquiry) becomes a present statement of "heightened risks" tied to TSMC's own position (+ disclosure)

FY2023: "We are subject to antitrust laws and regulations in multiple jurisdictions, and from time to time receive related inquiries from enforcement agencies. For example, on September 28, 2017, we were contacted by the European Commission"
FY2024: "With our success in the foundry business and the increasing criticism on the concentration of the semiconductor industry and sometimes directly on us, we are subject to heightened risks of antitrust investigations."

History: "sometimes directly on us" appears in FY2024 and FY2025 only. The 2017 European Commission inquiry (closed May 2020) stays in FY2024 and is deleted in FY2025.

The filing for the first time in the set ties antitrust exposure to TSMC's success and to criticism aimed "directly on us", in the same year both "largest" claims were removed.

Export Controls & Trade

▶January 2025 rules: a license may be needed to ship 16nm-and-below products "to any global destination"New

Named: new rule (January 2025); scope: China-specific controls extended to "any global destination" (+ disclosure, more risk)

FY2024: "Under the new rules, we may need to obtain an export license prior to shipping products using 16-nanometer or below process to any global destination unless specific conditions are met. As a result, shipments of certain products may be delayed or prohibited due to the license requirements and our financial results may be adversely affected."

History: "any global destination" appears in FY2024 only; FY2025 narrows it to "specified destinations".

The earlier controls in this paragraph were about China-bound items and China fabs. This rule reaches every destination and nodes several generations behind the leading edge, so most advanced revenue became potentially subject to licensing.

▶October 2024 notice to U.S. and Taiwan authorities that a customer's chip "might have been diverted to a restricted entity"New

Realized: a compliance incident disclosed as having happened (ADDED paragraph) (+ disclosure); hedge: "no assurance" of compliance

FY2024: "In October 2024, we notified relevant U.S. and Taiwan authorities that one type of our customer's chip manufactured by us might have been diverted to a restricted entity or incorporated into a restricted entity's product, and since then have been cooperating with the authorities' requests for additional information and documents."
FY2024: "Despite our best efforts to comply with all relevant export control and sanctions laws and regulations, there is no assurance that our business activities will not be found incompliant with export control laws and regulations."

History: "might have been diverted" appears in FY2024 and FY2025 (still described as ongoing cooperation); absent in FY2023.

This is an incident involving TSMC's own production rather than a customer ban, and the paragraph opens by conceding that TSMC's place in the supply chain "inherently limits our visibility" into end use. The closing sentence leaves open a finding of non-compliance.

▶Tariffs enter the trade-tension risk: the April 2, 2025 baseline and reciprocal tariffs name Taiwan; chips exempt, but "significant uncertainty"New

Named: U.S. President, Taiwan, April 2, 2025 tariffs (+ disclosure); quant added (10%)

FY2024: "For example, U.S. President Donald Trump announced on April 2, 2025 a 10% baseline tariff on imports into the United States from all countries, as well as varying reciprocal tariffs on certain trading partners, including Taiwan."
FY2024: "Semiconductors were not subject to the reciprocal tariffs announced on April 2, 2025. However, given ongoing discussions between the United States and its trading partners, there remains significant uncertainty about whether the United States may further change the scope and level of tariffs it imposes, including any potential tariffs it may impose on semiconductor imports."

History: "baseline tariff" appears in FY2024 and FY2025; "Donald Trump" in FY2024 only (FY2025 says "the U.S. President").

The filing went in two weeks after the announcement. Its risk is customers' demand: tariffs on chip-containing products "may, in turn, lead to decreased demand for our products". TSMC's own cost of operating in the U.S. is not mentioned until FY2025.

▶Nanjing VEU moves from "applying for" to obtained, called "a permanent authorization"; Nanjing drops out of the capacity-addition listChanged

Realized: hypothetical -> actual (risk_factors, top-ranked in signals file) (+); named: +China, Commerce

FY2023: "We are also applying for a Validated End-User (the "VEU") authorization for our Nanjing fab, which, once obtained, would be a permanent authorization that allows us to receive exports of eligible items from the U.S. without separate licenses."
FY2024: "In response, we obtained from the U.S. Department of Commerce a Validated End-User (the "VEU") authorization for our fab located in Nanjing, China, which is a permanent authorization that allows our fab in Nanjing to receive exports of eligible items from the U.S. without separate licenses."

History: "permanent authorization" appears in FY2023 (as a prospect) and FY2024 (as fact); FY2025 reports the VEU expired in December 2025. "in Nanjing, China, in Kumamoto" (the capacity-addition list) appears in FY2023 only.

Nanjing's supply of U.S. tools was secured, yet the capacity risk factor now lists additions only "in Taiwan, in Arizona, U.S., in Kumamoto, Japan and in Dresden, Germany". Consistent with Nanjing becoming maintain-only. The "permanent" label lasted one filing.

Subsidies & Overseas Expansion

▶CHIPS Act signed: up to US$6.6B grants and US$5B loans, backed by an "irrevocable, absolute and unconditional" parent guarantee and milestone conditionsNew

Quant added (US$6.6B, US$5B) (+); named: Direct Funding Agreement, Loan Agreements; condition added (milestones)

FY2024: "Under this agreement, the DOC agrees to award TSMC Arizona up to US$6.6 billion in direct funding related to TSMC Arizona's semiconductor manufacturing facilities in Phoenix, Arizona"
FY2024: "the Company entered into certain guarantee agreements, under which the Company agrees to provide an irrevocable, absolute and unconditional guarantee to the DOC regarding any financial obligations owed by TSMC Arizona to the DOC. The awards under the Direct Funding Agreement are subject to various conditions, compliance requirements, program requirements and project milestone requirements."

History: "irrevocable, absolute and unconditional" appears in FY2024 and FY2025, not FY2023.

The headline amounts were public in November 2024. What the filing adds is that the Taiwan parent guarantees its Arizona subsidiary's obligations to the U.S. government and that the awards depend on milestones. ESMC separately signed for up to EUR5 billion of German state aid.

▶Subsidy risk rewritten: forfeiture, repayment, and a grantor that may "be unable to provide the funding" even if TSMC compliesChanged

Condition added: risk now applies "even if we satisfy the conditions" (+ disclosure); scope: recovery of funds becomes forfeiture plus repayment

FY2023: "the grantors could seek to recover any funds provided to us, or cancel, reduce or deny our requested subsidies or grants in the future."
FY2024: "Noncompliance with the terms and conditions of the grants that we may receive could result in a delay or forfeiture of all or a portion of any future amounts to be received, as well as obligate us to repay all or a portion of amounts already received pursuant to the grants. Even if we satisfy the conditions and requirements for the funding disbursement, it is possible that the grantor may delay the disbursement or be unable to provide the funding."

History: "be unable to provide the funding" and "equal access to governmental financial incentives" both appear in FY2024 and FY2025, not FY2023.

The new sentence covers a grantor that does not pay even when TSMC complies. The competition heading adds "equal access to governmental financial incentives, especially those available to our competitors", and the overseas list adds "non-receipt, delay and potential claw backs of government subsidies".

▶March 2025 "intention" to build three more U.S. fabs, two advanced packaging facilities and an R&D center; Arizona's "three" fabs become "multiple"New

Commitment at "intention" strength (ADDED); scope: "three" -> "multiple" (quant removed, open-ended)

FY2023: "TSMC Arizona plans to build and operate three advanced semiconductor manufacturing facilities in Phoenix, Arizona."
FY2024: "TSMC Arizona plans to build and operate multiple advanced semiconductor manufacturing facilities in Phoenix, Arizona."
FY2024: "In March 2025, we further announced our intention to build three additional semiconductor manufacturing facilities, two advanced packaging facilities and a R&D center in the United States."

History: "our intention to build" and "three additional semiconductor" appear in FY2024 only; "multiple advanced semiconductor manufacturing facilities" in FY2024 and FY2025.

The site count is left open-ended and the larger U.S. commitment appears only as an "intention". Track this sentence: the FY2025 filing deletes it.

▶JASM: the stated plan to own "approximately 86.5%" after the second-fab investment is deleted; stake reported at 72.6%Removed

Quant removed: forward ownership figure no longer stated (-); "will also take" -> "also took" (realized)

FY2023: "As of February 29, 2024, we owned approximately 71.4% of the equity interest in JASM. After the completion of the new investment, we will own approximately 86.5% of the equity interest in JASM."
FY2024: "By way of such investment, Toyota also took a minority stake in JASM. As of February 28, 2025, we owned approximately 72.6% of the equity interest in JASM."

History: "we will own approximately" appears in FY2023 only. "72.6%" appears in FY2024 and again, unchanged, in FY2025.

A year after saying its stake would rise to 86.5%, TSMC reports 72.6% and drops the target without explanation. Read with FY2025 (second Kumamoto site under construction from October 2025, stake still 72.6%), the planned increase has not happened, or has been restructured; the filing does not say which.

Costs, Power & Disclosure

▶Carbon fees move from "could be required to pay" to "we will start to pay" from 2026Changed

Certainty: could -> will (+, firmer); quant added (2026 start date); the line that the rate "has yet to be determined" removed

FY2023: "We could be required to pay any incurred carbon fees since our emission levels exceed applicable thresholds pursuant to the current regulatory requirements, which will result in increased operating costs for us and affect us financially to a certain extent."
FY2024: "As our emissions from each fab in Taiwan exceed the current regulatory threshold, we will start to pay carbon fees from 2026 (for the 2025 fees), which will result in increased manufacturing costs for us."

History: "could be required to pay any incurred carbon fees" appears in FY2023 only; "will start to pay carbon fees" in FY2024 and FY2025.

A possible cost becomes a scheduled one. The same paragraph drops "as well as of the carbon fee" from the sentence saying the impact of climate rules is "currently indeterminable".

▶Electricity hike becomes actual (25%, then 14% more), and the warning that utility shortages could curtail expansion is deletedRemoved

Risk sentence removed (risk_factors REMOVED) (-); realized: "is estimated to increase" -> "increased" (+)

FY2023 (deleted in FY2024): "Moreover, our future capacity expansions in Taiwan and elsewhere could be curtailed by utility shortages."
FY2024: "effective from April 1, 2024, we became subject to a higher electricity tariff rate in Taiwan, which increased by 25% as compared to the tariff rate applicable to us in 2023. In October 2024, the electricity tariff rate further increased by 14%."

History: "could be curtailed by utility shortages" appears in FY2023 only; "is estimated to increase" in FY2023 only. FY2025 restores a version ("our future capacity expansions could be curtailed").

Power becomes a named margin headwind (the MD&A cites "higher electricity cost" in the gross-margin bridge) in the same year the filing stops warning that shortages could cap expansion. The deletion lasted one year.

▶Raw-wafer supplier names and their 96% share of supply removedRemoved

De-named (six suppliers) (-); quant removed (96.0%, 95.0%, 96.2%) (-)

FY2023: "The principal suppliers for our wafers are Formosa SUMCO Technology Corporation of Taiwan, GlobalWafers of Taiwan, Shin-Etsu Handotai of Japan, Siltronic AG of Germany, Soitec Microelectronics of Singapore, and SUMCO Corporation of Japan. Together they supplied approximately 96.0%, 95.0% and 96.2% of our total wafer needs in 2021, 2022 and 2023, respectively."
FY2024: "In pursuit of high quality raw wafers, the majority of our raw wafers are supplied by a limited number of suppliers located in Taiwan, Japan, Germany, and Singapore."

History: "Formosa SUMCO" appears in FY2023 only; "limited number of suppliers located in" in FY2024 and FY2025.

A named, quantified supplier concentration becomes an unquantified "majority". The same filing drops Infineon from the named-customer roster; FY2025 removes the roster entirely.

FY2024 → FY2025: Tariffs Hit U.S. Costs, the U.S. Plan Goes Quiet, Taiwan Drops Out of the "Ecosystem"

Filed April 16, 2026 | Revenue: NT$2,894.3B → NT$3,809.1B (+31.6%; US$121.4B) | Gross margin 56.1% → 59.9% | Operating margin 45.7% → 50.8% | Net income attributable NT$1,158.4B → NT$1,697.6B (+46.5%) | Capex US$29.8B → US$40.9B (FY2025 figures from the 20-F text; not yet in SEC XBRL)

Tariffs & Export Controls

▶Chips were exempt from reciprocal tariffs, but the tariffs "nonetheless affected our cost of operations in the U.S."New

Realized: a demand risk (FY2024) becomes a cost already incurred (+ disclosure); named: IEEPA, Supreme Court, Section 122, Section 301

FY2024: "Semiconductors were not subject to the reciprocal tariffs announced on April 2, 2025."
FY2025: "Collection of the reciprocal tariffs was delayed until August 2025, and while they excluded semiconductors, semiconductor manufacturing equipment and their respective derivative products (collectively ... these tariffs nonetheless affected our cost of operations in the U.S. These tariffs were declared unlawful by a February 2026 U.S. Supreme Court ruling."

History: "cost of operations in the U.S." appears in FY2025 only; "Section 301" likewise FY2025 only.

FY2024's tariff risk was about customers' demand. FY2025 says tariffs raised TSMC's own Arizona costs even with chips exempt; the filing does not size the cost. The Supreme Court ruling did not end the exposure: a Section 122 replacement tariff followed, and two Section 301 investigations name Taiwan.

▶January 2026 U.S.-Taiwan deal: reciprocal rate capped at 15% and "preferential Section 232 treatment" for producers "investing in U.S. manufacturing capacity", details "still unclear"New

Named: Section 232, U.S.-Taiwan agreement (+); quant added (25%, 15%); hedge: "still unclear" (-)

FY2025: "the U.S. issues a proclamation imposing a 25% ad valorem tariff on the import of certain advanced computing chips and derivative products meeting specified technical performance thresholds, unless such products are imported for designated use in the U.S."
FY2025: "The agreement also grants preferential Section 232 treatment for Taiwanese semiconductor producers investing in U.S. manufacturing capacity. However, details regarding the impacts of the U.S. Supreme Court decision striking down the IEEPA reciprocal tariffs as well as the preferential treatment with respect to Section 232 tariffs, including any offsets, are still unclear."

History: "Section 232" and "no more than 15%" appear in FY2025 only.

Tariff relief is tied to "investing in U.S. manufacturing capacity", which gives weight to the deletion of the March 2025 U.S. expansion plan below. A separate added sentence says tariffs on imported materials and equipment "may result in increased costs for our manufacturing operations in the U.S. and reduce our profitability".

▶Possible diverters change from "business partners" to "customers"; foundries "may not be able to verify" whether a design is export-controlledChanged

Named: "business partners" -> "customers" (+, more specific); "with an intent of circumvention" dropped; new limitation clause added

FY2024: "This constraint impedes our ability to fully ensure that semiconductors manufactured by us will not be diverted to unintended end use or end-user, including potentially by our business partners, or by third parties with an intent of circumvention."
FY2025: "This constraint impedes our ability to fully ensure such semiconductors will not be diverted to unintended end use or end-user, including potentially by our customers, or by third parties. In addition, export controls over certain semiconductors often depend on the products' specific features or performance characteristics that foundries may not be able to verify independently."

History: "by our business partners" appears in FY2024 only; "foundries may not be able to verify" in FY2025 only. The October 2024 diversion notice is still described as ongoing cooperation.

TSMC now names its own customers as possible diverters and argues that a foundry cannot check whether a design crosses export thresholds. That reads as laying the ground to place compliance responsibility on customers if the diversion inquiry leads to penalties (an inference).

▶Nanjing's "permanent" VEU expired in December 2025; the fab now runs on an annual export license that may not be renewed in timeChanged

Certainty: "permanent authorization" -> "annual export license" (-); condition added: renewal risk

FY2024: "which is a permanent authorization that allows our fab in Nanjing to receive exports of eligible items from the U.S. without separate licenses"
FY2025: "the U.S. Department of Commerce has granted TSMC Nanjing Company Limited (... an annual export license that allows U.S. export-controlled items to be supplied to TSMC Nanjing, which ensures uninterrupted fab operations and product deliveries. However, there is no assurance that this annual export license will not be terminated, or we can timely get the license renewed in the future."

Themes

1. Trade Policy & Export Control Escalation Ladder

Export controls went from absent (FY2018) to generic risk (FY2019) to named customer cutoff (FY2020 Huawei) to comprehensive regime (FY2022 October rules) to global 16nm licensing requirements (FY2024). FY2025 added a Section 232 25% chip tariff (December 2025) with a tariff offset program for U.S. domestic manufacturing, IEEPA reciprocal tariffs that were reversed by the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling, two new Section 301 investigations targeting Taiwan (March 2026), and a January 2026 U.S.-Taiwan trade and investment agreement granting preferential Section 232 treatment for Taiwanese semiconductor producers investing in U.S. capacity. The Nanjing VEU expired December 2025, replaced with an annual export license. The 16nm rule scope was narrowed from "any global destination" to "specified destinations." The "Donald Trump" attribution from FY2024 was removed in favor of "the U.S. President" / "the U.S. government."

2. Geographic Diversification Under Geopolitical Pressure

Arizona appeared in FY2020, Japan (JASM) in FY2021, Germany (ESMC) in FY2022. By FY2024, TSMC had secured $6.6B + $5B from U.S. CHIPS Act and EUR5B from the European Chips Act. Nanjing was demoted from second in location ordering (FY2021) to dropped entirely (FY2024). FY2024 added a March 2025 "intention" to build three more U.S. fabs, two advanced packaging facilities and an R&D center; FY2025 deletes that sentence and describes only Fab 1 in high-volume production, Fab 2 ongoing, Fab 3 construction commenced 2025, and an additional parcel of land acquired 2026, even as the U.S.-Taiwan deal ties Section 232 relief to U.S. investment. North America revenue share rose to 75% (from 70% in FY2024); China dropped to 9% (from 11%). North America headcount nearly doubled in two years (2,668 to 5,290). FY2025 also stops locating TSMC's core advantage in Taiwan: the "ecosystem of interconnected semiconductor fabs, employees and suppliers" is no longer "in the R.O.C."

3. AI as Revenue and Risk Driver

AI was absent from all filings through FY2022. In FY2023, it appeared in risk factors, cybersecurity warnings, and internal operations language simultaneously. By FY2024, HPC was rebranded "HPC (including AI applications)" at 51% of revenue, and TSMC warned that AI-sector growth may be "volatile or not sustainable." FY2025 inserted "AI" into the megatrends list ("5G, AI and HPC"), reframed the technological-leader risk around "AI smartphones, AI PCs, and AI accelerators" (replacing "smartphones"), and HPC reached 58% of revenue while Smartphone fell to 29%. The "volatile or not sustainable" warning was retained verbatim.

4. Market Definition as Strategic Tool

TSMC's share metric changed four times: 56% of dedicated foundries (FY2018), 28% of semiconductor ex-memory (FY2023), 34% of "Foundry 2.0" (FY2024), and no metric cited (FY2025). FY2025 also dropped the named-customer roster (AMD, AWS, Broadcom, Intel, MediaTek, NVIDIA, NXP, Qualcomm, Renesas, Sony) from the Business Overview, folded the standalone antitrust risk factor into the compliance heading, and deleted the 2017 European Commission inquiry, its only example. FY2024 had already removed the named raw-wafer suppliers and their 96% supply share, and both "largest" self-descriptions. The "increasing criticism on the concentration of the semiconductor industry and sometimes directly on us" language is retained.

5. Pricing Power Inflection

Earlier filings described "the historical and current trend of declining average selling prices"; by the FY2023 filing the phrase was already "the historical trend" (this theme previously dated the change to FY2024, which the FY2023 text contradicts). FY2024's pricing signal is different: price must now "sustain healthy financial conditions to expand capacity and support customers' growth." FY2024's competition heading also added "equal access to governmental financial incentives" -- TSMC now frames competition as subsidy-driven rather than price-driven. "Highly competitive" became "fierce" in FY2023, but the underlying competitive dynamic shifted from price to capacity access. FY2025 complicates this: competitors' "aggressive pricing initiatives" are no longer limited to "one or several technology nodes," and customers' capacity-reservation prepayments (temporary receipts) fell from NT$291.1B to NT$189.9B.

6. Customer Concentration and Platform Shift

Top-10 customer concentration rose from 68% (FY2018) to 78% (FY2025). The #2 customer crossed 10% at 14% in FY2019, dropped below 10% after FY2020, then returned above 10% in FY2023 (11%), 12% (FY2024), and 17% (FY2025). The #1 customer share moved 25% → 22% → 19% over the same period -- the gap between #1 and #2 narrowed from 14 to 2 percentage points. As concentration rose in FY2025, the filing changed "a more concentrated customer base will subject our revenue to seasonal demand fluctuations" to "may", and removed the named-customer roster. HPC overtook Smartphone as the largest platform in FY2022 (41% vs 39%) and reached 58% in FY2025; Smartphone fell to 29%.

7. Capex Step-Change and 2nm Production

Capex stepped from US$29.8B (FY2024) to US$40.9B (FY2025), with FY2026 guidance of US$52-56B. 2-nanometer entered volume production in 2025 at Fab 20 (Hsinchu) and Fab 22 (Kaohsiung); 16-angstrom risk production targeted for 2026. Advanced node mix (7nm and below) reached 74% of wafer revenue. The FY2026 capex bullets dropped Fab 23 (Kumamoto) from the specialty/packaging expansion list, leaving only Fab 24 (Dresden), although JASM's second site began construction in October 2025. The capacity risk factor now adds "if the market demand does not materialize" to its overbuild warning, while the business section expects utility prices to keep rising and the MD&A no longer names electricity or overseas start-up costs as margin drags. Gross margin reached 59.9% and operating margin 50.8% -- both new highs in the filing history.
