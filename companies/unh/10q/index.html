UNH 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of ten SEC filings: eight 10-Qs from Q1 2024 (quarter ended March 31, 2024) through Q2 2026 (June 30, 2026), with the FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks interleaved at the year boundaries. Nine consecutive transitions covering MD&A (business trends, medical cost trends, regulatory trends, segments, liquidity), the Commitments and Contingencies note that Part II Item 1 incorporates by reference, and Item 1A. Across the set, the medical-cost language went from “as expected and contemplated” to “well-short” and back again, and prior-year reserve development went from $30M to $1.05B.

Q1 2024 → Q2 2024

Filed Aug 9, 2024 | Revenue $98.86B | MCR 85.1% (Q1 84.3%) | Diluted EPS $4.54 | UnitedHealthcare earnings from operations −8%

MD&A

▶ UnitedHealthcare earnings said to have "increased" while the segment table shows −8% for the quarter Signal

The Q1 2024 sentence, accurate then (segment earnings $4,395M vs $4,343M), was carried into Q2 2024 unchanged:

"Earnings from operations increased due to the factors impacting revenue, partially offset by Medicare Advantage funding reductions and incremental medical costs for accommodations to support care providers as a result of the Change Healthcare cyberattack."

The Q2 2024 segment table reports UnitedHealthcare earnings from operations of $4,004M vs $4,358M (−8%) for the quarter and $8,399M vs $8,701M (−3%) for six months. The narrative was not corrected until Q3 2024, when "increased" became "decreased" and "decreased favorable reserve development" entered the explanation. It was the first sign in the series that the insurer's margin decline was running ahead of the text.

▶ Warning that the cyberattack could add "variability" to reserve development deleted after one quarter Removed

Q1 2024:

"Additionally, the business disruption impacted claims receipt timing for payer customers, including UnitedHealthcare, which could potentially result in increased variability to our medical cost reserve development in future periods."

Q2 2024 replaces it with a cost figure:

"For the three and six months June 30, 2024, medical costs related to the temporary suspension of some care management activities were $290 million and $630 million, respectively."

The only reserve-variability caveat in the series ran for one quarter. Year-to-date favorable development then went from $390M (H1 2024) to $30M (nine months 2025), and in Q1 2026 to $1.05B (see Tracked Signal #2). The filings never connect those swings to the claims-timing warning.

▶ Breach scope hardens: "which could cover" becomes "which cover" a substantial proportion of Americans; provider loans $3.9B → more than $9B Changed

Q1 2024:

"In April 2024, we also announced that based on initial targeted data sampling to date, we have found files containing protected health information (PHI) or personally identifiable information (PII), which could cover a substantial proportion of people in America."

Q2 2024:

"Based upon our ongoing review of the impacted data, we have found files containing protected health information (PHI) or personally identifiable information (PII), which cover a substantial proportion of people in America. In June 2024, Change Healthcare gave public notice of the breach under HIPAA and began notifying affected customer entities in June and individuals in late July."

The hedge "could" was dropped. Interest-free provider loans went from "approximately $3.9 billion through March 31, 2024" to "more than $9 billion through June 30, 2024". Direct response costs were $776M in the quarter and $1.4B for six months. Optum Insight disruption was $334M in the quarter and $613M for six months.

▶ Remaining South American operations put up for sale: $1.2B loss; new forward-looking factor New
"In the second quarter of 2024, the Company initiated a plan to sell its remaining South American operations, which were classified as held for sale as of June 30, 2024. As a result, the Company recorded a loss of $1.2 billion, of which $867 million related to the impact of cumulative foreign currency translation losses."

The forward-looking list gained "risk and uncertainties associated with the continuing sale of operations in South America". It came on top of the $7.1B Brazil loss booked in Q1.

▶ No buybacks in Q2, yet a 35M-share authorization and an 11.7% dividend increase Signal

The six-month repurchase line is identical to the three-month line ("approximately 6 million shares at an average price of $505.46 per share"), and cash repurchases stayed at $3,072M, so no shares were bought in Q2. In the same quarter:

"In June 2024, our Board of Directors amended our share repurchase program to authorize the repurchase of up to 35 million shares of Common Stock, in addition to all remaining shares authorized to be repurchased under the Board’s 2018 renewal of the program."

The dividend went to an annual rate of $8.40 from $7.52. Provider loans of $8.1B appear as a new use of cash in the same table.

MD&A: Medical Costs & Segments

▶ New cost drivers named: hospital "coding intensity" and high-cost specialty drugs (cardiovascular, auto-immune, cancer) New

Shift: ADDED sentence; named cost drivers added (+ more disclosure)

Q3 2024 (added): "In the third quarter, we also experienced higher medical costs due to a pronounced upshift in coding intensity by hospitals and an acceleration of the prescribing of certain high-cost specialty medications, primarily those used to treat cardiovascular disease, auto-immune disorders and cancer."

History: "coding intensity" first appears in the Q3 2024 10-Q; repeated in the FY2024 10-K; absent from all five 2025 filings and Q1 2026; returns in Q2 2026 as commercial "coding intensity".

This is the first time the filings blame providers' billing behavior rather than member utilization. "Hospitals" was also added to the parties UNH engages to mitigate costs ("engaging hospitals, physicians and consumers"). The opening clause "As expected and contemplated in our benefits design" stayed, so the new pressure was presented as an addition to an expected trend, not as a miss.

▶ Medicaid state rates "remain well short of current care activity": the rate/acuity gap first appears New

Shift: ADDED sentence; realized: a gap stated as present fact (+)

Q3 2024 (added): "The Medicaid redetermination process has also caused a timing mismatch between the current health status of people served through Medicaid and state rate updates, which remain well short of current care activity."

History: "well short of current care activity" appears only in the Q3 2024 10-Q and the FY2024 10-K ("remained"); from Q1 2025 the gap is described in other words ("more closely align", then "continues to be a mismatch").

Until this quarter the Medicaid paragraph covered only membership losses from redeterminations. The new sentence says the members who stayed are sicker than the rates assume, which starts an eight-filing thread (Tracked Signal #3).

▶ UnitedHealthcare earnings explanation flips from "increased" to "decreased", now citing "decreased favorable reserve development" Changed

Shift: direction reversed (increased → decreased); new driver named (+ candor)

Q2 2024: "Earnings from operations increased due to the factors impacting revenue, partially offset by Medicare Advantage funding reductions and incremental medical costs for accommodations to support care providers as a result of the Change Healthcare cyberattack."
Q3 2024: "Earnings from operations decreased due to Medicare Advantage funding reductions, the impacts of Medicaid redeterminations and decreased favorable reserve development, partially offset by the factors impacting revenue above."

History: the Q2 "increased" sentence stood unchanged in Q1 and Q2 2024 (sentence_signals persistence). "Decreased favorable reserve development" was already in the MCR explanation in Q1 and Q2 2024; Q3 2024 is the first time it is given as a reason segment earnings fell.

The segment earned $4,212M vs $4,592M (−8%) in the quarter. Year-to-date favorable development was $600M vs $760M a year earlier. The Q2 sentence had said "increased" while the Q2 segment table showed −8%; the text caught up with the numbers here.

▶ Optum Health earnings growth now credited partly to "business portfolio and asset dispositions"; businesses with $241M carrying value sold for $1.0B Changed

Shift: driver added (non-operating gains) ahead of investment income (+ disclosure; - earnings quality)

Q2 2024: "Earnings from operations increased due to cost management initiatives, partially offset by costs associated with serving newly added patients under value-based care arrangements."
Q3 2024: "Earnings from operations increased due to cost management initiatives, business portfolio and asset dispositions and increased investment income, partially offset by Medicare Advantage funding reductions and costs associated with serving newly added patients under value-based care arrangements."
Q3 2024 (notes, added): "The Company also sold other businesses and assets for $1.0 billion in total consideration, with a carrying value of $241 million, and the difference reflected in the Condensed Consolidated Statement of Operations."

History: "business portfolio and asset dispositions" appears in the Q3 2024 10-Q only; the FY2024 10-K renames it "gains related to business portfolio refinement" (FY2024 10-K, Q3 2025 10-Q, FY2025 10-K).

Optum Health earnings rose 38% to $2,161M (8.3% margin), the high point of the series. The note implies roughly $760M of gains on sales booked inside operating results (our arithmetic: $1.0B less $241M). The operating cost ratio sentence adds the same dispositions as a reason costs fell. Four quarters later the segment earned $255M.

▶ Change Healthcare drag extended into 2025 "as we work to ... win new business" Changed

Shift: certainty: continue → will (+ firmer); time horizon extended into 2025

Q2 2024: "We expect to continue to incur direct response costs and experience business disruption impacts over the remainder of the year, including costs to continue to restore Change Healthcare’s services."
Q3 2024: "We expect to continue to incur direct response costs and experience business disruption impacts over the remainder of the year, which will continue at a lesser extent in 2025 as we work to bring transaction volumes back to pre-event levels and win new business."

History: "at a lesser extent in 2025" appears in the Q3 2024 10-Q and the FY2024 10-K only; the Q1 2025 10-Q drops the Change Healthcare section from the Executive Overview.

This is the first time the impact is said to run past 2024, and "win new business" suggests clients left after the outage. "Notifications of impacted persons" joined the list of direct response costs ($341M in the quarter, $1.7B year to date).

▶ Provider-loan figure revised down: "accelerated funding and ... more than $9 billion" becomes "nearly $9 billion" Changed

Shift: scope narrowed ("accelerated funding" dropped); degree: more than → nearly (-)

Q2 2024: "To support care providers, we accelerated funding and provided interest-free loans of more than $9 billion through June 30, 2024."
Q3 2024: "To support care providers we provided interest-free loans of nearly $9 billion through September 30, 2024."

History: "accelerated funding and" appears in Q1 and Q2 2024 only; the FY2024 10-K goes back to "more than $9 billion through December 31, 2024"; the loan sentence is absent from every 2025 and 2026 filing.

A cumulative figure cannot fall unless the definition changed: the Q3 sentence no longer counts accelerated claim payments, only loans. This matters because the loans, not the accelerated payments, are what a year later needed a $799M collection reserve (FY2025 10-K).

▶ Medicare Advantage: 2026 Advance Notice "also well short of forward medical cost trend" Changed

History: the Q3 2024 sentence stood unchanged in Q1, Q2 and Q3 2024 (persistence 3 periods). Q1 2025 says the 2026 Final Notice was "beginning to approach" trend.

The company extends its funding complaint to a third rate year, now against a proposed increase rather than a decrease. The next filing describes the final 2026 rates as nearer to trend, so this was the low point of the rate language.

▶ Medicaid margin warning reappears; the 10-Q sentence that government programs carry "lower cost trends" is gone for good Changed

FY2024 10-K: "In Medicaid, we believe the payment rate environment creates the risk of continued downward pressure on Medicaid margin percentages."

History: "lower cost trends" appears in every 10-Q from Q2 2023 to Q3 2024 and in no 10-K; it is absent from all 10-Qs from Q1 2025. The Medicaid margin sentence is not new: it is in the FY2023 10-K, was absent from the three 2024 10-Qs, returns here, and is in every filing through Q2 2026.

An earlier version of this page called the margin warning new; it is a restored FY2023 sentence. The durable change is the other one: the claim that government programs have lower cost trends stopped appearing once the filings were describing elevated Medicaid and Medicare Advantage costs.

Themes

1. The Text Moved Before the Ratio, in Both Directions

Q1 2025 replaced "As expected and contemplated" with "above what we expected" while the MCR was 84.8%, one quarter before it reached 89.4%. The pricing admission ("well-short") and the regulatory-capital warning followed in Q2 2025. In Q1 2026 the "as expected" clause returned and "well-short" was deleted in the same filing that reported $1.05B of favorable development and an 83.9% MCR. By Q2 2026 a new commercial driver (the No Surprises Act) had been added inside the restored framing, and the MCR was back at 86.7%. On this evidence the opening clause of the Medical Cost Trends paragraph is the earliest indicator in these filings, but it has turned once each way. The FY2025 10-K also repeated, for 2026, the benefit-design assurance that failed in 2025 ("contemplated in our 2026 benefit design approach"); through Q2 2026 it has held, whereas the 2025 version was withdrawn after one filing.

2. 2026 Earnings Rest Partly on 2025 Reserves

Favorable development fell to $30M (nine months 2025) and $140M (FY2025), then reached $1,050M in Q1 2026, the first quarter with a named driver. The FY2025 loss-contract reserve ($623M) was being released through 2026 ($187M year-to-date), and Optum Health's Q2 2026 increase is attributed first to "increased favorable reserve development and the net decrease in loss contract reserves." This is consistent with conservative year-end 2025 reserving. Readers should treat part of the 2026 margin recovery as reserve-driven, not trend-driven.

3. Contraction Became the Strategy

Each forecast arrived one filing ahead of the result. "Markets we continue to participate" (Q2 2025) became "to contract" (FY2025 10-K) and then "has contracted" (Q1 2026). "Membership losses in 2026" (Q2 2025) became the "exit from one state" (FY2025 10-K), reported as a cause in Q2 2026. By Q2 2026 UnitedHealthcare served 1.6M fewer people, Medicare Advantage was down 785K and Medicaid 710K year over year, and Optum Rx scripts fell year over year in both 2026 quarters. The reduced membership also reduced Optum Rx volume.

4. Capital Stress Appeared in the Liquidity Section, Not Item 1A

Item 1A said "no material changes" in all eight quarters. Meanwhile the liquidity section recorded negative outlooks (Q2 2025), an A.M. Best downgrade (Q3 2025), subsidiary dividends going from $9.2B paid to $535M received, capital-relief reinsurance, three quarters without buybacks, and a 2026 restart through forward contracts. The DOJ factor widened in the forward-looking list while the contingencies note stayed the same. For UNH, the 10-Q's capital and legal changes show up in the MD&A liquidity text and the forward-looking factors, not in the risk factors. The debt-covenant ratio, reported at about 43% in Q3 2024, has not been disclosed since, while operating cash flow has leaned on a receivables facility and payment timing in 2025–2026.
