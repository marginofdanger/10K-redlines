UNITEDHEALTH GROUP (UNH): INVESTMENT THESIS PILLARS
Written October 2026 from the 10-K dashboard themes and tracked signals, the 10-Q scanner, and the
eight filings from the Q3 2024 10-Q (filed November 4, 2024) to the Q2 2026 10-Q (filed August 10, 2026).
Every pillar started the window at Intact. A stance moves only when findings point the same way across
two or more filings; the filing named is the one that moved it.

P1 Medical cost trend and pricing (the MCR)
  Bull assumes: pricing and benefit design catch up with cost trend, so the medical care ratio (MCR)
    returns toward the 83-85% range of 2022-2024 and UnitedHealthcare margins rebuild.
  Bear fears: cost trend (utilization, unit costs, coding intensity, specialty drugs, now commercial
    billing under the No Surprises Act) keeps outrunning pricing, as it did in 2025 (MCR 89.1%).
  Stance: Intact. Moved to Weakening at the Q2 2025 10-Q (Q1 2025: costs "above what we expected and
    contemplated"; Q2 2025: pricing "well-short of the medical cost trends incurred", MCR 89.4%).
    Moved back to Intact at the Q2 2026 10-Q (Q1 2026 MCR 83.9%, Q2 2026 86.7% vs 89.4%, "as expected
    and contemplated" restored in both). Not Strengthening: part of the 2026 improvement is reserve
    releases, and Q2 2026 names a new commercial cost driver.
  Tests: quarterly MCR versus the prior year with reserve development stripped out; the opening clause
    of the Medical Cost Trends paragraph; UnitedHealthcare operating margin.

P2 Reserves and earnings quality
  Bull assumes: medical cost reserves are set conservatively; reported earnings are mostly recurring.
  Bear fears: earnings depend on prior-year reserve releases, loss-contract releases, portfolio gains,
    investment income and a low tax rate, and reserve development can turn adverse.
  Stance: Weakening. Moved at the FY2024 10-K (Q3 2024: Optum Health earnings partly from "business
    portfolio and asset dispositions" and lower favorable development; FY2024: development $840M ->
    $700M, Optum Health gains led by "business portfolio refinement"). 2025 made it worse (Q3 2025
    implied adverse development of about $290M; FY2025 $140M, plus $2.5B of Q4 actions). 2026 points both
    ways: $1.25B of releases in H1 shows year-end 2025 reserves were conservative, but the same
    releases plus $187M of loss-contract releases carry part of 2026 earnings. No move.
  Tests: "Prior years" line of the medical costs payable rollforward; loss-contract reserve releases;
    portfolio gains and losses in operating costs; effective tax rate.

P3 Government programs: Medicare Advantage and Medicaid
  Bull assumes: Medicare Advantage rates move back toward cost trend, Medicaid rates catch up with
    member acuity, and the 2026 repricing restores margin even on fewer members.
  Bear fears: multi-year rate shortfalls, risk-model cuts and Medicaid rate gaps; membership shrinks
    (Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, a state exit) and CMS concentration rises (44% of 2025 revenue).
  Stance: Weakening. Moved at the FY2024 10-K (Q3 2024: Medicaid rates "well short of current care
    activity"; FY2024: 2026 Advance Notice "also well short", Medicaid margin warning). Since then the
    forecast gap narrowing failed (Q2 2025), Medicare Advantage was forecast to contract (FY2025) and
    did (Q1 2026 -690K, Q2 2026 -785K), offset by better pricing.
  Tests: Medicare Advantage and Medicaid membership, Final Notice language, Medicaid rate language,
    UnitedHealthcare earnings on Medicare & Retirement and Community & State revenue.

P4 Optum Health and value-based care
  Bull assumes: fully accountable value-based care is a durable margin source (7%+ operating margin)
    and patient growth resumes after repricing.
  Bear fears: Medicare funding cuts and cost trend make risk contracts unprofitable; the business
    shrinks, exits markets and books loss-contract reserves.
  Stance: Weakening. Moved at the Q2 2025 10-Q (Q1 2025: revenue -5%, earnings -15%, "conversion of
    risk-based contracts"; Q2 2025: earnings -67%, "markets we continue to participate"). Q3 2025 (1.0%
    margin) and FY2025 ($(278)M loss, $623M loss-contract reserve) deepened it. 2026 margins (4.7%, 5.1%)
    are helped by reserve releases; Q1 2026 earnings fell, Q2 2026 rose, so no move.
  Tests: Optum Health operating margin excluding loss-contract releases, people served under
    value-based arrangements, portfolio losses.

P5 Optum Rx and Optum Insight (including Change Healthcare)
  Bull assumes: Optum Rx keeps growing scripts and earnings; Optum Insight recovers its pre-attack
    earnings and the Change Healthcare provider loans are repaid.
  Bear fears: UnitedHealthcare's shrinking membership cuts Optum Rx volume; Optum Insight loses
    business-services volume; provider loans prove partly uncollectible.
  Stance: Intact. No two consecutive filings pointed the same way: 2025 recovery at Insight and script
    growth at Rx were offset by the Q3 2025 volume decline and the FY2025 $799M loan reserve; 2026 script
    declines (-6%, -7%) came with Q2 2026 earnings growth at both segments.
  Tests: adjusted scripts, Optum Rx and Optum Insight earnings, business-services volume, provider
    loan repayments.

P6 Cash flow, capital return and balance sheet
  Bull assumes: operating cash flow above net income funds dividends and buybacks; regulated
    subsidiaries pay the parent; debt stays within an A-rated profile.
  Bear fears: subsidiaries need capital instead of paying dividends, ratings slip, and buybacks and
    acquisitions are debt- or commercial-paper-funded with little parent cash.
  Stance: Weakening. Moved at the Q2 2025 10-Q (Q1 2025: $3.0B of buybacks with $3.9B of net borrowing
    and $5.2B of commercial paper; Q2 2025: three Negative outlooks and subsidiary dividends that "may
    be impacted"). Q3 2025 (A.M. Best A-) and FY2025 ($535M net capital infusions) confirmed it. 2026 cash
    improved in both 10-Qs (H1 operating cash flow $20.0B, debt down $5.1B, commercial paper zero), but
    the filing credits "timing of government payments", outlooks are still Negative and parent cash is
    $1.1B; the FY2026 10-K subsidiary dividend figure is the test before moving it back.
  Tests: subsidiary dividends net of infusions, ratings table, cash available for general corporate
    use, buybacks versus free cash flow, total debt and commercial paper.

P7 Legal, regulatory and tax exposure
  Bull assumes: the DOJ risk-adjustment case resolves in UNH's favour and investigations stay routine.
  Bear fears: wider DOJ scrutiny of Medicare Advantage, RADV audits with OIG, shareholder suits and IRS
    transfer-pricing adjustments with an open-ended reach.
  Stance: Weakening. Moved at the FY2025 10-K (Q2 2025: the DOJ factor widened to "legal actions
    concerning our participation in the Medicare program" and shareholders joined the litigant list;
    FY2025: subpoena and "no assurance" language added, gross unrecognized tax benefits $4.1B -> $5.6B).
    Q1 2026 added IRS notices for 2017-2020; Q1 2025's Special Master report is the bull offset.
  Tests: the DOJ paragraph, IRS NOPA outcome, unrecognized tax benefits, any new named matter.
