UNH 10-K Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of UnitedHealth Group's SEC filings (FY2018-FY2025). Revenue grew from $226B to $448B. MCR rose from 81.6% to 89.1%. FY2025 disclosed an Optum Health operating loss of $(278M), $672M of premium-deficiency and loss-contract reserves, $2.5B of restructuring and other actions, an A.M. Best downgrade to A- with Negative outlooks at Moody's, S&P and Fitch, and a new CEO and CFO.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0000731766) | Fiscal Years 2018-2025 | Analysis: March 2026; FY2023-FY2025 transitions re-analyzed October 2026

FY2018 → FY2019: Steady Growth

Employees: 300K → 325K | Revenue: $226B → $242B | MCR: 81.6% → 82.5%

Business

▶Backlog growth signals pipeline strength; Optum revenue accelerationSignal
The backlog continued to grow steadily, reflecting strong pipeline conversion across both UHC and Optum segments. Optum's revenue growth outpaced UHC for the first time in several metrics. The filing language remained largely template-stable -- incremental updates rather than structural rewrites.
▶Template-stable filing: minimal language changes year-over-yearSignal
The FY2018 to FY2019 filing was stable in language and structure. Risk factors, business descriptions, and segment narratives were updated with new numbers but the framing was nearly identical. This stability contrasts with the extensive rewrites that began in FY2020.

FY2019 → FY2020: COVID

Employees: 325K → 330K | Revenue: $242B → $257B | MCR: 79.1%

Business / Structure

▶Optum listed before UHC for the first time in filing orderChanged
For the first time, Optum's business description appeared before UnitedHealthcare's in the filing structure. Previously, UHC's business description appeared first. In the FY2020 filing, Optum segments were described before UHC for the first time, placing the growth business ahead of the insurance business in the narrative order.
▶Human Capital section added per SEC mandate; health equity language introducedNew
The SEC's new Human Capital disclosure requirement led to the addition of a dedicated section. UNH used this as an opportunity to introduce "health equity" language for the first time, framing workforce diversity and community health outcomes as strategic priorities. This language would grow in subsequent filings before being cut approximately 70% in FY2025.
▶MCR plunges to 79.1% as COVID suppresses utilizationSignal
The medical care ratio dropped sharply as COVID-19 lockdowns suppressed elective procedures and routine care. This created a one-time profitability windfall that would reverse in subsequent years as deferred care returned. The MCR of 79.1% was the lowest in the company's recent history. MCR would not return to this level in any subsequent year, rising to 82.6% (FY2021), 83.2% (FY2023), 85.5% (FY2024), and 89.1% (FY2025).

FY2020 → FY2021: Witty Era

Employees: 330K → 350K | Revenue: $257B → $288B | MCR: 82.6%

Business / Identity

▶Witty becomes CEO; UNH rebrands as a "well-being company"Changed
Andrew Witty became CEO, joining from GlaxoSmithKline. The filing introduced "well-being company" framing, broadening UNH's self-description beyond health insurance and health services.
▶Value-based care dollar figures removed from disclosureRemoved
Previously, UNH had disclosed specific dollar figures for value-based care arrangements. In FY2021, these quantitative disclosures were removed and replaced with qualitative language about VBC's strategic importance. Specific dollar figures did not return in subsequent filings. By FY2025, VBC language shifted to "to contract."
FY2020: "contracts with value-based elements totaled $79 billion in annual spending, including $20 billion through risk-transfer agreements"
FY2021: specific dollar figures removed; replaced with qualitative VBC growth language

FY2021 → FY2022: Change Healthcare

Employees: 350K → ~400K | Revenue: $288B → $324B | MCR: 82.0%

Business / Strategy

▶Change Healthcare acquisition closes; backlog surges +$7.6BNew
The $13.6B acquisition of Change Healthcare was the largest in UNH's history. The backlog immediately surged by $7.6B, reflecting Change's existing contract base. Change Healthcare processed billions of healthcare transactions annually, adding health IT infrastructure to UNH's existing insurance, care delivery, and pharmacy operations.
▶"Global" segment removed; international operations de-emphasizedRemoved
The UnitedHealthcare Global segment was removed as a separate reporting unit, with international operations folded into or de-emphasized in the disclosure. This signaled the beginning of UNH's international retreat, which would culminate in the FY2024 sale of the Brazil business at a $7.1B loss. The filing quietly reduced the visibility of international operations.
▶"Those who serve" language appears in mission/values framingNew
New language emphasizing service to "those who serve" -- military, veterans, public sector workers -- appeared in the values and mission framing. UNH's government business includes Tricare and VA partnerships.

FY2022 → FY2023: Peak Profitability

Employees: ~400K → 440K+ | Revenue: $324B → $372B | MCR: 83.2%

Business / Innovation

▶AI/ML language appears for the first time in business descriptionNew
For the first time, UNH's 10-K incorporated artificial intelligence and machine learning language in its business description, describing how AI/ML was being applied to claims processing, clinical decision support, and fraud detection. UNH's described AI applications were focused on operational efficiency: claims processing, clinical decision support, fraud detection, and care management optimization.
▶"Quality" and "inclusion" added to corporate values languageNew
The corporate values section was expanded to incorporate "quality" as a named value and strengthen "inclusion" language. These additions represented the peak of UNH's DEI and quality-focused corporate narrative. Both terms were removed in FY2025.
▶Medicaid redeterminations risk factor added; membership pressure anticipatedSignal
A new risk factor disclosed the impact of Medicaid redeterminations as the COVID-era continuous enrollment provision expired. States began re-verifying eligibility, leading to membership losses in the Medicaid/dual-eligible population. The filing language acknowledged this would pressure UHC Community & State membership and revenue.

FY2023 → FY2024: Cyberattack

Filed Feb 27, 2025 | Revenue: $371.6B → $400.3B (+8%) | Operating income: $32.4B → $32.3B | Net income: $22.4B → $14.4B | Diluted EPS: $23.86 → $15.51 | MCR: 83.2% → 85.5% | Employees: 440K+ → ~400K

Medical Costs / Pricing

▶"80% to 85%" medical-cost range deleted from the risk factors as the MCR reaches 85.5%; cost misses go from "can result" to "have resulted"Removed
The core risk factor on medical costs used to state the share of premiums UNH expects to spend on care. FY2024 deletes that sentence in the same year the MCR rose 2.3 points to 85.5%, the top of the old range, and changes the sentence about cost-prediction errors from a hypothetical to something that has already happened.
FY2023: "We generally use approximately 80% to 85% of our premium revenues to pay the costs of health care services delivered to these customers. ... Relatively small differences between predicted and actual medical costs, or utilization rates as a percentage of revenues, can result in significant changes in our financial results."
FY2024: "[sentence deleted] ... Relatively small differences between predicted and actual medical costs, or utilization rates as a percentage of revenues, have resulted and in the future may result in significant changes in our financial results."
The range was not replaced with a higher one. FY2025's MCR of 89.1% would have been well outside it.
▶Cost trend reframed as "expected and contemplated in our benefits design"; hospital coding intensity and IRA-driven drug prescribing added as new driversChanged
FY2023 described a specific 2023 surge (senior outpatient procedures) and predicted it would last through 2024. FY2024 says the higher care patterns were anticipated in pricing, then names two new cost drivers (hospital coding and high-cost drugs prescribed early in response to the IRA) that it expects to continue.
FY2023: "During 2023, we observed increased care patterns, primarily related to outpatient procedures for seniors, which we expect will persist throughout 2024, and may continue in future periods."
FY2024: "As expected and contemplated in our benefits design, we have continued to observe increased care patterns, which may continue in future periods. We also observed an upshift in hospital coding intensity and an acceleration in the prescribing of certain high-cost medications in early response to the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA). We expect these additional factors to continue into future periods."
One year later FY2025 reversed this to "above what we expected and contemplated in our pricing and benefits design" (see FY2024 → FY2025), so the reassurance did not hold up.
▶MCR explanation grows from one driver to six: Medicare funding cuts, Medicaid rate mismatch, coding intensity, specialty drugs, mix and cyberattack accommodationsChanged
The MCR rose 230bps (83.2% → 85.5%) and operating income was flat at $32.3B even though revenue grew 8%. FY2023 blamed senior outpatient care and business mix. FY2024 lists funding-side causes first, putting the revenue side (CMS and the states) ahead of utilization.
FY2023: "The MCR increased as a result of elevated care activity, primarily relating to outpatient care for seniors, and business mix."
FY2024: "The MCR increased as a result of the revenue effects of the Medicare funding reductions, Medicaid timing mismatch between people's health status and rates, upshift in hospital coding intensity, specialty pharmaceutical prescribing patterns, member mix and due to incremental medical costs for accommodations made to care providers as a result of the Change Healthcare cyberattack."
▶New Medicaid warning: state rates "remained well short of current care activity"; gap expected to "narrow in 2025"New
Redeterminations removed healthier members first, leaving a sicker Medicaid population priced on older rates. FY2024 adds the problem and a dated forecast that it will improve.
FY2024 (added): "The Medicaid redetermination process has also caused a timing mismatch between the current health status of people served through Medicaid and state rate updates, which remained well short of current care activity. We expect this gap between people's health status and rates will narrow in 2025."
The forecast was wrong. FY2025 says the mismatch "continues" and that the rate environment "remains insufficient".
▶Favorable prior-year reserve development falls $840M → $700MSignal
Prior-year reserve releases fell 17%, from about 2.9% to 2.2% of beginning medical costs payable ($29.1B and $32.4B). This was the first step down after FY2023's $840M. FY2025 cut it further to $140M.
FY2023: "Medical costs in 2023, 2022 and 2021 included favorable medical cost development related to prior years of $840 million, $410 million and $1.7 billion, respectively."
FY2024: "Medical costs in 2024, 2023 and 2022 included favorable medical cost development related to prior years of $700 million, $840 million and $410 million, respectively."

Change Healthcare Cyberattack

▶New cyberattack section: $2.2B direct response costs, $867M lost Optum Insight revenue, $9B+ in interest-free provider loans, ~190 million people affectedNew
The MD&A opens with a new section on the February 21, 2024 attack. The direct costs plus disruption total about $3.1B, and the loans put more than $9B of UNH's cash with care providers.
FY2024: "To support care providers we provided interest-free loans of more than $9 billion through December 31, 2024. For the year ended December 31, 2024, we incurred $2.2 billion of direct response costs ... Optum Insight also experienced estimated business disruption impacts of $867 million"
FY2024: "We have determined the estimated total number of individuals impacted by the Change Healthcare cyberattack is approximately 190 million."
Optum Insight operating income fell 27% ($4.27B → $3.10B). The $9B in loans led to a $799M collection reserve in FY2025.
▶Risk factors now cite the breach and admit acquired businesses brought "more acute" IT vulnerabilitiesNew
The generic cyber risk factor gains a concrete example, and the acquisitions risk factor adds a sentence that reads as a lesson from Change Healthcare: UNH says it may not be able to fix weaknesses in a business soon after buying it. Vendor operations are now described as "outside our direct oversight and control."
FY2024: "For example, we previously reported our Change Healthcare business, which we had recently acquired, was subject to a cyberattack in 2024, in which the data involved contained protected health information or personally identifiable information."
FY2024 (added): "For example, we have experienced and in the future may encounter more acute information technology system vulnerabilities or different litigation risk profiles in recently acquired business than we have historically managed. We may be unable to address such vulnerabilities, inadequacies, differences, or failures soon after acquiring a business"

Portfolio / Earnings Quality

▶South America exit: Brazil sold at a $7.1B loss, remaining operations held for sale at a $1.2B loss; currency-risk disclosures deletedRemoved
The $8.3B loss on sale ($5.0B of it from currency translation losses already in equity) is why net income fell 36% to $14.4B while operating income held flat. The business section drops the 7.8 million global members, and market risk no longer covers the Brazilian real or Chilean peso.
FY2024: "On February 6, 2024, the Company completed the sale of its Brazil operations. During the year ended December 31, 2024, we recorded a loss of $7.1 billion, of which $4.1 billion related to the impact of cumulative foreign currency translation losses"
FY2024: "In the second quarter of 2024, the Company initiated a plan to sell its remaining South American operations, which were classified as held for sale as of December 31, 2024. During the year ended December 31, 2024, we recorded a loss of $1.2 billion"
FY2023 (deleted in FY2024): "Globally, UnitedHealthcare Employer & Individual serves 7.8 million people with medical and dental benefits, typically in exchange for a monthly premium per member, residing principally in Brazil, Chile, Colombia and Peru"
▶Optum Health's 18% profit gain now partly credited to "gains related to business portfolio refinement"; same gains cut the consolidated operating cost ratioSignal
Optum Health operating income rose from $6.56B to $7.77B. The FY2024 explanation puts gains on transactions first, ahead of cost management. Those gains also offset cyberattack costs in the company-wide operating cost ratio (14.7% → 13.2%) and in UnitedHealthcare and Optum Insight earnings.
FY2023: "Earnings from operations increased due to cost management initiatives and increased investment income, partially offset by higher senior outpatient and behavioral health care activity"
FY2024: "Earnings from operations increased due to gains related to business portfolio refinement, including strategic transactions, increased investment income and cost management initiatives, partially offset by Medicare Advantage funding reductions"
This suggests FY2024's flat $32.3B operating income depended on one-time gains. FY2025 lists "gains on dispositions in 2024" as one reason Optum Health fell to a $(278)M loss.

Capital / Key Relationships

▶Debt-covenant figures dropped (60% maximum, 38% actual) and replaced with "in compliance" as long-term debt rises $58.3B → $72.4BRemoved
UNH stopped reporting its leverage ratio against the covenant in the year it borrowed a net $14.7B (loans to providers, acquisitions, buybacks). Cash available for general corporate use also fell from $1.3B to about $800M.
FY2023: "covenants requiring us to maintain a defined debt to debt-plus-shareholders' equity ratio of not more than 60% ... As of December 31, 2023, our debt to debt-plus-shareholders' equity ratio, as defined and calculated under the credit facilities, was 38%."
FY2024: "As of December 31, 2024, we were in compliance with the various covenants under our bank credit facilities."
▶AARP name removed from the filing (17 mentions → 0); now "a membership organization" after a July 2024 contract amendmentRemoved
AARP, UNH's best-known Medicare partner, is no longer named. The distribution paragraph anonymizes it, the Medicare Supplement count drops "in association with AARP", and the AARP Program accounting note is replaced by a disclosure that the program was amended. Program assets previously held off the books are now on UNH's balance sheet.
FY2023: "direct marketing to consumers on behalf of its key clients, including AARP, the nation's largest membership organization dedicated to the needs of people age 50 and over"
FY2024: "direct marketing to consumers on behalf of its key clients, a membership organization, and state and U.S. government agencies."
FY2024: "In July 2024, the Company amended its Medicare Supplement Program with a membership organization (the Medicare Supplement Program). The amendments provide the Company the right to use a trade name and other intellectual property in marketing efforts for Medicare Supplement offerings."
The relationship continues: 4.3 million Medicare Supplement members are still reported. The filing no longer identifies the counterparty.

Leadership / Structure

▶Three of seven executive officers leave (McMahon, President and COO; Thompson, UHC CEO; Bondy, CLO); Rex becomes President and CFO; Cianfrocco runs Optum; Noel runs UHC from January 2025Changed
Andrew Witty stays CEO, but the operating team under him is almost entirely new. Brian Thompson's biography is removed without explanation. His employment agreement is the only remaining reference to him, as an exhibit. Headquarters moves from Minnetonka to Eden Prairie, and a Washington, DC address is listed as an executive office.
FY2023: "Brian Thompson has served as Chief Executive Officer of UnitedHealthcare since April 2021."
FY2024: "Tim Noel has served as Chief Executive Officer of UnitedHealthcare since January 2025."
FY2024: "Our executive offices are located at 1 Health Drive, Eden Prairie, Minnesota 55344 and 655 New York Avenue, Washington, DC 20001"

FY2024 → FY2025: The Crisis

Filed Mar 2, 2026 | Revenue: $400.3B → $447.6B (+12%) | Operating income: $32.3B → $19.0B (-41%) | Net income: $14.4B → $12.1B | Diluted EPS: $15.51 → $13.23 | MCR: 85.5% → 89.1% | Buybacks: $9.0B → $5.5B (none in Q4)

Medical Costs / Pricing

▶Cost trend goes from "as expected and contemplated" to "above what we expected and contemplated in our pricing"; provider pricing and per-visit bundling namedChanged
This is the most direct reversal in the filing: last year's claim that cost trends were priced in becomes an admission that they were not. The new paragraph also names causes partly outside UNH's control: provider price increases, more services billed per visit, members picked up where other Medicare Advantage plans exited, and the individual exchange pool. The FY2024 paragraph about coding intensity and IRA drug prescribing is deleted.
FY2024: "As expected and contemplated in our benefits design, we have continued to observe increased care patterns, which may continue in future periods."
FY2025: "We have observed increased care patterns that are above what we expected and contemplated in our pricing and benefits design. We have also observed an increase in health care unit costs and in the intensity of services delivered, driven by increases in provider pricing and additional services bundled per visit."
▶Pricing paragraph adds a direct admission: 2025 pricing and health-status assumptions "were well-short of the medical cost trends incurred"New
The pricing paragraph had changed by only one word in two years. It now adds member health status as a pricing input and says the 2025 assumptions missed. The MCR rose 3.6 points to 89.1%, and UnitedHealthcare's operating margin fell from 5.2% to 2.7%.
FY2024: "we start with our view of expected future costs, including medical care patterns, inflation and labor market dynamics."
FY2025: "we start with our view of expected future costs, including medical care patterns, the mix and health status of people served, inflation and labor market dynamics. For 2025, our pricing trends and patient and member health status assumptions were well-short of the medical cost trends incurred, significantly impacting our earnings."
▶Prior-year reserve releases fall $700M → $140M; first $672M premium-deficiency and loss-contract reserve; "decreased favorable development" listed as an MCR driverSignal
Favorable development fell 80%, to 0.4% of beginning medical costs payable. Over three years it went $840M → $700M → $140M. FY2025 is also the first year with a charge for contracts expected to lose money: $672M, of which $623M is the Optum Health loss-contract reserve for 2026 value-based care losses. The new MCR explanation names both items.
FY2025: "Medical costs in 2025, 2024 and 2023 included favorable medical cost development related to prior years of $140 million, $700 million and $840 million, respectively."
FY2025: "The MCR increased as a result of the revenue effects of the Medicare funding reductions, elevated medical cost trend, the member profile of newly added patients under value-based care arrangements, the acceleration of anticipated future losses in 2026 related to certain Optum Health value-based care contracts, decreased favorable development"
Smaller releases reduce the earnings cushion from prior reserves at the same time the 2025 claims year was underpriced.
▶Medicaid forecast missed: last year's "gap ... will narrow in 2025" becomes "there continues to be a timing mismatch"; rates "remain insufficient"; one-state exit plannedChanged
The separate redeterminations section, which held the 2025 forecast, is deleted. The Medicaid paragraph changes from "we believe ... creates the risk" to a flat statement that funding is insufficient, names the cost categories driving the problem, and forecasts membership losses.
FY2024: "We expect this gap between people's health status and rates will narrow in 2025."
FY2025: "Due to elevated care activity in Medicaid, specifically related to behavioral, pharmacy and home health, there continues to be a timing mismatch between the health status of people served and state rate updates. The funding and payment rate environment remains insufficient to meet the health needs of patients ... We expect Medicaid membership losses in 2026 as a result of reduced Medicaid eligibility and the exit from one state."
▶Membership contraction forecast: Medicare Advantage "will contract in 2026" after growing 7.8M → 8.4M; 2027 Advance Notice "far below" trend; exchange exits impliedNew
Medicare Advantage grew by 600,000 members in 2025 (some from markets where other plans exited), and the filing ties those members to higher costs. FY2025 now forecasts shrinkage from benefit and pricing actions. The rate paragraph changes from "at times" to "for numerous years" and adds a "compounding" effect from multi-year shortfalls. A new commercial paragraph refers to exchange "markets where we choose to remain" and to possible lower membership. CMS premiums rose from 40% to 44% of consolidated revenue.
FY2024: "Our 2025 benefit design approach contemplates these trends."
FY2025: "As a result of continued funding pressures, which have resulted in benefit and pricing actions, we expect that our Medicare Advantage membership will contract in 2026."
FY2025: "While the Final Notice for 2026 approached the expected industry forward medical cost trend, the Advanced Notice for 2027 is far below. Additionally, increased medical costs in 2025, which are expected to continue in future periods, have added to the compounding impact of the previous multi-year rate shortfalls"
FY2025 (added): "Continued increased medical costs may impact both future pricing and benefit design, including for our individual exchange products in markets where we choose to remain ... These potential changes, along with certain regulatory impacts, may result in decreased membership in future periods."

Optum Health / Restructuring

▶Optum Health swings from $7.77B profit to a $(278)M loss; value-based care patients now expected "to contract"; risk contracts converted to fee-basedSignal
The segment built on value-based care growth now forecasts that business will shrink. Revenue fell 3% to $102.0B, partly from "the conversion of risk-based contracts to fee-based", and consumers served fell from 100M to 95M. The earnings explanation lists $1.7B of restructuring impact, including the $623M loss-contract reserve, plus $821M of portfolio losses.
FY2024: "Revenues at Optum Health increased primarily due to organic growth in patients served under value-based care arrangements."
FY2025: "Revenues at Optum Health decreased primarily due to the conversion of risk-based contracts to fee-based, Medicare Advantage funding reductions and the profile of members served"
FY2025 (added): "As a result of increased pricing in response to anticipated care patterns in 2026 and decreased people served through UnitedHealthcare Medicare Advantage offerings, we expect the number of people served under value-based care arrangements to contract."
▶$2.5B Q4 restructuring itemized, plus a $799M reserve against Change Healthcare provider loans and a $568M net gain on divestituresNew
A new section replaces last year's cyberattack narrative with Q4 2025 charges. The $799M collection reserve is the first sign that the 2024 interest-free provider loans ($9.0B lent; $4.5B repaid in 2024 and $1.7B in 2025, per the cash flow table) will not be fully repaid. A $1.5B gain at Optum Rx from deconsolidating a business offset the portfolio losses at Optum Health.
FY2025: "the Company took restructuring and other actions that resulted in a total impact of $2.5 billion, which included real estate rationalization and workforce reductions of $746 million, contractual reassessments of $573 million, the establishment a loss contract reserve related to anticipated future losses in 2026 for certain value-based care businesses of $623 million, net valuation losses on equity securities of $329 million and the advance funding of the United Health Foundation of $250 million."
FY2025: "In the fourth quarter of 2025, the Company increased its reserves for net collection expectations associated with provider loans and other customer balances of $799 million"
Optum Rx was the only segment with higher earnings ($5.84B → $7.19B), and part of that gain came from the deconsolidation.

Capital / Ratings / Liquidity

▶All four agencies act: Moody's, S&P and Fitch outlooks Stable → Negative; A.M. Best A → A-; risk factor now says "We have been the subject of downgrades"Changed
The ratings table showed Stable at every agency for FY2023 and FY2024. Now three outlooks are Negative, and A.M. Best has downgraded both senior debt (A → A-) and commercial paper (AMB-1+ → AMB-1). The ratings risk factor moves from a hypothetical to a past event.
FY2024: "Senior unsecured debt A2 Stable A+ Stable A Stable A Stable"
FY2025: "Senior unsecured debt A2 Negative A+ Negative A Negative A- Stable"
FY2025: "We have been the subject of downgrades and other negative credit rating actions in past periods, and may not be able to maintain our current credit ratings in future periods."
▶Regulated subsidiaries go from paying $9.2B of dividends to receiving $535M of net capital; required statutory capital "expected to increase"; reinsurance used to ease itSignal
This is the first reversal of upstream cash flow in the dataset ($8.8B, $8.0B, $9.2B in 2022-24). A new Regulatory Capital paragraph explains that a higher MCR raises required capital and discloses reinsurance agreements, accounted for as deposits, that reduce capital requirements. The capital risk factor adds "and medical costs incurred" as a driver of required capital.
FY2024: "Our U.S. regulated subsidiaries paid their parent companies dividends of $9.2 billion and $8.0 billion in 2024 and 2023, respectively."
FY2025: "Our U.S. regulated subsidiaries received capital infusions, net of dividends, of $535 million"
FY2025 (added): "As a result of an increased MCR impacting our regulated insurance and HMO subsidiaries, the specified levels of required statutory capital required to be maintained are expected to increase. We have entered into various agreements with reinsurers that could limit our risk of loss under certain circumstances, thus reducing our capital and surplus requirements."
▶Buybacks cut $9.0B → $5.5B with none after Q2 and none in Q4; operating cash flow of $19.7B helped by $3.0B of receivables sold under a new facilitySignal
XBRL figures show $5.54B repurchased by June 30 and nothing after. Item 5 confirms no Q4 repurchases. Operating cash flow fell for a second year ($29.1B → $24.2B → $19.7B), and the decline would have been larger without a new $3.3B receivables financing facility, under which $3.0B was sold and counted as operating cash. A $9.7B loan book appears, and the investment risk factor becomes "investment and loan portfolio".
FY2025: "There were no repurchases of the Company's common stock during the three months ended December 31, 2025."
FY2025: "In 2025, the Company entered into a $3.3 billion 364-day uncommitted receivables financing facility under which certain receivables may be sold to financial institutions. The sales of the receivables under the facility are recorded as a reduction to other current receivables on the Consolidated Balance Sheets and classified as an operating cash flow"
FY2025: "For the year ended December 31, 2025, the Company sold $3.0 billion of receivables under the receivables funding facility"

Leadership

▶Hemsley returns as CEO (May 2025); Wayne DeVeydt, ex-Bain Capital and former Elevance CFO, becomes CFO (Sept 2025); Dr. Patrick Conway, former CMS chief medical officer, runs OptumChanged
The CEO, CFO and Optum CEO from FY2024 (Witty, Rex, Cianfrocco) are all gone within the year. Hemsley, 73, was CEO from 2006 to 2017 and had been non-executive chair. DeVeydt joins from Bain Capital; his Elevance CFO role ended in 2016. Conway was promoted from Optum Rx CEO and previously served as CMS chief medical officer.
FY2025: "Stephen Hemsley has served as Chief Executive Officer and Chair of the Board of UnitedHealth Group since May 2025. Steve previously served as Non-Executive Chair of the Board from November 2019 to May 2025"
FY2025: "Wayne DeVeydt has served as Chief Financial Officer of UnitedHealth Group since September 2025. Prior to joining UnitedHealth Group, Wayne was Managing Director of Bain Capital, a private investment firm, from March 2022 to August 2025."

Themes

The Crisis Year

FY2025 metrics: MCR 89.1% (+360bps), operating income $32.3B → $19.0B, Optum Health $(278M) loss vs $7.8B prior year, A.M. Best downgrade to A- and Negative outlooks at Moody's, S&P and Fitch, subsidiary cash flows reversed ($9.2B dividends to $535M net infusions), buybacks $9.0B → $5.5B (none after Q2), $672M premium-deficiency and loss-contract reserves (first in the dataset), $2.5B restructuring and other actions, prior-year reserve releases down to $140M. Net earnings fell from $14.4B to $12.1B, and FY2024 had already absorbed the $8.3B South America loss. The FY2024 claim that cost trends were "expected and contemplated" proved wrong within a year.

The VBC Wall

VBC filing language progressed from quantified dollar figures (FY2018-20, including "$79 billion in annual spending") to removed dollar figures (FY2021) to qualitative growth language (FY2022-23) to "to contract" (FY2025). Optum Health operating income went from $7.8B (FY2024) to $(278M) (FY2025), a $623M loss-contract reserve was recorded for 2026, and risk-based contracts are being converted to fee-based.

CMS 30% → 44%

CMS premium revenue rose from approximately 30% of total revenue (FY2018) to 44% (FY2025). MA membership grew from approximately 5.2M to 8.4M over the same period, including 600,000 added in 2025 partly from markets where other plans exited. FY2025 cites "Medicare funding reductions" as a driver of both MCR deterioration and Optum Health's loss, calls the 2027 Advance Notice "far below" trend, and expects MA membership to "contract in 2026."

Change Healthcare: Moat to Breach

Change Healthcare was acquired for $13.6B (FY2022), added $7.6B to backlog, and processed billions of healthcare transactions annually. In February 2024, a cyberattack on Change Healthcare affected about 190 million individuals and cost $2.2B in direct response costs plus $867M of lost revenue. UNH lent providers $9.0B interest-free, and in Q4 2025 reserved $799M against collection of those loans and other balances. The acquisitions risk factor now admits acquired businesses can bring "more acute information technology system vulnerabilities."

The Hemsley Reset

The CEO changed twice: Wichmann (FY2018-20), Witty (FY2021-24), Hemsley (from May 2025, age 73, also Chair). FY2024 replaced most of the team under Witty: McMahon, Thompson and Bondy out; Noel at UHC, Cianfrocco at Optum. FY2025 replaced the top three: Wayne DeVeydt (ex-Bain Capital, Elevance CFO until 2016) became CFO, and Dr. Patrick Conway (ex-Optum Rx CEO, former CMS chief medical officer) runs Optum. Hemsley previously served as CEO from 2006 to 2017.

OptumRx: The Survivor

Optum Rx revenue grew from $99.8B (FY2022) to $154.7B (FY2025), while adjusted scripts rose from 1.44B to 1.66B and revenue per script went from about $69 to $93. Its operating income rose from $5.84B to $7.19B in FY2025, helped by a $1.5B net portfolio gain. It was the only segment with higher earnings while Optum Health posted a $(278M) loss and the consolidated MCR reached 89.1%.

The Vertical Integration Question

UNH operates across insurance (UHC), care delivery (OptumHealth), pharmacy (OptumRx), and health IT (OptumInsight/Change Healthcare). Consolidated eliminations, mostly UnitedHealthcare spending at Optum, grew from $108.3B (FY2022) to $168.0B (FY2025), about 60% of Optum's revenue. FY2025 added a warning that states are considering broader corporate-practice-of-medicine laws that "could adversely impact how we structure transactions and contract with and support physicians." UHC segment earnings fell from $16.4B (FY2023) to $9.4B (FY2025); Optum earnings fell from $16.7B (FY2024) to $9.5B (FY2025).
