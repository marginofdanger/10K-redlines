VEEV 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of Veeva Systems' 10-Q filings, FY2025 Q1 (quarter ended April 30, 2024) through FY2027 Q2 (quarter ended July 31, 2026, filed August 27, 2026), with the FY2025 and FY2026 10-K boundary transitions included. Veeva's fiscal year ends January 31. The quarterly chain covers the Salesforce agreement's expiry, the end of eight years of IQVIA litigation, the Vault CRM migration turning into named customer losses, and the arrival of Veeva AI. Several of these moves show up in the quarterly risk factors one to three quarters before the annual 10-K comparison picks them up.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0001393052) | 8 quarterly 10-Qs + 2 annual 10-Ks | FY2025 Q1 – FY2027 Q2 | Analysis: October 2026

Transition Overview

FY25 Q1 → Q2

China CRM losses move from hypothetical to "some customers have chosen." The Washington health-data law moves from "may curtail" to modifications already made to Crossix audience segments. The G&A outlook flips from "relatively consistent" to an increase tied to the CEO equity grant, and professional services revenue starts declining.

FY25 Q2 → Q3

Two risks become facts: customers "have exercised" termination-for-convenience rights, and competitors "have offered" price concessions. A new risk names the elimination of direct-to-consumer drug advertising as a threat to Crossix. Veeva Data Cloud and data costs drop out of the MD&A, and the expense outlook horizon shortens to "near term."

FY25 Q3 → FY2025 10-K

The quarterly Commercial-share risk factor (core CRM penetration, 46% of revenue) is deleted. Salesforce becomes the "primary CRM competitor" and a large customer defects. The CRM migration moves from "intend" to "have begun." Federal agency funding cuts (FDA, NIH, Medicaid) enter the risk factors, and normalized billings now adjusts for "delayed renewals."

FY2025 10-K → FY26 Q1

"New customers" disappears from both growth-attribution sentences, and price increases are added to the R&D driver. Veeva AI is announced as an initiative across all major areas. The May 12, 2025 drug-pricing Executive Order and tariffs on life sciences products are added. The IQVIA non-compete case settles on May 30, 2025.

FY26 Q1 → Q2

All IQVIA litigation settles on August 13, 2025 with no damages either way, but Veeva accrues $31M of law-firm success fees for "non-monetary outcomes." The IQVIA data-access risk factor is deleted outright, and IQVIA exits CRM after 2029. "A large" customer that left for Salesforce becomes "certain large" customers.

FY26 Q2 → Q3

Salesforce "has developed" (not "is developing") its life sciences CRM, and the Salesforce agreement "expired." The EU Data Act lets EU customers cancel without cause, and customers "reduce usage." Professional services gross margin falls 22%→18% and gets its own disclosure sentence for the first time.

FY26 Q3 → FY2026 10-K

Veeva CRM end of support moves earlier, from September 1, 2030 to December 31, 2029. "A number of our customers have informed us of their intent to move to Salesforce." Customer loss moves out of the migration risk and into a rewritten competition headline. A $2B buyback starts, and LLM costs become a named profitability risk.

FY2026 10-K → FY27 Q1

Price increases rise from "to a lesser extent" to a co-equal growth driver in the same filing that deletes the "lower of 4% or CPI" inflation-clause disclosure. Commercial subscription growth slows to +$32M. Professional services grow 23%, and the "increased scrutiny" of services projects is deleted. Ostro is acquired.

FY27 Q1 → Q2

Every forward expense-outlook sentence is deleted. Anthropic is named as an LLM supplier, with a new risk of "sudden suspension" of frontier models. Aspen CRM, a CRM for non-life-science companies, is disclosed, and the CRM-specific clause leaves the competition headline. Offerings become "applications, agents, data, and consulting."

Risk Factors

▶ China CRM: customers "may choose" a competitor becomes "some customers have chosen" Changed

Losses in China were hypothetical in Q1 and are a fact in Q2. The product is also renamed from "China SFA" to "the China CRM Suite." China is quantified at about 2% of revenue, so the change is small in dollars. It is the first quarter in this set where a CRM loss to a competitor is admitted as having already happened, two quarters before the FY2025 10-K admits a Salesforce defection.

Q1 FY2025 (prior)

"While we offer a CRM solution, called China SFA, that does not require data to be transferred outside of China, certain of these customers may choose to implement a competitor’s CRM solution and our CRM business in China may be negatively impacted."

Q2 FY2025 (current)

"While we offer the China CRM Suite, a CRM solution that does not require data to be transferred outside of China, some customers have chosen and other customers may choose other CRM providers, which may negatively impact our CRM business in China."
▶ Washington health-data law: "may curtail" becomes "we made modifications" to Crossix audience segments Changed

The My Health My Data Act took effect on March 21, 2024. One quarter later, Veeva says it has already changed the Crossix product in response, and the "accuracy" concern is dropped. The same factor adds "have and" to "may continue to require us to modify our solutions."

Q1 FY2025 (prior)

"The law may curtail our ability to use data of Washington consumers, which may limit the accuracy of and reduce demand for our Crossix products"

Q2 FY2025 (current)

"In response to the Washington law, we made modifications to our audience segments that may reduce demand for our Crossix products"
▶ Quarterly Commercial Solutions share: 50% / 49% to 48% / 47% of subscription / total revenue Signal

The core-CRM risk factor carried a single-quarter Commercial share, which the MD&A (year-to-date only) does not provide. Q1's sentence is dated to the "fiscal quarter ended January 31, 2024," a stale period label. The series falls again to 46% of total revenue in Q3, and then the entire risk factor is deleted at the FY2025 10-K (see that transition).

Q1 FY2025 (prior)

"In our fiscal quarter ended January 31, 2024, we derived approximately 50% of our subscription services revenues and approximately 49% of our total revenues from our Commercial Solutions."

Q2 FY2025 (current)

"In our fiscal quarter ended July 31, 2024, we derived approximately 48% of our subscription services revenues and approximately 47% of our total revenues from our Commercial Solutions."

MD&A

▶ G&A outlook flips from "relatively consistent" to "an increase," tied to the CEO equity grant Changed

The June 2024 CEO grant is the only named reason. G&A actually fell $2M in the quarter because of lower litigation fees, so the outlook change reflects a forward stock-compensation charge, not run-rate spending. The guidance stays in place until the FY2026 Q1 10-Q quietly drops it.

Q1 FY2025 (prior)

"We expect general and administrative expenses to be relatively consistent in the fiscal year ending January 31, 2025 when compared to the fiscal year ended January 31, 2024."

Q2 FY2025 (current)

"We expect an increase in general and administrative expenses in the fiscal year ending January 31, 2025, primarily related to the stock-based compensation associated with the equity grant to our Chief Executive Officer."
▶ Professional services turn from +$5M to −$5M as implementation work declines Changed

Q1 attributed services growth only to business consulting. Q2 names a decline in implementation and deployment work as the cause of the first services decline in the set ($114.9M vs $119.6M). Cost of professional services fell "as headcount decreased." The same pattern holds in Q3, and it reverses completely by FY2027, when services grow 23–24% (see Professional Services signal).

Q1 FY2025 (prior)

"Professional services and other revenues for the three months ended April 30, 2024 increased $5 million. The increase was primarily driven by increased demand for our business consulting services."

Q2 FY2025 (current)

"The decrease in professional services revenue was primarily due to a decline in implementation and deployment-related services, partially offset by an increase in business consulting services."
▶ Q1's 29% subscription growth was flattered by a contract change; Q2 restates the drivers at 19% Signal

Q1's only explanation for its 29% subscription growth was an easy comparison: termination-for-convenience rights added in February 2023 had depressed the prior-year quarter. Q2 drops that explanation and replaces it with organic driver language. Subscription growth reads 29% → 19% → 17% across FY2025 Q1–Q3, and the 29% should not be read as a run rate. Q2 is also the baseline for the driver wording tracked through FY2027: "new and existing customers," with pricing "to a lesser extent."

Q1 FY2025 (prior)

"The addition of termination for convenience rights starting on February 1, 2023, reduced subscription services revenue attributable to R&D Solutions for the three months ended April 30, 2023."

Q2 FY2025 (current)

"The increase in subscription services revenue attributable to Commercial Solutions was primarily due to expanding use of our Veeva Commercial Cloud and Veeva Data Cloud products by both new and existing customers and to a lesser extent due to higher prices in connection with our annual inflation adjustment."

Risk Factors

▶ Termination for convenience: customers "may exercise" becomes "have exercised" Changed

Multi-year customers have used their right to exit early. Veeva gives no count or dollar figure. The FY2025 10-K then adds termination for convenience to the sentence on how non-renewals hit future quarters. In FY2026 Q3 the risk widens again to "reduce usage" and to the EU Data Act (see Customer Exit Rights signal).

Q2 FY2025 (prior)

"our master subscription agreements that govern multi-year orders generally include a right to terminate the master subscription agreement for convenience and certain customers may exercise that right prior to the contracted end date."

Q3 FY2025 (current)

"Certain customers have exercised that right prior to the contracted end date, and other customers may also choose to do so in the future."
▶ Competitors "may offer" price concessions becomes "have offered" Changed

Competitive pricing pressure moves from hypothetical to experienced in the same filing that admits early terminations. Read together, these two changes suggest a harder renewal environment in late calendar 2024, and that is an inference. The sentence still blames "the recent macroeconomic environment." "Recent" is only dropped in FY2027 Q2.

Q2 FY2025 (prior)

"In addition, our competitors may offer price concessions, delayed payment terms, or other more favorable terms and conditions in light of the recent macroeconomic environment."

Q3 FY2025 (current)

"In addition, our competitors have offered price concessions, delayed payment terms, or other more favorable terms and conditions in light of the recent macroeconomic environment."
▶ New: elimination of direct-to-consumer drug advertising named as a risk to Crossix New

The 10-Q was filed December 9, 2024, after the U.S. election. A specific policy outcome, a ban on pharma DTC advertising, is added as the lead example of regulatory change. The same edit deletes the sentence noting drugmakers' litigation against the Inflation Reduction Act. Crossix sells measurement for DTC campaigns. By the FY2026 10-K, "regulation of pharmaceutical advertising" has been promoted into the summary risk bullet.

Q3 FY2025 (new)

"For example, regulatory changes with respect to life sciences advertising, such as limitations on or the elimination of the ability of pharmaceutical companies to engage in direct-to-consumer advertising, could negatively impact certain of our product offerings, including our Crossix business."
▶ New-markets language added, seven quarters before Aspen CRM New

Two insertions anticipate Veeva selling outside its core: a competition sentence and a new acquisition-risk bullet ("difficulty entering into new markets in which we have little or no experience or where competitors have stronger market positions"). The annual dashboard tracks non-life-sciences ambitions narrowing to nothing by FY2025. In FY2027 Q2 the 10-Q names Aspen CRM, "our recently announced CRM offering for non-life science companies."

Q3 FY2025 (new)

"Moreover, if we enter new markets, we will likely face competition and will need to adapt to competitive factors that may be different from those we face today."

MD&A

▶ Veeva Data Cloud and data costs disappear from the MD&A Removed

Three data references were removed in one quarter. "Veeva Data Cloud" is dropped from the Commercial growth driver. "Increased data costs related to our data solutions" is dropped from the cost-of-subscription outlook. "Data acquisition costs" is dropped from the list of operating cash uses. Data Cloud returns to the attribution sentence in FY2026 Q1, and data costs return in FY2026 Q2 as "continued investment in our data solutions." The removal coincides with the IQVIA data dispute still being live.

Q2 FY2025 (prior)

"We expect cost of subscription services to increase in absolute dollars in the near term due to increased usage of our subscription services and increased data costs related to our data solutions."

Q3 FY2025 (current)

"The increase in subscription services revenue attributable to Commercial Solutions was primarily due to expanding use of our Veeva Commercial Cloud products by both existing and new customers"
▶ Expense outlook horizon shortens from "the fiscal year ending January 31, 2025" to "the near term" Changed

All four "We expect" expense sentences lose their fiscal-year anchor. This is the first step in a three-stage loosening: "near term" becomes "the future" in FY2026 Q2, and every expense outlook is deleted in FY2027 Q2 (see Expense Outlook signal).

Q2 FY2025 (prior)

"We expect operating expenses to increase in the fiscal year ending January 31, 2025, primarily due to employee compensation-related costs."

Q3 FY2025 (current)

"We expect operating expenses to increase in the near term, primarily due to employee compensation-related costs."
▶ Housekeeping: Commercial share 46%, U.S. tax-proposal sentence deleted, AI-enabled attacks added Signal

The quarterly Commercial share of total revenue falls again, 47% → 46%, with subscription share at 48%. The sentence "the current U.S. administration has released various tax legislation proposals" is removed after the election. A generative-AI cyber-threat sentence is added. The IQVIA non-compete trial date moves June 13 → June 16, 2025. Several "have in the past and may in the future" hedges are boilerplate and not signals.

Risk Factors

▶ Core-CRM risk factor deleted, taking the quarterly Commercial-share disclosure with it Removed

This factor carried the only single-quarter Commercial share figure (50% → 48% → 46% of total revenue over FY2025 Q1–Q3), along with the admission that core CRM has "substantial market penetration." It is deleted in the same filing that first names Salesforce as the primary CRM competitor and admits a large-customer defection. The sales-rep-reduction example survives elsewhere. The quarterly share number does not come back in any later filing.

Q3 FY2025 (prior, now removed)

"Our core CRM application has achieved substantial market penetration of pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies. If our efforts to sustain or further increase the use and adoption of our core CRM application do not succeed, the growth of our Commercial Solutions revenues may be negatively impacted."
"In our fiscal quarter ended October 31, 2024, we derived approximately 48% of our subscription services revenues and approximately 46% of our total revenues from our Commercial Solutions."
▶ Salesforce becomes "primary CRM competitor"; first defection of a large customer New

This is covered on the annual dashboard. The quarterly view adds the sequence: Q3 still named IQVIA as "the principal such competitor" for Commercial Cloud. The defection count then grows from "a large Veeva CRM customer" (here) to "certain large Veeva CRM customers" (FY2026 Q2) to "a number of our customers have informed us of their intent to move to Salesforce" (FY2026 10-K).

FY2025 10-K (new)

"As we transition from our legacy Veeva CRM application to our Vault CRM application, as discussed in more detail below, certain customers have chosen, and other customers may in the future choose, to purchase CRM solutions from a competitor. For example, Salesforce, our primary CRM competitor, recently announced that a large Veeva CRM customer has committed to purchasing its CRM solutions."
▶ Customers not migrating to Vault CRM: "may decide" becomes "have decided," with Salesforce named Changed

The migration itself moves from "we intend to migrate" to "we have begun to migrate," and the line "the Veeva Vault platform will be ready for migration on our intended timeline" is deleted. This sentence survives until the FY2026 10-K, which deletes it as customer loss is moved into the competition risk factor.

Q3 FY2025 (prior)

"Further, certain customers may decide not to migrate to Vault CRM and use a different CRM solution."

FY2025 10-K (current)

"Further, certain customers have decided, and other customers may in the future decide, not to migrate to Vault CRM and use a different CRM solution, including a CRM solution provided by Salesforce."
▶ New: FDA, NIH and Medicaid funding cuts named; biotech funding squeeze recast from current to past New

Filed in March 2025, the 10-K adds federal agency funding as a customer-industry risk. At the same time, the early-stage funding sentence loses "Since 2022" and "may continue for the foreseeable future" and becomes a generic past-and-future statement. The two edits move the source of customer-industry risk from private biotech funding to government policy. FY2026 Q1 adds "staffing" of agencies and promotes the language to the summary bullets.

Q3 FY2025 (prior)

"Since 2022, there has been a reduction in funding for early-stage life sciences companies, which has resulted in reduced sales and adversely affected our financial results and may continue for the foreseeable future."

FY2025 10-K (current)

"In addition, reductions in funding of government agencies and programs relevant to the life sciences industry—such as the Food and Drug Administration, the National Institutes of Health, and Medicaid—or changes in funding priorities relevant to the life sciences industry could adversely affect the life sciences industry."
▶ Normalized billings now adjusts for "delayed renewals that have closed and billed after the period end" New

Veeva calls normalized billings guidance one of "the best indicators of the momentum of our business." A second adjustment is added to its definition, so renewals that slip past quarter-end no longer depress the metric. Adding it suggests late renewals were frequent enough to distort reported billings, which is an inference. The same factor changes "accurate" indicators to "precise" indicators.

FY2025 10-K (new)

"We define the term normalized billings for any period to mean calculated billings adjusted for the impact of (i) term changes in our customer renewals, such as changes to renewal date (for example, changing the renewal date of multiple products to be coterminous) or changes to billing frequency (for example, changing from annual to quarterly billings), and (ii) delayed renewals that have closed and billed after the period end."
▶ IQVIA litigation dropped from the competition factor; patent claim by a non-practicing entity disclosed Changed

The 10-K removes "We also continue to be subject to litigation from our competitors. For example, as disclosed elsewhere in this report, we are in active litigation with IQVIA." The case was still pending and settled five months later. It also adds a new IP exposure, which moves to "aware of a non-practicing entity alleging that we infringed its patents" in FY2026 Q3. The standalone AI risk factor that debuts here is covered on the annual dashboard and in the AI Language signal.

FY2025 10-K (new)

"We are also in discussions with a non-practicing entity relating to alleged infringement of its patents."

MD&A

▶ "New customers" dropped from both growth drivers; price increases added to the R&D driver Changed

Every attribution sentence from FY2025 Q2 through the FY2025 10-K credited "both new and existing customers." From FY2026 Q1, both R&D and Commercial growth come from "existing customers" only. Price increases, previously cited only for Commercial, now also lift R&D. The customer count still grew (1,432 → 1,477 in FY2025), so new logos exist, but management no longer calls them a driver. The same quarter restores "Veeva Data Cloud" to the Commercial driver, two quarters after it was dropped.

FY2025 10-K (prior)

"The increase in subscription services revenue attributable to R&D solutions was primarily due to expanding use of Veeva Development Cloud products by both existing and new customers."

Q1 FY2026 (current)

"The increase in subscription services revenue attributable to R&D solutions was driven by Veeva Development Cloud products primarily due to the expanding use by existing customers and, to a lesser extent, due to higher prices in connection with our annual inflation adjustment."
▶ CEO-grant G&A outlook dropped; Commercial share of total revenue down to 46% Removed

The G&A "increase" outlook, present since FY2025 Q2, is removed, leaving four expense outlooks. Year-to-date Commercial share falls to 48% of subscription and 46% of total revenue (FY2025: 48% / 47%). Commercial subscription growth was $44M in the quarter, the strongest of the set, against R&D's $57M.

FY2025 10-K (prior, now removed)

Risk Factors

▶ Veeva AI announced: "began incorporating AI capabilities into certain of our solutions" becomes AI "across all major areas" Changed

The AI risk factor is one quarter old. Its opening is rewritten from a limited rollout to a company-wide initiative, and "solutions, including AI technologies," is added to the new-products risk. The MD&A still contains zero AI mentions. The tension between AI risk language and AI-free financial discussion lasts until FY2027 Q1.

FY2025 10-K (prior)

"We recently began incorporating AI capabilities into certain of our solutions, which presents new risks and challenges that could affect the adoption of our solutions and our business."

Q1 FY2026 (current)

"We recently announced Veeva AI, an initiative that will add AI to our applications across all major areas, including clinical, regulatory, safety, quality, medical, and commercial."
▶ May 12, 2025 drug-pricing Executive Order and tariffs on life sciences products added New

The customer-industry risk now names the most-favored-nation pricing order and a new tariff mechanism: tariffs on drugs and inputs cutting customers' IT spend. The summary bullet is rewritten to list "government funding and staffing of relevant agencies and research, drug pricing regulation, healthcare funding and eligibility reforms." The same list is added to the stock-price factors.

Q1 FY2026 (new)

"drug pricing reforms proposed by the recent Executive Order signed on May 12, 2025, which would impose certain limits on drug prices in the U.S. relative the the prices paid in other countries."
"Tariffs imposed on the end products of the life sciences industry or on inputs relevant to the life sciences industry could increase costs for our customers or reduce demand for their products, which could delay or reduce their IT spending."

Legal Proceedings

▶ California non-compete case with IQVIA settled May 30, 2025, two weeks before trial New

The 2017 suit against Medidata, IQVIA and Sparta was down to IQVIA alone, with trial set for June 16, 2025. It settles confidentially. The federal trade-secret and antitrust cases were still awaiting summary-judgment rulings after January 2025 hearings. They settle one quarter later.

FY2025 10-K (prior)

"On March 25, 2024, the trial court judge set a trial date of June 16, 2025 on the consolidated claims."

Q1 FY2026 (current)

"On May 30, 2025, we entered into a confidential settlement agreement with IQVIA and the parties agreed to dismiss their claims against each other."

Notes / Legal

▶ All IQVIA litigation settled August 13, 2025: no damages, but a $31M "success fee" accrued New

Q1 described the law-firm arrangements as paying out of "any damages we recover from IQVIA" or as "a success fee if certain non-monetary outcomes are achieved." Q2 reports no damages either way, yet accrues $31M. That means the non-monetary condition was triggered, and the fee description drops the damages-sharing clause. The settlement terms are not disclosed. The deletion of the IQVIA data-access risk factor in the same filing (next finding) is consistent with the non-monetary outcome involving data access, but that is an inference. The charge cut quarterly operating income from $233.7M to $195.9M.

Q1 FY2026 (prior)

"Pursuant to those arrangements, such law firms are entitled to an agreed portion of any damages we recover from IQVIA or may be entitled to payment from us of a success fee if certain non-monetary outcomes are achieved."

Q2 FY2026 (current)

"Under the terms of the settlement agreement, neither party will pay damages to the other party and both parties agreed to dismiss with prejudice all claims and counterclaims currently pending."
"Consequent to the settlement of the IQVIA litigations, as of July 31, 2025, we accrued approximately $31 million due to such law firms in accordance with the terms of such arrangements."

Risk Factors

▶ IQVIA data-access risk factor deleted outright, including the summary bullet Removed

Since well before this set, the risk factors described IQVIA blocking its data from Veeva Network, Nitro, OpenData transitions and "new IQVIA data types." The full factor and its summary bullet ("such as IQVIA for instance") are gone in Q2, and no softened version replaces it. The annual dashboard's Competition signal ends on the FY2025 "blanket denial." This is the reversal. The "currently being sued" bullet also becomes "have been and may in the future be sued."

Q1 FY2026 (prior, now removed)

"In addition, IQVIA has stated publicly that it will deny all customer requests for use of new IQVIA data types in Veeva applications, including, as examples, real world data, real world evidence, and genomics."
▶ IQVIA exits CRM after 2029; Salesforce becomes the sole named CRM rival New

The competition paragraph is rewritten around IQVIA's withdrawal: it will support its CRM "only through 2029" and has licensed it to Salesforce. One quarter later Salesforce "has developed" its product, and the 10-K drops the joint-marketing clause.

Q2 FY2026 (new)

"However, IQVIA has announced that it will market and support its CRM solution only through 2029, that it has licensed its CRM software to Salesforce, Inc., and that it will collaborate with Salesforce to accelerate the development of a CRM solution for life sciences that it will jointly market with Salesforce."
▶ "A large Veeva CRM customer" defected becomes "certain large Veeva CRM customers" Changed

The defection count goes from singular to plural, and "recently" is dropped. Veeva still sources the disclosure to Salesforce's own announcements rather than its own count.

Q1 FY2026 (prior)

"For example, Salesforce, our primary CRM competitor, recently announced that a large Veeva CRM customer has committed to purchasing its CRM solutions."

Q2 FY2026 (current)

"For example, Salesforce, our primary CRM competitor, has announced that certain large Veeva CRM customers have committed to purchasing its CRM solutions."
▶ New patent-enforcement paragraph: PTAB, IPR, and the risk of losing injunctive relief New

With IQVIA settled, the IP section turns from defense to offense: Veeva's ability to "defend, protect, and enforce" its patents. U.S. patents issued rise 91 → 98 in the quarter and reach 137 by FY2027 Q2, up from 74 in FY2025 Q1. The new text is consistent with preparing to assert patents, which is an inference. No enforcement action is disclosed in the set.

Q2 FY2026 (new)

"A successful challenge to our patents or a failure to adequately protect or mark our intellectual property could prevent or limit our ability to seek injunctive relief or recover damages for infringement."

MD&A

▶ Outlook horizon loosens again: "near term" becomes "the future"; data investment returns to the cost outlook Changed

Data costs, removed in FY2025 Q3, return as "continued investment in our data solutions." That is a deliberate spending choice, not a cost increase. Cost of subscription rose $15M, with $14M from "data and computing infrastructure costs." Professional services grew $15M (+13%), now attributed to "implementation and business consulting services," the reverse of FY2025's implementation decline. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act's R&D expensing raises the FY2026 operating cash flow outlook.

Q1 FY2026 (prior)

"We expect cost of subscription services to increase in absolute dollars in the near term due to increased usage of our subscription services."

Q2 FY2026 (current)

"We expect cost of subscription services to increase in absolute dollars in the future due to increased usage of our subscription services and continued investment in our data solutions."

▶ New exit rights: EU Data Act allows cancellation "without cause"; customers have "reduce[d] usage" New

Themes

1. CRM Losses Were Admitted One Rung at a Time

The ladder of admissions: China "some customers have chosen" (FY25Q2), "a large Veeva CRM customer" (FY2025 10-K), "certain large" customers (FY26Q2), "a number of our customers have informed us" (FY2026 10-K). Each step was sourced more directly. The end-of-support date then moved earlier to December 31, 2029. In the latest filing the CRM clause leaves the competition headline while the defection sentence below it stays. None of the steps carries a count.

2. The IQVIA Settlement Removed a Constraint, Not Just a Lawsuit

The August 2025 settlement involved no damages, yet it triggered $31M of success fees payable only for "non-monetary outcomes." The IQVIA data-access risk factor was deleted in the same filing, and IQVIA announced its CRM exit. The combination is consistent with Veeva gaining data access or similar terms, which is an inference, since the terms are confidential. Data costs and Data Cloud attribution returned to the MD&A within the same two quarters.

3. Growth Is Increasingly Existing Customers and Price

"New customers" left the driver sentences in FY26Q1. Pricing went from "to a lesser extent" (Commercial only) to a co-equal driver for both segments by FY27Q1. In that same filing the "lower of 4% or CPI" cap disclosure was deleted. Commercial's share of subscription revenue fell from 49% to 46% over the set, and subscription growth slowed from 19% to 15–16%.

4. Forward Cost Guidance Was Loosened, Then Withdrawn

Expense outlooks were anchored to a fiscal year (FY25Q1–Q2), then the "near term" (FY25Q3), then "the future" (FY26Q2). All were deleted in FY27Q2, the same quarter "agents" entered the offerings sentence and Anthropic entered the risk factors. Services margin commentary shows the same selectivity: it appears only in quarters when the comparison is unfavorable.

5. Services Swung From Shrinking to the Fastest-Growing Line

FY2025 cited declining implementation work, lower services headcount, and "increased scrutiny" of services projects. By FY27Q1–Q2 services grew 23–24%, outpacing subscription, and the scrutiny language was deleted. Headcount added ahead of demand pushed services gross margin down to 18–20% in two quarters. Implementation demand rising alongside forced CRM migration away from Salesforce is a plausible link but not one the filings state.

6. Veeva Moved From AI-Restrained to AI-Dependent in Six Filings

The annual dashboard's "AI Restrained" theme no longer holds. AI risk-factor mentions grew from 5 to 28. The language went from "began incorporating" to "Veeva AI" across all areas, then "AI strategy," LLM costs as a profitability risk, a named model supplier, and a frontier-model suspension risk. Alongside this came a $2B buyback and a non-life-sciences CRM (Aspen), which reverses the annual dashboard's narrowing-focus theme.
