VEEV 10-Q Quarterly Scanner

Quarter-over-quarter textual analysis of Veeva Systems' 10-Q filings, FY2025 Q1 (quarter ended April 30, 2024) through FY2027 Q2 (quarter ended July 31, 2026, filed August 27, 2026), with the FY2025 and FY2026 10-K boundary transitions included. Veeva's fiscal year ends January 31. The quarterly chain covers the Salesforce agreement's expiry, the end of eight years of IQVIA litigation, the Vault CRM migration turning into named customer losses, and the arrival of Veeva AI. Several of these moves show up in the quarterly risk factors one to three quarters before the annual 10-K comparison picks them up.

Transition Overview

FY25 Q1 → Q2

China CRM losses move from hypothetical to "some customers have chosen." The Washington health-data law moves from "may curtail" to modifications already made to Crossix audience segments. The G&A outlook flips from "relatively consistent" to an increase tied to the CEO equity grant, and professional services revenue starts declining.

FY25 Q2 → Q3

Two risks become facts: customers "have exercised" termination-for-convenience rights, and competitors "have offered" price concessions. A new risk names the elimination of direct-to-consumer drug advertising as a threat to Crossix. The February 10, 2025 IQVIA trade-secret trial date is withdrawn. Veeva Data Cloud drops out of the Commercial growth driver, and the expense outlooks lose their fiscal-year anchor for "the near term."

FY25 Q3 → FY2025 10-K

Salesforce replaces IQVIA as the main CRM competitor, a large customer defects, and customers "have decided" not to migrate. "We intend to migrate" becomes "we have begun." The core-CRM risk factor and its quarterly Commercial share are deleted. FDA, NIH and Medicaid funding cuts enter the risk factors, and normalized billings now adjusts for "delayed renewals."

FY2025 10-K → FY26 Q1

The IQVIA success fees move from "have not accrued any related liability" to "reasonably possible," for "non-monetary outcomes," one quarter before the settlement. "New customers" disappears from both growth drivers. Veeva AI is announced across all major areas. The May 12, 2025 drug-pricing Executive Order and tariffs are added, and the IQVIA non-compete case settles on May 30, 2025.

FY26 Q1 → Q2

All IQVIA litigation settles on August 13, 2025 with no damages either way, and the success fee becomes a $31M accrual. The IQVIA data-access risk factor is deleted, and the "currently being sued" summary bullet goes. IQVIA exits CRM after 2029. "A large" customer that left for Salesforce becomes "certain large" customers. The OBBBA "is expected to increase" operating cash flow.

FY26 Q2 → Q3

Salesforce "has developed" (not "is developing") its life sciences CRM, and the Salesforce agreement "expired." IQVIA's 2029 exit date is dropped one quarter after it appeared. The EU Data Act lets EU customers cancel without cause, and customers "reduce usage." Professional services gross margin falls 22%→18% and gets its own sentence.

FY26 Q3 → FY2026 10-K

Veeva CRM end of support moves earlier, from September 1, 2030 to December 31, 2029. "A number of our customers have informed us of their intent to move to Salesforce." Customer loss moves out of the migration risk and into a rewritten competition headline. The China revenue share (2%) is deleted. A $2B buyback starts, and LLM costs become a named profitability risk.

FY2026 10-K → FY27 Q1

Price increases rise from "to a lesser extent" to a co-equal growth driver in the same filing that deletes the "lower of 4% or CPI" inflation-clause disclosure. Professional services grow 23% at a lower margin, and the "increased scrutiny" of services projects is deleted. AI regulation may now "force" changes to "our AI strategy." Ukraine leaves the risk factors.

FY27 Q1 → Q2

Every forward expense-outlook sentence is deleted, and the services-margin sentence goes once the margin stops falling. Anthropic is named as an LLM supplier, with a new risk of "sudden suspension" of frontier models. Aspen CRM, a CRM for non-life-science companies, is disclosed, and the CRM clause leaves the competition headline. Offerings become "applications, agents, data, and consulting."

Risk Factors

▶ China CRM: customers "may choose" a competitor becomes "some customers have chosen" Changed

Losses in China were hypothetical in Q1 and are a fact in Q2. The product is also renamed from "China SFA" to "the China CRM Suite." China is quantified at about 2% of revenue, so the change is small in dollars. It is the first quarter in this set where a CRM loss to a competitor is admitted as having already happened, two quarters before the FY2025 10-K admits a Salesforce defection.

Q1 FY2025 (prior)

"While we offer a CRM solution, called China SFA, that does not require data to be transferred outside of China, certain of these customers may choose to implement a competitor’s CRM solution and our CRM business in China may be negatively impacted."

Q2 FY2025 (current)

"While we offer the China CRM Suite, a CRM solution that does not require data to be transferred outside of China, some customers have chosen and other customers may choose other CRM providers, which may negatively impact our CRM business in China."

The My Health My Data Act took effect on March 21, 2024. One quarter later, Veeva says it has already changed the Crossix product in response, and the "accuracy" concern is dropped. The same factor adds "have and" to "may continue to require us to modify our solutions."

Q1's sentence is dated to the "fiscal quarter ended January 31, 2024," a stale period label. The June 2024 CEO grant is the only named reason. G&A actually fell $2M in the quarter because of lower litigation fees, so the outlook change reflects a forward stock-compensation charge, not run-rate spending. The guidance stays in place until the FY2026 Q1 10-Q quietly drops it.

▶ Professional services turn from +$5M to −$5M as implementation work declines Changed

Q1 attributed services growth only to business consulting. Q2 names a decline in implementation and deployment work as the cause of the first services decline in the set ($114.9M vs $119.6M). Cost of professional services fell "as headcount decreased." The same pattern holds in Q3, and it reverses completely by FY2027, when services grow 23–24% (see Professional Services signal).

Q1 FY2025 (prior)

"Professional services and other revenues for the three months ended April 30, 2024 increased $5 million. The increase was primarily driven by increased demand for our business consulting services."

Q2 FY2025 (current)

"The decrease in professional services revenue was primarily due to a decline in implementation and deployment-related services, partially offset by an increase in business consulting services."
▶ Q1's 29% subscription growth was flattered by a contract change; Q2 restates the drivers at 19% Signal

Q1's only explanation for its 29% subscription growth was an easy comparison: termination-for-convenience rights added in February 2023 had depressed the prior-year quarter. Q2 drops that explanation and replaces it with organic driver language. Subscription growth reads 29% → 19% → 17% across FY2025 Q1–Q3, and the 29% should not be read as a run rate. Q2 is also the baseline for the driver wording tracked through FY2027: "new and existing customers," with pricing "to a lesser extent."

Q1 FY2025 (prior)

"The addition of termination for convenience rights starting on February 1, 2023, reduced subscription services revenue attributable to R&D Solutions for the three months ended April 30, 2023."

Q2 FY2025 (current)

"The increase in subscription services revenue attributable to Commercial Solutions was primarily due to expanding use of our Veeva Commercial Cloud and Veeva Data Cloud products by both new and existing customers and to a lesser extent due to higher prices in connection with our annual inflation adjustment."

Themes

1. CRM Losses Were Admitted One Rung at a Time

The ladder of admissions: China "some customers have chosen" (FY25Q2), "a large Veeva CRM customer" (FY2025 10-K), "certain large" customers (FY26Q2), "a number of our customers have informed us" (FY2026 10-K). Each step was sourced more directly. The end-of-support date then moved earlier to December 31, 2029. In the latest filing the CRM clause leaves the competition headline while the defection sentence below it stays. None of the steps carries a count, and the one related figure the filings did give, China's share of revenue, was deleted at the FY2026 10-K after ten straight filings.

2. The IQVIA Settlement Removed a Constraint, Not Just a Lawsuit

The August 2025 settlement involved no damages, yet it triggered $31M of success fees payable only for "non-monetary outcomes." The fee note had already moved from "have not accrued any related liability" to "reasonably possible" in FY26Q1, a quarter before the settlement was announced. The IQVIA data-access risk factor was deleted in the same filing, and IQVIA announced its CRM exit. The combination is consistent with Veeva gaining data access or similar terms, which is an inference, since the terms are confidential. Data costs and Data Cloud attribution returned to the MD&A within the same two quarters.

3. Growth Is Increasingly Existing Customers and Price

"New customers" left the driver sentences in FY26Q1. Pricing went from "to a lesser extent" (Commercial only) to a co-equal driver for both segments by FY27Q1. In that same filing the "lower of 4% or CPI" cap disclosure was deleted. Commercial's share of subscription revenue fell from 49% to 46% over the set, and subscription growth slowed from 19% to 15–16%.

4. Forward Cost Guidance Was Loosened, Then Withdrawn

Expense outlooks were anchored to a fiscal year (FY25Q1–Q2), then the "near term" (FY25Q3), then "the future" (FY26Q2). All were deleted in FY27Q2, the same quarter "agents" entered the offerings sentence and Anthropic entered the risk factors. Services margin commentary shows the same selectivity: it appears only in quarters when the comparison is unfavorable.

5. Services Swung From Shrinking to the Fastest-Growing Line

FY2025 cited declining implementation work, lower services headcount, and "increased scrutiny" of services projects. By FY27Q1–Q2 services grew 23–24%, outpacing subscription, and the scrutiny language was deleted. Headcount added ahead of demand pushed services gross margin down to 18–20% in two quarters. Implementation demand rising alongside forced CRM migration away from Salesforce is a plausible link but not one the filings state.

6. Veeva Moved From AI-Restrained to AI-Dependent in Six Filings

The annual dashboard's "AI Restrained" theme no longer holds. AI risk-factor mentions grew from 5 to 28. The language went from "began incorporating" to "Veeva AI" across all areas, then "AI strategy," LLM costs as a profitability risk, a named model supplier, and a frontier-model suspension risk. Alongside this came a $2B buyback and a non-life-sciences CRM (Aspen), which reverses the annual dashboard's narrowing-focus theme.
