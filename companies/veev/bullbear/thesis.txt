VEEVA SYSTEMS (VEEV): INVESTMENT THESIS PILLARS
Written October 2026 from the 10-K dashboard themes (Salesforce migration, first CRM defections,
IQVIA truce), the 10-Q scanner, and the eight 10-K/10-Q filings from the Q3 FY2025 10-Q (quarter
ended October 31, 2024) to the Q2 FY2027 10-Q (quarter ended July 31, 2026). Veeva's fiscal year
ends January 31: FY2026 = year ended January 31, 2026. Stances move only when two or more filings
point the same way.

P1 Subscription growth and the R&D and Quality engine
  Bull assumes: Development Cloud and Quality Cloud keep growing near 20% on expanding use by
    existing customers, so R&D and Quality rises as a share of revenue and carries total
    subscription growth in the mid-to-high teens.
  Bear fears: subscription growth fades from 20% (FY2025) toward low teens as R&D and Quality
    matures and early-stage biotech customers fail; deferred revenue growth slows first.
  Stance: Intact (R&D subscription growth eased from +24% in Q3 FY2025 to +19% in Q2 FY2027, and
    deferred revenue grew only +11.2% in Q3 FY2026; but deferred revenue was back at +18% in Q1 and
    Q2 FY2027 and Q2 FY2027 revenue grew +18%, the fastest in the window).
  Tests: R&D and Quality subscription growth, total subscription growth, deferred revenue growth,
    R&D and Quality customer count.

P2 CRM franchise and the Vault CRM migration
  Bull assumes: most Veeva CRM customers move to Vault CRM before Veeva CRM support ends, IQVIA's
    exit from CRM removes a competitor, and Commercial subscription keeps growing low-to-mid teens.
  Bear fears: Salesforce's life sciences CRM (with IQVIA's software) wins large accounts at the
    forced migration point; Commercial growth stalls as seats are lost.
  Stance: Weakening (FY2025 10-K: "a large Veeva CRM customer" committed to Salesforce; Q2 FY2026:
    "certain large Veeva CRM customers"; FY2026 10-K: "a number of our customers have informed us of
    their intent to move to Salesforce", and Veeva CRM support pulled in to December 31, 2029 from
    September 1, 2030. Commercial subscription growth +10.6% in Q1 FY2027, +13.0% in Q2 FY2027).
  Tests: Commercial subscription growth, defection language in risk factors, Commercial customer
    count, any disclosed Vault CRM progress.

P3 Margins and operating leverage
  Bull assumes: subscription gross margin holds at 86-88% and operating expenses grow slower than
    revenue, lifting non-GAAP operating margin toward the mid-40s and beyond.
  Bear fears: data, AI model and hosting costs plus a services-heavy mix and faster hiring stall
    the margin.
  Stance: Intact, moved back from Strengthening (FY2025 10-K and Q1 FY2026: GAAP operating margin
    25.2% and 30.8%, gross margin 75% and 77%; then Q1 FY2027 non-GAAP operating margin 44.8% vs
    46.1% and Q2 FY2027 44.8% vs 44.7%: leverage paused for two filings after headcount +637).
  Tests: non-GAAP operating margin, subscription and services gross margin, headcount growth.

P4 Earnings quality and dilution
  Bull assumes: GAAP earnings track operations; stock-based compensation falls as a share of
    revenue and buybacks offset dilution.
  Bear fears: interest income, tax-rate swings and excluded "non-recurring" charges flatter EPS;
    the 2026 equity redesign (four-year cliff RSUs) and the CEO's 2.65M-option grant add cost.
  Stance: Intact (SBC 15.9% of revenue in FY2025, 14.8% in FY2026; diluted shares -1.6% in Q2
    FY2027; but other income is 21-25% of pre-tax income, and Q2 FY2027 EPS growth leans on a
    lower tax rate and lapping a $30.6M charge).
  Tests: SBC as a share of revenue, unrecognized compensation, other income share of pre-tax
    income, effective tax rate drivers.

P5 Cash generation and capital return
  Bull assumes: operating cash flow runs at about 1.5x net income with tiny capex, funding
    buybacks from free cash flow without touching the $7B cash pile.
  Bear fears: cash growth relies on the one-time OBBBA R&D deduction catch-up, and buybacks
    accelerate as the cash tax tailwind ends.
  Stance: Strengthening (Q2 and Q3 FY2026: operating cash flow +30% and +28% year to date; FY2026
    10-K: $1,415M, +30%, and a $2B two-year buyback; H1 FY2027 buybacks $467M = 34% of operating cash
    flow).
  Tests: operating cash flow vs net income, cash taxes paid, buybacks vs operating cash flow.

P6 Balance sheet and legal overhang
  Bull assumes: no debt, $5-7B of cash and investments, and the IQVIA litigation resolved without
    damages.
  Bear fears: litigation costs or adverse rulings; capital tied up in a bond portfolio with mark-to-
    market losses; acquisitions grow.
  Stance: Strengthening (Q1 FY2026: California non-compete case settled with IQVIA; Q2 FY2026: all
    IQVIA litigation settled with no damages either way; cash and investments $7.2B at July 31,
    2026, no debt).
  Tests: new proceedings, success-fee or settlement costs, cash and investments, acquisition size.

P7 Customer base and end-market exposure
  Bull assumes: customer count keeps rising, the top 10 customers stay near 28% of revenue, and
    life sciences IT spending holds.
  Bear fears: drug pricing reform, sales-force cuts and the EU Data Act's no-cause cancellation
    right lead customers to cut seats or terminate multi-year orders.
  Stance: Intact (FY2026 10-K: customers 1,552, +75 vs +45 a year earlier; top 10 at 28% for three
    years; but Q3 FY2026 first said customers "have in the past exercised their right to reduce usage
    or exercise termination rights").
  Tests: customer counts in the 10-K, top-10 share, termination and seat-reduction language.
