Veeva Systems 10-K Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of SEC filings tracking Veeva's evolution from a Salesforce-dependent CRM provider to a four-cloud platform company for life sciences, now racing a December 2029 deadline to move CRM customers off Salesforce.

FY2018 → FY2019: Data Cloud Emerges

Filed Mar 2019 | Employees: 2,171 → 2,553 (+18%) | Revenue: $678M → $862M (+27%) | Sub %: 80%

Business Overview

▶"A leading" upgraded to "the leading"Changed
FY2018: "Veeva is a leading provider of industry cloud solutions for the global life sciences industry."
FY2019: "Veeva is the leading provider of industry cloud solutions for the global life sciences industry."
A single-word change from "a" to "the" signals confidence in market dominance. This language persisted unchanged through FY2025.
▶"Products" replaced by "solutions" throughoutChanged
FY2018: "Our products are designed to meet the unique needs of our customers"
FY2019: "Our solutions are designed to meet the unique needs of our customers"
"Products" became "solutions" across the Business section. This reflects a repositioning from product vendor to strategic partner.
▶Crossix acquired; Nitro commercial data warehouse addedNew
"Crossix, which we acquired in November 2019, provides pharmaceutical brands privacy-safe U.S. patient data and a best-in-class analytics platform to maximize media investments and drive greater marketing effectiveness."
Crossix added patient-level data capabilities. Veeva Nitro added as a commercial data warehouse with "industry-specific data model and standard data connectors." Both laid the groundwork for what would become Veeva Data Cloud.
▶Non-LS target industries expanded from unnamed to namedChanged
FY2018: "a new set of customers in process and discrete manufacturing, consumer packaged goods, and highly regulated services industries"
FY2019: "a new set of customers outside of life sciences in regulated industries, including consumer goods, chemicals, and cosmetics"
The target industries were refined from generic manufacturing categories to three specific regulated sectors.

Salesforce Relationship

▶"Mutually beneficial strategic relationship" with salesforce.comSignal
"We believe that we have a mutually beneficial strategic relationship with salesforce.com."
Veeva described as salesforce.com's "preferred and recommended" application provider. Agreement expires September 1, 2025, renewable for five-year periods. Minimum order commitments of $500 million over the agreement term. This language would be entirely rewritten within four years.

FY2019 → FY2020: COVID & Crossix

Filed Mar 2020 | Employees: 2,553 → 3,501 (+37%) | Revenue: $862M → $1,104M (+28%) | Sub %: 80%

Business Overview

▶Product areas expanded from two to three: Veeva Data Cloud concept introducedNew
FY2019: "grouped into two key product areas--Veeva Commercial Cloud and Veeva Vault"
FY2020: "grouped into three key product areas--Veeva Commercial Cloud, Veeva Data Cloud, and Veeva Vault"
Veeva Data Cloud first appeared as a named product area, described as providing "longitudinal U.S. patient data for both retail and specialty distribution channels." It was "powered by the Crossix Data Platform."
▶Safety.AI announced; Veeva Vault Safety suite expandedNew
"Veeva Vault Safety.AI is an artificial intelligence application that automates case intake to reduce the time and effort of manual data entry for more efficient case processing. Safety.AI is planned for availability in 2020."
This is the earliest AI-specific product announcement in Veeva's 10-K filings.
▶Non-LS narrowed to three named industriesChanged
"We are now also bringing the benefits of our content and data management solutions to a new set of customers outside of life sciences in three regulated industries: consumer goods, chemicals, and cosmetics."
The word "three" was added, making the non-LS target more specific and bounded.

Risk Factors

▶COVID-19 added as standalone risk factorNew
"The worldwide outbreak of COVID-19 may negatively impact our business."
Added as a new standalone risk factor. COVID was described as potentially affecting customer operations, field representative interactions with healthcare professionals, and professional services delivery.

FY2020 → FY2021: PBC Conversion

Filed Mar 2021 | Employees: 3,501 → 4,506 (+29%) | Revenue: $1,104M → $1,465M (+33%) | Sub %: 80%

Business Overview

▶Public Benefit Corporation conversion disclosedNew
"On February 1, 2021, after approval by our stockholders, we became a Delaware public benefit corporation (PBC). A PBC is a for-profit company operating under subchapter XV of the General Corporation Law of the State of Delaware (i) that has adopted a public benefit purpose intended to provide benefits beyond just stockholder financial returns, and (ii) whose directors have a fiduciary duty to balance the financial interests of stockholders, the best interests of other stakeholders materially affected by the company's conduct."
Public benefit purpose: "to provide products and services that are intended to help make the industries we serve more productive, and to create high-quality employment opportunities in the communities in which we operate." Core values stated: "do the right thing, customer success, employee success, and speed." This PBC language remained unchanged through FY2025.
▶Product areas reduced from three to two; Data Cloud absorbed into Commercial CloudChanged
FY2020: "grouped into three key product areas--Veeva Commercial Cloud, Veeva Data Cloud, and Veeva Vault"
FY2021: "grouped into two key product areas--Veeva Commercial Cloud and Veeva Vault"
Data Cloud was folded back into Commercial Cloud as a data and analytics sub-section. Products like Crossix, OpenData, Link, and Data Cloud were listed under "Veeva Commercial Cloud data and analytics solutions."
▶Human Capital Resources section added; DEI metrics disclosed for first timeNew
"Our workforce is diverse in many respects. As of January 31, 2021, 42% of our global employee population self-identified as female, and as of December 31, 2020, approximately 38% of our U.S. workforce self-identified as members of underrepresented racial or ethnic groups."
This was the first time Veeva disclosed workforce demographics. Also added: "Our rate of voluntary attrition has been comparatively low historically."
▶MyVeeva for Doctors and Veeva Data Cloud go liveNew
"MyVeeva for Doctors is a digital platform to connect healthcare professionals with the life sciences industry."
Veeva Data Cloud also described as providing "longitudinal U.S. patient data for both retail and specialty distribution channels for commercial use cases." Veeva Link expanded from oncology experts to "key scientific experts" with planned expansion to additional therapeutic areas.

FY2021 → FY2022: Development Cloud

Filed Mar 2022 | Employees: 4,506 → 5,482 (+22%) | Revenue: $1,465M → $1,855M (+27%) | Sub %: 80%

Business Overview

▶Vault reorganized as "Veeva Development Cloud"Changed
FY2021: "grouped into two key product areas--Veeva Commercial Cloud and Veeva Vault"
FY2022: "grouped into two major areas--Veeva Commercial Cloud and Veeva Development Cloud"
The Vault platform was rebranded as "Development Cloud" encompassing clinical, regulatory, quality, and safety applications. This established the cloud-as-category naming pattern that would expand to four clouds by FY2025.
▶MedTech CRM announced; Digital Trials Platform introducedNew
"We have also announced our intent to build Veeva MedTech CRM"
A dedicated MedTech section was added describing unified cloud solutions for medical devices and diagnostics companies. The clinical platform was described as enabling "seamless execution and flow of data between clinical trial stakeholders--including patients, research sites, contract research organizations (CROs), and trial sponsors."
▶Employee attrition disclosed as "higher than historical norms"Changed
FY2021: "Our rate of voluntary attrition has been comparatively low historically."
FY2022: "While we experience intense competition for talent and in our fiscal year ended January 31, 2022 we experienced employee attrition higher than our historical norms, we believe we have been effective at attracting and retaining talented employees."
The first explicit acknowledgment of elevated attrition, coinciding with the broader tech industry's Great Resignation period.
▶Non-LS narrowed from three industries to twoChanged
FY2021: "three regulated industries: consumer goods, chemicals, and cosmetics"
FY2022: "customers in the consumer products and chemical industries"
"Cosmetics" was dropped as a separately named target industry. The three-industry framing reduced to two.

FY2022 → FY2023: Salesforce Migration

Filed Mar 2023 | Employees: 5,482 → 6,744 (+23%) | Revenue: $1,855M → $2,155M (+16%) | Sub %: 80%

Business Overview

▶Salesforce migration announcedNew
"We recently announced that we intend to migrate our applications built on the Salesforce platform to our own Veeva Vault platform."
This single sentence in the Technology Infrastructure section was the first 10-K disclosure of the platform migration. It would transform the company's competitive positioning over the following two years.
▶Development Cloud listed before Commercial Cloud (priority flip)Changed
FY2022: "two major areas--Veeva Commercial Cloud and Veeva Development Cloud"
FY2023: "two major product families--Veeva Development Cloud and Veeva Commercial Cloud"
Development Cloud was listed first for the first time, signaling its growing strategic importance relative to Commercial Cloud. Revenue reporting also changed: R&D Solutions classified first.
▶Veeva Compass replaces "Data Cloud" naming within Commercial CloudChanged
Veeva Data Cloud was rebranded as Veeva Compass within the Commercial Cloud data offerings, described as "a suite of de-identified U.S. longitudinal patient, projected prescriber, and national data." The "Data Cloud" name was preserved but repositioned as a brand under which Compass, OpenData, and Link operated.
▶Veeva Way section removed from Business overviewRemoved
The "Executing in the Veeva Way" section, present since FY2018 and describing the company's disciplined approach (clear and correct target markets, running to complexity, product excellence, customer success, reference selling), was removed in FY2023. The Veeva Way framework was no longer described in the 10-K.

Risk Factors

▶COVID standalone risk factor removedRemoved
The standalone COVID-19 risk factor was removed after three years of inclusion (FY2020-FY2022). COVID-related language was absorbed into general macroeconomic risk factors.

FY2023 → FY2024: Vault CRM & Data Cloud

Filed Mar 2024 | Employees: 6,744 → 7,172 (+6%) | Revenue: $2,155M → $2,364M (+10%) | Sub %: 82%

Business Overview

▶Vault CRM introduced; Data Cloud elevated to third product categoryNew
"Veeva Vault CRM is our next generation CRM solution that is built on our proprietary Veeva Vault platform and will include the full functionality of Veeva CRM. Vault CRM is currently used by early adopters and planned for general availability in April 2024."
Product categories expanded from two to three: Veeva Development Cloud, Veeva Commercial Cloud, and Veeva Data Cloud. Data Cloud was elevated back to a standalone third category for the first time since FY2020.
▶"Supported until September 1, 2030" first disclosedNew
"Veeva CRM and some of its applications are built on a platform provided by Salesforce, Inc. and will be supported until September 1, 2030."
This was the first explicit end-of-support date for the Salesforce-based Veeva CRM in the Business section. The five-year wind-down period from September 2025 was publicly framed as a hard deadline.
▶Salesforce named as competitor for first time in Competition sectionNew
FY2023's Competition section did not name Salesforce as a direct competitor. FY2024 added Salesforce to the Competition section for the first time as a platform that customers "may also choose to use" for functions Veeva applications provide. The relationship shifted from partner to named competitive alternative.
▶Hiring deceleration: +428 employees vs. +1,262 prior yearSignal
"As of January 31, 2024, we had 7,172 employees worldwide, up by 428 from the previous year."
Employee growth decelerated from +1,262 (FY2023) to +428 (FY2024), a 66% reduction in net new hires. Revenue growth also decelerated from 16% to 10%.

FY2024 → FY2025: Quality Cloud & Legacy

Filed Mar 2025 | Employees: 7,172 → 7,291 (+2%) | Revenue: $2,364M → $2,747M (+16%) | Sub %: 83%

Business Overview

▶Quality Cloud carved out as fourth product categoryNew
"Our industry cloud solutions for the life sciences industry are grouped into four major product categories--Veeva Development Cloud, Veeva Quality Cloud, Veeva Commercial Cloud, and Veeva Data Cloud"
Quality Cloud was separated from Development Cloud as a standalone category. It was described as unifying "quality applications, processes, and partners across content management, training, quality management, quality assurance, and quality control lab solutions." Notably, Quality Cloud serves "customers in the life sciences and consumer products industries"--the primary vehicle for non-LS expansion.
▶Veeva CRM called "legacy product" for the first timeChanged
"Vault CRM includes the full functionality of our legacy product, Veeva CRM, with additional applications like Campaign Manager for coordination across engagement channels and Service Center for customer support."
The Salesforce-based Veeva CRM was explicitly described as a "legacy product" for the first time. The Vault CRM description was expanded to include new applications not available on the Salesforce platform.
▶Non-LS overview paragraph removed; "chemical" droppedRemoved
FY2024 included a standalone overview paragraph: "We also bring the benefits of our content and data management solutions to customers in the consumer products and chemical industries." FY2025 removed this paragraph entirely. Non-LS activity is now mentioned only within the Quality Cloud description and a brief customer reference. "Chemical" is no longer named as a target industry.
▶Goal statement rewritten to include quality and manufacturingChanged
FY2024: "Our goal is to become the most strategic technology partner to the life sciences industry and achieve long-term leadership with our solutions that support the R&D and commercial functions of life sciences companies."
FY2025: "Our goal is to become the most strategic software, data, and business consulting partner to the life sciences industry, supporting the industry's most critical drug development, quality and manufacturing, and commercialization functions."
"Technology partner" became "software, data, and business consulting partner." "Quality and manufacturing" was added as a named function for the first time, aligning with Quality Cloud's new standalone status.
▶Employee growth nearly flat: +119 (vs. +428 prior year)Signal
"As of January 31, 2025, we had 7,291 employees worldwide, up by 119 from the previous year."
Net employee additions declined from +428 to +119, the lowest level since before COVID. Headcount growth of 2% is a fraction of the 22-37% annual growth seen in FY2019-FY2022.

Risk Factors & Competition

▶Salesforce named as "primary CRM competitor" with IQVIA partnershipNew
"Our CRM solutions primarily compete with Salesforce, Inc., which is developing a life sciences industry-specific CRM application and has entered into a partnership with IQVIA Holdings, which also offers various data products and other applications that compete with our products."
FY2024 did not name Salesforce as the primary CRM competitor or mention its IQVIA partnership. This language appeared in both the Competition section and Risk Factors.
▶Customer loss acknowledged for the first timeNew
"As we transition from our legacy Veeva CRM application to our Vault CRM application, as discussed in more detail below, certain customers have chosen, and other customers may in the future choose, to purchase CRM solutions from a competitor. For example, Salesforce, our primary CRM competitor, recently announced that a large Veeva CRM customer has committed to purchasing its CRM solutions."
The first acknowledgment that a specific large customer had committed to a competing CRM solution. The language "certain customers have chosen" confirms this was not merely hypothetical.
▶AI risk factor addedNew
"Incorporating AI in our solutions may result in reputational harm and increased liability."
"We recently began incorporating AI capabilities into certain of our solutions, which presents new risks and challenges that could affect the adoption of our solutions and our business."
A new standalone AI risk factor covering: controversy around AI impact on privacy, security, efficacy, bias, and discrimination; loss of user confidence; evolving legal obligations; and potential restrictions on AI use. This risk factor did not exist in prior filings.
▶DEI metrics removed entirelyRemoved
FY2024 disclosed: "44% of our global employee population self-identified as female and approximately 42% of our U.S. workforce self-identified as members of underrepresented racial or ethnic groups." FY2025 removed all diversity statistics. The Human Capital Resources section retained only headcount, compensation overview, and talent retention language. No explanation was provided for the removal.
▶IQVIA data restrictions expanded; Salesforce termination details disclosedChanged
"IQVIA has stated publicly that it will deny all customer requests for use of new IQVIA data types in Veeva applications, including, as examples, real world data, real world evidence, and genomics."
IQVIA restrictions expanded from specific product limitations to a blanket denial of new data types. Separately, Salesforce agreement wind-down terms were disclosed in detail: no new customers after September 2025, existing customer seats capped at 150% of September 2025 levels, full termination after September 2030.

FY2025 → FY2026: CRM Deadline & IQVIA Truce

Filed Mar 20, 2026 | Employees: 7,291 → 7,928 (+9%) | Revenue: $2,747M → $3,195M (+16%) | Sub %: 84% | Operating income: $691M → $916M | Diluted EPS: $4.32 → $5.44 | Operating cash flow: $1,090M → $1,415M

CRM Migration & Salesforce

▶Veeva CRM end of support pulled forward eight months: September 1, 2030 to December 31, 2029Changed
FY2025: "Veeva CRM and some of its related applications are built on a platform provided by Salesforce, Inc. and will be supported until September 1, 2030."
FY2026: "Veeva CRM and some of its related applications are built on a platform provided by Salesforce, Inc. and will be supported until December 31, 2029."
The first change to the end-of-support date since it appeared in FY2024; the same change is made in the Vault CRM migration risk factor. The Salesforce wind-down still runs to September 1, 2030 in the risk factors, so Veeva now plans to stop supporting the legacy product eight months before its contractual right to sell it ends. The 10-K gives no reason; a shorter window is consistent with pushing remaining customers to decide sooner.
▶CRM defections go plural: "a large Veeva CRM customer" becomes "certain large Veeva CRM customers," plus customers who "have informed us"Signal
FY2025: "For example, Salesforce, our primary CRM competitor, recently announced that a large Veeva CRM customer has committed to purchasing its CRM solutions."
FY2026: "For example, Salesforce, our primary CRM competitor, has announced that certain large Veeva CRM customers have committed to purchasing its CRM solutions and a number of our customers have informed us of their intent to move to Salesforce as their CRM provider."
The loss count goes from one customer to an unspecified plural, and a second source is added: notices from Veeva's own customers, not just Salesforce's announcements. No customer count or revenue figure is given. Top-10 customer concentration held at 28% for a third year.
▶Customer loss moved from the migration risk to the competition risk; "or at all" deletedChanged
FY2025: "The migration of our customers to our Vault CRM applications built on our own Veeva Vault platform could cause business disruptions for customers, lead to the loss of our customers to competitors, and adversely affect our operating results."
FY2026: "We face intense competition in markets in which we operate—particularly in the CRM market as we transition customers from our legacy CRM application to our Vault CRM application—and if we do not compete effectively, we may lose customers and our business and operating results could be adversely affected."
In the summary list the migration bullet loses "lead to the loss of our customers to competitors" and drops from sixth to eighth; the competition bullet, second, now names CRM. The full migration risk factor also deletes "Further, certain customers have decided, and other customers may in the future decide, not to migrate to Vault CRM" and the outcome "or our customers do not migrate to the Vault CRM in a timely manner, or at all." Migration risk is now framed as execution ("disruptions, delays"), and losses as competition. That is consistent with treating the defections as a known outcome rather than an open migration risk.
▶Salesforce "is developing" becomes "has developed"; IQVIA "historically offered" CRM and "has licensed its CRM software to Salesforce"Changed
FY2025: "Our CRM solutions primarily compete with Salesforce, Inc., which is developing a life sciences industry-specific CRM application and has entered into a partnership with IQVIA Holdings, which also offers various data products and other applications that compete with our products."
FY2026: "Our CRM solutions primarily compete with Salesforce, which has developed a life sciences industry-specific CRM application. IQVIA, which historically offered a competitive CRM solution, has licensed its CRM software to Salesforce."
The competing product is now described as shipped, not in development. IQVIA moves from Salesforce's "partnership" to a former CRM vendor that has licensed its software to Salesforce, so the CRM contest is described as two-way. Separately, the Salesforce agreement is now in the past tense: "Our agreement with Salesforce expired on September 1, 2025."

Business Overview

▶Veeva AI introduced: agentic AI across the Vault platform, with Anthropic and Amazon models named and LLM costs added as an expense driverNew
FY2026: "Veeva AI adds agentic artificial intelligence ("AI") to our proprietary Veeva Vault platform and deep, industry-specific agents for Veeva applications."
FY2026: "Moreover, our AI Agents use large language models from Anthropic and Amazon, hosted on Amazon Bedrock."
FY2025: "We recently began incorporating AI capabilities into certain of our solutions, which presents new risks and challenges that could affect the adoption of our solutions and our business."
FY2026: "Veeva AI, an initiative that adds AI to our applications across all major areas, including clinical, regulatory, safety, quality, medical, and commercial, presents new risks and challenges that could affect the adoption of our solutions and our business."
AI goes from "certain of our solutions" to "all major areas." Vault CRM and PromoMats now list integrated AI Agents. A new expense bullet names "costs incurred with our use of large language models associated with our Veeva AI offering." Investors' comparable set gains "AI companies." The MD&A still does not mention AI, so there is no revenue or margin figure attached to it yet.
▶Hiring re-accelerates: +637 employees (+9%) after +428 and +119Signal
FY2025: "As of January 31, 2025, we had 7,291 employees worldwide, up by 119 from the previous year."
FY2026: "As of January 31, 2026, we had 7,928 employees worldwide, up by 637 from the previous year."
This ends the two-year deceleration tracked in Signal #8. Net adds are more than five times FY2025's. The MD&A attributes increases in cost of services, R&D, and sales and marketing to "increases in salaries and benefits, as well as headcount." Revenue still grew faster (+16%) than headcount (+9%), and operating income rose 33%.

Risk Factors & Legal

▶IQVIA litigation settled with no damages; the IQVIA data-access risk factor deleted entirelyRemoved
FY2025: "If the third-party providers of healthcare professional and healthcare organization data and prescription drug sales data, such as IQVIA for instance, do not allow our customers to upload and use such data in our solutions, the demand for our solutions may decrease, and our business may be negatively impacted."
FY2026: "On August 13, 2025, Veeva and IQVIA entered into a settlement agreement that resolved all ongoing litigations between Veeva and IQVIA."
FY2026: "Under the terms of the settlement agreement, neither party paid damages to the other party and both parties agreed to dismiss with prejudice all claims and counterclaims currently pending."
The summary bullet and the full risk factor, including "IQVIA has stated publicly that it will deny all customer requests for use of new IQVIA data types in Veeva applications", are removed. The 10-K does not say whether data access was part of the settlement, so reading the removal as a resolved data dispute is an inference. Item 3 shrinks from 311 to 140 words; the California non-compete case (filed 2017, trial set for June 16, 2025) is gone. The cost was non-monetary to IQVIA but not to Veeva: "We paid approximately $31 million to certain law firms with whom we entered into partial contingency fee arrangements, pursuant to which such law firms were entitled to a success fee if certain non-monetary outcomes are achieved." Litigation settlement-related charges, up a net $26M, drove most of the $35M rise in G&A, and Veeva now expects G&A "to decrease in the next fiscal year." IQVIA remains a named competitor in Data Cloud, Development Cloud and Quality Cloud.
▶First share repurchase program: $2B over two years; $180M (801,735 shares) bought by year-endNew
FY2026: "In January 2026, our board of directors authorized a share repurchase program of up to $2 billion of our outstanding shares of common stock."
FY2026: "During the fiscal year ended January 31, 2026, we repurchased and subsequently retired 801,735 shares of our common stock for an aggregate amount of approximately $180 million."
Neither the FY2024 nor the FY2025 10-K mentions a repurchase program. Financing cash flow is now described as relating "primarily to share repurchases," and share repurchases join the list of primary uses of cash. A new risk factor, "Our share repurchase program may not enhance long-term shareholder value," adds that a failure to repurchase "may negatively impact our reputation and investor confidence." Cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments were $6.6 billion, up from $5.2 billion.
▶China: "approximately 2% of our total revenue" and the CAC-denial disclosure removedRemoved
FY2025: "In 2023, certain of our Veeva CRM customers in China were required to request such approval from the CAC and had their requests denied."
FY2025: "While we offer the China CRM Suite, a CRM solution that does not require data to be transferred outside of China, some customers have chosen, and other customers may choose, other CRM providers, which may negatively impact our CRM business in China. Currently, approximately 2% of our total revenue is attributable to China."
FY2026: "In China, for example, we offer the China CRM Suite, a CRM solution that does not require data to be transferred outside of China."
The rewrite of the privacy risk factor keeps the China CRM Suite but drops the revenue figure, the denied approvals, and the admission that China CRM customers have gone to other providers. A number that was disclosed has disappeared, and the filing does not say why.
▶EU Data Act named: EU customers may now cancel subscriptions "without cause"New
FY2025: "Certain customers have exercised that right prior to the contracted end date, and other customers may also choose to do so in the future."
FY2026: "In addition, pursuant to the EU Data Act, which came into effect September 12, 2025, our EU customers may cancel their subscriptions without cause upon providing the notice and after the transition period specified by the Act."
A statutory termination right is added on top of the contractual termination-for-convenience clause, in both the renewal risk factor and the privacy risk factor. The prior sentence about customers who had exercised termination rights is broadened to include customers who "reduce usage." Europe was 28% of subscription revenue in FY2026.

MD&A

▶Growth mix flips: Commercial subscription increase $109M to $153M, R&D and Quality $274M to $247M; "new customers" dropped as a driverSignal
FY2025: "The increase in subscription services revenues consisted of $274 million of subscription services revenue attributable to R&D Solutions and $109 million of subscription services revenue attributable to Commercial Solutions. The increase in subscription services revenue attributable to R&D solutions was primarily due to expanding use of Veeva Development Cloud products by both existing and new customers."
FY2026: "The increase in subscription revenues consisted of $247 million attributable to R&D and Quality Solutions and $153 million attributable to Commercial Solutions. The increase in subscription revenue attributable to R&D and Quality Solutions was primarily driven by the expanding use by existing customers of our Veeva Development Cloud and Veeva Quality Cloud products"
Commercial incremental subscription dollars rose 40% in the year Salesforce defections were disclosed, while R&D and Quality incremental dollars fell 10%. Both explanations now cite "existing customers" only, where FY2025 cited "both existing and new customers," even though the customer count rose 1,477 to 1,552. Subscription growth slowed from 20% to 17%. Total growth held at 16% because services revenue grew: FY2025's increase was "all of which was from growth in subscription services revenues", while FY2026's was "$449 million, of which $400 million was from growth in subscription revenue." The segment is renamed "R&D and Quality Solutions" and is now 55% of total revenue.
