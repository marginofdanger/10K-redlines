FY2020 → FY2021: The Metaverse Pivot

Filed Feb 2022 | Headcount: 58,604 → 71,970 (+23%) | Revenue: $86.0B → $117.9B (+37%) | Op Margin: 38% → 40%

Business Overview

▶Mission statement reframed around metaverseNew
FY2020's mission: "to give people the power to build community and bring the world closer together." FY2021 appended:
"All of our products, including our apps, share the vision of helping to bring the metaverse to life."
The entire company reframed around a new strategic concept in a single sentence.
▶Two-segment reporting (FoA / Reality Labs) introducedNew
Products reorganized under "Family of Apps" and "Reality Labs" with separate financials. First time investors could see FoA operating income ($56.9B, 49% margin) versus RL operating loss ($10.2B). Previously a single segment.
▶European data transfer: "may be unable" escalated to "will likely be unable"Changed
FY2020: "we may be unable to operate material portions of our business in Europe"
FY2021: "we will likely be unable to offer a number of our most significant products and services, including Facebook and Instagram, in Europe"
▶IGTV removed, Reels elevated; Digital Markets Act disclosed; remote work codifiedSignal
Instagram dropped "IGTV" from its product listing. DMA disclosed as "proposed" regulation for the first time. Remote work policy formalized: "We offer most of our full-time employees the option to work remotely on a regular basis."

Risk Factors

▶New standalone risk: "We may not be successful in our metaverse strategy"New
Acknowledged the metaverse bet may fail and "may also divert resources and management attention from other areas of our business." RL losses quantified at "$10 billion" in risk factors for the first time.
▶Apple ATT: prospective → past tense ("has negatively impacted")Changed
FY2020: Apple "plans to make certain changes... that will reduce"
FY2021: Apple "made certain changes... which has negatively impacted, and we expect will continue to negatively impact"
Specific attribution added: "our advertising revenue in the second half of 2021 was negatively impacted."
▶TikTok explicitly named; first-ever global DAU decline; Haugen whistleblowerNew
TikTok named as having "reduced some users' engagement" (previously only "ByteDance"). Q4 2021: first-ever global quarter-over-quarter DAU decline. Frances Haugen spawned new "user well-being" language, government investigations, and securities class actions.
▶Reels monetization gap; climate risk; "opt in or out" replaces "opt out"New
"We plan to continue to promote Reels, which we do not currently monetize at the same rate as our feed or Stories products." Climate change risk added. "Opt out" updated to "opt in or out" reflecting Apple's ATT opt-in framework.

Legal Proceedings

▶FTC antitrust survived dismissal; whistleblower litigation; IDPC stay liftedSignal
FTC amended complaint sustained January 2022 -- existential divestiture risk for Instagram/WhatsApp remains. Illinois biometrics ($650M) and 2018 cyberattack settlements resolved. New stockholder derivative action. Whistleblower-related securities class actions filed. IDPC data transfer inquiry stay lifted.

Key Forward Guidance

▶Unprecedented bearish margin warning; CapEx guided $29-34B; headcount "foreseeable future"Signal
"In 2022, we also expect that our year-over-year total expense growth rates may significantly exceed our year-over-year revenue growth rates, which would adversely affect our operating margin and profitability."
This sentence appeared only once in the entire 5-year corpus and proved prophetic. CapEx guided at $29-34B (vs $19B actual). Headcount guidance still: "We expect headcount growth to continue for the foreseeable future."

FY2021 → FY2022: The Reckoning

Filed Feb 2023 | Headcount: 71,970 → 86,482 (+20%, but 11K layoff announced) | Revenue: $117.9B → $116.6B (-1%) | Op Margin: 40% → 25%

Business Overview

▶"Discovery engine" and "generative AI" appear for the first timeNew
FY2022 Technology section: "we are making significant investments in AI and machine learning, including to recommend relevant unconnected content across our products through our AI-powered discovery engine... and to develop new product features using generative AI." This is the earliest filing signal of the AI pivot.
▶All named competitors removed from Business sectionRemoved
FY2021 named 8 competitors (Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, ByteDance/TikTok, Microsoft, Snap, Tencent, Twitter). FY2022 deleted the entire list. Coincides with FTC antitrust discovery -- naming competitors defines relevant markets.
▶"Expect headcount growth to continue" → 11,000-employee layoff disclosureChanged
FY2021: "We expect headcount growth to continue for the foreseeable future." FY2022 replaced this with detailed layoff disclosure -- "one of the most difficult changes in Meta's history" -- including severance terms (16 weeks + 2 per year of service). "Growth" replaced by "scale" throughout the filing.
▶FoA/RL cost split disclosed; Portal killed; DEI goals removed; "Feed" replaces "News Feed"Signal
First-ever FoA/RL cost split (82%/18%) with admission: "our ability to support our metaverse efforts is dependent on generating sufficient profits from other areas of our business." Portal removed from product listings. Specific diversity targets deleted. "News Feed" became simply "Feed"; Reels moved to 2nd position.

Risk Factors

▶First-ever debt ($10B) as risk; "financial results will fluctuate" elevated to standaloneNew
Meta went from zero debt to $10B in fixed-rate notes. New risk: "the incurrence of indebtedness." "Financial results will fluctuate" elevated to its own risk factor -- the era of predictable growth officially declared over.
▶Cost-cutting acknowledged as risk; Apple accused; DMA/DSA enacted; Section 230 SCOTUSChanged
"Cost reduction measures... may adversely affect these or other business initiatives." Apple described as "expanding their own advertising business" while restricting others. DMA/DSA shifted from "proposed" to enacted. Section 230 Supreme Court review disclosed. Russia/Ukraine war added.
▶Risk ordering: Competition now #1 (was COVID); COVID dropped to bottomSignal
The first operational risk factor in FY2021 was COVID. FY2022 moved Competition to #1 and pushed COVID to near the bottom. The top-3 operational risks became: (1) Competition, (2) Metaverse strategy [new], (3) Financial fluctuations.

Legal Proceedings

▶$725M Cambridge Analytica; social media addiction lawsuits; EU Marketplace tyingNew
$725M Cambridge Analytica settlement (largest consumer privacy settlement at the time). Social media addiction lawsuits (new category): alleging "addiction" in teenagers. FTC challenge to Within Unlimited acquisition. European Commission Marketplace tying complaint. Texas biometrics lawsuit. Ad audience inflation class certified. Q4 2021 earnings securities class action filed.

Key Forward Guidance

▶CapEx guidance $30-33B ("anticipate" replaces "expect"); no total expense range; restructuring $4.6BSignal
Modal verb shifted from "expect" to "anticipate" (weaker commitment). Total expense dollar range eliminated for the first time -- replaced by qualitative warning: "investments will continue to drive expense growth in 2023, which will adversely affect our operating margin." Restructuring section (new): $4.61B in charges across facilities ($2.30B), severance ($975M), data center assets ($1.34B).

FY2022 → FY2023: The Turnaround

Filed Feb 2024 | Headcount: 86,482 → 67,317 (-22%) | Revenue: $116.6B → $134.9B (+16%) | Op Margin: 25% → 35%

Business Overview

▶AI elevated to strategic centerpiece; six investment priorities named for first timeNew
New sentence:
"Across our work, we are innovating in artificial intelligence (AI) technologies to build new experiences."
Six explicit priorities: AI, metaverse, discovery engine, monetization, regulatory readiness, developer efficiency. FY2022 had no structured framework. Investment Philosophy rewritten from aspirational to: "We expect to continue to build on the discipline and habits that we developed in 2022."
▶Threads, WhatsApp Channels, "Subscription for no ads" in EUNew
Three major disclosures: Threads (Twitter competitor), WhatsApp Channels (broadcast messaging), and "subscription for no ads" -- a fundamental change to the European business model where Meta changed its legal basis for behavioral advertising from "legitimate interests" to "consent."
▶Metaverse stripped from RL segment definition; Ray-Ban glasses get Meta AIChanged
FY2022: "RL reflects our efforts to develop the metaverse and generates revenue..."
FY2023: "RL generates revenue from sales of consumer hardware products..."
Ray-Ban upgraded from "Stories" (no AI) to featuring "Meta AI, our advanced conversational assistant."
▶Headcount -22%; "Equity" dropped from DEI heading; COVID language removed; Shops dropped from InstagramSignal
Headcount declined from 86,482 to 67,317. "Diversity, Equity and Inclusion" renamed "Diverse and Inclusive Workplace." Specific ethnicity breakdowns replaced with broad "underrepresented people" percentages. COVID language almost entirely removed. Instagram dropped "Shops" and "Video" from its feature list. Horizon Workrooms dropped.

Risk Factors

▶Entirely new AI risk factor (7 paragraphs) -- most significant addition in the filingNew
Covers: harmful content, misinformation, bias, IP infringement, open-source risks (Llama), hardware dependency ("processing hardware and network capacity, as to which we cannot control the availability or pricing"), and regulatory uncertainty. Did not exist in any form in FY2022.
▶Zuckerberg key-person risk: "combat sports, extreme sports, recreational aviation"New
"Mr. Zuckerberg and certain other members of management participate in various high-risk activities, such as combat sports, extreme sports, and recreational aviation, which carry the risk of serious injury and death."
Unusual and specific enough to suggest legal/insurance counsel required it.
▶COVID standalone risk eliminated; infrastructure risk expanded for AI compute; EUR 1.2B IDPC fineChanged
COVID absorbed into general "catastrophic events" risk. Infrastructure risk adds "availability of components, power, or network capacity." "Platform Integrity" added to risk category name. "Costs are continuing to grow" softened to "We incur significant expenses." EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework adopted but CJEU invalidation warning added. EUR 1.2B IDPC fine disclosed.

Legal Proceedings

▶Section restructured with new categories; FTC administrative proceeding; AI copyright suitsNew
Legal Proceedings reorganized into labeled categories: Privacy, Competition, Securities, Youth-Related (new), Other. Opening preamble: "the number and potential significance of the litigation and investigations involving the company have increased." FTC proceeding seeking to prohibit minors' data for commercial purposes. AI copyright litigation (Kadrey v. Meta, Chabon v. Meta). Multi-state AG youth lawsuits expanded. $725M Cambridge Analytica settlement paid.

Key Forward Guidance

▶RL losses "increase meaningfully in 2024"; first dividend; FB metrics discontinued; China advertisers disclosedSignal
RL: "we expect our RL operating losses to increase meaningfully in 2024" -- the intensifier was deliberately strong. Dividend: First-ever cash dividend ($0.50/quarter). Metrics: "we will no longer report DAUs, MAUs, ARPU, and MAP" -- major transparency reduction. Revenue: Growth "driven mainly by marketer spending in online commerce, which benefited from marketers based in China" -- Temu/Shein implied. CapEx guided $30-37B ($7B range = widest ever, peak uncertainty).

FY2023 → FY2024: AI Infrastructure Era

Filed Feb 2025 | Headcount: 67,317 → 74,067 (+10%) | Revenue: $134.9B → $164.5B (+22%) | Op Margin: 35% → 42%

Business Overview

▶Mission statement rewritten for first time in a decadeChanged
FY2023: "Our mission is to give people the power to build community and bring the world closer together."
FY2024: "Our mission is to build the future of human connection and the technology that makes it possible."
Shifts from empowering users ("give people the power") to Meta building technology. A decade-old mission statement abandoned.
▶"Metaverse" systematically replaced with "next computing platform"Changed
"Helping to bring the metaverse to life" -- removed entirely. "Build the metaverse" replaced with "build the next computing platform" throughout. Metaverse risk factor retitled from "metaverse strategy" to "Reality Labs strategy." RL restructured into "metaverse" (~50%) and "wearables" (~50%). The term became a liability.
▶Meta AI, Llama, and Orion AR glasses disclosed; Threads + infrastructure added as prioritiesNew
Meta AI assistant disclosed as a named product across apps/glasses/web. Llama open-source models disclosed in Business section for first time. Orion AR glasses prototype unveiled. Investment priorities reshaped: "generative AI" (more specific), "Threads" and "infrastructure capacity" added, "regulatory readiness" removed. Meta Spark shut down.
▶DEI completely overhauled: all stats removed, programs ended, "political views" addedChanged
"Diverse and Inclusive Workplace" renamed "The Strength of Our Workforce and People Processes." All diversity statistics removed. "In early 2025, we announced changes to our diversity programs in light of the shifting legal and policy landscape." "Political views" added as a valued form of diversity alongside knowledge, skills, backgrounds, and perspectives.

Risk Factors

▶Content moderation relaxation self-flagged as riskNew
"We may also be subject to increased risk as a result of changes to our content policies and enforcement efforts which we began to implement in January 2025 to further free expression on our platform and mitigate over-enforcement of certain of our content policies."
Meta voluntarily flagged its own January 2025 policy change as a risk factor.
▶Tariffs, energy, AI as co-equal margin drag, ad enforcement as revenue headwindNew
Tariffs risk: "the implementation of tariffs by the United States, China, or other governments." Energy: "computing power, and related energy requirements." AI investments now co-equal with RL as named margin drag. New admission: ad policy enforcement "will have an impact on our revenue in the future, which may be material."
▶EU regulatory progression; youth laws expanded; deepfakes; EU AI Act + UK DMCCChanged
EU "subscription for no ads" DMA investigation detailed (March 2024 proceedings, July 2024 adverse findings). "Less personalized ads" option launched. Youth regulation: named Arkansas, Utah, Texas, California, Florida + Australia social media ban for under-16s. "Deepfakes" added to AI risks. EU AI Act and UK DMCC added as new regulatory regimes. Google Chrome cookie deprecation language softened ("previously proposed").

Legal Proceedings

▶FTC antitrust trial April 2025; EUR 798M Marketplace fine; mass youth arbitration; CFPBSignal
FTC antitrust trial set for April 14, 2025 after summary judgment partially denied. EUR 798M European Commission Marketplace fine (graduated from Statement of Objections). FTC consent order modification proceeding advancing -- Meta's constitutional challenge largely rejected. Youth-related: Brazil, Canada, tribal nations added; mass arbitration from thousands of claimants; DSA investigation opened. CFPB NORA process initiated for financial advertising. Derivative litigation trial April 2, 2025 -- insider trading claim against Zuckerberg survived.

Key Forward Guidance

▶RL "meaningfully" dropped; CapEx $60-65B; ad price +10% inflection; depreciation boost $2.9BSignal
RL: "we continue to expect our RL operating losses to increase in 2025" -- "meaningfully" dropped, "continue to" added. Actual RL loss growth decelerated to +10% (from +18%). CapEx: $60-65B guided for 2025 "to support our core business and generative AI efforts." Ad pricing: Inflected from -9% to +10% -- the most important monetization signal. Depreciation: Server useful life extended to 5.5 years, expected to reduce FY2025 depreciation by ~$2.9B. Online commerce disclosed as largest ad revenue vertical.

FY2024 → FY2025: The Superintelligence Bet

Filed Jan 2026 | Headcount: 74,067 → 78,865 (+6%) | Revenue: $164.5B → $201.0B (+22%) | Op Margin: 42% → 41%

Business Overview

▶Superintelligence declared as a corporate goal -- most material additionNew
"We are also working to develop the next generation of AI models and advance our vision to build superintelligence, which we define as AI that surpasses human intelligence."
FY2024 did not mention "superintelligence" at all. FY2025 uses it multiple times, including the new framing: "deliver personal superintelligence for everyone." The company is defining and committing to a specific AI end-state.
▶Meta AI elevated to standalone FoA product; standalone app launchedNew
Meta AI now listed as a full bullet-point product under FoA, between Messenger and Threads: "Meta AI is an assistant that's available across our apps, as a stand-alone app, on our AI glasses, and on the web." The standalone app is new.
▶Investment priorities reshuffled: "Metaverse" dropped, "Youth" and "Reels" addedChanged
FY2024: "generative AI, our discovery engine, the metaverse and wearables, Threads, monetization..."
FY2025: "AI, Reels and our discovery engine, wearables, monetization, youth, platform integrity..."
"Generative AI" simplified to "AI." "Metaverse" dropped from priorities entirely. "Threads" dropped. "Youth" added as a new named priority. "Reels" promoted to #2.
▶RL expense split: wearables 70% / VR 30% (was 50/50); "metaverse" renamed to "VR and Horizon"Changed
FY2024: "approximately 50% wearables, 50% metaverse initiatives"
FY2025: "approximately 70% wearables, 30% VR and Horizon initiatives"
A dramatic 20-point reallocation toward wearables. "Metaverse initiatives" renamed to "VR and Horizon initiatives." Also: "mixed reality" dropped from all descriptions; "AR" rebranded as "AI glasses."
▶Open source language hedged; new hardware products; Threads/WhatsApp as ad surfacesChanged
FY2024: "We also believe in the power of open source innovation and have released our Llama models"
FY2025: "We have not released everything we have developed historically and expect to continue training a combination of open and closed models going forward."
Open source commitment explicitly hedged. New hardware: Oakley Meta glasses, Meta Ray-Ban Display, Meta Neural Band (EMG wrist device -- now shipping, not exploratory). Threads and WhatsApp added as named ad placement surfaces for the first time. Pay equity disclosures removed. Meta stores disclosed as RL sales channel.

Risk Factors

▶AI risk factor expanded: "superintelligence," "products liability," FTC/AG scrutiny of AI chatbotsChanged
AI risk factor now covers "including generative AI and superintelligence" (FY2024: only "generative AI"). Added "products liability" to AI risk list. Added: "we have been the subject of media and government scrutiny relating to AI and AI chatbots, including inquiries and investigations by the FTC, members of Congress and state attorneys general." New: "specialized personnel in a limited and competitive talent market." Frontier AI model competition explicitly called out.
▶Tariff language broadened; DMA fine landed; capital-raising language added to expense riskChanged
"Tariffs" broadened to "tariffs or other existing or future trade policies." DMA updated: "In April 2025, the European Commission issued a final decision that our 'subscription for no ads' model does not comply." New: "From time to time we may also seek to raise additional capital through debt, equity, or other financing arrangements." TikTok removed from named competitors in Risk Factors user engagement paragraph in FY2025 (was still named in FY2024).
▶Computing needs driver officially shifted from user engagement to frontier AI trainingChanged
FY2024: "as engagement with products like video, VR, and MR increases... our computing needs continue to expand"
FY2025: "Historically, the increases in our computing needs have been driven by growth in our user base... Our increased efforts toward building frontier AI models have driven a significant further expansion in our computing needs"
The driver of computing expansion has officially shifted from user engagement to frontier AI model training.

Legal Proceedings

▶FTC antitrust trial won; derivative settled; youth exposure "hundreds of billions"; Flo Health $8B riskSignal
FTC antitrust: Meta won. Trial concluded May 27, 2025; judgment in Meta's favor November 18, 2025. FTC appealing. Derivative litigation settled in principle July 2025. FTC consent order modification: DC Circuit reversed on jurisdictional grounds, proceeding stayed. Youth litigation: Trials commencing (first personal injury Jan 2026, first AG trial Feb 2026). Mass arbitration expanded from "thousands" to "over one hundred thousand" demands. New disclosure: "the maximum aggregate monetary damages or penalties sought across our various legal proceedings could amount to an aggregate of up to hundreds of billions of dollars." Flo Health Meta Pixel case: adverse jury verdict, potential $8B exposure. AMI v. Meta (Spain): EUR 542M adverse judgment. DMA "subscription for no ads" fine: EUR 200M. AI copyright: won fair use summary judgment for named plaintiffs, but new cases added.

Key Forward Guidance

▶RL losses "remain similar" (the inflection signal); "discipline and habits" retiredSignal
RL losses -- the exact inflection signal we were watching for:
FY2024: "we continue to expect our RL operating losses to increase in 2025"
FY2025: "we expect our 2026 RL operating losses to remain similar to 2025"
"Increase" → "remain similar." Also: "continue to expect" (reiteration framing) → "we expect" (new statement). And in the Business section, the RL purpose framing shifted from "the next chapter of the internet" (FY2024 Business + MD&A) to "the next computing platform" (FY2025 MD&A), though the Business section inconsistently retained "the next chapter of the internet."

"Discipline and habits" retired entirely:
FY2024: "We expect to continue to build on the discipline and habits that we developed in 2022 when we initiated several efforts to increase our operating efficiency, while still remaining focused on investing in significant opportunities."
FY2025: "We remain focused on operating efficiently while investing in significant opportunities."
The Year of Efficiency callback -- retained verbatim for 2 years -- is gone. What was a 2-line sentence became half a line.
▶Headcount dropped from forward investment language; R&D definition reordered for AI infrastructureSignal
Forward AI investment -- "headcount" silently removed:
FY2024: "we expect our AI initiatives will require increased investment in infrastructure and headcount."
FY2025: "we expect our AI initiatives will require significantly increased investment in infrastructure."
Three changes in one sentence: (1) "headcount" removed entirely, (2) "significantly" added as intensifier, (3) the object is now singular. This signals headcount growth may moderate while capex accelerates.

R&D definition -- infrastructure costs promoted ahead of RL tech:
FY2024 R&D definition: "...employee compensation; RL technology development costs; infrastructure costs; facilities"
FY2025 R&D definition: "...employee compensation; infrastructure costs; RL technology development costs; facilities"
Infrastructure costs moved from 3rd to 2nd position, ahead of RL tech costs. AI infrastructure spend now exceeds RL-specific technology development within R&D.

R&D expense driver -- SBC explicitly called out again:
FY2024: "higher employee compensation was mainly from a 13% growth in employee headcount... in engineering and other technical functions supporting our continued investment in our family of products and Reality Labs."
FY2025: "higher employee compensation was primarily from an 8% growth in employee headcount... in engineering and other technical functions and an increase in share-based compensation expense."
Headcount growth decelerated (13%→8%). SBC explicitly named as a driver again (only happened once before, in FY2023). The "supporting our continued investment in our family of products and Reality Labs" purpose clause was dropped entirely.
▶CapEx $115-135B guided; AI listed first; ad surfaces expanded; OBBBA tax; debt doubledSignal
CapEx guidance nearly doubled:
FY2024: "approximately $60 billion to $65 billion in 2025 to support our core business and generative AI efforts"
FY2025: "approximately $115 billion to $135 billion in 2026 to support our AI efforts and core business"
Ordering flipped: "AI efforts" is now first, "core business" second. "Generative AI" simplified to "AI." Range width expanded from $5B to $20B -- significant uncertainty at this scale.

Ad surfaces expanded:
FY2024: "ads that can appear... on Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, and third-party applications"
FY2025: "ads that can appear... on Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, Threads, and WhatsApp, as well as third-party applications"
Threads and WhatsApp added as named ad placement surfaces for the first time -- material monetization milestone.

Open source hedged:
FY2024: "We also believe in the power of open source innovation and have released our Llama models"
FY2025: "We have not released everything we have developed historically and expect to continue training a combination of open and closed models going forward."

OBBBA tax: $15.93B one-time charge. Effective rate 12%→30%. Net income declined -3% despite revenue +22%. Debt: Doubled $29B→$59B. $103.77B in not-yet-commenced lease obligations. FX hedging initiated for the first time. Non-marketable equity investments tripled ($6B→$27.5B including new equity method investments).

2 R&D: Headcount vs. SBC

FY2023 is the only year SBC was named as the R&D driver -- because headcount fell -22% yet R&D still grew +9%. The entire increase came from higher SBC per remaining employee.

  • Headcount % disclosed when growing (40%, 30%, 26%, 13%)
  • Refused to disclose decline % during layoff year
  • FY2024: "infrastructure costs for R&D" appears as new named driver -- the AI compute signal
  • FY2025: R&D +31%, driven by "higher employee compensation and infrastructure costs related to research and development, including our AI initiatives." SBC explicitly mentioned again. Infrastructure costs moved ahead of RL tech costs in the R&D definition -- reflecting AI infrastructure now dominating.

3 Cost of Revenue: Depreciation Signature

FY2024 cost of revenue growth: "primarily due to higher operational expenses... mostly from higher depreciation expense." The dominant cost of delivering Meta's products is now depreciation on infrastructure -- not people, not content. This is the financial signature of an AI infrastructure company.

The FY2024 return included "headcount" alongside "infrastructure." FY2025 then silently dropped "headcount" from the same sentence while adding "significantly" to infrastructure. This is the clearest signal yet that Meta's growth model has shifted from people-intensive to capital-intensive. Headcount growth decelerated from +10% to +6% while capex nearly doubled.

5 Headcount % Disclosure Asymmetry

In expense driver explanations, management always quantified headcount growth percentages by function:

  • FY2020: R&D +40%, M&S +16%, G&A +23%
  • FY2021: R&D +30%, M&S +9%, G&A +25%
  • FY2022: R&D +26%, G&A +20% (M&S % dropped)
  • FY2023: No % for any line -- only "a decrease in employee headcount"
  • FY2024: R&D +13% (M&S, G&A not given)
  • FY2025: R&D headcount % not separately broken out; total headcount +6% but forward guidance drops "headcount" from investment language entirely

Management is comfortable quantifying growth but refuses to quantify contraction. The disclosure granularity is also narrowing -- FY2020 gave all 3 functions, FY2024 gives only R&D.

6 The Layoff Language Arc

  • FY2020-21: No mention of restructuring or layoffs anywhere
  • FY2022: "One of the most difficult changes in Meta's history." Detailed severance terms. Restructuring charges ($4.61B) appear in every expense line. Restructuring added to expense definitions.
  • FY2023: "We took a number of steps to reduce our expense base." Emotional framing replaced with operational language. Restructuring "substantially completed."
  • FY2024: "Lower restructuring charges" appears as a benefit in M&S and G&A. Restructuring removed from expense definitions entirely. The cycle is over.
  • FY2025: "Lower restructuring" no longer mentioned at all. Restructuring cycle fully behind. Headcount growing +6% but forward guidance drops "headcount" from investment language.

Identical language FY2023-24. Positions efficiency as cultural, not cyclical. FY2025 REMOVED this language entirely, replacing it with "We remain focused on operating efficiently." The Year of Efficiency callback is retired after 2 years. The spending acceleration cycle has begun.

The Superintelligence Bet

Meta became the first major public company to name superintelligence as a corporate goal in its 10-K. Combined with $115-135B capex guidance and debt doubling to $59B, this represents the largest single-company infrastructure bet in corporate history. The filing defines superintelligence as "AI that surpasses human intelligence" and commits to delivering "personal superintelligence for everyone." This is no longer an R&D aspiration -- it is a stated business objective with tens of billions in committed capital behind it.
