Primo Brands 10-K Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of SEC filings tracking Primo Water Corporation through European divestiture, transformational merger with BlueTriton Brands, and first-year integration stress.
Source: SEC EDGAR | Fiscal Years 2022-2025 | Analysis: March 2026 | FY2022-23: Primo Water Corp (Ontario) | FY2024-25: Primo Brands Corp (Delaware)
FY2022 → FY2023

European Divestiture

Sold European business for $575M to Culligan. Collapsed from 2 segments to 1. Dropped from 21-country to North America-only. Headcount fell from 9,240 to 6,400. Facilities: 63 to 38.

FY2023 → FY2024

BlueTriton Merger

Primo Water + BlueTriton closed November 2024. New entity (Delaware). Brand portfolio replaced. $5B+ debt. One customer at 24% of net sales. Headcount doubled to ~13,700. Complete filing rewrite.

FY2024 → FY2025

Integration Stress

CEO replaced within one year. Infrastructure aggressively rationalized (70+ to 50+ facilities). Securities and consumer class actions filed. Brand growth claims softened. Spring asset exclusivity retracted. $5.16B debt.

FY2022 → FY2023: European Divestiture

Filed Feb 2024 | Primo Water Corporation | Headcount: 9,240 → 6,400 (-31%) | Net Sales (cont. ops): ~$2.2B → $1,771.8M | Facilities: 63 → 38 | Branches: 318 → 186

Business Overview

▶Geographic description narrowed: Europe removed, revenue figure droppedChanged
FY2022: "Primo is a leading pure-play water solutions provider in North America and Europe and generates approximately $2.2 billion in annual revenue."
FY2023: "Primo is a leading North America-focused pure-play water solutions provider that operates largely under a recurring revenue model in the large format water category"
Europe removed from geographic description. Revenue figure ($2.2B) eliminated. "North America-focused" inserted.
▶European divestiture disclosed as first step in full international exitNew
FY2023: "This deal is the first of several transactions that will occur in 2024 as part of a Board-approved plan to sell all of our international businesses representing a strategic shift in our operations."
European Business sold to Culligan for $575.0M ($565.9M after adjustments). Remaining International Businesses (UK, Israel, Portugal operations) reclassified to discontinued operations.
▶Segments collapsed from two to oneChanged
FY2022 reported two segments: North America and Europe (plus Other category including Israel, Aimia, Farrers). FY2023 collapsed to a single North America segment. Eden Springs brand removed from listing. All international businesses moved to discontinued operations.
▶Infrastructure contracted: facilities, branches, country footprintChanged
Production facilities: 63 to 38. Branches: 318 to 186. Country footprint: 21 countries to North America only. "Self-service refill drinking water machines" changed to "stations" in FY2023. Water filtration offering narrowed from "across its 21-country footprint" to "across North America."
▶"Global" removed from strategic pillar languageChanged
FY2022 strategic pillars referenced "global" operations multiple times. FY2023 removed "global" throughout, consistent with the North America-only focus. Watercoolers Europe association membership dropped; only International Bottled Water Association retained.
▶Carbon neutrality: European claim dropped, U.S. only retainedChanged
FY2022: "Our European operations have maintained carbon neutrality for more than eleven years, and our U.S. operations achieved carbon neutral certification in 2020 under the CarbonNeutral Protocol... In 2021, the Company achieved carbon neutrality on a global basis."
FY2023: "Our U.S. operations achieved carbon neutral certification in 2020 under the Carbon Neutral Protocol, an international standard administered by Climate Impact Partners."
European carbon neutrality claims and the global achievement statement removed.
▶DEI: Chief Diversity Officer paragraph removedRemoved
FY2022 included a paragraph naming CDO Shayron Barnes-Selby and describing the DEI Strategy Committee and recruitment lens. FY2023 removed this paragraph entirely.

Risk Factors

▶Supply chain tense shifted from present to pastChanged
FY2022 used present-tense language for supply chain disruptions ("are having," "have experienced," "has been able"). FY2023 shifted to past tense ("had," "experienced," "was able"), indicating supply chain pressures had eased.
▶COVID-19 replaced by extreme weather as named disruption riskChanged
FY2022 referenced "COVID-19 pandemic" as a supply chain and operational risk. FY2023 replaced this with "extreme weather-related incidents and natural disasters" as the named disruption risk.
▶Nanoplastics added to BPA risk factorNew
FY2023 added "or the presence of nanoplastics in water generally" to the existing BPA/packaging safety risk factor.
▶Dividend increased: $0.08/share to $0.09/shareChanged
Quarterly dividend per share increased from $0.08 (FY2022) to $0.09 (FY2023).

FY2023 → FY2024: BlueTriton Merger

Filed Feb 2025 | Primo Water Corp → Primo Brands Corp | Headcount: 6,400 → ~13,700 (+114%) | Net Sales: $1,771.8M → $5,152.5M | Facilities: 38 → 70+ | Complete filing rewrite

Business Overview

▶Entity changed: Ontario corporation to Delaware corporationChanged
Primo Water Corporation (Ontario) became Primo Brands Corporation (Delaware). Organized in 2024 initially as Triton US HoldCo, Inc. The entire filing was rewritten for the new entity.
▶Self-description transformed: water solutions provider to branded beverage companyChanged
FY2023: "Primo is a leading North America-focused pure-play water solutions provider that operates largely under a recurring revenue model"
FY2024: "Primo Brands is a leading North American branded beverage company with a focus on healthy hydration, delivering responsibly and domestically sourced diversified offerings across products, formats, channels, price points and consumer occasions"
▶"Razor-razorblade" recurring revenue model language eliminatedRemoved
FY2023 described the business strategy as "commonly referred to as 'razor-razorblade' because the initial sale of a product creates a base of users who frequently purchase complementary consumable products." FY2024 eliminated this framework entirely. The dispenser-driven recurring revenue model was replaced by a retail-heavy branded beverage model.
▶Brand portfolio wholesale replacement: BlueTriton brands addedNew
Legacy HOD brands (Crystal Springs, Crystal Rock, etc.) replaced by BlueTriton brands: Poland Spring, Pure Life, Arrowhead, Deer Park, Ice Mountain, Ozarka, Zephyrhills, Saratoga, AC+ION, Splash Refresher. Multiple billion-dollar brands (those generating more than $1B in annual net sales) now in portfolio.
▶Customer concentration: from "no customer >10%" to one customer at 24%Signal
FY2023: "We have limited customer concentration as no customer accounts for more than 10% of our net revenues."
FY2024: "we have one customer who makes up approximately 20% of Trade receivables, net and 24% of Net sales."
▶Spring water sources and asset ownership first disclosedNew
FY2024: "We have more than 90 spring water sources located across North America, and we are the only branded beverage company whose brands own their spring assets, which strengthens our profitability and helps us further support our sustainability and water stewardship efforts."
Also disclosed: 38 company-owned sites, 5 joint-relationship sites, 3 owned artesian wells plus 1 leased.
▶Infrastructure scaled: 70+ facilities, 240+ depots, 200,000+ retail outletsNew
Post-merger infrastructure: 70+ production facilities (was 38), 240+ depots (was 186), delivery fleet of approximately 5,900 vehicles, 200,000+ retail outlets. Combined Primo Water and BlueTriton footprints.
▶$300M cost synergy target and $204.1M integration costs disclosedNew
FY2024: "we expect more improvements to our distribution capabilities as we look to capture an estimated $300 million cost synergy opportunity that includes optimization of our combined manufacturing locations, routes, branches, and inventory management."
Integration costs: $204.1M. Management fees to One Rock Capital Partners: $53.4M.
▶"Water Your Way" strategic pillars and "Customer for Life Promise" eliminatedRemoved
FY2023 strategic framework "Inspire Healthier Lives With Water Your Way" and its pillars, including the "Customer for Life Promise," were entirely eliminated in FY2024. Replaced by a new framework organized around brand portfolio, distribution infrastructure, and product diversification.
▶DEI section eliminated; carbon neutrality claims droppedRemoved
DEI section entirely removed from the filing. Replaced with "values-driven culture" language. All carbon neutrality claims dropped -- no reference to carbon neutral certification in FY2024.
▶Seasonality statement added; competition section expandedNew
FY2024: "Our water sales are generally higher during the warmer months."
Competition section expanded from brief paragraph to multiple paragraphs covering private-label competition and tap filtration. Government Regulation section expanded from approximately 2 paragraphs to multiple pages covering FDA, FFDCA, FSMA, FTC, EPA, Prop 65, SB 253/261.

Risk Factors

▶Risk factor #1: Integration and expanded operations (new category)New
The first risk factor in FY2024 concerned the merger integration and challenges of managing the combined entity. This risk category did not exist in FY2023.
▶$5B+ debt introduced; new "Risks Related to Our Indebtedness" categoryNew
An entirely new risk category for indebtedness appeared. Total debt exceeded $5 billion.
FY2024: "we may not be able to generate sufficient cash flows from operations to service all of our indebtedness and may be forced to take other actions to satisfy our obligations under our indebtedness, which may not be successful"
▶PFAS first mentioned as contamination riskNew
Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) first disclosed as a contamination and regulatory risk. Not present in any Primo Water filing.
▶Geographic concentration: U.S. 97.4%, Canada 2.6%New
First explicit geographic revenue split disclosed. U.S. accounted for 97.4% of net sales, Canada 2.6%. Named leadership: CEO Robbert Rietbroek, Non-Executive Chairman Dean Metropoulos, CFO David Hass, COO Rob Austin.

Legal Proceedings

▶Legal proceedings collapsed to minimal disclosureSignal
Legal proceedings text went from ~24,050 characters (FY2023) to 595 characters (FY2024). The only matter mentioned was the ongoing Patane class action against Nestle Waters (Poland Spring labeling). This minimal disclosure coincides with the merger year transition.

FY2024 → FY2025: Integration Stress

Filed Feb 2026 | Primo Brands Corporation | Headcount: ~13,700 → ~12,600 (-8%) | Net Sales: $5,152.5M → $6,664.0M | Facilities: 70+ → 50+ | CEO replaced

Business Overview

▶CEO replaced: Rietbroek out, Foss in as Executive Chairman & CEOSignal
FY2024 named CEO Robbert Rietbroek and Non-Executive Chairman Dean Metropoulos. FY2025 executive officer list shows Eric J. Foss as Chief Executive Officer and Executive Chairman. The CEO was replaced within one year of the merger close (November 2025).
▶Brand growth claim softened: "Nearly all" to "A majority"Changed
FY2024: "Nearly all of our core brands have demonstrated strong, volume-driven growth in recent years."
FY2025: "A majority of our core brands have demonstrated strong, volume-driven growth in recent years."
▶Spring asset exclusivity claim retractedChanged
FY2024: "we are the only branded beverage company whose brands own their spring assets, which strengthens our profitability"
FY2025: "We are a leading branded beverage company whose brands own a material amount of spring assets, which strengthens our ability to supply our products"
"The only" became "a leading." "Own their spring assets" became "own a material amount of spring assets." "Profitability" softened to "ability to supply our products."
▶Infrastructure aggressively contracted in a single yearChanged
Production facilities: 70+ to 50+. Depots: 240+ to 200+. Delivery fleet: ~5,900 to ~4,800 vehicles. Spring sources: 90+ to 80+. Company-owned spring sites: 38 to 36. Artesian wells disclosure removed entirely. Headcount: ~13,700 to ~12,600 (1,100 reduction).
▶Delivery quality language softened: "ensuring" to "supporting"Changed
FY2024: "Our differentiated logistics model enables us to deliver water efficiently from the spring source directly to customers, ensuring reliability and quality."
FY2025: "Our differentiated logistics model enables us to deliver water efficiently from the spring source directly to customers, supporting reliability and quality."
▶"Domestically" removed from sourcing descriptionChanged
FY2024: "delivering responsibly and domestically sourced diversified offerings"
FY2025: "delivering responsibly sourced diversified offerings"
"Domestically" was removed. "Every state and Canada" became "every U.S. state and Canada."
▶Coffee removed from product listing entirelyRemoved
FY2024: "Our products consist primarily of premium spring and sparkling water, purified water, self-service refill drinking water, flavored and enhanced beverages, water dispensers, filtration equipment and coffee."
FY2025: "Our products consist primarily of premium spring and sparkling water, purified water, self-service refill drinking water, flavored and enhanced beverages, water dispensers, and filtration equipment."
Coffee and coffee services removed from all product descriptions.
▶$300M synergy target removed; integration language addedChanged
FY2024 disclosed "an estimated $300 million cost synergy opportunity." FY2025 removed this specific target, replacing it with general optimization language. Simultaneously, FY2024's aspiration about "opportunities to further streamline our operations" was replaced with:
FY2025: "The optimization of our complete footprint across our manufacturing locations, routes and branches remains in focus."
▶Beverage market size estimates revised upwardChanged
Non-alcoholic beverage market: "$130+ billion" (FY2024) to "$150+ billion" (FY2025). Bottled water segment: "$25 billion" (FY2024) to "$30 billion" (FY2025).

Risk Factors

▶Integration disruption explicitly acknowledgedSignal
FY2025: "We have experienced disruption to our supply chain as well as technology transfer issues, and we may continue to experience interruptions in our business activities, including deterioration in our associate and customer relationships, increased costs of integration, and harm to our reputation"
This acknowledgment of actual disruption replaced FY2024's forward-looking integration risk language.
▶Cash flow language escalated: "may not" to "do not expect"Changed
FY2024: "we may not be able to generate sufficient cash flows from operations to service all of our indebtedness"
FY2025: "we do not expect to be able to generate sufficient cash flows from operations to service all of our indebtedness"
The modal verb shifted from conditional possibility ("may not") to stated expectation ("do not expect"), a material escalation.
▶Moody's downgrade disclosed (March 2025)New
FY2025: "in March 2025, Moody's Ratings downgraded the ratings on certain of our debt securities, and it is possible that a ratings agency may take action to downgrade our credit ratings, or the ratings of our debt securities, in the future."
▶PFAS risk expanded with specific liability paragraphChanged
FY2024 mentioned PFAS as a general contamination risk. FY2025 expanded with a specific paragraph about PFAS liability at current and former properties.
▶SEC climate rules: "SEC voted to end its defense"New
FY2025: "We may also be subject to the SEC's climate disclosure rules, if enforced by the SEC leadership and if such rules survive litigation pending in the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals challenging them. In March 2025, the SEC voted to end its defense of the rules and as a result, the rules remain in effect but are not currently enforceable."
▶Geographic shift: U.S. 97.4% to 98.5%, Canada 2.6% to 1.5%Changed
U.S. share of net sales increased from 97.4% to 98.5%. Canada declined from 2.6% to 1.5%. UK and Israel operations (Eden Springs) divested.
▶Customer concentration slightly improved: 24% to 21% of net salesChanged
FY2024: "we have one customer who makes up approximately 20% of Trade receivables, net and 24% of Net sales."
FY2025: "we have one customer who makes up approximately 20% of Trade receivables, net of allowance for expected credit losses and 21% of Net sales."
▶Raw materials: polycarbonate removed; glass, aluminum, LDPE addedChanged
FY2025 added glass, aluminum, and LDPE to the raw materials listing. Polycarbonate bottles removed. Intangible asset impairment of $35.6M disclosed (not present in FY2024).

Themes

Merger Stress

The Primo Water + BlueTriton merger produced a $6.7B revenue company with $5.2B debt, integration disruption acknowledged in the filing, CEO replacement within 12 months, three lawsuits including a securities class action, and a consumer class action alleging post-merger service failures from facility closures and staff reductions. Integration costs totaled $271.8M in FY2025 (up from $204.1M in FY2024).

From Subscription to Retail

Pre-merger Primo Water was a "razor-razorblade" recurring revenue business with no customer exceeding 10% of net revenues. Post-merger Primo Brands is a retail-heavy branded beverage company with one customer at 21% of net sales and competition from private-label water. The razor-razorblade framework was eliminated entirely from the filing.

Claims Retracted Under Pressure

  • "The only" branded beverage company owning spring assets → "a leading"
  • "Nearly all" core brands with strong growth → "A majority"
  • "Ensuring" reliability and quality → "supporting"
  • "Responsibly and domestically sourced" → "responsibly sourced"
  • $300M synergy target → removed

Debt Dependency

$5.16B in total debt with the filing explicitly stating the company does not expect to generate sufficient cash flows to service it at maturity. Moody's downgrade in March 2025. Management fees to One Rock ($53.4M in FY2024) eliminated in FY2025. Debt structure requires sustained refinancing access. Interest expense: $326.5M in FY2025.
