UNH 10-K Redline Analysis

Year-over-year textual analysis of UnitedHealth Group's SEC filings (FY2018-FY2025). Revenue grew from $226B to $448B. MCR rose from 81.6% to 89.1%. FY2025 disclosed an OptumHealth operating loss of $(278M), a $672M premium deficiency reserve, a $2.5B restructuring charge, an AM Best downgrade to A-, and a CEO change.
Source: SEC EDGAR (CIK 0000731766) | Fiscal Years 2018-2025 | Analysis: March 2026

FY2018 → FY2019: Steady Growth

Employees: 300K → 325K | Revenue: $226B → $242B | MCR: 81.6% → 82.5%

Business

▶Backlog growth signals pipeline strength; Optum revenue accelerationSignal
The backlog continued to grow steadily, reflecting strong pipeline conversion across both UHC and Optum segments. Optum's revenue growth outpaced UHC for the first time in several metrics. The filing language remained largely template-stable -- incremental updates rather than structural rewrites.
▶Template-stable filing: minimal language changes year-over-yearSignal
The FY2018 to FY2019 filing was stable in language and structure. Risk factors, business descriptions, and segment narratives were updated with new numbers but the framing was nearly identical. This stability contrasts with the extensive rewrites that began in FY2020.

FY2019 → FY2020: COVID

Employees: 325K → 330K | Revenue: $242B → $257B | MCR: 79.1%

Business / Structure

▶Optum listed before UHC for the first time in filing orderChanged
For the first time, Optum's business description appeared before UnitedHealthcare's in the filing structure. Previously, UHC's business description appeared first. In the FY2020 filing, Optum segments were described before UHC for the first time, placing the growth business ahead of the insurance business in the narrative order.
▶Human Capital section added per SEC mandate; health equity language introducedNew
The SEC's new Human Capital disclosure requirement led to the addition of a dedicated section. UNH used this as an opportunity to introduce "health equity" language for the first time, framing workforce diversity and community health outcomes as strategic priorities. This language would grow in subsequent filings before being cut approximately 70% in FY2025.
▶MCR plunges to 79.1% as COVID suppresses utilizationSignal
The medical care ratio dropped sharply as COVID-19 lockdowns suppressed elective procedures and routine care. This created a one-time profitability windfall that would reverse in subsequent years as deferred care returned. The MCR of 79.1% was the lowest in the company's recent history. MCR would not return to this level in any subsequent year, rising to 82.6% (FY2021), 83.2% (FY2023), 85.5% (FY2024), and 89.1% (FY2025).

FY2020 → FY2021: Witty Era

Employees: 330K → 350K | Revenue: $257B → $288B | MCR: 82.6%

Business / Identity

▶Witty becomes CEO; UNH rebrands as a "well-being company"Changed
Andrew Witty became CEO, joining from GlaxoSmithKline. The filing introduced "well-being company" framing, broadening UNH's self-description beyond health insurance and health services.
▶Value-based care dollar figures removed from disclosureRemoved
Previously, UNH had disclosed specific dollar figures for value-based care arrangements. In FY2021, these quantitative disclosures were removed and replaced with qualitative language about VBC's strategic importance. Specific dollar figures did not return in subsequent filings. By FY2025, VBC language shifted to "to contract."
FY2020: "contracts with value-based elements totaled $79 billion in annual spending, including $20 billion through risk-transfer agreements"
FY2021: specific dollar figures removed; replaced with qualitative VBC growth language

FY2021 → FY2022: Change Healthcare

Employees: 350K → ~400K | Revenue: $288B → $324B | MCR: 82.0%

Business / Strategy

▶Change Healthcare acquisition closes; backlog surges +$7.6BNew
The $13.6B acquisition of Change Healthcare was the largest in UNH's history. The backlog immediately surged by $7.6B, reflecting Change's existing contract base. Change Healthcare processed billions of healthcare transactions annually, adding health IT infrastructure to UNH's existing insurance, care delivery, and pharmacy operations.
▶"Global" segment removed; international operations de-emphasizedRemoved
The UnitedHealthcare Global segment was removed as a separate reporting unit, with international operations folded into or de-emphasized in the disclosure. This signaled the beginning of UNH's international retreat, which would culminate in the FY2024 sale of the Brazil business at a $7.1B loss. The filing quietly reduced the visibility of international operations.
▶"Those who serve" language appears in mission/values framingNew
New language emphasizing service to "those who serve" -- military, veterans, public sector workers -- appeared in the values and mission framing. UNH's government business includes Tricare and VA partnerships.

FY2022 → FY2023: Peak Profitability

Employees: ~400K → 440K+ | Revenue: $324B → $372B | MCR: 83.2%

Business / Innovation

▶AI/ML language appears for the first time in business descriptionNew
For the first time, UNH's 10-K incorporated artificial intelligence and machine learning language in its business description, describing how AI/ML was being applied to claims processing, clinical decision support, and fraud detection. UNH's described AI applications were focused on operational efficiency: claims processing, clinical decision support, fraud detection, and care management optimization.
▶"Quality" and "inclusion" added to corporate values languageNew
The corporate values section was expanded to incorporate "quality" as a named value and strengthen "inclusion" language. These additions represented the peak of UNH's DEI and quality-focused corporate narrative. Both terms were removed in FY2025.
▶Medicaid redeterminations risk factor added; membership pressure anticipatedSignal
A new risk factor disclosed the impact of Medicaid redeterminations as the COVID-era continuous enrollment provision expired. States began re-verifying eligibility, leading to membership losses in the Medicaid/dual-eligible population. The filing language acknowledged this would pressure UHC Community & State membership and revenue.

FY2023 → FY2024: Cyberattack

Employees: 440K+ → ~400K | Revenue: $372B → $400B | MCR: 85.5%

Business / Crisis

▶Change Healthcare cyberattack: $3B+ total cost; new cybersecurity risk factorsNew
The February 2024 cyberattack on Change Healthcare affected the personal data of approximately 100 million individuals. Total costs exceeded $3 billion including remediation, notification, business interruption, and provider support. The filing added extensive new cybersecurity risk factors and incident disclosures.
▶Brazil operations sold at $7.1B loss; international retreat acceleratesSignal
UNH completed the sale of its Brazil health insurance operations, recognizing a $7.1 billion loss. The "Global" segment had been removed from reporting in FY2022. The "Global" segment that had been removed from reporting in FY2022 was now being financially unwound.
▶MCR jumps +230bps to 85.5%; medical cost pressure acknowledgedSignal
The medical care ratio surged 230 basis points to 85.5%, the largest single-year increase in recent history. Filing language shifted from confident cost management to acknowledging "elevated" medical cost trends, particularly in Medicare Advantage.
FY2023: "and we have observed increased care patterns as discussed below in 'Medical Cost Trends.' Our 2024 benefit design approach contemplates these trends."
FY2024: "and we have observed increased care patterns as discussed below in 'Medical Cost Trends.' Our 2025 benefit design approach contemplates these trends."
Note: identical language retained between FY2023 and FY2024 -- MCR rose 230bps but the narrative language did not change, suggesting management viewed the increase as within expected bounds.
▶Headquarters adds Washington DC office to existing Minnetonka HQNew
For the first time, UNH disclosed a DC office alongside its Minnetonka, Minnesota headquarters. CMS revenue concentration was 40% of total revenue in FY2024, and the filing added new language about congressional scrutiny and state-level investigations.

FY2024 → FY2025: The Crisis

Employees: ~400K → 390K+ | Revenue: $400B → $448B | MCR: 89.1%

Medical Costs / Financial Distress

▶MCR 89.1%: "well-short of medical cost trends" -- admission of pricing failureChanged
The medical care ratio hit 89.1%, up 360bps from 85.5%. The filing language changed from describing cost trends as "elevated" to stating that pricing was "well-short of medical cost trends."
FY2024: "To price our health care benefits, products and services, we start with our view of expected future costs, including medical care patterns, inflation and labor market dynamics."
FY2025: "To price our health care benefits, products and services, we start with our view of expected future costs, including medical care patterns, the mix and health status of people served, inflation and labor market dynamics. For 2025, our pricing trends and patient and member health status assumptions were well-short of the medical cost trends incurred, significantly impacting our earnings."
▶OptumHealth operating loss $(278M) vs $7.8B prior year; VBC "to contract"; $623M loss reserveSignal
OptumHealth swung from $7.8 billion in operating income to a $(278 million) loss -- an $8.1 billion reversal. The VBC language shifted from expansionary to "to contract." A $623 million loss reserve was established.
FY2024: "organic growth in patients served under value-based care arrangements"
FY2025: "we expect the number of people served under value-based care arrangements to contract"
▶Favorable prior-year development collapses: $840M → $140MSignal
Favorable prior-year reserve development dropped from $840 million to $140 million -- an 83% decline. Favorable development as a percentage of reserves fell from approximately 2.3% to 0.3%.
▶Premium deficiency reserve $672M established; $2.5B restructuring chargeNew
A $672 million premium deficiency reserve was established for the first time -- an accounting recognition that premiums on certain contracts are insufficient to cover expected claims. Simultaneously, a $2.5 billion restructuring charge was recorded.

Leadership / Governance

▶Hemsley returns as CEO at age 73; DeVeydt from Elevance; Conway from CMS; Thompson ZERO referencesSignal
Stephen Hemsley returned as CEO at age 73, replacing Andrew Witty. The filing disclosed critical leadership changes: John Rex DeVeydt joined from Elevance Health (the direct competitor), Heather Conway came from CMS (the regulator). Thompson had zero remaining references in the filing.
▶Investment policy oversight: Board → Audit Committee; "and loan" added to portfolioChanged
Oversight of the investment policy was transferred from the full Board to the Audit Committee, and the phrase "and loan" was added to describe the investment portfolio. The "and loan" addition indicates the portfolio now includes direct lending instruments.
FY2024: "under an investment policy authorized by our Board of Directors. This policy limits the amounts which may be invested in any one issuer and generally limits our investments to U.S. government and agency securities, state and municipal securities and corporate debt obligations of investment grade."
FY2025: "under an investment policy authorized by the Audit & Finance Committee of the Board of Directors. The investment policy establishes defined limits on credit quality, security selection, and permissible asset classes to ensure a disciplined and risk-appropriate investment approach."

Capital / Ratings

▶Credit Negative: AM Best A → A-; subsidiary dividends $9.2B → $535M infusionsSignal
AM Best downgraded the insurance subsidiary financial strength rating from A to A-. The cash flow direction reversed: subsidiaries went from paying $9.2 billion in dividends to the parent to receiving $535 million in capital infusions. The direction of cash flow between parent and subsidiaries reversed.
FY2024: "Our U.S. regulated subsidiaries paid their parent companies dividends of $9.2 billion."
FY2025: "Our U.S. regulated subsidiaries received capital infusions, net of dividends, of $535 million."
▶Buybacks halved; zero share repurchases in Q4Signal
Share repurchases were cut approximately in half year-over-year, and the company executed zero buybacks in Q4. The company had repurchased approximately $7-8B annually in FY2023-24.

Language / Tone Shifts

▶"frequently" → "continually"; "will continue seeking" → "seek" -- hedging languageChanged
Across the filing, confident forward-looking language was systematically replaced with more cautious alternatives. "Frequently" became "continually" (less committal). "Will continue seeking" became simply "seek" (removing the forward commitment). These changes appeared across multiple sections of the filing.
FY2024: "We frequently evaluate and adjust our approach... We will continue seeking to balance growth and profitability across all these dimensions."
FY2025: "We continually evaluate and adjust our approach... We seek to balance growth and profitability across all these dimensions."
▶"Quality" dropped from values; "health equity" dropped; DEI language -70%Removed
The corporate values section was significantly pared back. "Quality" was removed as a named value. "Health equity" language, introduced in FY2020 and expanded through FY2023, was dropped entirely. Overall DEI-related language was reduced by approximately 70%.
FY2024: "integrity, compassion, inclusion, relationships, innovation, performance and quality... advance health equity... data-driven and leader-led... enterprise and business scorecards... hiring, developing, advancing and retaining diverse talent"
FY2025: "integrity, compassion, inclusion, relationships, innovation and performance"... [quality removed, health equity removed, scorecards removed, diverse talent pipeline language removed, mentorship programs removed]

Regulatory / Operational

▶CMS revenue concentration: 40% → 44%; OI backlog first declineSignal
Government revenue concentration increased to 44% of total, with CMS (Medicare + Medicaid) as the dominant source. Simultaneously, OptumInsight's backlog declined for the first time in the company's history -- breaking a multi-year streak of consecutive growth.
FY2024: "Premium revenues from CMS represented 40% of UnitedHealth Group's total consolidated revenues"
FY2025: "Premium revenues from CMS represented 44% of UnitedHealth Group's total consolidated revenues"
▶Prior authorization: "elevated audit tools" language appearsNew
New language around prior authorization described the use of "elevated audit tools" in the authorization process. This language appeared in the same filing that added an AI-specific risk factor. CMS finalized rules on prior authorization timelines, and multiple states enacted PA reform legislation.
FY2025 (new sentence, not in FY2024): "Additionally, we have elevated our audit, clinical policy and payment integrity tools to protect customers and patients from unnecessary costs."
▶AI risk factor added; behavioral health and mental health parity language expandedNew
A new AI-specific risk factor was added, acknowledging regulatory uncertainty around AI use in healthcare decision-making. Separately, behavioral health and mental health parity language was significantly expanded, reflecting both regulatory pressure (the Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act enforcement) and litigation risk around parity compliance.
▶Corporate practice of medicine risk acknowledged in filingNew
For the first time, the filing acknowledged corporate practice of medicine as a regulatory risk. UNH operates both insurance (UHC) and care delivery (OptumHealth). Corporate practice of medicine laws restrict corporate entities from directly employing physicians or controlling medical decisions in many states.
▶Employee mix shift: -10K total headcount but +25K clinical employeesSignal
Total headcount declined by approximately 10,000, but clinical employees increased by approximately 25,000. This means non-clinical (administrative, technology, corporate) headcount was cut by roughly 35,000. The $2.5B restructuring charge was concentrated in administrative functions. OptumHealth's care delivery workforce continued to expand while the segment posted a $(278M) operating loss.
FY2024: "Our nearly 400,000 employees... including more than 140,000 clinical professionals"
FY2025: "more than 390,000 employees... nearly 165,000 were clinical professionals"

Themes

The Crisis Year

FY2025 metrics: MCR 89.1% (+360bps), OptumHealth $(278M) loss vs $7.8B prior year, AM Best downgrade to A-, subsidiary cash flows reversed ($9.2B dividends to $535M infusions), buybacks halved (zero in Q4), $672M premium deficiency reserve (first-ever), $2.5B restructuring charge, net earnings approximately $9B vs $16B prior year.

The VBC Wall

VBC filing language progressed from quantified dollar figures (FY2018-20, including "$79 billion in annual spending") to removed dollar figures (FY2021) to qualitative growth language (FY2022-23) to "to contract" (FY2025). OptumHealth operating income went from $7.8B (FY2024) to $(278M) (FY2025), and a $623M loss reserve was established.

CMS 30% → 44%

CMS premium revenue rose from approximately 30% of total revenue (FY2018) to 44% (FY2025). MA membership grew from approximately 5.2M to 7.3M over the same period. The FY2025 filing cited "Medicare funding reductions" as a driver of both MCR deterioration and OptumHealth's operating loss.

Change Healthcare: Moat to Breach

Change Healthcare was acquired for $13.6B (FY2022), added $7.6B to backlog, and processed billions of healthcare transactions annually. In February 2024, a cyberattack on Change Healthcare affected approximately 100 million individuals and cost $3B+. Ongoing remediation and litigation continued through FY2025.

The Hemsley Reset

The CEO position changed three times: Wichmann (FY2018-20), Witty (FY2021-24), Hemsley (FY2025, age 73). FY2025 simultaneously added DeVeydt from Elevance Health and Conway from CMS. Thompson references were reduced to zero. Hemsley previously served as CEO from 2006 to 2017.

OptumRx: The Survivor

OptumRx revenue grew from approximately $87B (FY2020) to $130B+ (FY2025). Adjusted scripts increased from 1.3B to 1.6B. Revenue per script rose from approximately $67 to $81, partly driven by GLP-1 volumes. OptumRx continued growing through FY2025 while OptumHealth posted a $(278M) operating loss and UHC's MCR reached 89.1%.

The Vertical Integration Question

UNH operates across insurance (UHC), care delivery (OptumHealth), pharmacy (OptumRx), and health IT (OptumInsight/Change Healthcare). Intersegment revenue grew from approximately $40B (FY2020) to $75B+ (FY2025), representing roughly one-third of Optum's revenue. In FY2025, the filing added "corporate practice of medicine" as a new regulatory risk, expanded language on prior authorization scrutiny, and acknowledged vertical-integration-related regulatory and legislative activity. UHC segment earnings fell from approximately $14B (FY2023) to approximately $5B (FY2025); Optum segment earnings fell from approximately $16B (FY2023) to approximately $10B (FY2025).
